Attitude towards relationships: Closed systems tend toward entropy. The relationship should be monogamous, but not the total focus of our attention. Even if we lived together, we should have our own lives and hobbies.

Life goals: Should have/want a job either in the nonprofit or federal sector- nothing corporate (or, if he *must* work in the private sector, be an IT geek). Must want to live in a large city, preferably on the East Coast. Should realize that learning and self-improvement is an ongoing, exciting process that never ends. General question for the group: When you consider what you want in a boyfriend/girlfriend, for purposes of sharing that information with others, do you define it by what you feel you would prefer, what you know gets your motor running, or both?

The reason I ask is that I know some people (including me in past days) find certain aspects of the opposite sex intriguing or appealing from afar, and will acknowledge those traits as attractive if asked. However, what often happens is that when that person eventually does hook up with someone that fits the bill, so to speak, they find those quirks they found potentially wondrous are, in fact, nothing they care to deal with on a consistent basis.

This generally holds for personality traits, not physical ones. If I think brunettes are hot, I'm going to keep thinking brunettes are hot, even if one or two turn out to be shitheads. But I've seen, for example, a guy think a tough-minded, straightforward girl would be great to have, then buckle when he got together with one and found out she was too much for him to deal with. I'm curious how many people might be leaving themselves open to similar disappointment.

As for me, now.. if I find her attractive and she's cool, that's all I need. Cool is an undefinable concept, so further description would be useless, but most of you should know what I mean.
