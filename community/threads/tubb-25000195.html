Einstein had an prediction about light and gravity- he said that if you traveled in space at the speed of light and shined a light (perpendicular to the direction of travel) that the point at which you could see the light's beam on a surface opposite the source would be lower than the source itself.
As to light being a wave or particles i think that einstein (or maybe some other guy) proved that light is made up of particals by shining a light at a sensor from 100 metres away. Funnily enough the sensor measured the speed of the light as going at the speed of light. He then got in a car, drove at 30mph and shined the light at the sensor again. The sensor reported that the light was still moving at the speed of light (no change). Although this doesn't appear to prove anything is you do an experiment with waves. Get a small trough of water. Put your hand in one end of the trough (fingers down, back of hand facing the direction you are going to move your hand). Now move your hand at a regular pace until you reach the middle of the trough at which point you should stop moving your hand- stop and note how fast the wave moves. Now, wait for the water to settle and do the same again but flick your wrist forward when you reach the middle point of the trough. You will see that the wave moves faster than before. If you imagine your moving hand to be the light beam and the flick of the wrist to be the additional speed of the car then its plain to see that waves act in a differently manner than light.

From what I understand, Chunk, you're disregarding the wave properties of light. Both the particle and wave properties of light can be shown using a piece of paper with slits in it and a sensor at one of the slits. With the sensor off, the light goes through like a wave would, producing a constant and uniform bar of light (blocked by the piece of paper at points obviously). With the sensor on, to record the light passing through, the results are as if light was a particle, with dots as a result. Both the particle theory and the wave theory explain different aspects of light.

The big thing about this light experiment is how can both things happen? It breaks into new barriers, showing that perhaps a multiverse it does exist. In one universe the light behaves as a wave. In another alternate universe the light behaves as a particle.

My apologies if I got any of that wrong.
