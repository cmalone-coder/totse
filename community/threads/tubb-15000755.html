Said like a true modern philosopher. Which is to say it doesn't have anything to do with the question.

Is there a God? Well, what's God? Maybe a spiritual (i.e., doesn't obey the laws of physics as we know or can know them) entity with a will/intelligence that is the cause of the universe, or at least plays a role in its existence.

That the universe can't explain itself seems pretty reasonable given things like the second law of thermo--there's a "winding down" to the universe, a decay and dispersal. It hasn't gone to infinity, so the simplest explanation is that the universe has existed a finite time, so it must have had a cause, and the cause for the universe can't be physical, since it must exist outside space and time; it must be spiritual. Aristotle thought this was *necessarily* true, but of course it's not; it just seems reasonable. If you postulate that the universe has existed backward into infinity, it's easier to imagine spiritual (extra-physical) forces keeping the decay from going to infinity.

So some sort of spiritual entity seems reasonable to explain the universe. As for intelligence and a will...one can argue either way, although personally the idea that any kind of multicellular life, let alone mammals, let alone us, came to exist without some kind of supernatural nudge is kind of hard for me to swallow. The thermodynamics, again, says that if you put the chem prof out on the rock and leave him there till the reaction goes to completion, you wind up with simple carbon compounds. It don't go the other way, naturally. (Yes, that's an oversimplification, but it's fun to say.)

So I would say that it's more likely than not that there's a God...but that's all you can say without that God revealing itself in some way. Then you're off "theism" anyway.

WOW! And I mean WOW!

I am impressed. My singular statement really generated myriad comments.

Now BuD, I will expand on my comment but only briefly (I hope).

In trying to look at basic man, I look to the caveman: simple, uneducated, curious, and utimately a survivalist (Xeno's definition). The caveman would look up at the sky and wonder about the Sun, Moon, Stars, Clouds, Lightning, et al and attempt to explain the unknown through multiple stories and tales. Why? Because he didn't understand and wanted an answer. His answers became the myths and legends which evolved into the religions we are stuck with today.

Everyone already knows what I just said, so now I go back to the initial reason that the caveman asked the question...the caveman had an internal desire to understand (know) why things are the way they are. We are fortunate today to have a bunch of ready made scientific answers to explain away most of the primal questions and those scientific answers give many folks the same satisfaction that the caveman derived from his stories.

I am not of that ilk.

A mere brushoff answer to the question: Why am I here?...to breed, while true, is insufficient. Case in point...man himself. Multiple times I have talked about the lack of evolutionary proof that modern man evolved. Each new skeleton falls directly into one of the catagories already established by archeologists (ie. Neanderthal, Cromagnon, Homo Habilus, Homo Erectus, et al). No skeletons are examples of the transition being (transition being defined as having distinct elements of both groups- an intermediate being if you will). The argument can be made that we just haven't found it yet is getting tedious. We haven't found any transitional skeleton for any of the groups. Each new discovery only reconfirms and fits into one and only one of the original boxes. Now we've done a lap and we are back to the original question: Why?

If we indeed fulfilled the sole purpose of 'just breeding' then the concept of evolution would make sense because we would have a continuous flow of skeletal remains from primordial soup, through the euglena, to the ameoba, to modern man. However, we don't and it is not for want of having looked and looked hard.

My latest information says that modern man (Cromagnon) was an established earthly inhabitant by 100,000 BCE and first appeared as early as 300,000 BCE. Lucy (a Neanderthal) is credited with having lived 2,000,000 BCE and the last of Lucy's descendants died 50,000 BCE. And all of this "evolution" with zero transitional beings between the two species.

This is precisely why I am a theist.

Modern man exists here on the planet Earth for a reason. We are not products of Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. We did not just 'poof' come into existence as the principle claims is the case in atomic particles. Whatever is the reason for mankind's existance is what my theism is all about. I still search. I am not content with the easy, brushoff answers provided by the organized religions or by quantum physics.

Sermon over.

Blindart,

I hope I never come across as a 'modern philosopher'. I am merely a seeker and have no answers, as demonstrated in the above tome. I hope I'm not misunderstanding your point.
