Gearbox oil?
First worriesome noise from my new car, woo!

Anyway, when I had my old Rover and I thought the gearbox oil was low, the mechanic dude said it is usually 5th gear that makes noises first, not 1st, because that is at the top of the gearbox (turned out to be a crownwheel bearing or something, gearboxes are beyond me).

Anyway, I've noticed in this new car, that whenever I use a gear at the top of the gearstick, i.e. 1st, 3rd and 5th, and apply even slight power, there is a strange noise almost like an air leak. I surmised it isn't because 2nd and 4th don't make a noise, and there is still plenty of power.

Then I was driving back home just now and I noticed 5th was making a strange kind of whooshing/whirring sound, but also a sound that could be some kind of metal spinning against another metal, as gearboxes do.

Then I noticed it makes a strange whirring noise when I even push the gearstick over to the right from neutral and not engage the gear.

Then I decided to make a video to aid in anyone who cares to help's analysis: http://s355.photobucket.com/albums/r...Video-0007.flv

You have to turn the sound right up to hear it, and it does make another sort of clacking noise when 5th is engaged, but by phone mic couldn't pick it up. The strange little whirr when I push the gearstick over.

So, is it likely that that is the early warning sign of low gearbox oil? Cause I really don't want it to get worse and have to fork out £400 for a reconditioned box and as today is Sunday there are no garages open.

DO NOT DO THIS!

Frink, yours probably takes real gear oil, but not all manual transmissions do that anymore. For instance, all the FWD GM manual trannies that I'm aware of mad ein the last 30,40 years use fluid similar to 10W30 motor oil. Using a much higher viscosity will rob certain places of oil. In fact, the transmission in my Fiero had heavyweight gear oil in it when I did my engine rebuild and there were assloads of metal that came out with it.

Use whatever fluid your owners manual tells you, unless you have something official tell you otherwise, like a technical service bulletin. (Fiero owners manuals say to use 10W30 but a TSB indicated to use Synchromesh fluid at some point after the manuals printed. The gearboxes run MUCH smoother with the Synchromesh fluid.)
