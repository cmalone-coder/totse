Re: Are humans meant to be monogamous?
So far, scientists have only found one species that's actually monogamous. It's a type of worm that has the male and female actually fuse bodies for life. Aside from that one species, monogamy does not exist.

They've done studies in animal populations that were once thought monogamous. As it turns out, a high percent of "fathers" weren't actually the fathers of the animals being raised.

That being said, we do have reason, which we can use (to an extent). Personally, I think that marriage was extremely useful in a time when people might be living to 30 years old. The point then was to have and raise as many kids as possible before you die. Anymore, that just seems silly because if you get married it could be for 50-60 years. Marriage is for having kids. Staying with someone for that long might work, but I think it's a dying tradition.

If you somehow find a person and you can happily stay with him or her for 50-60 years, then you're far luckier than I think I'll ever be.

Re: Are humans meant to be monogamous?
On an animal level, it is the female's role to secure mating with the best genetic candidate, and ensure that she, and only she, breeds with him. It's the male's role to breed with as many females as possible, providing they're not dire.

The difference, of course, comes because females can only be pregnant with one male's offspring at once. Of course, once she has bore this offspring, she may choose to mate with another male.

There is no reason why we are "meant" to be monogamous. The feelings we have evolved, what we call love, are more accidental than anything.

Most people, at least most men, find they lose the spark from their relationship at some point. That's a hormonal hint that it's time to spread your seed elsewhere.

We consider ourselves so distinct from other animals, but we're not really. Our actions are just a battle between the animal instincts we're born with, and the social constructs we're brainwashed with from that point onward.

But really, there's no "meant" involved anyway. We're just accidents.
