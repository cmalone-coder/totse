Human attraction, genetics, and gold digging
First, a certain degree of physical attractiveness is necessary for two people to have consensual sex. This is well known. Often times, particularly as the stereotype goes this applies to women, the personality of a person can go a long way towards making them more attractive. This still does not change the fact, however, that physical appearance means a good deal. He could be Prince motherfucking Charming, but no woman will find Brian Peppers attractive, ever. So we can all agree that physical attractiveness is at least somewhat important? If you do not agree, please do me a favor and do not respond to this thread.

Second, physical attractiveness is largely a matter outside of our control. For example, it is well known in evolutionary biology we desire a mate with an appearance that suggests they will be a good parent. So men are hardwired to look for child bearing physical traits, and women are hardwired to look for traits that signify a man can provide for children through such things as a physically strong body. Naturally, I am not going to deny that there are people that do not fit this mold due to things such as culture and upbringing. For the most part, this still holds true though, yes? We thus find certain people more attractive than others for reasons entirely not of their own making. All of this moves on to my next point.

Third, because we have no control over our genetics, is it not more honorable to attach yourself to a person with traits that they had to work for, instead of traits they were genetically assigned? For example, if a man works hard or studies hard and becomes wealthy, is this not a "more honorable" reason to be attracted to him than he just so happens to be genetically blessed? However, in our modern culture, that would be considered "gold digging" and the equivalent of a "whore".

You may be thinking that I am misconstruing terms and words, and that in this case, a woman wouldn't be a "gold digger" because she is actually admiring traits that happened to get the man money, not the money itself, and the primary trait the woman finds attractive is his character.

But allow me to retort. This brings up another valid point about cultural and likely personal reflections on attraction. What about another person, say, that inherited wealth? If they did not have a particularly good character and no physical attractiveness, would it still be "wrong" for a woman to be attracted to him, in this case primarily for financial assets? If yes, then we must consider a slightly skewed situation. What if, instead of financial assets, the exact same man possessed striking physical attractiveness? Would it be any better for a woman to be attracted to the man now, that even though he had lackluster character she felt his appearance "made up for it"?

I am bringing all of this up because of the people on this board and their reactions to women and men in relationships. When a woman who was unsure of if she loved her man stated that she is partly staying with her boyfriend because he provides a roof over her head and she doesn't want to provide one for herself, she was berated for being a "manipulative whore" by Totse. But when the same situation arises, but this time with a man using his partner being very physically attractive as an important reason against breaking up with her, almost no one raises an eyebrow. They are essentially the same thing.

Instead of a "gold digger" in the traditional sense, they are a "gold digger of the sensuous", i.e they use something ultimately extremely superficial as a reason to do or not do something.

I just wanted to bring this point up. Why is this sort of thing done, and why is it tolerated? I hope you don't think I'm talking about feminism, as this applies across the sexes in our culture.
