Re: rockets and space satelites
the guy who was building a cruise missile in NZ got his shut-down through pressurisation from the government

I read about that, I thought he just ran out of money or something. Guess not.

Anyway, your space agency would be limited by the fuels available to you. LOX/Liquid Hydrogen is the most efficient liquid fuel, but it takes a massive amount of energy and equipment to cool it to 3 or 4 degrees Kelvin, to get it in its liquid state. Fuels like hydrazine are way too volatile and hard to produce in the right quantities. Maybe a real good chemist could make a few ounces, but they won't be making tonnes and tonnes of the stuff. Probably the easiest rocket fuel to use is a hybrid, like the one in Virgin Galactic's ship. Next to that, probably a monopropellant peroxide rocket.

Just about anyone could build a rocket to reach the boundary of space, 100 kilometers. But that doesn't matter if you don't have the speed to maintain an orbit, your payload would just fall back down. To reach between 50-250km altitude, you would only need a relatively small rocket, like a sounding rocket. (1-2m wide, 10-15m tall)

To get an orbital satellite, you would need a much more powerful rocket. The Soviet R-7 that launched sputnik had four boosters, each with a diameter of 2.7 meters. The main engine was 3 meters wide. The whole craft weighed 267 tonnes, most of which was liquid oxygen and kerosene. Not the kind of thing you can keep hidden in your garden shed.
