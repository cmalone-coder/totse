Re: shotgun
I think that your best bet would be with a slide-action, or more commonly known as pump.
Any of the major brands would be good. Excellent choices are:
Remington 870
Mossburg 590
Ithaca 37
Winchester 1300

You should have little trouble finding a cheap used one. Then have a qualified gunsmith inspect it and cut the barrel down to 18 or 20 inches. After that, train with it by taking it to the range at least once every two weeks and putting a box of shells through it.

PS. If you're serious about home defense, take a tactical firearms course and learn about protecting your home with other lines of defense, like impenetrable windows and deadbolts. Volumes have been writen on the subject in dozens of gun magazines and books.

Re: shotgun
There are a few companies that sell "special" ammo for shotguns (ie: Dragon's Breath flame-thrower and bean-bags to name a couple). They sell 12 gauge rounds that use hollow copper balls instead of pellets, these flatten on impact and will not over penetrate (say, your standard in-house sheetrock/gypsum wall). As for pistol grip versus standard shoulder gunstock, you should shoot a 12 gauge with just a pistol grip first before you decide that is for you. Your shoulder absorbs the recoil with much less felt pain (think of it as holding out your hand and letting someone whack you in the palm with a baseball bat). The only way to counter the recoil is to lighten the weight of the load, or use less powder in the charge, or make the gun heavier. As for tube or clip fed and capacity, tube is less likely to stovepipe jam and if you didn't hit him with your first two shots, you are already dead. A 20 gauge is equal to a 12 in the hands of a trained person, less felt recoil and little difference otherwise.

Re: shotgun
1. A shotgun is a 2 handed weapon, use it as such

2. Any gauge will kill a person when used correctly

3. Loads should be based on the set up of your home, do you have dry wall or plaster walls? Small home or no?

4. Pistol grip stocks are for very special situations, such as very cramped spaces or when you will be doing a lot of hip firing, which is not recommended. A pistol gripped shoulder stock will give you the best control.

You should pick a reliable design, The Rem 870, Win 1300, or Mossberg 500 are all good guns, but are not defense ready out of the box. Guns built on these models do great. However the Mosserberg 500D, the Mossberg 590 and the Winchester 1300D are quite ready out of the box. My 590 came with a heatshield, sling, and bayonet lugs. I had installed ghost ring sites, and a pistol gripped stock. It'll hold 9 shots and is very accurate, though it has a cylinder bore.

If you have drywall use a light game load, a modified choke though may be prefferable. Plaster walls can stand up to heavier loads, such as #4 buck, which is NOT used in Turkey Loads! #4 Shot and #4 buck are not the same thing! Both of which though will kill a person.

A Gunsmith does a better job on nearly all modifications to a gun. You can use a hack saw, but you will have a rough cut that needs to be finished an blued. I have used a pipe cutter with good results, but then I've been doing much of my own gunsmithing for years, except for drilling and tapping (I don't have the proper jigs), I do my own.

Stay away from fantasy ammo or less lethal ammo. None of it has been proven effective. And I doubt that shooting fish hooks at people will stop them. Dragonsbreath is a joke for all a practical purposes, neat to see, it's just a novelty.

Taking a class is about the best thing yo can do, buy from a gun store that has a range so that you may try out the weapons before you buy them. Find a gauge you feel comfy with, you may decide you'd rather have a 20ga, and there is nothing wrong with that. I've hunted with a 20ga for years and can out shoot all my buddies, mind you they all carry 12ga pumps, my favorite gun is a my 20ga NE Topper Model. I can get off well aimed shots just as fast as they can with their trumpet guns. Kinda odd though I've always thought. I paid $100 for that NE used with 2 barrels, I paid over $250 for each of my other shotguns and old Stevens, Ithaca 37 Featherlight, 3 Mossberg 500's, and a Remington 870E (which does hold 6rnds), and time after time, I'm picking up the cheapy. It's a lot lighter to carry around all day, and damn it's fun. But I wouldn't go to war with it.

Accessories you many be interested in are a side saddle shell carrier, ghost ring sights, and sure fire light on the forearm. Some people add a laser, but that's personal prefference for fast shooting I'm much better at instinctive shooting than using sites on a shot gun, even some of my rifles, and definitely handguns. But a light is very useful. Make 911 the first thing you use. It's during your wait you should try to leave the building taking your family with you, if things get complicated during that time, well, that's what you have the gun for.

Whatever you get practice clearing your home often, of course with the weapon unloaded. If you rearrange things, practice twice as much for a few weeks. But then your arrangements should have some tactical thought to them as well. Also keep a cell phone or a cordless phone near your weapon at all times. Make
