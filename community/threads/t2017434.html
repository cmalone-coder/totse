I have a proposal for fixing the issues with users.
I've been thinking about this for awhile, and I've come up with a plan. Previously, I've talked about the plan here and there to a few people, but feel it's time to make it public.

The problem.
The problem is that there is an increasingly large number of bullshit posts, threadshitting, no-content posts, and users who think the epitome of posting is calling each other faggots in every thread. I'm not talking about spammers, who are blatanly breaking the rules. I'm talking about posters who are degrading the quality of the forum with this ridiculous "content".

So far, I've heard solutions like "Well, just ignore them", which sounds like a great idea in theory, except for the fact that these people have more accounts than the Mormons have bibles. Ignoring the problem doesn't solve it, and people posting "OMG IGNORE THEM" is only going to encorage these users, and cause them to make more accounts.

What about banning them? Again, it sounds good in theory, until they just come back, again and again, with a multitude of ways to get back in and continue to shit up the place. Obviously, banning users doesn't work, and only begins to create infamy, which many of them are trying for.

Of course, infractions are equally as useless, as they don't do anything except eventually get the user banned, or the user can make a new account as soon as a few infractions are received, essentially making them immune to infractions. Infractions do nothing to solve this problem.


The Solution
So, what's the solution here? It's quite simple, and extremely effective. Require a small amount, such as $5 for registration of an account. Everybodies first reaction here is "PAY FOR FORUM? WHAT?" It's understandable, if they don't see the whole picture.

The point of this isn't about money. The amount is just some arbitrary amount, and it shouldn't be shown as a way to somehow try and make money off this site. This place has ads for income generation, so this obviously isn't the issue.

The point of this is simple: equating value to something that otherwise has no value. Face it, users accounts have no value. None. When a user can make 50 accounts in a row and start posting whatever the hell they feel like, they don't give a shit about following the rules, getting "ignored", banned, infracted, or anything. Should all of their accounts run out, they just make more. Accounts are an infinite resource, and like supply and demand, their value is zero.

By equating value to users accounts (monetary is the easiest way, but any other sort of value works as well, should anybody think of some), it makes posters realize that what they've got is no longer just a throwaway item. Now, by getting rid of the shitposters, spammers, and users who otherwise break the rules, they have lost something of value, by breaking the rules.

Nobody is going to spend $5 just to post "ive had ur mum to appear" or making threds titles "ur all r faggots lolololol" when they know they're going to get banned for it. This will improve the overall quality of the posts, and the forum on the whole, as well as making things more enjoyable to read, which in turn will attract more (legit) users to the site.

Now, there's a few things that have been brought to my attention as possible drawbacks here. One being, that it would be "unfair" to current users. This isn't the case, as every user who currently has an active account, can keep their account. Only new accounts are subject.

The second being, that users who would otherwise contribute, won't join, if this is implemented. Perhaps a few users will be turned away, yes, but the elimination of spam, threadshitting, no-content posts, and other rule-breaking which degrades the forum will be eliminated. Seeing this, new users would be more likely to join, as they see their account not only having value as they've attached $5 to it, it also has value in the fact that they know that their posts and contributions to the forum won't be drowned out by a heap of shitty users, causing better posts, and better threads.

Third, the case of getting the account fee, since the average age on this site tends to be in the hischool range. Every major bank around will open a checking account and give a visa check card to anybody, so nobody should be somehow prevented from registering if they choose. PayPal or other such methods are also an easy way for it. I had a Visa check card I could use online when I was 15, no parents required. Again, all current users will keep their accounts, so this isn't meant to ostracize anybody. $5 is less than the price of a movie ticket nowadays. Maybe it'll get you a Big Mac meal at McDonalds. This is one time, not recurring.

Wrap-up

Bans would work like bans, except a user isn't banned permanently, they just have to reactive or reregister a new account (and are subject to the $5). Permanently banning by could be done for serious offenses, like say, child porn. Probationary periods could be offered for minor infractions, to serve more like a warning to users. The infraction system could apply towards a probation of an amount of time, depending on offense, ranging from a few hours, to a few days.

Of course, to keep some of the "atmosphere" of the old site, so to speak, forums like Half Baked could be designated to where users CAN post like that. There they can spread internet memes, post garbage, or do whatever the feel like (provided it doesn't break one of the fundamental rules of the site) without repercussion. Bitch & Moan could be the same way. This allows users who feel the need to post Bel-Air to be able to do so without shitting up the rest of the site and irritating other users. However, all "standard" forums should be expected to have a sense of decency when posting.

This can be applied to thinks like leet speak, AOL-speak, binary, or any other ways of otherwise not having a sense of decency when posting, but it's entirely up to the administration what would qualify and what would not. For the record, I vote leet speak, AOL speak, or other shit speak should not be allowed. I really don't see it being too much to ask to add a y and an o to "your". This is all based on intent. Nobody is saying that your posts have to be 100% perfect and clear of typos, and nobody is saying that posting like that can't be used satirically for example. It's very obvious who's using it in context, and who's using it just because they don't feel like taking the extra six seconds to make a decent post.

The big question is, who's going to front this? I have no issue being the one, being the one to post about the changes, and organize a detailed set of rules and guidelines, provided I have support from the administration. Essentially, be the face of the proposal.



The first few days after implementation can be a crossover period, with no severe penalties for problems, aside from blatant rule-breaking. Sure, some users are going to feel the need to "test" the administration, and think it's absolutely hilarious to try and post "lol fag" at the first opportunity, but very quickly, users will realize (perhaps after an example or two of people breaking the rules) that the forums are getting cleaned up, and turned around, the quality of the threads are going up, better posts will be made, spam will be all but eliminated, and overall, Totse will be a genuinely awesome forum to be a part of.


Any feedback is welcome.

I think that charging for immediate access and having a waiting period for free access would probably work well to cut down on spam.

The problem isn't a waiting list. The problem is that even with a waiting list, the account is still worth nothing. So what if you have to wait 2 weeks for it? People will still register a dozen accounts and relax for two weeks until they become active. Not to mention they can continue to register accounts as they want and will have new ones every few days that way.

The auto-banning via ignore list has the potential to be abused. If such a system were used, it may be wise to not let it be made known to everyone.

This is why it's a bad idea. So what, somebody registers 10 bullshit accounts and then goes and starts autobanning everybody they see? Terrible. However, with the $N a pop for an account, that won't even be needed, as when somebody gets ignored, it's a hell of a lot bigger step to hop on another account to get "unignored" again. And it is NEVER a good idea to assume that these ideas can be hidden from users. See, with the huge spread of mods now (as opposed to say, 4 years ago), it's impossible to hold any information secret from users. Sure, maybe I could pull something like that off on my site, but just because it's me and I'm not telling it to anybody. But when you've got 20-30 people with access to that info, it's going to leak, and then work retroactively. Shit, I'm sure there are some mods who have already been telling their friends "OMG HURRY AND REGISTER EXTRA ACCOUNTS TOTSE MIGHT CHARGE FOR THEM SOON". I wouldn't even be surprised if there were MODS here who have done the same thing.

I wouldn't do monthly, just because people generally tend to dislike subscriptions, even when they amount to the same amount as just a one-time thing. Maybe it would work, or maybe it could be an additional option ($N once or $N recurring). Then again, I know there's some people here who have no problem paying $9 a month for webhosting on some WP blog they run which gets 150 hits a month, so you might be on the right track there.


The issue with pay for immediate access is the fact that the waiting time has to be substantial enough to warrant immediate access. A week-long waiting period isn't substantial enough to make somebody want to activate it immediately.. maybe if the wait time was three months, it would work. On the other hand, it could also screw over some users who are genuinely interested in posting (and happy to drop the few bucks), but since they see they can have an account without dropping the $N, they'll decide to wait it out and possibly lose interest by the time it's ready. I think an all-or-nothing approach is better.

Also, by making some accounts free, it lessens the value of the other accounts. Users are less likely to feel they've got something of value by dropping a few bucks on an account, if they can see people with free accounts running wild (and potentially shitting everything up). Also, the free account people are still less likely to make worthwhile posts, because they have no value attached to their account, since anybody can still make an army of accounts and just hang for a few days until they're active. Unless the waiting period is some over-the-top extreme amount of time, it's not going to do a damn thing to help.

The problem with trying to find inactive accounts is that it's stupidly easy to forge that. All they have to do is make a few bullshit posts somewhere to make it seem like they're legit accounts. This will happen just as soon as people realize that their inactive accounts are getting pruned, and not only do they now have MORE account, they're just going to make shitposts with their other accounts since it takes less effort.

PMing, avatars, and the like are a good incentive, yes, but I don't think they're a big enough incentive to make it work on a big level (there will be people who choose to go for it, regardless, but not the majority by a longshot) to get users into it.
