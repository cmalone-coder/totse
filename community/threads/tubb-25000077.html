From this, your statement must have been in response to one of the following:

1. It is a great misconception that everyone thought that the Earth was flat.
2. That was pretty much limited to Judeo-Christianity and certain primitive cultures.
3. The Greeks, for example, knew the Earth was round.

Or some combination thereof.

If you were in fact referring to the Christian suppression of heliocentricity, I don't see how it's relevant to this discussion, though I did address that after you brought it up.

The idea that the earth is flat originated in religion, and there was no real opposition or attempted suppression of spherical earth theory. There was no good argument against a spherical earth, and many for it, unlike Coperican heliocentricity, which had several problems, along with attempting to explain it within the framework of Aristotelian physics.

So yes, the suppression of heliocentricity is an example of societal influence on the dissemination of scientific thought, but I really don't see the relevance to either evolution or the difference between theory, law and hypothesis.

Now, have you any arguments or evidence against evolution, or not?
