Thats not a perfect example of religion. Religious beliefs are formed on some sort of logic that would have been applicable at the time of that religion's creation.

An example I once heard supporting this referenced the fact that a banana is not completely round. It has a certain number of flat facets, that coincidentally fit into the bends made by human hands. Obviously, we know now that those two things have no correlation, but from the perspective of early man, it wouldn't have been too far out for them to have thought so. Another example is the ancient belief that inside all humans burned a fire. Now, that thought is ridiculous, but following the idea that: fire is hot, and the human body is kept warm by unknown means, it makes marginal sense to people who don't know better.

There are a million other natural coincidences that early people could have easily attributed to some sort of intelligent design. And quite reasonably (for the time), since man was the highest order on earth, and they did not create such fascinating coincidences, something higher must have.

The thing you need to keep in mind is that religions arent founded because one guy says "this is what happened, so thats how it goes". Religious theories of creation, and all early theories of creation for that matter, are based on some sort of worked out thought bytheir founders. The fact that they have long since been proven wrong doesn't mean they were just pulling their ideas out of their ass, which is what you would be doing with your mock-theory.
