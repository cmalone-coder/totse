My pistol, me, and my father
I just wrote this for the Weapons and Combat forum, but it drifted off topic.

Writing this is one of the numerous study breaks I'm taking so that I can put off writing my final paper for my very very interesting and thought provoking Heritage of Western Civilization 55 (55 is for Juniors, even though I'm technically a 2nd semester sophmore and failed 22, and haven't taken 44, oh well.) I'm also on the second bottle of champagne in the last 2 hours (drinking and writing is great), so this might be a bit long and circuitous, not to mention dealing with numerous peripheral information not necessary to the question at hand, and just plain weird (hey, this is how I get when I start writing.)

Anyway, after I turn in my paper tomorrow and take my Spanish final, I get to do my laundry, pack my shit, and be out of my dorm room (yes, I do live in a dorm. I know I suck, but I've lived off campus before and that's why I have a .5 GPA for last fall and had to take off last spring.) In my room, I keep a locking metal briefcase that I refer to as my "situation case." It contains my important documents (diploma, passport, birth certificate, medical records, &c.), some cash, toiletries, and first aid supplies. Since the weekend after I turned 21, it's also contained my CZ-52 pistol in its holster with two loaded mags and the ammo that I have left after visiting the range.

During the winter break, the building staff have access to all the rooms and apparently sometimes conduct searches (which is also apparently legal, as I learned when I tried to defend myself against other landlord abuses based on TX state landlord-tenant law... I digress). Naturally, I'm concerned about my situation case and what would happen if they broke into it. So, I can either give my case to a friend to hold off-campus over the break or I can bring it home with me. I trust the either of the people I'd give the case to, but I'd rather not do that.

So, I plan to bring my case home with me. It's possible that they won't notice the case or won't ask about it, but they're both very smart, observant, and curious people, and I doubt that will happen. If they ask about the case, I want to be able to show them what's in it.

So, that finally brings me to my question of, "How should I explain to my folks that I exercise my 2nd ammendment right by keeping a pistol?" The only reasons I can think to give my parents are the real ones: That I want the gun for personal protection; that it's fun to shoot; and, a very minor reason, being a 50 year old pistol, it has both collectors and possible investment value.

The second and third reason are fairly straight-forward, but can anyone help me find good ways to explain the need for self-protection? Can you think of any other reasons I could give?

Of course, both of my parents are very liberal (although in a more groovy, hands off kind of sense than most people who call themselves liberal today.) A few years ago, I think around the time I turned 18, I mentioned to my parents that I wanted weapons the to be able to carry them concealed, and my parents said that while they understood the need for protection and lauded my refusal to "be a victim" (that I refuse to be a victim was the reason I gave them for wanted weapons at the time), they argued that I would be more likely to shoot myself of someone I love.

Don't construe all that to mean that they're gun-grabbers. My mother might be; I'm not sure. On the other hand, my father is more of the libertarian type liberal. He served in the Army in the 70's (Signal corp, working with the 1st gen satellite technology.) He also owns guns. When I was young, my father hunted deer and dove. A few weekends each fall he and I would wake up early and meet up with one of my father's friends and his son, who was my friend, and drive to a deer leese in the part of central Texas that called "hill country." My friend and I would stay near the cabin and shoot pellet guns or chase around the goats and cows that the owner of the land had. If we were lucky we got to tag along with our fathers and if we were really lucky we could shoot a milk jug or two with their rifles at the end of the weekend. Right around the time that I turned 12 my father bought me a semi-automatic Marlin .22.

I never got to fire my Marlin. It's probably at my parents' house still in the box somewhere. I never got to fire my rifle because a short while after he bought it for me, my father started contracting with an oil company. Since then, he's been on a routine where he works for a month in western Africa then is home for a month minus travel time. He wasn't able to go hunting with that kind of schedule.

So, I guess the real question of this thread is about bridging a gap. How would you bridge the gap between someone who grew up in a rural area around guns, served in the military, and used guns for sporting purposes and someone who owns a pistol for self-defense? I know it can be done, but I don't know how.

I'm actually starting to get teary eyed right now, because, if it's not obvious, there's another gap. There is a gap between my father and me. I've spent the last 9 years of my life with a father who was around less than half the time. I wish I could get that time back. I wish I could go to Africa with him. I have always been cool and unemotional. I learned young to disassociate my emotions from my thoughts as a defense mechanism. Despite my deficiency at feeling emotions, right now I am overwhelmed. I love my father more than anyone on this earth. I cannot continue to write.
