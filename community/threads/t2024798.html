Sparklers are a handheld 'fireworks' that don't explode (pyrotechnic devices). They are easy to make, plus you can use your knowledge of chemistry to make colored fire.
Difficulty: Average
Time Required: minutes to make, several hours drying time
Here's How:
1. Mix the dry ingredients with enough dextrin solution to make a moist slurry. Include the strontium nitrate if you want a red sparkler or the barium nitrate if you want a green sparkler.
2. Dip the wires or sticks in the sparkler mixture. Be sure to leave enough uncoated space at one end to safely grasp the finished sparkler.
3. Allow the mixture to dry completely before igniting the sparkler.
4. Store sparklers away from heat or flame, and protected from high humidity.
Tips:
1. Source is L.P. Edel, "Mengen en Roeren", 2nd edition (1936), p.22, as cited from Wouter's Practical Pyrotechnics
2. Parts are by weight.
3. Be certain the sparkler is 'out' and cooled before discarding it. This is easily accomplished by dipping the stick in a bucket of water.
4. Firework use is restricted or prohibited in some areas. Please check your local laws before burning sparklers, whether you purchase them or make them yourself.
What You Need:
• iron wires or wooden sticks
• 300 parts potassium chlorate
• 60 parts aluminum fines, flitter, or granules
• 2 parts charcoal
• 10% dextrin in water solution
• 500 parts strontium nitrate (optional, for red color)
• 60 parts barium nitrate (optional, for green color)

Re: Help please with sparkler mixture
Basically yes ...

First of: Thanks for being such a great newbie! If everybody would put as much effort in research as you have (or even just any), my job would be quite a bit easier.

Yes, a sparkler composition is basically any slow-burning composition that has a bit of extra fuel/reducer added to it to make it the LE equivalent of oxygen negative.
As the composition burns, some of the unburned fuel is propelled away and continues to burn upon contact with atmospheric oxygen.

Me, I like to add some iron filing to BP for an easy sparkler composition. Don't bother with a binder, dip a thin stick in wood glue, then roll it through your composition.

I told you, you wouldn't get flamed!

I cant say I know a massive amount about sparklers but I would have thought it would be similar to a fountain mix stuck to a stick and unconfined.

If you want to experiment and learn you could try different metals.

Iron filings for Golden yellow branching sparks.

Aluminium filings or powder for bright white sparks. Flake Aluminium will burn faster thus reducing the distance the sparks fly but also making the sparkler burn faster. Magnesium powders will also have a similar affect.

Titanium is probably creates the most interesting spark affect with a unique fine branching affect.

You could add Barium if available for a green color or instead use copper powder if available.

Or strontium for a red affect

Sorry if this information does not apply for sparklers these are a few of the things I have experimented with in fountains.

You could try mixing different metals, and color compositions for a lucky dip!

Both the barium and stronium require a chlorine donor such as PVC for a color affect but if possible it may well be worth investigating.

Also bear in mind that many powdered metals and barium are harmful and often very light. Wear a dust mask, gloves and avoid static with the more flammable metals in case a cloud is produced.

Of course most of this is pretty usless unless you go a bit more advanced. For something simple I would go with Aluminum, Iron or Titanium for the best affects. The smaller the particle size the smaller and shorter lasting the sparks will be.
