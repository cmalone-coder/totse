I suppose if you're going to ask yourself that question, you should start with a different question, "What is the significance (if any) of female virginity?"

I mean, it wasn't so long ago that a woman's worth was defined almost solely by her virginity, and even today there are cultures where, if no evidence of her virginity is displayed for all to see on the day after her marital nuptials, she is diminished in status.

Think about it. Women ... kept under lock and key for most of their adolescent lives by their fathers, occasionally let out under the strictest of supervision to be 'courted' by young men in the same manner that one might shop for a pair of shoes, married off with an impressive dowry to get one's self off of father's bill, bound to a husband who might not have the slightest bit of sexual empathy (or skill), and expected to perform 'wifely duty' on command. And, of course, if women weren't virginal, they were regarded as damaged property and weren't expected to amount to anything.

And we know precisely what this kind of attitude toward women's worth created, in the long run -- women who were sexually repressed, timid, and doomed to live lives which were little more than empty shells of servants. Servitude of a different sort, of course.

Now think about the 20th century. The women's sexual revolution. Scores upon scores of women discovering the female orgasm (and no, it's not a myth). And besides that, discovering self-worth in independent self-improvement, study, and in entrepreneurship and pioneering in fields they had never before entered. Experimenting with their lives, with their self-expression, and with their newfound sexual freedom.

And we know precisely what this kind of new attitude toward a woman's worth created, in the long run -- women who were happier and who lived lives which were fully their own and richer with experience.

The value of female virginity is an anachronism ... it only exists in our minds because it has for so long in the past. It used to be that a man could sue if his bride-to-be was found to be a non-virgin, and likewise it used to be that a father could sue if his unmarried daughter was deflowered before she could be married off. If we place any value on female virginity these days, it is probably for the wrong reasons.

Anachronisms aside, we can certainly consider the practical aspects of virgins vs. nonvirgins.

For example, a virgin woman, although almost certainly free of STDs and very, very tight, deserves some special consideration. She may never have had an orgasm, or if she did it wasn't likely from intromission/intercourse-type masturbation. On the other hand, nonvirgins can have a sordid sexual history or a clean one (and it helps to check). Condoms are a pain in the ass but they are necessary these days. Nonvirgins can be tight, loose, or somewhere in-between. If she is lucky, she may already have some idea of her orgasmic potential, and may already know some of the tricks you might want her to know. She might even be able to teach you some.

As an interesting aside, the same anachronism that dictates that all women should be virginal says all men should not. It's an interesting paradox. I mean, nobody expects us to be virgins anymore, and we certainly didn't fuck each other if we managed to lose our virginities. So, there's either one really dirty whore out there and three billion blushing virgins, or that same anachronism teaches us hypocrisy: Fuck the sluts and marry the good girls. It's ridiculous. The only fair thing to do is not to expect anything of women -- don't judge them, but take them as they are.

As for my personal tastes, I would say that nonvirgin women are perhaps slightly more favorable in general. They are less inhibited, and you can usually find more responsible ones these days who, while having the right amount of sexual experience, have also practised safer sex all the way along, making the whole endeavor safer for the both of you.
