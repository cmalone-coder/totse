As long as we're on the environment...
/begin rant

Given the surface area of the average home's roof, and the average energy consumtion factors of that home... solar panel energy conservation- even the cheap, slightly inefficient ones- could provide enough energy to run every electrical applience in that house every day of the year except one. Air conditioning condensers take 18% more power on average per hour than an energy-efficient panel can gather in that hour.

Also, solar technology is NOT an effective option for the 46% of urban Americans who do not own their own home, but rent apartments instead where the energy consuption/available surface area ratio is three times greater than necessary.

So, solar technology is great if you 1- have enough money to own your own home, and 2- don't mind paying extra for air conditioning electricity. I still like the idea, and will be getting them on the house I build, when I actually get the funds up to build it. Anyone who cries about the contributions I make to the capitalist destroyers of the environment until then can come up with the money to get me a mortgage, or put up with being ignored. The average consumer has spoken.

Who knows anything about fuel cells? They're those neat little inventions whereby electrical energy is created when oxygen is run by one electrode and hydrogen by another, with a catalyst in between where the hydrogen proton attracts the split hydrogen electron, thus creating current. Using pure oxygen and pure hydrogen, the resulting process creates electricity, heat and perfectly clean water, strict H2O. Taking oxygen from your average joe air and using clean hydrogen, the resultant byproduct is slightly dirty water, like in that spring water everyone likes to drink... slightly mineralized.

This is not a new technology folks. It's been around since the sixties. Why are we only now getting around to creating mass technology upgrades with it? Because, gasoline is simply more prolific than hydrogen. Fuel cells are cleaner and cheaper, but you can't just go down to the corner market and pick up a liter of pressurized hydrogen, can ya?

Every major automobile manufacturer has already built a fuel cell hybrid vehicle. Every one of them works. Every major automobile manufacturer says they can have these vehicles mass-produced by the year 2008-2010. Imagine, your car has no emissions but water... less water in fact than your current exhaust system dumps on the road now.

Why don't we do it then? Like I said before, where are we going to get the hydrogen from? Would you want to drive a vehicle with onboard hydrogen tanks? (actually, it would probably be safer than carting around something that not only is flammable itself, but creates flammable fumes.. but try telling that to most people) Consumerism rules once more. To buck the system in place, someone has to come up with a better, cheaper, easier one. Therein lies the problem.

Besides that, even having hydrogen-powered vehicles will not free the world from oil refining. Guess what plastic is made from? You know... the plastic that makes up your computer, your chair, your refigerator, your microwave, fuel cell car panels, that band-aid on your arm. That stuff. It's oil-based yanno. Sure, consumption would go down... but it'll still be there.

I guess my whole point is this; environmentally sound technology is on its way, but slowly. Implimenting it is the real pain, because the average joe just doesn't have the capacity... either time-wise or economically... to make that sudden change. It HAS to be gradual, because nobody in the world has the money or time to bring this technology to the world overnight.

I can admire Nihil's campaign. Can't say I like his methods, but the ideology is sound. If someone could offer me a fuel cell vehicle with convenient hydrogen refuelling stations where I need to go, a home with solar energy technology, and do it within my financial means... hell, I'd do it in a heartbeat. Problem is, I don't see anyone willing to do that, and I sure as hell can't do it myself.

/end rant

In college I did a project. I built a scale house with running water, a solar water heater, lights, and a fan. Extra lights were added to make the energy consumed proportional to the size of the house. I used a high effeciency 12v solar panel, it cost me about $500 and was one square foot.

The flywheel idea is good for some things, but the only way it could retain it's spin for a long time would be in a vaccum on saphire bearings. That's not practical. However for emergency power it would do the job nicely, as long as it's recharged regulary.
