An Idea I Got From Working in Biotech [as mentioned in BLTC]
Most of my adult life has been spent working in various Biotech companies [I went from lab tech to facility tech to facility coordinator/manager - not that you care ].

One of the things that is big in this industry is taking a cell line and introducing a foreign gene into it so that it produces something of value.

For example: isolating the gene that makes insulin, getting a cell line to accept it such that the cell then produces insulin. You could then grow large bioreactors [read: Vats] of the cells and then harvest insulin from it .

In the job where I got the idea, we were using some cell line [I don't even remember what it was] and using it to produce bacillo viruses, which were used as a bio-pesticide.

There were and are other companies doing like type stuff - so I know that the technique is doable and viable.

So I got to thinking - and I'll say right now that I have no idea how hard it would be or if it's even possible, but just imagine:

The gene [or genes] that are responsible for production of THC in marijuana plants;
Finding a cell line that would accept the gene[s] and start producing THC.

You could then harvest vast vats of THC much like the insulin in the first example.

You could also substitute mescaline for THC, or Psilocybin and/or Psilocin.

That would be biotech put to the good of mankind


Since the method of making cells produce what you want is - more or less - established [at least in a rudimentary enough form], the difficulty would lie in the same place that the projects of almost all endeavors like this are:

In finding and then isolating - if possible - the gene or genes responsible for producing THC [or whatever it is that your final product is to be].

Of course it would be time consuming, but isn't anything that has such potential payoff worth at least an initial effort?

If I had the knowledge I would have gladly used my spare time to do my own experiments. But, alas...
