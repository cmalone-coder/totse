Re: Guys: Opinion on poetry?
Well, thing is, I can write most sorts of poetry if you give me a little time to learn the basics (when it comes to new styles I don't already have down pat, like Quattrains and shit), and I actually wrote very good poems all the way back in mid-school when I had to, and I'm even better a wordsmith now than I was then. I can do it, but I lack the passion to make it my life's work, I tend to dislike limitations in the forms of stanza length or syllable limitation or fucking iambic pentameter or what have you, because I feel they negate the entire purpose of poetry: Freedom of expression and the creation of something that you find beautiful and meaningful, no matter the form it's written in. When you tell someone "Write about anything you like, but you have to keep it at 5 lines a stanza, it has to rhyme and you have to use alliteration at least twice!" like teachers always do (from elementary on up through college English), you force the person to change around their original vision to keep it within the given parameters, and usually, the quality of the poetry suffers immensely. This isn't the writer's fault; it's the fault of whichever fag poet/ess decided they enjoyed writing within strict limitations and making others do the same. I'm no exception to this; when I'm forced to work within too many parameters, or certain complicated ones (like alternating stressed/unstressed syllables the whole fucking way through; that is HARD shit to work with), my poetry comes out shitty. However, when I have a vision in mind and a drive to write a poem, and I have no limitation, my work can be very good. Good, but not great, because I lack the true passion of a poet. Not a bad thing; it just means poetry isn't really my bag, baby. My bag's psychology, medicine and reading/writing.
