the belief system of drug users
Bored analysis of the ever progressing belief system of the drug user

Stage 1

Drugs are evil. The (future) drug user does not understand why people use drugs. They're addictive, bad for your health, and simply not worth the risk. Beer and coffee are okay, though, because they're not nearly as addictive as illegal drugs, aren't cut with toxic adulterants and most illegal drugs are distributed by violence gangs and terrorist groups in the middle east. 'I'm not old enough to drink yet though, as I haven't turned 21 yet', thinks the future drug user.

Stage 2

The drug user turns 13 and gets drunk for the first time at a friend's house. Alcohol rules! Whoooooooh!! Who needs illegal drugs when you can drink 5 shots of Breezer and get totally wasted? 'I've seen some kids smoke cigarettes and marijuana, but they're just losers trying to act cool in front of their friends', thinks the drug user.

Stage 3

The drug user turns 14 and smokes weed for the first time at a friend's house. Weed rules! Whoooooooh!! Who needs hard drugs when you can smoke dank weed? Unlike hard drugs such as heroin and meth, marijuana is not addictive and has no real disadvantages to your physical and mental health worthy of mention. The drug user registers on pro-legalization forums, discusses the merits of weed decriminalization tirelessly with his friends, and readily shows off his Bob Marley posters and hemp keychains to his fellow classmates in an effort to spread the good word about the green herb. The drug user is hesitant about trying psychedelics however, although his newfound pals from the internet seem to have only positive things to say about them.

Stage 4
This stage has two possibilities, one or both of which may occur:

Option A
Finally the drug user lets his curiosity get the best of him, and proceeds to order some 10X Salvia extract, some Hawaiian Baby Woodrose seeds and a syringe full of spores after recovering from last month's failed nutmeg experiment (shouldn't have trusted those totse text files!). The Salvia doesn't seem to do anything, the HWBR made him puke for 2 hours straight and the few moldy mushrooms that sprouted from their spores don't look very edible.

Option B
The drug user's curiosity gets the best of him, but decides to place his bet on prescription drugs upon realizing that 'real' psychedelics are too hard to find. Besides, a drug that's prescribed by doctors and hospitals can't be nearly as addictive or harmful as hard drugs. After a few failed experiments with cough syrup (which in the end turned out to contain too much guafenesin) and allergy pills, the drug user is finally frustrated at how much research it actually takes to find out which pharmaceuticals are psychoactive and what the appropriate dosage ranges are. All the drug user's perils are however solved upon his discovery of erowid. The drug user proceeds to take pills from his greataunt's medicine cabinet and enjoys them, until his supplies run out.

Option C
Unfortunately the drug user has to resort to inhalant drugs because psychedelics and prescription drugs are too hard to find or get hold of. The drug user enjoys a brief, blissful high from a few swiped whip cream cans, and has a brief stint involving lighter fluid and ether (which he obtained through water extraction from starter fluid). He decides not to do inhalants anymore after negative comments made by his peers.

Stage 5
This stage has two possibilities, one or both of which may occur:

Option A
At last a 23 year old hippy with dreadlocks offers him an 8th of shrooms at a psytrance party. The resulting trip blows, vivisects and reassambles his mind completely. Psychedelics rule, Woooohoo! The great thing about psychedelics is that they aren't addictive and have no negative aspects at all to speak of. The drug user dismisses "flashbacks" as propaganda but admits that hallucinogens may exacerbate existing or dormant mental disorders, but knows this can and will never happen to him, despite not having checked his family's background concerning hereditary psychiatric ailments. The drug user joins the crusade to legalize hallucinogens for psychonauts and psychiatric researchers alike, while diving deeper into the psytrance scene, finally converting to Budhism and dedicating his soul to living the alternative lifestyle espoused by his new peers.

Option B
At last a 16 year old candy kid dressed in a colourful raver outfit sells the drug user's friends 10 quad-stacked high quality clean rolls at a rave party. The drug user's friends decide to take the rolls together. After each taking one pill and not feeling too much, they decide to take the rest of the pills. The drug user and his friends get sick and vomit without getting high. 2 months later the drug user's friends try again with pills from a different source, this time at the drug user's house just in case they get sick. They have an amazing time, although the drug user's parents threaten to send him off to rehab after noticing his strange behaviour and gigantic pupils. E rules! Wooooh!! The drug user uses MDMA on 35 more occasions during the next 6 months.

Final Stage

The drug user's interest is once again piqued while contemplating the last category of drugs he hasn't tried yet: hard drugs. The drug user has enjoyed many over-the-counter drugs that have effects which are similar to well known hard drugs, such as desoxyn and morphine, but surely that's an entirely different league than meth or heroin? Ultimately, the drug user will not be able to withhold his urge to seek out new and exciting experiences.

Option A
cocaine / methamphetamine / heroin rules! Woooohoooo! The drug user finally realizes that hard drugs have been demonized far too harshly by the conformist media, and that the cause of addiction actually lies with other people being weaker-willed than the drug user himself is, and that it is perfectly okay to abuse hard drugs in moderation. The drug user is hesitant to try his hand at injecting drugs at first, but ends up letting his curiosity get the best of him. Ultimately the drug user overestimates his own capacity to resist addiction, and ends up not realizing that he is getting progressively more dependant on the drug until it is too late. The drug user's life goes to hell, his family ostracizes him, and he either dies or kicks the habit, in that case renouncing his previous drug use and making a living as a public speaker for drug abuse and addiction awareness campaigns at schools across the country.

Option B
The drug user realizes that hard drugs have been demonized far too harshly by the media, but he also realizes that the problem of addiction affects us all and that he is no exception. Because of this careful attitude concerning drugs, he is able to stay clear from addiction. The drug user is hesitant to try his hand at injecting drugs at first, but ends up letting his curiosity get the best of him, and enjoys it. He finally decides to save hard drugs for special occasions such as birthdays or new year's eve, and lives happily ever after.

Which stage are you at?

Here's my story:
When in high school I had to give a presentation in 7th on an illegal drug, we could choose which drug to present. The purpose of the presentation was to basically tell your class about the dangers of drugs. I chose to research LSD, knowing very little about it but feeling somewhat intrigued by the unique class of drugs that made you have hallucinations. This was when I first stumbled upon Erowid and took up an interest in psychedellic drugs. I actually became quite interested in what I had read about it, and spent many hours reading basically everything on erowid about it as well as reading books about it. This is when I discovered that pretty much everything I had been told about acid before was wrong. I ended up skipping school for the presentation, but I can still remember telling my one friend shortly after this whole thing that "If I ever did do drugs, I think I would do LSD."
The first time I really did anything was some exploration into DXM shortly when I was 17 and 18. I had a handful of really good times on DXM before I starting getting sick and feeling shitty from it. Started smoking cigs two months after I turned 18. Then I got turned on to pot, and I really really liked it. Just to note, up until this point I never even drank alcohol before and found drunks to be very annoying. I sought out LSD and mushrooms, but I could never find them. I did find some people selling pharmies though. And once I got up to college I started drinking.
Fast forward about a year and I was smoking weed all day every day, pack of cigs a day, getting drunk 3-4 times a week, and taking pills or whatever I could find to get me high. Never did coke, meth, heroin or any really hard shit though because I fear the addiction.
Fast forward another few months, I drop acid for the first time.
Fast foward two years, I've taken acid about 20 times, mushrooms 10, and mescaline 2 times. I have quit smoking (I was tripping on acid, decided that I should quit, and haven't smoked since last December). I also barely drink now, and when I do it's only on special occasions and a few beers. I also completely swore off all drugs except for pot and psychedellics. I still smoke pot, but I smoke a lot less.
I feel I am much happier, healthier, socialiable, and spiritual person than I have been in many, many years.
I'm also consistently making the Dean's List (A- average or higher) in College with a Dual Major in English and Philosophy with a minor in French.

And I am 100% confident that I will never do hard drugs. I've known multiple people that have completely fucked up their lives because of shit like crack, coke, meth, and heroin. I've seen first hand what hard drugs turn people into. I went to my one friend's funeral who ODed (on coke mixed with oxys), I swore to myself that I would never touch the shit. I've had multiple opportunities to do all of those drugs as well. I must have been offered coke at least 50 times but I never even hestitated to consider it.
