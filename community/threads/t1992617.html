Self Control
If you don't feel like reading this, just skip to the question below the break.

Recently I have been thinking a lot about this, and I would like to see what others thoughts are on the topic.

I feel that humans have greatly less control over themselves then they think they do, me included. I think that who we become is 90% genetic and 10% external influences.
I believe that it is our emotions that dictate our doings and that its our brains electro-chemical reactions that dictate our emotions.
I have noticed that many of the things I do, or almost all the things I do are done sub consciously (Take note of how you are sitting right now, of the position of your limbs and such, did you consciously decide all those tings?).
Take a conversation for instance, I noticed that I don't really choose what I say, I mean i can if i want to, but most the time the words someone says to me are processed sub-consciously and milliseconds later a well formed replay comes to my mind.
Also there is non-verbal communication that most people don't even realize is going on (or at least to the extent that it is.) Most communication between people happens non-verbally.

I know this skips around a bit and doesn't flow to well, but i didn't want to write 2 pages on my thoughts, as I am really after what others have to say.
So...
*************************************

Do we control our minds, or do our minds control us?

Since this is a science forum, scientific answers would be most favorable (Science doesn't recognize a human soul for example.)

Re: Self Control
If by 'control', you mean HMFIC of decision-making, then the mind controls us. Even having the illusion of control, such as when we decide that we're going to act one way even though we're feeling another way - is a little bit of smoke & mirrors *trick* of consciousness...

Some people would disagree with that or object otherwise.

Memetics is a considered a new "science" of the mind. It's not completely ironed out yet, but it gives some interesting models of how our minds operate and where the controls really are. So new, it's widely debated and damn near controversial in some circles.

I've also enjoyed anything the cognitive scientist Steven Pinker has ever written on the topic. How The Mind Works is his best, IMO.
