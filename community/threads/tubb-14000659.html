Tonight I saw on the tube a story about a black man carrying a Confederate Battle Flag across the south. He is doing this to show that the CBF is not a sign of racism but a flag that people carried into battle fighting for an ideal (States Rights).

The politically correct crowd is trying to rewrite history. The PC version of the events of 1861-1865 is that that war was fought over slavery. This is false. On the first of January 1863, Abe Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation which freed the slaves in Union controled areas. This proclamation almost brought the war to a halt because of mass desertions from both armies. The average soldier on both sides was not interested in fighting to free slaves. The Northern soldiers were fighting to "preserve the Union", the Southern soldiers were fighting for "States Rights". The irony is that the Confederacy had a law exempting anyone who owned seven or more slaves from military service. Only a moron would believe that some poor dirt farmer would voluntarily go to war and fight for the right of the rich landowners to keep slaves. Let us not forget that the thirty-year veteran of the United States Army, Colonel Robert E. Lee, resigned his commission (after he was offered command of the entire Union Army) to go be a part of the Army of Northern Virginia (his home State). Jefferson Davis (President of the Confederacy) also offered Lee command of the Confederate Army and Lee declined that offer for the same reason. Lee felt that he was a Virginian first. This is the best example of why the War began. The primary issue (there were multiple lesser issues) was States rights. Was the State government supreme, or was the Federal government supreme. This is why the War fought between 1861-1865 is called the Civil war by the North, and the War Between the States in the South. The Southern terminology is correct. However, the North won, and the winners write the history books.

It appears that we are going off on a tangent of slavery so I'll ride that pony for a while.

As an institution, slavery was dying in this country until our friend Eli Whitney invented the Cotton Gin in 1793 (patented 1794). With the advent of that device, slavery really became profitable. The number of slaves in this country was declining steadily until then. The climatology of the south was such that a slave could provide useful labor for more of the year than was possible in the north. One problem with slavery is that the slave must be fed the entire year, not just when productive labor is performed. The north had its share of slaves (although it's not PC to talk about it since slavery was a disease of the south) many of whom helped build the ports and docks (not to mention Wall Street) of New York City. But the winters in the north precluded extensive use of slaves because of the sheer lack of cost/effectiveness and slavery was a minor factor in the overall labor market in the north.

However, in the south, tobacco was labor intensive and the European demand for a good smoke (coupled with local demand) ensured that a mediocum of slavery would persist in this country for some time. However, in the Caribbean, slavery was off to the races. Sugar cane is a labor intensive crop (especially during harvest and refining) and then there were the ever popular diseases called malaria and yellow fever.

A favorite Hollywood theme is the severe beatings inflicted on the slaves. While there is truth about the practice, common sense dictates otherwise. Example: I own you. I have paid money for you. You are a capital investment for me. The only way I get a return for my capital investment is for you to work. It is not in my best interest to disable you which will prevent you from working. I must continue to provide you food and the necessities for life (however meager). You as the slave are only interested in staying alive and obtaining freedom. A return for my capital investment does not even appear on your radar screen.

My point: Slavery is a totally inefficient means of labor. However, the cotton gin eliminated the most labor intensive portion of growing cotton (removal of the seeds and husks from the cotton balls) and finally made slavery in this country profitable (it already was in the Caribbean). But agrarian slavery was where the profit was.
