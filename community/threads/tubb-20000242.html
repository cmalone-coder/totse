I finally got around to starting these shelves today. I used tryp's idea but slightly in reverse. I bought 2 planks of wood (4cm x 20cm x 120cm each) from the local carpenters yard, then i had a flash of inspiration and went to SeaLife the local marine supplies shop and bought a long metal rod thats 2cm in diametre (its cylandrical and supposed to be for boom supports or something) and four 0.5cm thick circles (3cm in diameter) of metal (used for at sea repairs apparently). I took all these directly to my shed and started the first shelf. I marked the wood on the 120 x 4 sides the locii between the longest edges (2cm from the top which is exactly 2cm from the bottom) and then i marked 30cm in from each the other 2 edges.
Ie
code:
<---------120cm--------->
 <30> <----60cm----> <30>
 ________________________
|    |              |    | 
|____|______________|____|4cm
|    |              |    | 
|____|______________|____|


Then, where each line crossed i boared a 2cm wide hole 15cm deep. Then i boared a 3cm wide hole in the same place as the other holes but this one was only a little over 0.5cm deep.
Next i drilled 4 4mm wide holes on each metal disc and used wet and dry paper to rough up one side of the discs.(it looks like a disc if you quint a bit, * are the holes, # is the roughed up bit):
code:
    _____
   /     \
  / * # * \
  |  ###  |
  \ * # * /
   \_____/



Now i cut the metal rod into 4 15cm long rods and used wet and dry paper to rough up one end of each.
Next i soldered the rough end of each rod onto the roughed up part of each disc to hold it in place and i then used WG weld (cold welding putty) to fix each rod firmly onto each disc. I ended up with this (disc is the vertical line on the left, rod is horizontal):
code:
 _
| |
| |_________________
| |_________________)
| |
|_|


Then i used the wet and dry paper again to really rough up the rod and disc front.
I tested this on my previously boared wood and it fits perfectly. The rods slot into the 15cm deep holes and the discs sit in the 0.5 cm deep hole perfectly. Then i painted the wood blue (to match my decor). Tommorow i will use masonary screws to fix the disc-rods to my walls (2 rods 55cms from each other and then another 2 rods 55cm away from each other) and i will then cover the disc-rods in epoxy and slide the wood onto them. The shelves should go flush up against the wall and there will be no visable supports!
Hurrah
