Re: Food bill: Surviving on less than $605 over 10 weeks
Eating cheap is very doable, but...you don't have to suffer either. You should, btw, learn to love pasta, because it's cheap, filling, and the biggest food group. Off the top of my head, I'd recommend things like Sloppy Joes (easy, tastes good, and lasts a long time in the freezer), red beans and rice (probably the cheapest thing you can eat while still getting lots of nutrients), pasta dishes, and potato salads as some key meals to have in rotation.

At the beginning of the month you want to stock up on pasta, rice, beans, onions, garlic cloves, cans of tomatoes (diced and whole), a couple cans of tomato sauce, potatoes, butter, and a bag of apples. You'll probably spend around 25, possibly 30 bucks on that, but that alone would be enough to last you the month. (You'd generally be alternating between pasta with basic tomato sauce and plain red beans and rice every night, with potato salad, mashed potatoes, or a baked potato as a side. You might have a potato salad sandwich or an apple for lunch)

(Now, you can improve this a lot by stocking up on spices and seasonings. IMO, the essentials would be: salt, pepper, sugar, cumin, basil, oregano, curry powder, Worcestershire sauce, mustard, and ketchup. Most of this stuff will last for several months, so it's a good investment. [Probably 20 bucks again, but it allows you to cook diverse foods])

So, you keep all of this shit as your base-line, and then every week you can go out and spend some money on fresh products as needed. Your best bet for meat is to find something on sale and in bulk. Like, if you find a big thing of ground beef or chicken breasts, you could easily space it out for a couple of weeks. Then all you have to buy is a loaf of bread every week, some milk, and stuff like celery or bell peppers (well, those will last you about 2 weeks sitting the fridge).

So... if I'm not underestimating anything too much, I'd say you could eat well and cheaply for around 60/month.

EDIT: I forgot: Pizza is REALLY cheap if you make it on your own. A 2.50 dollar bag of flour and 2 packets of yeast (1.50 for 3 packets) will give you around 4-5 large pizza crusts, or around 10 calzones. You can make sauce with a large can of diced tomatoes, and then you can top it with vegetables, meat, whatever to make it more filling. Cheese is good, but you only need a sprinkling of it...too much is unhealthy and it's expensive. Pizza dough will last a long time when frozen, 2-3 days when put in the fridge. You'd need a table to knead it on, a measuring cup, a big bowl to mix it in, and a pan or pizza pan to cook it on.
