The opinion of one ignorant 17yo...

Ever since people evolved to the point of actually thinking about what's goin on, they've wondered what's behind the unexplainable forces in the world. This awe of forces outside their control (ie, the sun, the weather, love, fertility) was personified and became deities...
This same awe was translated into myths, etc. concerning the above deities.

I see this as a sort of primitive 'science' in that they they attempted to explain their observations with a form of logic based on their beliefs. Keep in mind that their belief in the gods would have served the same purpose in understanding the world as our 'belief' in subatomic physics...

I'm not sure what'd happened, but I suppose religion has become more elaborate as societies and people have become more complex, taking their existing beliefs to even further logical extremes. And I'm sure codes of ethics and things fit in there somehow as well...

What I see is that science has gradually become a lot less willing to make assumptions (ie, that gods exist) and has tried to explain the observed in terms of what is indeed actually observed, and some whacked-out theory about cause and effect is not accepted until it can be confirmed by further evidence.
So a lot of what religion could expain with assumptions could now be explained through more or less completely logical scientific principles... and so science has replaced religion as our guide to the universe.

Back to the parallel between physics and mythology, maybe our ultimate realisation of God will be what makes the universe tick...

Maybe science will never answer all our questions... and so maybe we'll always need gods
