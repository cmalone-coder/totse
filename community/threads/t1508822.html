Re: Do clothes really matter/make an impression?
When I was in high school and my early years of college, I dressed like a lot of the people in this thread say they dress. I wore band shirts, plain clothes, etc. I thought this was because I didn't care what I wore.

But that was bullshit.

Now, I dress heavily on the preppy side. I look like I shop at Abercrombie and Hollister. Sometimes I do, but a lot of that stuff is expensive so I'm more than happy to buy stuff that looks the same for a cheaper price elsewhere. I own four pairs of shoes (Eastland, New Balance, Steve Madden, and Florsheim) in addition to the combat boots issued to me along with my military issued dress shoes. I own three Versace suits and one Brioni suit, but I typically wear Linea Dome (which is a Japanese suit manufacturer) because it's cheaper.

I give less of a shit now than I did in high school and college. This is because I would go OUT OF MY WAY to not dress preppy.

Now, however, I dress in whatever looks good on me. I don't consider it to be "dressing like something I'm not" because the clothes don't make the person. I dress the way I dress because I look damn good in these clothes.

Whether or not you want to believe it, the way you dress holds water to the way people perceive you, and more often than not, it also represents how you WANT to be perceived. If you dress like you don't care, then you are perceived to be apathetic, and that's a characteristic many people would consider to be a flaw. If you dress to look good, you are perceived as wanting to be good.

You can be confident as fuck in whatever you want, but if it looks ugly to someone else, they're less likely to want to be around it.

You're not your fucking khaki's, but you are the choices you make.
