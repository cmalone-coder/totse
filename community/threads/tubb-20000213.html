The behavior you're describing doesn't sound like rabid bats. Bats that are rabid usually are full-grown and flopping around on the ground. If your pets pick up a bat, then more than likely they picked it up off the ground, and it has a chance of being rabid, unless it's a cat, they catch them at the openings of their homes. Bats don't harm anything and if they're insect eating species (more than likely) then they eat their weight in mosquitos each night, that's alot of mosquitos. They also eat other flying insects. But them flying towards you and then flipping backwards is probably just them showing off, I did a study on bats one time and the juvenilles would often try to out-stunt each other. Once they realize you're a person they probably just scoot away, or else they continue showing off. lol But unless you're finding bats on the ground, or colliding into things, then they're probably not sick. Bats really don't get rabies very often, and even if they did they're not going to hunt you down and bite you. I am far too short of time at the moment to write everything I learned 5 years ago about BATS when my wife, two little girls, and myself had to go through $9600.00 worth of shots (at our expense thanks to the idiots at our public health department disposing of the specimen) do to a possible exposure to rabies. No one in our home was scratched or bitten, however we were exposed. My information comes directly from the head of the infectious disease department at the CDC.

Some important things you should know are that bats are the #1 carrier of rabies in the United States (and believe it or not cows are second). Of the bats I have killed at my home, seven have tested positive for rabies.

A person does not have to be bitten to contract the virus as it can be transferred through a scratch, open wound or simply through the air that comes in contact with any mucus membrane (eyes, mouth, nose, lungs, etc).

The virus is transferred by coming in contact with bat saliva, blood, urine, or droppings. A rabid bat can seem normal and unless the bat's rabies is very advanced, there is no way to tell if it has the disease with out sending it to a lab and having it's brain tested. However the mere fact that the bats are coming in such close proximity to humans, as you describe (which is not normal behavior for bats) they should be considered suspect. Any time a bat is found in the same room as a human that is; an infant, asleep, intoxicated, incapacitated, mentally diffective or several other circumstances, that bat needs to be sent to a health forensic lab to be tested for the rabies virus, even if there is no known exposure.

Rabies is nothing to take chances with since it is an automatic death sentence (there has only been one known case in history of a human surviving).

If you wish to telephone me I will be happy to share my knowledge with you. Email me for my phone number.

Proceed with extreme caution.
