Re: Considering getting into Night Ops. Advice?
Your equipment is more than adequate for right now. You gotta learn to travel undetected and get up to buildings before you can learn to pick locks. The best novice targets, in my opinion, are back yards. A lot of people will say construction sites, but I feel this creates a lazy operative who's used to staying as far away from people as he can. Press yourself against fences, peek over and look for PIR lights, dog houses, anything that's going to cause you problems, and get into people's yards. If they've got sheds out back, see if you can get into them without making a big racket. Yard hopping will force you to try to maintain your best noise discipline and observation. You can't just shrug off mistakes like you can at a construction site.
Learn to get comfortable with hiding when people go by, learn to drop and roll behind shrubbery and tall plants and wait them out. If you're in a heavy urban environment and don't want to just walk down the sidewalk in a hoodie, start from the easiest place possible and scout overhangs for PIR lights and camera. Learn to crawl behind parking stubs, parked vehicles, garbage cans, anything and everything that can give you cover. Learn to move to areas you know people aren't going to just look at, think in your mind how people tend to view things, what would stand out, and avoid doing that.

The single most important thing for you, the new operative, to do right now is kill your nerves with some real, possibility-of-consequence experience. Yard hopping will definitely test your patience and abilities but at the same time isn't a high risk venture. It can be pretty nerve racking when you're in a shadow with 3 lit houses surrounding you (hey even for me still sometimes), but if you know you're not seen, just take your time and plot your next move carefully.

The whole "fear keeps you alive" thing is cute and all, but it's going to be a severe hindrance in a night operation. You have to learn confidence.
