700fps is high, but nothing amazing for .177.
With a good quality pellet ( crosman ) and a low grain weight, it can be achieved by a lot of 12 lb/lb air rifles here in the UK.

700 fps will, with a head shot, guarentee to kill a rabbit at between 30-35 yards. A street is maybe 5 yards wide.....

Re: airgun .177 @ 700fps
Firstly, I have seen 9mm and .40 S&W both be stopped by auto glass, and only penetrate when two shots hit close to eachother, so .177 will not likely do much. .22 sabots fired from a RWS Diana spring-piston air rifle at an estimated 900 fps were completely stopped by two .20 inch plexiglass plates. Auto glass is designed not to shatter, so all that any round will do to it is produce a hole.

To Shadow Spawn: Single-stroke pneumatic air rifles are very low powered. Multi-stroke pnuematic air rifles may be more powerful, but pumping quickly becomes a nusance, and since the rate at which you pump can effect the heat of the compressed air, they are potentially less accurate. CO2 can be expensive, but it allows quick follow-up shots, and if you want medium power at low cost, they are your best option. If you can afford it, buy a spring-piston rifle, because they require only one stroke to cock, and are potentially the highest powered type of air rifle. There are a few downsides to spring-piston air rifles, however. Unlike the other types, you cannot leave a spring-piston air rifle cocked for long without damaging it. There is also the minor problem of the piston jerking the gun when you fire, but unless you plan to compete in a semi-pro match league, this should not be a problem.

Re: airgun .177 @ 700fps
Is it just me, or do 95 percent of the people in this thread have no idea what they're talking about?

I have a .177 air rifle that easily attains 700 FPS. I've been firing it at glass for ages. For the most part, pellets are softer than glass. They flatten when they hit it and either bounce or skim off, possibly cracking the glass through sheer transfer of momentum if you're lucky. Windsheld glass will chip if you're lucky. A BB will break most pane glass (house windows, some car side windows) and usually leave an odd cone shaped hole or a dent in heavier glass.

I'd recommend a spring piston airgun for most use, since you only need to 'pump' it once, and it never needs CO2 cartridges or refils from a scuba tank. All you need is the gun and a pocketfull of pellets.

If you're going to shatter windsheilds, though, just throw bricks. How far do you think you can wing one?
