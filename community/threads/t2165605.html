Re: poppy seed tea
As said, they meant pods.

I used to use lot of seed tea, and don't underestimate it. Get it from a bulk store, and start of with 700-800 grams of seeds. Lots of places say less, but even with no tolerance I'd need that much, and so do people I introduce to it.

Eventually got up to over 2kg, and found it too much work for the benefit.

You put the seeds in a large container (4L maybe) and then fill with enough water to cover the seeds well, but not so much that you couldn't drink it all. If you add decent amount of lemon juice extract it seems towork better, but not everyone agrees and it makes it taste slightly worse.

The water should be just under warm. Not cold, it's not as efficient, but heat will destroy the molecules you want. Kind of cool water, the kind you'd get when you turn on the cold tap but before it reaally cools down.

Let it sit for 0.5-2 hours, shaking periodically (ideally every 5mins or so). The longer you let it sit, the more opiates you dissolve, but also fats and oils and other nasty tasting stuff, so it's personal preference.

Strain out the liquid you can from the container, and then put the rest of the seeds in a clean thin sock (like a dress sock) or some panty hose, before squeezing all of the liquid you can out of the seeds. They soak up a lot of viable tea.

You can repeat the process with the same seeds for a much lessened yield. I usually never do.

You have options now. Let it sit in a dark place with good circulation, and over a while the amount of water will lessen, meaning less bitter tea to drink. But I find that lowers potency and takes too long. Your best bet is to just down it all as fast as you can manage and consider the taste the price you pay, or add juice powder and do the same but with shitty tasting kool-aid poppy tea, which I find worse.

During storage at 4°C of M solutions at a concentration above 20 mg/ml, a white precipitate was formed that was difficult to redissolve. In all samples the pH and the osmolality remained nearly unchanged over the study period, except when stored in PVC containers at 22 and 40°C where there was a gradual increase of the osmolality during storage. In the solutions stored in PVC containers at 22 and 40°C an increase in M concentration of up to 105% of the theoretical concentration was detected after 1 month and 1 week, respectively.The M stands for morphine, which increased in concentration at higher temperature.
Apparently too cold is bad, it lowers solubility. But low ph is very good, down to 5.

I've found conflicting reports of heat's effect on morphin solubility, but the general concensus, as I see it, is that high temperatures increase solubility, but increase degradation, so cold temperatures are needed, despite the loss of efficiency. I still think the lipids dissolve better in warm water too, so definately keep it cold, but too cold and you're not getting optimum solubility efficiency.

Alternatively:

IIRC opiate alkaloids dissolve way better in methanol than water (seriously, ridiculously better), so why not soak seeds in methanol at room temperature or colder for two hours, then let the methanol evapourate in a well ventilated but darkened area. That would leave you with nearly all the opiate alkaloids, along with lipids and other impurities. Much more efficient, and you'd have to consume less nastiness, though more concentrated nastiness.

According to that link hydrochloride dissolves 50mg/ml in water (with 5% dextrose, which increases solubility, so buy some if you can, it's the most basic sugar, look around) at 22 degrees Celsius. Remember that the seeds don't have much morphine on them, and you have to use a lot of water to cover them, plusthe lipids take up solute space, so you won't get 50mg/ml.

I also found that citric acid DOES increase solubility by forming morphine citrate, which is more soluble in water (it also helps through lowering the pH level)

Still no info on the perfect temperature.

I estimate (based on nothing concrete) that poppy seed extraction could achieve maybe 90-150mg per litre. Yeah, way less than the pure chemical, I know, but other things are dissolving too, and there isn't much morphine to dissolve in the first place. That's for 700g-1300g of seeds or so of course depending on strain and brand.

Because the solubility of morphine is relatively high at 22 degrees, and because of the very low quantities of desired solute compared to the high quantities of solvent, a cold temperature's only real purpose is to combat lipids and other compounds dissolving. Lowering the pH and adding citric acid would do far more than cold water.
