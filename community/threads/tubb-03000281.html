Re: IS YOUR COMPUTER SAFE FROM HACKERS???
Im going to try to answer this without sounding like a complete retard :]

First off a real hacker knows the system before he hacks it. He/She would gather as much information as possible about the target and then use this knowledge to begin hacking it. You can rely on changing your systems everytime someone figures out those systems. You would run out of systems very quickly. Honey pots and syslogs do not stop the hacker they make it easier for the person to get caught. Im talking prevention here. The only obscure piece of a security system should be the password. These are easy to change and have a lot of different combinations. If a password is compromised, it can be changed.
Unlike some obscure system you might have implemented. You would have to recode it, or find some binary executable somewhere. Even a binary can be hacked and exploited. Enter assembly language stage right. It is harder to weed your way through assembly code but it can be done. It is better to use systems that have withstood the test of time and public flaming. Much like my ideas are going to have to withstand your guys' flaming. Most exploits i have seen have never relied on knowing the password. Just exploiting some system to gain root access. (My fav being a stack smash. You know if they had a seperate segment as a data stack, and a seperate segment as an address stack this would be a problem. Although you would need 2 registers for pointers.)

Of course i am also aware of weaknesses in humans. Social Engineering, weak passwords, writing down the password on a piece of paper. Humans are the biggest weakness in most security systems. But what still amazes me is the lack of usage of this exploitation.

Now honey pots, logs, etc., are all parts of a security system. I am not denying their importance. But when a machine is compromised it is safer to assume that all logs, and auditing information will be tampered with. Honey pots are nice toys but are the embodiment of a security hole. The whole concept of a honeypot is to have 1 or many security holes, and then monitor the activity of a hacker or a group of them. The only reason they are useful is because of "script kiddies". They have no clue what is really going on and shouldn't get credit for hacking a system, their script should :] Fooling someone like that shouldn't be held as an accomplishment. Real hackers are few and far between. So most security options are vaild for most situations. But then again you could risk being vunerable, if you rely on such measures.

Im an advocate of prevention in most matters.

Re: IS YOUR COMPUTER SAFE FROM HACKERS???
gonna break this down point by point...

First, Say I secure my system as best as I know how, and still lay down tripwired Honeypots and an IDS system linked to say, a pager. That increases the liklihood that I'm gonna be able to go in there and prevent the hacker from doing any more damage and/or getting any more info. So yeah, honeypots, IDS, stuff like that is incredibly useful in prevention if used correctly, with the proper obscurity as to their roles in the system.

Secondly, Chroot. Root cage. Great thing. Tricks em into thinking their in the system but not, there is actually an improvemed system out there that incorporates a bunch of shit into that, mantrap or lantrap or some shit like that. Anyway, if the fact that the hacker is in a cage is obscured then your system is secure due to the obscurity.

As for the time tested systems, hell yeah, the external configuration should be just as such. But I really prefer to keep all my network configuration, file structure, etc.. as *ahem* unique as possible. 'course that means a shitload of documentation, but the way I look at it, a hacker without the documentation would have an extra hard time figuring out what the hell the configuration is....
