The M-14 is not the full auto version of anything. It looks like an M-1, operates functionally the same, but is not the same weapon. The M-1 Garand is .30-06, it appeared in WW2 designed by John Garand via the Springfield Armory. The M-14 is .308 and .223 versions do exist. The M1 is fed via a stripper clip, while the M-14 uses removable magazines. The M1 also held 8rnds while GI magazines for the M14 held 20rnds.
The only similarity in the weapons are their actions and sites.

However it was not issued as full auto and not even select fire to troops. The M14E2 was the full auto version of the M-14, it was a prototype for a squad auto weapon. But due to it's high rate of fire and being limited to magazines, it was uncontrollable and slow to operate. Select fire versions were reserved for specops and the OSS.

Today the M-14 is still used by the navy as a guard duty weapon, by specops, for ceremonies, and some have been revamped into Nat'l Match status for use in sniping as a role in the designated marksman rifle, started by the Army it quickly followed into the Marine Corps as well.

Also the M1 Carbine is not called the M1 Garand Carbine. Anyhow it is a good caliber. Some people call it weak but at 150yds it still has more juice than a point blank .357mag, not bad at all. It also has very manageable recoil. A good gun for urban combat.

Now to achieve its political objectives, a country creates a strategy that attempts to use its 'tools', or instruments, of national power to enforce its will against other countries (and even its own citizens) whether it is in peacetime or 'war'. These tools/instruments of national power are:

1) DIPLOMATIC INSTRUMENT-- Diplomatic tools consist of such things as negotiations, 'treaties', alliances, and even some form of political recognition. While used to best effect before any application of 'hostilities' it remains an important part of the overall strategy during a conflict as well.

Practices such as the regulation of trade, loans and guarantees, investment policies, foreign aid, subsidizing, the transfer of technology, economic sanctions and trade embargoes, all contribute to protect a nations own industries and people (as well as that of its allies) from destabilization caused by the actions of hostile nations/forces.

4) MILITARY INSTRUMENT -- This refers to the use of (or THREAT to use) force to achieve a nations objectives. Military power = weapons/equipment, trained men/women, organizational/doctrinal concepts, industrial base (military/industrial complex), and its ability to sustain conflict. Okay, your political philosophy explains why (and who) you're resisting;

Your political objectives define what you hope to accomplish;

And an assessment of your abilities/capabilities informs you of what you have to resist with (in the beginning) as well as what you lack.

Once these three things have been figured out, the next step is to begin developing a strategy that allows you to achieve your goals in the most efficient (cost-effective) manner possible. So let's look at the 'theory' of strategy now. In the most basic sense, your strategy is a plan, or road map, of HOW you have decided to reach the objectives you've set for yourself. Your strategy should encompass all of your instruments of power working together, not at cross-purposes with each other (this is why you should have a set of clear-cut political objectives beforehand--- so that each instrument is 'on the same sheet of music').

This plan of resistance is not meant to be inflexible. As the resistance you're beginning grows and the opposition begins reacting, situations and developments will dictate some tweaking (or a complete overhaul) of your overall strategy. Different phases of the resistance may also necessitate a shifting of the "focus" from one instrument to another.

Now when we talk about a strategy of resistance it might help to think of it as a coin. There're two sides to it. On one side is the political strategy, which will have a number of political objectives; and on the other side is the military strategy, which will have a number of specific military objectives (for the purpose of achieving your POLITICAL objectives).

The political side focuses mainly on the growth of the resistance movement; recruitment, the dissemination of propaganda, some form of economic/ material growth... the application of its instruments to achieve its objectives in a somewhat peaceful manner. Each instrument of power should have its own internal strategy that works cooperatively with the strategies of the other instruments.

As long as there is "peace" the military side also works towards growth; recruitment, training, the acquisition of equipment and the growth & maintenance of its 'industrial base' (mostly logistical functions). At the same time it should be developing its military objectives and the strategies to achieve them for the time when (if) hostilities break out.

At some point in the 'grand (overall) strategy', if the political strategy proves insufficient to achieve the political objectives, the shift of focus will turn to military strategy to achieve it goals. Once hostilities have broken out, however, this doesn't mean that 'political' work becomes unimportant (it remains important from beginning to end), it just means that the various instruments of power put a little more effort into helping the military instrument to achieve the political objectives. Throughout all the phases of resistance the military instrument must be subordinate to the political effort (unless you're TRYING to effect a military dictatorship).

For now we'll set the political strategy to the side and focus on the military aspect of strategy in a resistance... When you get right down to it, when the decision has been made to use military/ paramilitary force there are really only two strategic (militarily) objectives.

One objective is to completely overpower your enemy's "military capacity", making them helpless and unable to resist the demands made by the resistance. The enemy's military forces don't need to be physically destroyed, they just need to be made so demoralized/disorganized that they aren't able to interfere with the resistance movement's work to achieve its political objectives. This is known as a "strategy of annihilation" and, normally, is only a viable option when one of the participants has an obvious superiority (in terms of morale, strength, skill, leadership, technology, etc) over the other. A beginning resistance movement will NOT possess this superiority...

The other objective is for the resistance to make the conflict seem so costly to the enemy that they will be willing to negotiate an end to the fighting on the resistance's terms. Rather than destroying the enemy's ability to resist, his 'will to fight' is wore down or eroded instead. This is known as a "strategy of attrition or erosion" and is the strategy of the little guy. It has been used to great effect all over the world throughout history.

The strategy of annihilation is always used when trying to achieve unlimited political objectives (discussed earlier), while the strategy of erosion can be used to achieve both limited AND unlimited objectives. Knowing whether you will be pursuing limited or unlimited political objectives is a NECESSARY PREREQUISITE for deciding which form of strategy you will employ.

Remember;
Annihilation strategies target the enemy's military capacity--
Erosion strategies target the enemy's mind...

At first, a resistance will only be strong enough to pursue limited objectives and, depending on the strength & will of the enemy, may only enjoy limited success. But, if led well, as the resistance grows (in all of its phases and categories) its ability to pursue unlimited objectives with a good chance of success will grow as well. You just have to survive that long....

Now earlier in this thread I had called slit_yo and ravers_choice to task on their choices of targets and now I'll explain why (sort of). When developing your military strategy (deciding on your military objectives and how you intend to achieve them) there are two concepts that you should keep in mind. The military terms for these concepts are "the center of gravity" and "the critical vulnerability".

THE CENTER OF GRAVITY-- Basically, a center of gravity is a key source of the enemy's strength. It can provide them with either a physical or psychological ability to resist your actions. Using this concept, you should be able to focus your attention on what is MOST IMPORTANT to the enemy in a particular situation. The enemy's center of gravity may be its fighting forces, or its strength may come from a particular region or population center. Even public support can be their center of gravity in many situations.

THE CRITICAL VULNERABILITY-- Whereas the center of gravity concept pinpoints an enemy's key strengths, the critical vulnerability concept, in contrast, pinpoints a key (or potential) source of weakness. Any military force wants to attack their enemy where they can do so with the highest chance of success and the least exposure to danger. If you are a small force you certainly don't want to be exposing yourself to the enemy's strength. But what makes a vulnerability "critical"?
1) The capture, exploitation, or destruction of this vulnerability must either greatly undermine or eliminate a center of gravity (or strength), and
2) The 'critical vulnerability' must be something that we have the ability (or means) to capture, exploit, or destroy.

If you think about it long enough, you'll find that YOUR ENEMY'S 'centers of gravity' and 'critical vulnerabilities' will be found within one or more of his four instruments of power (diplomatic, informational, economic, and military). You can bet that once you have shown yourselves in a display of armed, and unarmed, resistance your opponent will be looking for YOUR centers of gravity and critical vulnerabilities within your instruments of power as well.

The most popular pieces of advice given to future resisters is always "avoid his strengths and attack his weaknesses" or "where an enemy is strong avoid him, where an enemy is weak attack him". Hopefully now you have a better understanding of what is meant. Attack his strengths by targeting those strengths vulnerabilities.....
