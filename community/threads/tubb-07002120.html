Flagrantly Obvious
I'm at the local Super Fresh today (grocery store, for you non USAnians in the audience) in search of chicken broth.

They've installed a set of swanky self-checkout lanes in this one. I hear these are big in Europe (what with the new universal Euro and all) and Kmart has similar items as well. They're supposed to make your checkout experience more faster and more rewarding or whatever marketingese they feel like putting behind it, but in reality they're little more than massive electronic pains-in-the-ass.

For starters, they have this really annoying patronizing female computer voice that not only barks instructions for the flagrantly stupid ("scan items now..."), but announces to the store the name and quantity of every single item you buy. And it does it in an extremely grating manner - Instead of "Condensed Soup" it will say "Cambels Homestyle Chicken and Noodle Condensed Soup" or some other inane and pointless trademark. This in itself is bad enough, but you can't scan another item until the damn thing shuts up.

That's enough of that tangent, though. For payment you can use a credit or ATM card, or pay with cash. The machines supposedly take all manner of change (even pennies) as well as all types of bills up to $100. They accept, for now, both new and old style US currency.

For your shopping convenience, however, the bill testing threshold seems to be set unusually low. I paid for my chicken broth with the rattiest, most wrinkled, crumpled ass five dollar bill I've seen in years. The machine sucked it up, OCR'ed it correctly as a fiver, and dutifully spat out three dollars-and-some in change. Nice, crisp one dollar bills.

I thinks to myself, I thinks, "Hmm. One could probably pass off a fifty one copied with one's computer and not only pay for groceries, but be presented with a hefty amount of real change as well."

What can I say? I have a criminal mind.

Either way, this seems like a pretty lucrative gig to me. I'd doubt they would easily be able to detect your counterfiet bills, should the machine accept them, but even if they did it would likely be a challenge for them to link the bill with a face.

Thoughts?

~Zero
