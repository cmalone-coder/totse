Re: uk gun laws
Bible belt is true, but they can be dealt with.

Air rifles under 12 ft/lb don't need one
Air Rifles over 12 ft/lb require a Class 1 fire arms certificate ( This means you need to be over 18)

Shotguns:
REalativly easy to get. No age limit, I believe a six year old is the current youngest user. Pump action shotguns must be converted to hold onlt three shots.
Requires a shotgun licence.

Rifles and pistols.
Pistols are illegal in any calibre, since Dunblaine.

Rifles ae legal, in most calibres. All rifles must be single shot bolt action. The action must conform to specific rules so that it can not be loaded too quickly. Any rifle may be owned as long as it is correctly converted. Rifles such as M-16's ( Which are popular in some shooting comp's) can only have 5 shot magasines.

.22 and .223 calibre require Class 1 FAC
Anything above requires Class 2 FAC

To get the certificate, you must have a legitimate reason for holding a weapon. Shotgun is easier because, unlike the FAC, you don't need to specify exact locations that you will shoot.

Either way, all certificates require:
1) Clean criminal Record
2) House inspection by the police. This is to check security. Double glazing and good locks make it easier
3) The purchase of a certified gun safe which has a seperate section with a different lock to hold the ammunition in.

4) The safe must be fited to an exterior wall, in a concealed space, by a certified fitter. Evidence must then be shown again to the police, or a second house inspection takes place.

Specific animals, by law require minimum ammo calibres. Fox's need .223, stags .405 and deer is somewhere in the middle ( forgotten)

Travel: Guns can be carried on public transport and aircraft if the action is removed from the rest of th weapon, and the pilot/driver is notified.

Hand loading saves money, but requires another licence.

It's not easy, but I got mine within 6 weeks.

I gues a lot of the american guys ( POOP especially, won't like all the rules, but I personally agree with them. As has been seen in Hungerford and Dunblaine, as well as shootings in columbine in the US, guns are deadly, and I don't think anyone who doesn't REALLY need one should be allowed one.

But, if you think this is too strict, just thank christ you don't live in Japan.

to get a rifle licence, you must be part of an air rifle club for ten years, and shoot a minimum number of rounds a yearfor those ten. You have to pass shooting tests, and have a minimum of a 1/2 MOA before you can even apply.

And damn right as well, I say. If you can't shoot well, you shouldn't be allowed a weapon. And it's no coincidence that Japan, with it's very strict gun laws, has virtually no gun crime. The UK has very little as well. And we have tight laws. And the tighter the laws have got here, the more gun crime has fallen off.
