<<<< If you used a powder charge (with a metal pipe, of course) and some manner of contact igniter I suppose you could make a military style mortar. I don't know exactly how they work, but my guess is a percussion cap on the bottom of the charge that hits a pin in the bottom of the launch tube. >>>>

At its simplest (description-wise) a military mortar round is comprised of three parts ; 1) Fuse Assembly 2) Main Charge and 3) tail assembly.

There're quite a few ways to make a decent fuse assembly, but propelling the round from the mortar tube seems to be the thing that gives folks the most problems.

For a real mortar round, would you believe it's as simple as a blank 12 ga. (remove everything but the powder charge and insert a soft wad).

Picture this...

A pipe bomb that slides easily (minimum side-play) through a 3" seamless steel pipe.....

Attached to one end (brazed BEFORE any explosive compositions enter the equation) is the tail assembly.... A 3/4' water pipe nipple several inches long. The nipple has had many holes drilled up and down the length of it (equally spaced). Ideally, two or three (even four) fins have also been, soldered or brazed to the nipple....

Pretty neat how the 12 ga. fits snugly inside (if not tape it until it's a tight fit). Best way to do the end, however, is to use a nipple with the threads left on the end. Then you can insert the blank shell and then cap it with a cap that has had a sizable hole drilled through the middle of it exposing the primer.

If your round has been made with a good centered alignment, and the mortar tubes' firing pin is also properly centered, then when the round is dropped down the tube it strikes the fixed firing pin. The gases expand behind the round and propel it from the tube where it goes on to do what it's supposed to do.

More powder... goes farther
Less powder... lands closer
Traditional military rounds use increments of powder attached to the round to control how far the round flies.

No matter how much powder you use in the round you need a base plate for the tube, unless you plan to leave it rather than unbury it when you're done. The base plate absorbs and spreads out the force of the recoil.

Simple.

What complicates it is acquiring materials for round and tube and getting them to mesh together with a minimum of machining....

And a good mechanism for aiming/fire control...

'Course you could always go with a spigot mortar too.

That's more or less what I was thinking. I was going to use Power Loads (the things you use in Remington nailguns), but my research suggests that falling down a tube onto a pin isn't nearly enough force to set them off.

I'll have to experiment with some shotgun shells- I've never played with the primers on them before.

The simplest design for a mortar is my old cratermaker launcher: A length of strong steel pipe is embedded in the ground, and a tablespoon or two of lift powder is poured in. When you drop a cratermaker in the tube, lit fuse first, the end of the fuse ignites the powder and off it goes. Simple, likely very dangerous (especially if you overload it with powder), but effective nonetheless.
