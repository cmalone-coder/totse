you might be able to use your dish to help determine them, but i'm preddy sure they are tv and other assorted ones like fon.

your right about the waves cycle. their length is determined by how long they take to have ONE complete wave over the course of ONE second.

but it is also MUCH more than that.. and that is where it gets confussing.

the signal 'goes out'.. but how?

like a balloon?

this is what i'm assuming-- partialy.. because out of all the diagrams that i'v seen and read, they usually make it look like a straight line, when the signal obviously is not a line, but 'something' more like a bubble that eminates from its antenna.

but balloon would not (and could not) be a very good description of a radio wave, because over the course of one second, a large wave-- like AM, are several thousand feet long!

which means the bubble would have to be several thousand feet 'thick'!!

sooo it would *seem* radio waves are like all of the above:

- a line--- but in ALL directions.

- a balloon or bubble-- but one that is as thick as the wave length!!

so i 'guess' a radio wave, sorta would 'look like' a GIANT *thick* blob-- of some sort, that gets larger & LARGER as it radiats (goes out from) its source (antenna usually).

that other thread that just got bumped tonight is a pretty cool animation, of what a signal might look like. but it still doesn't quite cut the mustard because it used little arrows to show where the waves are going.

with this little program you can control the frequency, power and other cool things, like a radio transmitter and see what direction the signal might go and other features of it.

here's the link again:

http://www.colorado.edu/physics/phet/web-pages/simulations-base.html
