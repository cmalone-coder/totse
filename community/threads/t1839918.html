So I've been putting quite a bit of thought into this. Most shipping containers are 8'(2.44m) wide by 8'6"(2.59m)" tall, by varying lengths. Usually 20'(6.10 m) long. I figure, depending on what you insulate it with, you will have about 150 sq feet of floorspace per container. One of these wouldn't be much space, but if you stick 2 or more together, you are getting somewhere.

You could stucco or side over one, put a little roof on top of it, and not many people could tell that it was a shipping container. I've actually seen one of these in real life(minus the roof), and it looked pretty good.

With 8 and and a half feet of headspace, you should have plenty of room to run overhead plumbing and electrical conduit.

With some good insulation, it wouldn't be tough to keep warm with a small potbelly woodstove. Im not sure what one would use on insulation. Perhaps a layer of bed liner stuff[ala Rhino Liner], as it has a decent r-value, and is watertight, with a layer of foam insulation[R-Max or similar], then a layer of sheetrock.

.In this order

Steel

Bed Liner

Foam Sheet

Textured Sheetrock

.

Pros

<UL TYPE=SQUARE>

<LI>They are cheap and easy to come by

<LI>They can be put together and stacked atop each other.

<LI>You don't need a building permit to bolt one down to a concrete runner(Applies to CA law)

<LI>They are a lot stronger and safer than an RV.

<LI>Termite proof.

<LI>Fairly easy to transport(much easier than a mobile home)

</UL>

As for cons, none really come to mind. Rust wouldn't be much of an issue, as most are built to withstand sea transport.

I don't see any reason why you couldn't make one of these things just as nice, if not nicer than a regular home. And for a lot less money too.

Post thoughts.

Do you not know how to think?

Price wise, it would be much cheaper.

Chances are, you wouldn't need a permit(One of those alone can run $15,000+). You wouldn't need to frame exterior walls, floors, or a roof, or have a full foundation(which includes the flatwork). Those are a huge part of the cost right there in a home.

You wouldn't need nearly as much expertice or time to construct one of those as you would a regular house. Doing a good job on a foundation takes some skill. I've seen some guys who have been doing it their whole life, and they still manage to fuck it up. Same thing goes with the framing of the walls and roof. With these, all you need is a concrete runner, which is about a 14" wide by 6" deep by however long you need the length to be with some bolts sticking out of it at even intervals.

One of these places would be really easy to make an addition to. Just level out the ground, pour out another runner or two, and bolt or weld the things together.

Many structural advantages. It would be practically invulnerable to hurricanes and earthquakes(I wouldn't say the same about tornados or volcanos though). And if a flood carries it away, it will probobly remain intact. These things are somewhat buoyant too. Even if it does sink, it isn't going to get waterlogged and fall apart. http://www.veromarine.co.nz/dirvz/ma...otoFeature0007



Read my above posts for more reasons.

Really, I think the only reason everyone isn't living in these already is they are either:

A. Haven't thought of it.

or

B. Their pride in their social class will not afford them to live in a metal box, despite the significant advantages.
