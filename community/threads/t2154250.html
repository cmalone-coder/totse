The files that control those systems may have been damaged or corrupted. If you have to run fsck manually, that's a serious issue, and you need to take action to fix the problem.

I would boot off of a liveCD and run fsck over the filesystems, and then mount the filesystem and check the config files for damage. Reconfigure syslogd to log as much as you can, then boot back into the system again and reproduce the crash. After reproducing it, boot off of the liveCD and examine the logs. Hopefully you'll find something you can use.
