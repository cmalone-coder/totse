Re: acid lovers / the culture
I'm not entirely sure what your question is, but I've been pretty deep into the "acid scene," though I think that wording simplifies something really beautiful. When I was 15 me and a close friend dosed for the first time. We ended up in the back of a squad car tripping balls, getting yelled at by his parents for an hour, and staying up all night talking and hallucinating in his room.

It was the first time I'd ever felt anything spiritual. It was like all my life before acid was just meaningless. I had ambitions and questions that would never have crossed my mind before. I don't think any description of an acid trip or any portrayal of such a thing can do it justice. The experience is nothing like I had imagined it to be, it was so much more deep and profound. I remember the next walking around in the park thinking how good it was to be alive.

Over the next year I took acid frequently and experimented with other psychedelics, always trying to delve deeper into those strange worlds and thoughts. I came to be friends with a lot of heavy acid users and some pretty major dealers. I had friends who would go on roadtrips to other states and come back with 10 vials and a bible's worth of blotter.

I would trip at least once a week, sometimes much more than that. Things got really crazy for awhile and after one of my main dealers got busted with enough acid to put him away for life I decided to cut back. My sanity started to return and I realized I wasn't treating acid with respect like I had the first several times I did it. It wasn't a spiritual tool anymore, it had just become my recreational drug of choice. I quit doing acid for a long time and have only recently starting tripping again. I find the experience much more rewarding and beautiful now than I did back then.

If I had to say what has changed my life more than anything else, I'd probably have to say taking LSD.
