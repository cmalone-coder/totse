Re: Acidmelt can you help me out man?
After a reaction is completed, one is left with many by-products, impurities, left over acids, some RP, probably a small amount of unreduced pseudo, and of course meth. This is commonly known as "Honey". The honey, is a polar layer, made up of a mostly acidic environment.

This "honey" can NOT be water washed, otherwise much product will be lost. In this step, one must first wash with a Non-Polar solvent, which will NOT mix with the honey. Once the layers have formed(honey/NP mix), then seperate the NP from the honey, and discard the dirty NP. Keep the honey, and do another NP rinse.

Then, base the honey by adding NaOH. Once the pH of 14 has been reached, then add more NP, wait till they form perfectly mirrored layers, then seperate. This time keep the NP. Now add fresh NP to the honey again, and do it again. Seperate the NP from the honey, pool the 2 lots of NP. Do this step one last time, keeping all Non-Polar "pulls".

What has happened, is that the Methamphetamine HCL contained in the "honey", was turned to Freebase Methamphetamine when the pH raised to 14. Unlike Meth HCL, Freebase Meth is Non-polar soluble. Meaning that it(the FB meth) will be absorbed from the polar layer(water layer/bottom layer), moving into the NP layer. It usually takes about 3 "pulls" to get all of the product from the "honey".

Now, with the 3 combined pulls of the NP, one can water wash the NP(as Freebase meth is NOT water soluble), to help clean the product.

Now it's time for titration. That's a whole other chapter in the book about the A/B.

Hope this helps,
