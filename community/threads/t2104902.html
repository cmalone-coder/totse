you are right i didn't read his post correctly. So calcium carbonate has no benefit what so ever?

Familiar with alkaloidal chemistry? Just the basic stuff?

Calcium carbonate is... a base. Drop it in solution, and it slurps the acid off the alkaloids like I was dosing liquid off your momma's nips last night, and prevents them - in their free base - from being soluble.

So... it keeps you from getting alkaloids in your tea. "Benefit" is often a bad word to use, since it depends on what you're trying to do, but it definately has an effect; it basifies the water and keeps alkaloids from dissolving. At all.

Now, crap with the morphinan nucleus, that's a bit different. It's an organic base, but it's a bit of a zwittwerion... and as you prior quoted, because of this, it has this really bizzare behavior or acting like an acid for forming soluble calcium salts.

To any sane chemist, this is slightly batshit insane - calcium is not known for having the most universally-soluble salts in any way, shape, or form, and one would expect something which is mostly a freebase alkaloid to make that even worse, even if it did form a complex of some sort. Nonetheless, it's empirically demonstrated, so hey... it's all good.

Remember that little tidbit about calcium and its salts. It may be on the test later.

So, we're preventing alkaloids from entering the tea, but drawing morphinan derivatives - morphine and codeine - into the water. As well as a lot of other non-alkaloidal crap, the way you set it out. You're not getting any more morphine - which I'm pretty willing to bet is more water-soluble than codeine - but you're getting a lot less thebaine, pavaprine, and other non-opiate opium alkaloids. "None at all" would be a pretty good estimate.

Apparently, some opeheads like it like that. 'n since the potency isn't really going to go down, that's all good... but you're not changing the morphine content.

Now, if one were really bored, one could make tea repeatedly with sodium bicarbonate solution, until the broth was colorless and flavorless, and then switch to tossing a basic calcium salt into the mix. Add citric acid 'till a precipitate is no longer formed, and one would have a fairly clean, clear aqueous solution consisting almost entirely of morphine citrate and codeine citrate. How does that work? Well - calcium citrate is not overly known to be water-soluble, so when the citric acid dissolves the complex and salts both the calcium and the opiods... there's no more calcium in your water. Since all other alkaloids were prevented by basic pH, and all non-alkaloidable water-soluble components were previously extracted, it's pretty clean. Toss in a little baking soda, extract the base with ether, gas out with a little sulfuric acid, and you should have relatively high-purity morphine sulfate. 'n some rocks of codeine base sitting in the filter paper.

So it's not that it has no effect... just that it has a different effect than you're claiming. If a good salt of morphine couldn't dissolve in water, no one would ever IV it, would they?

Yes, a base which forms soluble complexes with the morphinans will successfuly ensure that only these alkaloids make it into solution, via standard alkaloidal chemistry. 'n the really inventive psychonaut will probably pull the leftovers with lemon juice and find out wtf is left behind, which may be the far more interesting discovery...

...but you're not getting different amounts of morphine and codeine out of this. Just different amounts of other opiate alkaloids.
