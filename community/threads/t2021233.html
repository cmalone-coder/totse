Re: Name of the connector...
The simplest method is to wrap a small coil of wire around one of the wires, and amplifying the output. In fact, connecting the coil in place of an audio cassette head might form a pretty good amplifier for tapping telephones.

Since you're tapping digital signals, you'll need a 1-bit comparator to clean up the signal.

Another method is to use hall-effect sensors. Simply place them next to a wire, and read the output. Some even have built-in amplifiers and comparators. They might even work without removing the outer insulation. Place a tight ring of a dozen or so sensors around the cable, and measure the relative voltages of each. Ethernet uses balanced signal pairs, but the sensors closest to each wire will still be able to pick up some difference.

I have some analog hall-effect sensors, so hopefully I'll get around to trying this soon.

Re: Name of the connector...
The wires are twisted together, thus effectively making a common mode rejection circuit to any listening devices. You will need a very small receiver to pick up any signals. A Hall sensor might work (and I stress "might" very much), and you will need to adjust it to get a good signal.

Clipping or unhooking it and placing a splitter will be a better solution, though.

And, no, only one computer can use any port of any router at one time. You can sniff traffic using ethereal, but you cannot use it.

I also forgot to mention that the signal connections are half-duplex, so you will need some sort of rejection circuit to amplify the signal so it can only pick up the stronger signal, rejecting the other, and send that to whatever listening device you have. The same for the other line on the other side of the cable.
