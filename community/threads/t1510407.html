Re: Being more sociable/friendly
You know, same thing used to bother me a great deal. I'd actually get depressed over the fact that I sucked at making small talk.

I was very quiet and introverted, as well as painfully shy. It didn't bother me because it bothered me, though. It bothered me because it bothered other people. For as long as I could remember, people told me things like "You're quiet.", "You don't talk much, do you?", "Why are you so quiet?", etcetera.

It pissed me off, because that was just how I was. I talked when necessary.

Anyway, where I'm going with this is - It wasn't until I decided I didn't care that people were bothered by my lack of speech, that I gained the ability to speek freely. I stopped looking at it as a flaw and just - from what I thought, continued doing what I was doing. It became apparent that I had changed though, as everyone including myself noticed that I was significantly more social and whatnot.

I'm still quiet and introverted, but I'm not really shy. I don't suck at being friendly anymore, and I actually can make small talk. I just choose to not run my mouth when it's not necessary.

Screw people if they're bothered by the fact that you don't talk much. They just talk excessively. I'm not saying people that talk a lot are bad, I'm saying the ones who are uncomfortable because you don't, suck.

Several people have even said it's something about me they like. We're generally better listeners. It comes with pros and cons, just like anything else.

And, I leave you with :

"It is better to remain silent and be thought a fool, than to open your mouth and remove all doubt."

"I often regret that I have spoken; never that I have been silent."

"No one has a finer command of language than the person who keeps his mouth shut.

"Blessed is the man who, having nothing to say, abstains from giving us wordy evidence of the fact."

"Silence is the true friend that never betrays."

"The most basic and powerful way to connect to another person is to listen. Just listen. Perhaps the most important thing we ever give each other is our attention. A loving silence often has far more power to heal and to connect than the most well-intentioned words."

"Many attempts to communicate are nullified by saying too much."

Re: Being more sociable/friendly
What worked for me was to not care.

But, I also did some things that may have helped.

I really barely talked. Probably less than a hundred words a day or something. Like, for example, cashiers at Wal-Mart, or a gas station or something - It's their job but, when they'd ask how I was doing I'd ignore them. Small things like that. I started responding with "Fine, how about you?", and sometimes went as far as having a mini-conversation with them. I suppose it was good "practice".

Smile at people. If you're not going to talk much, at least laugh and smile.

As I said, I personally don't care if people don't like the fact that I don't talk much. BUT - admittedly, it does make people uncomfortable if you're just standing there emotionless while they make an effort to communicate with you. That's different. At the very least, nod your head and chime in with a few words here and there to indicate you're paying attention.

That's all I've got.
