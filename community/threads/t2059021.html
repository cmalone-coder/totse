Being Interesting (A step-by-step analysis)...
Alright, this has been an area of much research on my behalf lately (It belongs in S&A because it is about RELATIONSHIPS and building them.) Let's not let this die because it's an area of huge passion for me, and it's very important to the largely introverted members of &Totse. If this thread comes to fruition, it has A LOT of potential to change how people on this board carry themselves. So:

How do you be interesting?

First off, let me assuage the naysayers. Yes! You ABSOLUTELY have to learn to be interesting. Evolutionarily, socially, and just about any way you can think about it, we NEED to be qualified. We need to feel accepted. Some people are natural at being interesting, and you KNOW who those people are. You've already told them about your secret high school crush whose pictures lined your locker, and you've only met them 15 minutes ago! Interesting people nurture an environment of comfort which allows people to really connect with you and really like who you are. They get the job, make the friends (and the chicks), get out of trouble, etc etc. Yes, this is absolutely a skill that, if you don't have naturally, it would be widely beneficial to learn.

A quick caveat before I get to what I've come up with. This is the outcome of a few weeks of study. Much of it borrows from NLP (Neuro-linguistic programming), Mystery Method concepts, and my own review of people I deem interesting. This is about creating a sense of friendship in as little time as you can. It's in no way complete, and I fully intend it to be organic (Meaning: Please add concepts, realizations, conclusions, criticisms, etc.) I'm opening up what is MY project (to find out what makes interesting people have that special captivating demeaner), to you guys:

Alright...How to be an interesting person:

1.) Talking to other people -

(a) Talk slowly and deliberately (not creepy, just clearly) - This serves several purposes. Nervous people talk fast, and talking slowly depicts (even if its subconciously) a HUGE amount of confidence. You also don't stumble over your words as much or backtrack, which adds to the effect. People will literally hang onto every word you say when you talk slowly, and it creates a sense of anticipation. Next, it creates a sense of time distortion, where a rather short story will seem longer and more poignant. Seriously, talking slowly will give an immediately noticeable difference in how people treat you. (EDIT: Just a quick note...when I say slow, I really meant SLOWER than you probably talk right now. Sorry for the confusion!)

(b)Tell stories - Be sure to tell lots of stories about yourself. It creates an air of openness and comfort, and furthers the conversation. The stories HAVE to be relevant to the conversation, or else it will seem like you're boasting. All your stories must have a POINT, or a 'punch line' (though it doesn't necessarily have to be funny) that wraps it up. Also, avoid wordiness and keep it as clear as possible, or else people will get bored.

(c)Use Metaphors - I don't know why, but people LOVE metaphors. It's an NLP concept that states that metaphors connect with a person on an subconscious level, and allows them to tailor it personally to themselves. I use metaphors when explaining things, telling stories, arguing, etc. It conveys intelligence, too.

(d)Use Multiple Threads - This is a Mystery Method concept that works with anyone to create a sense of friendship. Friends never talk linearly. They are always starting up new conversations in the middle of old ones, going off on tangents, or whatever. People who just met talk in a linear fashion; Once one conversation dies out they start a new one. You can create a sense of friendship if you use multiple threading. Go off on a tangent when telling a story, then start another story, then finish the first one, then start a new one and finish the second one, etc. Leave conversations unfinished, so when one "thread" dies, you can continue with another. This is a great immediate change you can make to create a strong first-impression.

(e)Be insightful - Read people's body language (I'll talk about that more in #2), and feel free to make POSITIVE assumptions about them. People LOVE insightful people because they feel like they are being truly understood. If you tell them "You know how I can tell that you're a real laid back person? You're wearing sandals" they will be blown away by your insight. I told a girl yesterday that she was the type of girl who wore pajama pants in high school, because she seemed very chill. She was telling me her life story within 5 minutes of that statement (and within 15 minutes of meeting her.) Make sure that you're making POSITIVE assumptions, because otherwise you're just being a pretentious dick.

(f)Talk with your hands - I'll keep this one short, but people feel more involved in the conversation if talk with your hands. At the same time, don't overdo it...know when to scale back.

(g)BE PASSIONATE - Though this is the last one I have laid out, it IS the most important. If you are passionate and involved in what you are saying, you can disregard almost all the other advice. Passion is contagious, so speak passionately, and you will get WONDERFUL responses.

2.) Listening to other people -
(a)Listen closely - This is obvious, but I just want to touch up on it. Listen to details of their story, and bring the details up later to prove you were listening. Even if it's a boring topic, just listen and make it segue into a more interesting one.

(b)Mimic the person's level of thinking - This is an important NLP concept. People are predominantly visual, auditory, or kinesthetic thinkers. If they're visual, they'll say things like "This looks good!"; auditory - "Sounds good to me!"; kinesthetic - "This feels right!". While people do mix these 3 levels around, they predominantly go to one group. LISTEN to which level of thinking they use mostly. Then begin using that language back to them. Their subconscious mind will detect a level of congruence between you two, and they will automatically feel closer to you.

(c)Mimic the person's body language/posture - This is another NLP concept, where if you (subtly) mimic the person's body language, it will send more congruence cues to them. If you notice, friends have similar body language and mannerisms. If you recreate this, it creates a sense of friendship.

(d)Maintain eye contact when listening - This is a great tool to show you are interested that people overlook. Also, it allows you to read their body language, facial expressions, and eyes, which, at times, tell you more than what they're saying ever will.

(e)Be polite, but not overly polite - Being polite is common sense. However, we want to keep things interesting, and harbor a friendship. So, feel free to cut someone off, politely, with something relevant. Saying, "Let me stop you there for one second but..." is a great way to do this, and create multiple threads. Also, throwing a few 'teases', or in the Mystery Method it's called a 'neg', in the conversation is a great way to bring people closer to you. You have to gauge this one carefully, and calibrate it properly, but throwing in a "Yeah...we gotta work on that whole English thing..." and smirking when they fuck up what they're going to say is a great way to simulate a teasing friendship.


Again, this list isn't complete, nor do I really intend for it to ever be 'complete.' I'm sure there are thousands of hints to add, and that's why I created this thread. Personally, I love when people tell me that I'm an interesting person, and so I began to research what makes someone INTERESTING. Some of my advice is obvious, but most of it I feel is things that people don't think about. If you follow these guidelines, I guarantee you will see a difference in how people view and treat you. A lot of it is grounded in science and the research of others.

This is a very...well...interesting topic for me, and I am already reaping the benefits. Since I've tried this, I've had a few professional and social opportunities open up to me that I would not get unless people were enthralled in what I was saying. So let me know what you guys think,
