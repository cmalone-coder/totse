Re: Help with a guy (who is a snobby elitist)
Pay attention. Then decide if you want to take heed of this or not.

I understand where you're coming from. I've met guys like the one you're describing, and for a time I was one. He's capable of being the "good" him all the time, but he lacks the confidence to be himself 24-7. The elitist bullshit comes about because he knows he's got a leg up on plenty of people, but deep down he knows there are plenty of others that have a leg up on him. Because of that, he throws that attitude out when he's feeling insecure.

I've also seen these guys when they've had girlfriends. This has happened too many times- a girl, such as yourself, sees the upside, decides she likes that part more than she dislikes the other, and wants to give him a chance. It's a wonderful thing to see, one human being giving another a real chance.

Here's the problem.

You don't know the "real" him any more than he does yet. Not everyone who has the potential to be good does so. Sometimes, once people like that figure themselves out, they swing towards the complete asshole side of the fence because that suits their nature better than being a good guy. People like you wind up stuck in the middle- you've seen the positive side of him, and when that starts to slip away, it sucks so much you have to save him. You can't let go, and then you get pulled down with him.

If you hook up with him, you're going to get a close-up view of all his various sides, not just the ones you enjoy. It's an emotional rollercoaster too many people ride. If you like the good side of him so much, the best thing you can do is not go out with him. Express your interest? Sure. But do not do a goddamn thing with him until he knows that the only part of him that interests you is the kind young man you've seen time and again. You don't want anything to do with the snobby prick that shows itself too often.

Some people might tell him you're trying to change him. Technically, they're right, but don't let that sway you. Being a good person is better than being an asshole- there's nothing wrong with dangling yourself out there as motivation for him to change.

But, for the love of fucking god, if you do this and it works, don't fuck him over. Don't just be like, it's great you're all nice and stuff, but I don't like you anymore. Be as sure as you can that it's his good side, and NOT the idea of getting him to show is good side at all times, that is attracting you to him. This isn't something you can do halfway. If you become his reason for being a better person, you can't turn around and do something to make him feel like it was all a waste. Take a good, long look at the situation, and if you think that might happen, DON'T DO THIS.

But, anyway, going back and simplifying it all... he's not worth the hassle as is, I can promise you that. You may have to learn on your own, but enough people do that- learn from someone else's mistakes.
