The Forex
Alright, at the suggestion of dagnabitt I have made a thread on the Forex. Here is a quick rap up of the Forex for nubs:
http://www.tradingacademy.com/freeclasses/forex/

In this thread I want people to post links, trade plans, and trade methods. Both techinical and fundamental can be used.

When talking about a technical trade plan, describe a clear place for entry and exit from the market!

Personally I have found that if I buy three small lots, instead of one large one, I can make a 'trailing stop'. Basically, we sell off one so we make a profit right now, then we hold onto the two others. Read about Andrews Pitchfork on the link for a trading plan that uses this idea.

Of course we need a trading plan to 'speculate' when the market will go up, when to sell, ect. When we determine that the market is going up, we set up stop loses so we lose about 7% max, and then set stop losses so that we break even when the price goes up. But that is my opinion

I prefer candlestick, but a bar would do the exact same thing.

I suggest that you do not rely solely on Tech analysis. If you use a good system, good for you. But there are certain times in which the risk is increased because of information that cannot gradually effect the market, the information doesn't leak out into the market slowly anymore.

Keep in mind that the market will sometimes act in the exact opposite way than news source talks about. Like, you get strong, postitive news and the market takes a trip to the great below.

You should watch out for all types of economic meetings. When information is released from a meeting like this the market can run 200 points up or down in 15 minutes. It's not normal trading, and the risk is increased during times like this.

Also, when you gain experience, watch what other countries effect the currencies you trade. Once again, the more you know about the economy of your pairs the better off you are.

Re: The Forex
Regarding Banksters Article...

*I don't think Grenada issues passports like candy anymore. This article may of been made pre 9/11...

*Moving averages are lagging indicators. Only having one average isn't going to give you a good idea about the market.

*Makes a good point about de-centralized locations

*He tells you to make an RSI, but doesn't tell you what it does. He leaves out some details throughout the paper. Here is a good site for alot of information about investing: http://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/rsi.asp

*I don't think these two indicators, RSI and one moving average, are enough to predict a trend. I generally use RSI+ADX and 3 moving averages set at 4/9/20, or MACD and parabolic SAR. (I'm trying two methods)

*Where the hell is the trading plan? How am I going to know where to enter the market, or exit it? Where to set stop losses? What, I'm sitting out here with my dick in my hand waiting for the market to go my way?

*Forex, obviously, isn't a perfect money machine. The best you can hope for is to be right a percentage of the time. There are some techinques which have a good average, like Andrews Pitckfork (an old method) that has a 75-80% accuracy, but it is never right all the time.
