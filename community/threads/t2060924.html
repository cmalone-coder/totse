Social awkwardness...will I ever lose it?
I've never been a very social person. Throughout school I kept to myself and was a loner. I might have had 1 or 2 close friends, but I also went through many years without a single friend.

I just feel awkward in pretty much all social situations. It doesn't come naturally to me. It took me years to learn things very simple things, like the fact that if someone says "how are you?" an appropriate response would be "I'm good thanks, how are you?" versus mumbling "fine" and then looking away.

I think I've become pretty good at faking social adeptness, but that's come through years of observation. I'm very shy, but I just pretend that I'm not in order to make social interactions easier for all parties involved. I'm a tech at a medical clinic, and I notice that all the other techs make small talk with the patients, and it seems so easy for them. I hardly ever make small talk with patients, and I never initiate it. And alot of the time I don't know what to say to people, unless they ask a specific question. I mostly just giggle and say "Oh yeah?" or "that's nice!"

I've also just started to notice that I can hardly ever tell if someone is being sarcastic. Unless a person says something in a obviously sarcastic tone, it goes right over my head and then I get confused because I assume that they're being sincere.

And every once in a while people make little comments that kind of sting. I don't like to think that it's noticeable that I'm weird, lol. A doctor at my work was like "Chelsea, you're so quiet! You've been here a couple of months [it had actually been almost 6 months] and I hardly ever see you talk!" And the other day I was talking with a couple of my friends about making friends at university. As some of you may remember, I went to U of A and lived in res, but I didn't make a single friend. I mentioned that, and my friend was like "yeah, but you're weird like that..."

I can't pin myself down to any social disorder that I've heard of like Asperger's, etc. I guess if I have to diagnose myself with something, it would just be social anxiety.

But my question is, is this something I will grow out of? Or will I just have to accept that fact that I'm not a social person? It would just be so nice to not have a flutter of nervousness rise up in my chest pretty much any time I talk to anyone outside my family and a couple of close friends.

So yeah, can I get over this? Any tips?

Re: Social awkwardness...will I ever lose it?
It's like a vicious cycle. You avoid interacting with people because you don't know what to say, and you don't know what to say because you don't have much experience interacting with people.

I'm exactly similar to you. I'm pretty good at faking confidence around people, but sometimes I just get tired of it.

The only time I've ever been truly confident around people is when I stop caring about what they think of me and stop taking myself so seriously. I think most people will agree with me, but this is easier said than done.

I suggest you invent some affirmations to reflect the mindset I described above. I know it sounds lame but affirmations are an old psychological strategy that helps. Recite them every morning for a few minutes, and before you go into a social situation.
Brainstorm a bunch of ideas of things you should do when talking to people, and write them down. Analyze things you do wrong and find corrections, but DO NOT start to criticize yourself. Practice these things in front of a mirror. You will feel extremely awkward at first but don't give up, and be sure to smile to give yourself positive feedback. After this training you should start to feel more confident.
It's actually very good that you work with lots of random patients. You can act professional around them, and I'm sure with most of them you don't have to worry about seeing them again in the near future. Use them to practice things on your list.

Just take it slowly and practice a new thing on your list each week. Strive to be a bit more social each week and over a few months it will become natural to you.
