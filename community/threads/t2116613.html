AFAIK, leukotrienes have nothing to do with it.

The "allergic" symptoms are all because of histamine, and it has nothing to do with actually being allergic*. Also, oxycodone can cause the same symptoms. All opioids can. There are some people who get itchy as fuck from oxy, and some who don't. Just like there are people who get itchy as fuck from hydrocodone and those who don't. It's all about the individual.

* True and false. It can obviously be a sign of an allergy, but the vast majority of "allergic" reactions to opioids are just innocuous release of histamine. In your body, histamine is stored in cells that, for some reason, can be "triggered" by certain amines to release their stores of histamine. Re: Histamines, Leukotrines, and IV Opiates, oh my!
Codeine is metabolized into norcodeine, morphine, and codeine-6-glucuronide. The norcodeine is further metabolized into normorphine. The morphine is further metabolized into even more normorphine, as well as morphine-3-glucuronide and morphine-6-glucuronide.

Hydrocodone is metabolized into norhydrocodone, hydromorphone, and hydrocodol. The norhydrocodone and hydromorphone are then metabolized to norhydromorphone. The hydromorphone is also metabolized into hydromorphol as is the hydrocodol.

Oxycodone follows the same metabolic pathways as hydrocodone, just replace the words "hydro" above with "oxy" and you've got your chart :p

There's a very good article in an issue of the journal Current Pharmacogenomics from a few years back entitled "Influence of CYP2D6 Genetics on Opioid Kinetics, Metabolism and Response", which is where I got this information. It explains the metabolism of codeine, dihydrocodeine, hydrocodone, oxycodone, and tramadol, including which enzymes do what, and is pretty easy to understand. I'll see if I can find an online copy of it.
