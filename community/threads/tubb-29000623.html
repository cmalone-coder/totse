Searrations cause a "tearing cut" which the body responds to with a better healing response than a "clean cut". While there is more scarring inherent to a searrated cut, the arteries and capillaries will contract more in a tearing cut and nerves and other tissues will tear where they want to more than in a clean cut. You are far more likely to bleed to death from a very sharp straight blade than you are from a searrated cut. Not to mention the increased odds of getting a searrated blade lodged or stuck in bones. It's also harder to keep a searrated blade sharp compared to a straight blade. There's more reasons, but I don't feel like listing them all right now.
