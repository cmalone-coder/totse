I know on the Snapple machines a way to keep getting free drinks.

Here's what you do. After you put your money in bend down and stick your hand in one of the three slots. You will feel a flap that would normally open up to let the bottle through when it fell on it. Hold this flap closed tightly. Now make your selection. When the bottle falls on top of the flap keep holding the flap. You will notice that the display beeps and will read whatever money you put in. At this point you can either hit coin return or make another selection! The machine thinks the bottle got stuck at the top because it didn't go through the flap and set of a sensor of some sort. Anyway enjoy the free drinks and don't get caught.

Re: vending machine...read on
there is no keypress to unlock the machines or make change come from the coin return. i have reviewed all of the machine manuals, and there are only a certain few combinations on the coke machines that you can get anything 'usable' to display on the screen.

salt water works, but only on the really old machines. their coin slides, the thing the coins ride down into the coin box on the inside of the machine, are solid plastic. on newer machines, in an effort to combat 'salting', the machine makers have made the slide slotted. this allows water to pass right through the front of the machine and out the bottom of the machine, rather than sliding down the slide, disabling the controls.

tying a string to a quarter and dropping it down until it registers then back up, then down, then up, etc. works but only on old machines. the newer model changle assemblies have a mechanism in place to insire that coins go in only one direction. the same is true of the old 'tape a dollar bill' scam. this works on older machine, but no longer the new ones. there is a sort of 'shredder' in many of the bill validators that engage when any reversal of direction on the bill is detected, not caused by the machine (such as someone pulling a dollar bill back out.

my recommendation on how to enter into a machine (without destroying it) is to learn how to pick locks. i started about 3 months ago, and i've picked just about every type of vending machine lock they make. the picks are expensive, from $50 to $400, however. if you really want to go pro, you can purchase a tubular lock cutter/duplicator for around $1000.
