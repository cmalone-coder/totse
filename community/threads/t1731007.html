The Mom and Pop Shop Scam

This is an extremely easy short con that seems to have about a 50/50 success rate from my experiences. All it requires is two scammers working together, an item that can be passed off as having value, and a little bit of acting skill.

The Setup

One of the scammers goes into a small, locally owned and operated mom and pop shop. You are looking for a place that has the same person working behind the counter on a daily basis. This plays a crucial role later on in the scam. Any small mom and pop shop will do, but I seem to have a lot of luck with this scam at antique stores. Scammer #1 browses around the shop casually for about 20 minutes. Suddenly, he begins to panic and searches around frantically, pretending to be looking for something. After about 5-10 minutes worth of searching, the shop keeper should ask the frantic customer what is wrong. At that time, the scammer's acting skills come into play. The scammer tells the shop keeper that he seems to have lost something worth high sentimental value in the shop ( i.e his grandpa's gold pocket watch that was given to him 10 years ago after his death.) The scammer explains that he is in a rush as he needs to be somewhere. He tells the shop keeper that he may have lost the item in the store. He states the importance of the item to the shop keeper and offers a $500 reward if the shop keeper happens to find and return the item. He leaves behind his phone number and name in order for him to be contacted should the pocket watch pop up.

Next day, Scammer #2 goes in and as he is browsing through the store, finds the watch laying somewhere. Excited by this find, he informs the shop keeper ( it has to be the same one who was made aware of the $500 reward) and tells him he plans to keep it. It is at this time that the shop keeper, out of his own greed will usually offer money in order to buy the item (thinking that he will cash in on the $500 reward later on). The scammer requests $200-300 for the item as he is a pocket watch collector and values the item highly.

Conclusion

After scammer #2 has collected the cash, he leaves the shop and splits the cash with his accomplice.

The shop keeper of course was given fake details and a fake number. The retail value of the pocket watch was no more than $10-15.

This scam seems to work well a lot of the time as long as Scammer #1 truly makes the shop keeper believe that he will receive $500 in exchange for the item. This scam completely relies on human greed in order to take advantage of the mark. He thinks he is getting a great deal and will be profitting $200, but all he winds up with is a worthless pocket watch and a debt.
