God in a metaphysical sense comes from the natural tendency of the mind to combine particular categories into general categories, the tendency of the mind to attribute all events to causality, and the tendency of the mind to think it can actually know things beyond its own ability to sense.

Re: Where did God come from?
I think its interesting you brought discourse into the equation. I agree the absolute subjectivity of everything (loose generalization) makes communiaction an awkward philosophical issue. I see the nature of discourse as intrinsically entwined with power. Attempts to engage in discourse are attempts to force ones reality on another by will, using logic and reason as the tools they are. (I have never been able to shake Nietszche). Discourse acheives nothing when answering ultimate questions, given the easy deconstruction of rationality. But it is a way to "swap facts", or the agreed upon conventions you mentioned above. It enables people to feel good about themselves when they can assert the world as they see it (will to truth). When people argue about philosophy, rather than facts, the discourse itself becomes a battle of wills and nothing more (if nothing is true than no one is right, only more right, so to speak. Discourse itself is good for very little other than trying to assert propositions one holds true, and then arguing about them in some way. But no proposition is ultimately justified. We never speak the truth independent of our use of it. Life is Will to Power .

Also, the fact that infinite regressions are logically impossible begs the specifc question of what I was addressing above. Just because something makes sense in logic (or does not) doesnt mean we can use logic to make statements about entities outside of precisely that logical or reasoned system (systems themselves included). IE the system itself cannot be justified from within (lacking the Meta-axiom necessary for deductive or inferential validation) or without (being able to transcend reason to validate reason). Therefore logic always ultimately fails regarding "truth". It is only a tool for solving more relevant problems - like those of discourse (syntax, grammer etc...). As such, the first cause argument is NO LESS fallacous than to posit infinite regression (a Kantian contribution), as it relies on the same limited faculties.

It has everything to do with the logical systems we employ (like you said), but more importantly the philosophies surrounding those systems of logic. IE whether we view reason as a tool of human evolution and will to power, or as tool capable of bridging the gap into the metaphysical. My stance is firmly the former, which it seems yours is as well.

Anyway, good post.
