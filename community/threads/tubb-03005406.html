Self-awareness is a bitch
Greetings -
I've been at college for a couple of months now, and I consider myself a rather self-aware person.
I've since been able to deal with the multitude of people who have absolutely no connection with me whatsoever (I may be making broad generalizations here, but what else can I do besides write everyone off? Go from clone to clone, searching for a connection?), but the desire to connect with someone else, preferably on both an inward and physical level, comes back. And some times it comes back pretty strong, and it seriously gets in the way of my moment or hour, and sometimes is the only thing I can focus on during the day.
I want to solve this problem, hopefully through a bit of thought, because I don't want to get stuck in what is essentially a self-defeating feedback loop. But currently, I can't mentally write it off, all I can do wait until the negativity subsides -- at which point it lingers and interacts with whatever I was feeling before it started.
Maybe some of the artists out there stuck amongst droves of people who they cannot relate to in any way could give me a bit of advice here. Is it inevitable that this desire to connect with people (I've had moderate success with projecting myself into the environment so that I get some sense of self-manifestation) come back, meaning there's nothing I can do? Perhaps if it won't go away, then maybe I can at least channel it into something else? For now it just saps my creative energy and any desire to learn.
I'm pretty sure that if there is someone or few out there, then I'll eventually come across them, but in the meantime, well...
cheers~

"Don Hamachek (1982) answers this question this way: (1) we understand ourselves by comparing us with others. Example: we know how attractive or how irritable we are by noting how good-looking or crabby several others are. Especially when we are feeling afraid or upset, there is a strong need to compare notes with others, preferably similar others in a similar situation: "misery loves miserable company" (Schachter, 1959). (2) We overcome loneliness, which can be excruciating, by being with others. Also, living entirely alone is hazardous to your health (see chapter 5).

Humans are social animals, much of our joy comes from interactions with others."
