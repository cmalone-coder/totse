Re: David Lynch.
That's not how Inland Empire went down. Before he had conceived any idea of a movie, he had begun to shoot a few shorts: He'd write out a script and then shoot it a week later.

After he had a few of these shorts, he looked at what they were about and the idea of a movie finally hit him. He then crafted the movie around the shorts he already had.

It's not as if he was prepared to shoot an entire movie and not worry about how it fits together until the editing room. Once he had the concept of a movie, he planned the story.

The thing with Eraserhead, Inland Empire is that it's interpretive cinema. It's like seeing a painting and looking at it a thousand different ways. Lynch wants people to look at those movies like they look at a painting - he wants them to interpret it in their own way.

Inland Empire isn't so much composed of a bunch of scenes that create a story, it's composed of a bunch of a metaphors that create an idea. That's what he's going for. It's a different kind of movie.
