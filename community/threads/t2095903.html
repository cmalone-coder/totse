I've taken zoloft, paxil, ritalin, 5-htp, a GABA supplement, valium, xanax, and the amino acid loading cocktail described by joepedo, as well as psychedelics (I've tried a lot of them but not many times each. I just thought that I'd include them because I feel like they have changed my life a lot). I guess I've been lucky in that SSRI's haven't given me any real noticeable side effects. I went through a lot of problems with dealing with stress, social relations, and spirituality before highschool and all throughout it. I had some pretty bad OCD and I still kinda do have it but it's improved a lot.

I first went to a psychiatrist when I was 18 because I was just generally really confused starting college about life and how to fit in and stuff. My OCD was REALLY bothering me and I also had bad anxiety in almost every situation and even realized that I was really paranoid about people judging me all the time. At the time I had the view that most people on this forum share about mood altering drugs being bad for you and not being effective...in addition to all the cynicism I had about the pharmaceutical industry blah blah blah. Anyway, psych put me on zoloft, which made me feel kinda jittery and I had this odd tension at times in my jaw. Side effects both physical and mental didn't really seem that bad. I think I've always been really strong mentally especially in rationalizing my situation and not going crazy or anything. I don't know about other people when it comes to how SSRI's affect their mental state and make em psychotic.

Zoloft didn't seem to be doing much so I switched to Paxil. At this point there were a number of things going on in my life such as starting to get into psychedelics and drugs in general. I never took drugs before college so this was a very new thing to me. I am a rational and careful person and I saw through lots of the bullshit society tries to condition you about drugs. But for me psychedelics seemed to hold a certain promise of spiritual and personal growth. I read A LOT about using psychedelics for humanistic psychotherapy that agreed with my philosophical views and such. Tihkal really got me thinking and curious. I also began to start defining my individuality at this point and basically realized that society and other people around me didn't hold the answers to what life is and how I should fit in it. I had to experience for myself that the "popular" college kids were just a bunch of dumbasses getting drunk and that it was stupid to try to be like them because in actuality I was for more interesting and fun to be around and more importantly, at peace when I learned to be myself.

Back to topic haha...the paxil seemed to work on my OCD a bit and improved my mood, but that could just as well have been the changing views I had. Benzos really did nothing for me and I was really disappointed because of that. People report euphoria and being really sociable and stuff, but it didn't have those good effects. I think that somewhere in my mind I just knew that my problems weren't simply chemical imbalances and that there was no wonderdrug shortcut.

I stress that you can't simply rationalize things in your head in order to change feelings of anxiety that arise from your unconscious. You have to experience and feel them and put things into perspective. You can't reject the feelings of trying to be like other people into a void. There has to be an alternative path to add meaning to life and for me I had to gain confidence in my own thoughts and ideas about things and realize that mainstream culture really doesn't hold real answers or the way to view life. I could get into all of that and go on for hours but feel free to ask if you want to know more on that.

Psychedelics and substances like MDMA showed me a lot of things. It wasn't just the ideas that I got while I was tripping though, I think it simply opened new possibilities of experience and showed me that there is much more to life and experience than the feelings you hold onto and are attached to. The habitual thoughts and anxieties I had were out of fear and attachment. Psychedelics can just be confusing a lot of the time and most people do not know how to put psychedelic experiences into context with their lives. They would think things like "OMG the mushroom showed me that we all actually live on a giant turtle floating in space!" but you have to be intelligent enough to separate worthwhile ideas.

I took some MDMA and acid one time alone in a forest and had an incredible introspective experience and felt real love and empathy for myself. It didn't really have a lasting change to my mood or anything, but it's experiences like that that can break down barriers long enough to see more to the root of your mind and feelings.

Nowadays I'd say that I'm very very much improved from where I was at just a year ago. I have added real meaning to my life and found things that I am truly interested in. I also have had a lot of intellectual and spiritual growth that I think has been the most important thing to making me feel good and reducing OCD and anxiety. Spirituality is a very hard thing for people to relate to each other so it might sound cliche or something but my new views of life and the universe put life into a hopeful and elegant perspective that makes sense to me. I can see other people and events for what they really are and I do not hold anger, resentment, or ignorance to things, but rather acceptance and empathy. The reason I've taken the 15 minutes to write this is because if I were here a year ago feeling really down and hopeless and confused, I would want to know that someone like me actually found hope that isn't superficial and doesn't ignore my own thoughts and experiences.

I'm currently trying out joepedo's amino loading cocktail and I've noticed improved moods overall and I'm also taking ritalin because I noticed that it helps me control my automatic racing thoughts that take me out of the "current moment" and prevent me from feeling clear and grounded and in control.
