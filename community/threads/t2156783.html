Depression caused by antidepressants?
A little back story.

A year or two ago I went to the Dr. because I couldn't sleep. He sat with me for a while and asked me a bunch of questions about sleeping habits, school, social life, etc. Long story short he told me I was "suffering" from depression and wrote me out a prescription for Prozac.

Keep in mind he had me really sold on my condition and so I joined the prozac nation. I remained a citizen for a few months, all the time questioning my need for the pill.

So after a lot of consideration and research I decided to stop my "treatment" right away. It was apparent that even if I did have some mental issues, prozac was not the answer. All it did was turn me into a zombie. Rather than feeling depressed, I felt nothing. I thought that I would rather feel like shit than be in this state.

The following years, I wish I had not come to that conclusion. Granted, I wish more that I had not even began taking the prescription.

So for those who do not know, the correct way to stop taking prozac is to eventually "wean" yourself off of it; otherwise you tend to get... well to put it bluntly, suicidal.

At first this was a real trip to me as I had never thought about ending my life to this extent. It's a thought that crosses everybody's mind, but this was different. It wasn't long however that the novelty of this state wore off.

Now fast forward to present.

I'm not sure if the suicidal thoughts have thinned out or I have just become jaded to them but they don't seem to be too much of an issue anymore (I'm not going to jump in front of a train or anything). But the overwhelming depression is more than I would like to have to handle on a day to day, minute to minute basis.

So that all being said, what is the likely course of action or prescription I should expect if I go back to the doctor? Keep in mind I really don't think I needed prozac to begin with and feel that it is the source of my mental maladies presently.

Thanks in advance,

Re: Depression caused by antidepressants?
Don't go back to that doctor, first of all.

And, by the way, the FDA requires all anti-depressant medications now to carry the black box warning (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box_warning) in regards to their ability to cause suicidal thoughts.

If one SSRI didn't help much, it's likely others will be similar. Try talking to him about an SNRI or tricyclic anti-depressant, or an atypical anti-depressant like Wellbutrin.

I've gone through a gauntlet of anti-depressants over the past ten years or so, and have probably at one time or another taken about every common one there is-- and, in my opinion, the two that worked the best were Wellbutrin and Remeron. I still take the Wellbutrin now and have a bottle of Remeron I only use occasionally if I'm really feeling like dog shit or am having a hell of a time sleeping (it can cause drowsiness).

Remeron is approved for major depressive disorder so it will pretty much pull you out of the deepest pits of depression, but in some people it causes weight gain and excessive day-time drowsiness. Wellbutrin will do the opposite, actually, because it has adrenergic properties.
