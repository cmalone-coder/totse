Re: quitting smoking
What you need to do to quit smoking is cut back little by little.

For example, instead of smoking the entire cigarette smoke 3/4 of it. Keep smoking 75% of the cigarette for the next 2 weeks or so. Then cut back to 1/2. Smoke 50% of the cigarette for about 3 weeks. Then 1/4 or 25% of the cigarette for about a month. The cut back from there.

A friend of mine is doing this technique and it works pretty well.

I remember reading somewhere about a study done on light cigarettes. I believe they found that the effects where pretty much the same. The stronger filter made it harder to get the good stuff out of the cigarette. As a result, the people who smoked the light cigarettes sucked longer and harder until they were getting an equivalent amount of the "good stuff" as the regular brand smokers. Don't quote me on any of this though. My brain has a tendency to "remember" things that never really happened.

I, myself, smoke. I did try quitting once before and failed. I lasted 10 days quitting cold turkey. It was hell for 3 days. After that time passes, most of the physical addiction is gone. You then only have to deal with the mental stuff.

I personally believe that cutting back slowly only reinforces you perceived need for cigarettes. You're constantly extending your withdrawl period from nicotene. As any smoker knows, the longer you go without a cigarette the better that cigarette is. You're basically making the cigarettes more and more precious to you by "cutting back."

Different methods work better for different people though. Try one, and if you fail you can always try another. Cold turkey, cutting back, patches, gum, whatever. No bullshit though, it is probably the single hardest thing you will ever do in your life.

My mum managed to quit by buying herbal cigs (ones with no nicotine in them, still bad because of smoke but they don't have the tar) and she would slowly swap out real cigs for herbal ones. I think every week she would add one more herbal cig per pack (and therefore remove one real one). After 20 or so weeks she had managed to ween herself off of real cigs and onto the less damaging herbal ones. After another couple of weeks she halved the amount of herbals that she smoked. Thats where my mum started having problems with wanting "just one" real cig, she started smoking real cigs again but she only smoked a little more that 1/4 of what she once smoked. A few months later she tried the swaping method again but with Sherbet Lemons (boiled sweets with sherbet inside) and managed to get entirely off cigerettes. It took her nearly a year to get off them but shes never smoked another one since.

edit- I think that the swapping method (doesn't matter what you swap to) would have to be the best method to stop smoking. If you go cold turkey then the "Just one" thoughts will get you started again, if you go on the patch you will end up having to get off the patch in pratically the same way that you had to get of cigs in the first place and the idea about only smoking half a cig, i don't think that would work because you'd only end up puffing the left over bit on the sly.
