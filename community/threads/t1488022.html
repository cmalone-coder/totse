You know, if it had sealed borders, the population of the US would be increasing very slowly or even declining. The same is true of the rest of the industrialized world (some countries are already in decline). It's immigration and the higher birthrates of recent immigrants that really drive population growth.

Increasing education and wealth correlate well with declining birthrates. Of course these richer people also consume more resources.

What a pickle, eh? By the time someone is wealthy and educated enough to learn from the internet that they shouldn't breed for the good of the planet, they are already statistically unlikely to breed beyond or even up to replacement rates. Even if they stop breeding altogether, natural resource consumption will continue to rise as the billions of people on the threshold of industrialization industrialize.

In one sense, you could say this is a reason why it's advantageous for countries to develop under their own power. In most of today's industrialized nations, industrialization and agricultural/medical improvements grew in tandem. New technology permitted new growth but also tempered the urge to reproduce. For many of today's poor, highly populous countries, they've received the benefits of advanced agriculture and public health practices without building a complete industrial society. So it is possible to have many children, without starvation or disease keeping growth in check, yet wealth and education are not widespread enough to lead to voluntary birthrate reductions. By the time they actually go fully-industrial, they will have much larger populations all starting to consume resources at an industrial pace (compared to the experience of the West).
