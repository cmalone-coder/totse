What do you call his (or her) parents?
This was easy when I was a teenager. My boyfriend's parents were always "Mr Lastname" and "Mrs Lastname", except in the case of one liberal-minded divorcee that went by Roz.

However, now, I'm older, and definitely not a teenager, and I still have no idea what to call my fiance's parents. My contemporaries are no help, and other forums aimed at a more "mature" audience are no help. Everyone seems divided on the issue.

For some reason, not many parents think to tell their son's/daughter's significant other how to address them. Some just go ahead and do the Mr/Mrs thing, some boldly use first names, and some, like me, don't call them anything, opting to be sure to look right at them when addressing them.

Today I had to fill out a thank you card for my fiance's parents, and since he was in a hurry this morning, he just scribbled his name at the bottom, thus making the "Mr/Mrs" awkward and the "Mom/Dad" inappropriate, since I also signed the card. I just opened with a "Hello!"

So I turn to the brighter-than-average totse crowd for views on this pressing issue. Has this come up for anyone? Have you noticed how friends or older siblings deal with this issue?

I think the important thing here is to NOT let the relationship go too much further without having names for them. It's like meeting someone in a chance environment, and seeing them periodically, over and over and over... and then finally having to ask them "What's your name?"

I would opt for the first name basis. Nobody will take offense to this and will probably feel more comfortable that you're calling them something. If you call them Mr. so-and-so and Mrs. so-and-so... they might feel obliged to let you continue calling them that out of not wanting to make you feel uncomfortable, even if they don't like the salutations.

If they DO take offense to you calling them by a first name basis, you'll pick up on it, but I seriously doubt that will happen at your age, with your relationship to their son. Kids should be more formal with this but you're not a kid, you're on the same level with them, and it's not disrespectful at all to 'presume' you can address them as adults.
