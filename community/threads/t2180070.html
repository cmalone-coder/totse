Re: Alcohol Distillation
I'm not a expert on the subject, but I have some basic knowledge from my semester of college as a ChE.

Looking at a ethanol-water Txy diagram, at 80 degrees celsius you can get 6 moles of ethanol vs 4 mole of water in the vapor condensate. I'm too lazy to figure out the alc %, but I'm sure you could figure it out if you want (convert moles to grams, then use densities).

Be aware that its theoretically impossible to get anything purer than 8 moles ethanol to 2 moles water using your distillation technique (and in reality much lower).

I can't help you with methanol unless you tell me what purity you want. However, at 65 celsius, you will surely remove methanol in the vapor condensate.

SO theoretically this works, as to how to actually go about the process, you should probably ask someone who has actual experience.

source: Txy diagrams

Re: Alcohol Distillation
I built a reflux still in college, that thing was pretty sweet. I got it up to 90% volume of ethanol. The methanol is only a minor component, and not something you need to worry too much about. It's actually present in beer and orange juice in larger quantities than in the fermented wash. I still would toss the first quarter cup or so, which ostensibly had the highest methanol level, mostly because it gave me an excuse to light some of it on fire.

What you're describing is a pot still, which works, but it leaves a lot of water and nasty tasting stuff in the distillate. You'd get a similar alcohol content as vodka or rum. If you want to get purer alcohol, you need a reflux still, which consists of a copper tube packed with something with a lot of surface area, like stainless steel pot scrubbers (the reflux column). You then condense the vapors at the top and run them back into the reflux column. There they evaporate again and cool the vapors, which results in the alcohol vapors becoming more and more concentrated. The water vapors are forced to the bottom of the column, because they can't exist as a gas at as low of a temperature. You would have a thermometer at the top to measure the vapor temperature. If it's near 78C, you've got alcohol, and if it's higher, you've got water or something else. There is a bunch of nasty-tasting shit in your wash that boils between 78 and 100C, and ideally this should not get through.

If you're interested do a search for "reflux still plans" and see what comes up. It might be more than you're wanting to undertake, but just be warned that if you go the pot still route, you'll have 40% alcohol and it'll taste like shit. But it will get you wasted, I won't deny that. ;)

Refluxing isn't that bad. The only calculations I had to do were the ones to figure out how much heat I would be adding to my boiler based on the voltage applied to the heating element. Essentially, you just keep an eye on the thermometer and make sure it doesn't go too much above 78. It takes some practice, but eventually you learn how much alcohol you can expect out of a given volume of wash, and stop collecting when you get that amount. I paid about $150 in parts for my still, although most of that was copper pipe and the price of copper has come down since then.

I built the column, yeah. It's just 1.5 inch copper pipe stuffed with stainless steel pot scrubbers. It's also wrapped with pipe insulation to keep the inside nice and hot. The column just needs to be stuffed with something corrosion-resistant with a large surface area. In industry they use ceramic rings, but pot scrubbers are actually better for small scale applications.

http://www.psychodogstudios.com/pics/still1.jpg

There's a valve on the collection spout that lets me control how fast I'm taking alcohol out of the column, and I can adjust this as needed. The only danger is collecting too fast and then accidentally collecting that layer of shit between the alcohol and water layers.
