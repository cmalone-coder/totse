That's the whole issue: Getting shit to install and compile.

I'm thinking Debian will win.

Almost none of that really needs to happen. Especially not the whole kernel fork thing -- most Linux devs aren't using servers to develop their code, they're using desktops.

Apple contributions likely won't help, and "more games" won't help either. Those things might encourage more programs for the Gnu/Linux system, but they probably won't be free software, and without free software, Gnu/Linux won't be that different from Windows.

The main problem is that people don't realize the real point of Gnu/Linux. It isn't about being more secure or stable or easy to use or attractive, even though those things are all true -- The point of a Gnu/Linux system is to be Free-as-in-Freedom Software, and if people don't get that, Gnu/Linux is dead.

I think things will go in a different direction entirely.

The Linux kernel isn't free software. It won't get that much more free, either, for the reasons above. I think that Linux will fall to non-freedom, but HURD will finally dredge itself out of development hell due to the widespread propagation of virtualization technology being available to debug kernel servers. Then, Gnu/HURD will start along the same path that Gnu/Linux once was on, and the inherent pragmatic benefits of freedom will encourage more and more use. This time, with the FSF doing the main distribution, there won't be any loss of the freedom mindset, and the community will stay intact.

If this doesn't happen before RMS dies, we're screwed, because nobody can lead us out of non-freedom except RMS or someone damn like him.
