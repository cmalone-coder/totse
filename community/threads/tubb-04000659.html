Re: Tattoos
I don't know how old you are or how long you've had the ones that you do. But I know what you mean. I, myself, have 4 or 5. The first one I got 15 years ago, the last one about 8 years ago, which shows I of which I speak. With that in mind, I'd like to toss out a few things that you might want to consider. First, don't rush out and get tat after tat just because your addicted. Put more time between new tats; learn to enjoy and savor each new one. Also, make sure you put a lot of thought into what you are getting; make sure that the tat you are getting now is something you will still want years from now, since they are a forever type thing. Also consider:

1.] There is a finite amount of space on any body; the oftener you get inked, the sooner you fill this space up.

2.] Whereas a lot of people [myself included] like seeing a lot of tat work on people, there is a thing as too much. This is especially so with females - too many tattoos starts to be an unattractive thing.

3.] What you think is cool on you today might not be how you feel about it down the road [which is why getting a loved ones name is not really a good idea, love does go sour and then you'll have a constant reminder of that person]. Actually, there in an exception to this is, and it is in fact what I decided to use for my next one [once I get the 3D details worked out, that is]: I plan to get my daughter's name [Alchemy]done; and have it in 3D. I figured a child's name is one that [hopefully] you'll never stop loving.
An extreme example of later regret is a friend of mine. A few years out of high school he got one that said something like ''Fuck the World' or something [I forget exactly. Well, many years later he became an accountant, with a wife and three kids; went Republican [see what I mean by extream? ] and that tat was a constant embarrassment to him: he wouldn't go to the beach or anything that involved him needing to remove his shirt;in fear that someone might see the tattoo.
When I decide upon an idea for new ink work, I wait a few months, constantly picturing it over in my head. Not just what it will be, but where [I believe in only getting one where I can see it. That's just me, though].

4.] This last item is one not many people give a thought to, and they should; especially if they intend to have many tattoos: They fade over time. I think nothing looks as tacky as a faded tattoo, unless it is a body full of faded tattoos. So, another thing that I consider when I plan to get new ink work, is if it is one that I won't mind having to get redone years down the road. I plan to get each of mine re-inked as soon as they start to noticeably fade.

I by no means want to discourage you, or anyone else form having yourselves inked up. By all means go for it. I just thought that I'd toss out a few items, that will hopefully make it so that, 30 years down the road, every tat on you is one that you are still please to wear.
