I don't feel whole and I don't know what to do about it.

Existentialism is a word that gets tossed around the pretentious circles quite a bit, and, for the most part, has lost all of its meaning by the it arrives in a conversation outside of some abstract academic realm. Filtered through artists, psuedo-intellectuals, critics, and the frustrated it usually comes out as some perversion meaning depressed. The quote from the Ojise is a little closer to the actual original intention:

Existence precedes essence.

What this means, to put it briefly, is that life provides us with a blank slate and it is up to us to give it meaning and create purpose in our day to day existence. Sartre talked a great deal about por soi/en soi or being-in-itself and being-for-itself, these being distinctions between realms of reality. Life *as is* this can of soda, my pants, etc. just plain ol' being-in-itself, and then human consciousness. Being-for-itself isn't supposed to be taken in a solipsistic, self-centered attitude but stating that human consciousness exists without excuse or predetermined meaning and thus must be a constant project.

A lack to fulfill.

This isn't necessarily a bad thing, nor a depressing thing, but it is an inescapable thing, and what makes it a painful experience is trying to escape this forever freedom to create who it is that you are. Which is what you've been doing with the constant distraction thing, a mere attempt to delegate the responsibilty for your freedom and identity. Sartre called this "Bad Faith" and a lot of what he had hoped to develop into a new form of psychoanalysis and ethics was based on this psychological tendency that human beings fell into, which made them miserable since the denial is impossible we are "condemned to be free". Unfortunately, for psychoanalysis, Sartre then became interested in politics and never developed this into a process.

Of course, due to the nature of this existential dilema, an actual method may be difficult, if not impossible, to develop. Because, no matter what I say, you are still free and it is only *you* who can freely realize this. I try to present you with your responsibility of choice but I can't make you be free, I can't do your freedom for you, so I can only try to make you face your inescapable position.

You are condemned to be free: choose.

Choose this or choose that, chances are you'll regret either, but even not choosing is choosing, and each choice will take you to another choice. It can be intimidating since you can never stop, but it can be invigorating since your path is always up to you, and what you do will always make a difference. It may be, at times, tempting to try to be rid of this responsibility for your life by giving somebody else the rights to decide for you, but they are under the same restrictions and freedoms that you are, and, as has already been said, will inevitably become part of your life so intrinsically that you won't have escaped what you were running from after all.

So your best bet would be to just try creating yourself...you'll never be able to escape this feeling of not being whole, but isn't that a good thing? If you were whole then you'd be a finished project, but since you aren't there are an endless amount of possibilities for you.
