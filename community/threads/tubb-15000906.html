Refute me
I'm not Christian, but the following argument has been made which is very intriguing. I forget the name of the person who first proposed it at the moment (It starts with a "P" I believe). I figured that given the general ignorance of some of the basic philosophical arguments that have been proposed, I'd enlighten the unknowing.

The question here is not whether or not God (in the Christian sense) exists, but whether or not you should believe in Him.

If one believes in God, and He does exist, then you are given the ultimate reward - Heaven. An eternity of bliss and happiness.

If one believes in God, and He does NOT exist, you won't even know you're wrong. Your mind will be snuffed out, and you gain or lose nothing other than adhering to a doctrine that teaches some good things.

If one does not believe in God, and He does not exist, then you don't even get the satisfaction of being correct. You're dead, gone, kaput.

If one does not believe in God and He DOES exist, well then you're fucked. You're going to Hell, and you gonna burn (Amen).

So, by believing in God, you have everything to gain, and nothing to lose, while not believing in Him, you have everything to lose and nothing to gain.

The basic rebuttal of Pascal's Wager goes something along these lines:

If you were to 'believe' in a God simply for the sake of going to heaven, then you'd know, deep down inside, that this belief was indeed a load of bullshit.

Since belief is entirely a personal thing, the only person you'd be fooling was yourself.

Now assuming God knows what you believe, deep down inside, he would then know that you're in fact trying to cheat your way into heaven.

So to take full advantage of Pascal's Wager, you'd need some sort of method of selective memory removal.

Like all of the arguements for the existance of God, Pascals wager is easy to refute.

Consider this claim: There is no God but there is a heaven and hell. You will go to heaven if you are an atheist and you will go to hell if you believe that there is a God.

Now apply Pascals wager to it and see if you can "prove" that atheism is true.

There are two main mistakes that Pascal makes. First is he never even deals with the issue if God exists or not. It is just a sales pitch to get you to accept it. You can make up any infinite reward and punishment and claim to tie it to any issue with no proof whatsoever and make the same sales pitch. I am not buying.

The second is that he makes the "Christian" mistake of thinking that life in the theoretical heavan has meaning while life here on earth does not. This cannot be true even if there is a heavan. The difference between life on earth and in heavan would just be a matter of degree, not nature. There is pleasure and pain on earth. There would just be more pleasure in heavan and more pain in hell. Life on earth has duration, immortality would just be more duration. Now relativly speaking, heavan and hell would be so much more than anything life on earth could be that it would be worth a shitty life to make it to heavan if you knew that was your choice. But if life here has no meaning, then neither would more of it in heavan or hell. You can lose something here on earth and you should not give it up for a lie no matter how big the lie is. Keep in mind that a lie can always get bigger. Even bigger than the lie about the existence of God. If you cannot prove that the lie (or claim) is true, then you should not believe it. Otherwise you will be at the mercy of whoever comes up with the next bigger lie.
