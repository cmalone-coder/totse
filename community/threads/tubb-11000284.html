Re: usb.........why?
The goal of the developers of USB was to create a common "plug" that would not only be fast enough to handle devices other than a keyboard or mouse -- like a printer or scanner for example -- but also universal enough so that it would make the lives of hardware designers easier. After all USB was designed by hardware designers... USB offers a common bus architecture that allows a printer to be useable on a PC as well as a Mac, for example. One printer can be made and shipped making the whole thing cheaper to produce. Also, having computers with USB saturated in the market provides a platform for many novelty devices to launch from. Say... Those stupid game things as well as any number of dynamic input devices (force feedback?).

Whether or not the interface is the greatest is another issue. I thought my first post covered the point but oh well.

And since when has "fucked up on driver issues" ever stopped the industry from releasing a standard?

Re: usb.........why?
Didn't read through all the posts but wanted to contributed my two cents. Factually speaking, there were only a couple of Mac motherboards designed to use Apple Desktop Bus(ADB) and PS/2 peripherals. Pretty sweet deal if you like both Macs and PCs; they were all Alchemy and Tanzania(1 or 2, I forget) motherboards and I've only seen them on powercomputing computers. Its always been possible to use an adapter to put a PS/2 keyboard/mouse onto an ADB port but Macs no longer ship with ADB so thats not so much of a problem.

The idea of USB is pretty straight forward: create a standard for peripherals that really is plug and play. The earliest USB devices were like this and great; a USB disk drive was plug and play and hot swappable, same for burners, hard disks, flash media, and a panoply of other devices that seemed like good ideas at the time. The thing is USB is pretty weak, in terms of power and in terms of bandwidth. People thought it was cool at first how you could daisy chain hubs and devices til you had the number of ports you needed and without any ID conflicts but the reality of it was that a lot of devices either sucked up most of the power originating from the USB port or required extra power cables, requiring more wires and making things more complicated. Then, hardware makers decided that their devices were special enough to require drivers of their own, creating their own problems. Guess the point of that history was to show that USB began as a pretty bright idea for simplifying things and has evolved into just another semicomplicated standard with ID conflicts, driver conflicts, power conflicts, and not a fast enough data rate. Supposedly USB 2.0 should fix the power and data rate problems but I guarantee that more driver and ID conflicts will occur with it. Someone also mentioned how USB 2.0 is around to get people to buy more computers or hardware; thats probably true too. But a 480 Mbps data rate(I think thats it...) is more than fast enough for a lot of devices.

Kind of addressing the use of USB keyboards and mice, have any of you ever used a computer in a really bad location to get at the back? Either in some kind of a rack or maybe under a cramped desk? Probably. USB, done right hopefully in 2.0, should make it so you can plug that portable hard disk into your keyboard hub, saving you the inconvenience of reaching down. The principal idea behind USB was to simplify everything. By only using USB cables instead of serial and PS/2 parallel cables, things can be much more plug and play. Additionally, USB is supposed to be able to provide more power to devices. Isn't it easier to only have one cable for a device, for both data and power?

On another note, isn't firewire just Apple's marketing name for IEEE-1394? Apple's Yosemite G3s(the Blue and White desktops) were the first computers that featured firewire. I think 1394 carrys electrical power too, at least I'm about 99% certain that i've seen an iPod charging on a vaio. IEEE is just kind of a standards institute for new electrical devices.

um, one last thing. Anyone seen the new truly wireless keyboards? The ones that you just place near your computer to charge? Anyone have any idea how that is done? I read an old slashdot post speculating that they work kind of like microwaves, getting some kind of molecules to vibrate, which produces kinetic energy, which is somehow stored as potential energy. The post was from like 3 weeks back, anyone else read it? It would be cool to find out how to charge a battery wirelessly...
