Re: Does anybody know why certain drugs cause pupil dilation/constriction?
Depends on the drug.

Anticholinergics, as their name suggets, blocks the action of ACh, which is used by the PNS. Pupil constriction is controlled by the PNS, so when the PNS's neurotransmitter is unavailable, well, hello Mr. Mydriasis.

Dopamine tends to cause mydriasis through adrenergic receptor agonism. Excite the adrenergic receptors > increase adrenaline release > pupils dilate.

Opiates don't disprove this, it just shows that whatever is causing the miosis is moar powerful than dopamine's mydriatic action. In this case, opioids cause miosis through the K-OR. What exactly the K-OR has to do with miosis, you've got me, but that appears to be the rub...

As for serotonergic hallucinogens and pupil dilation... Mydriasis is a common symptom in serotonin syndrome, and 5-HT3 acts on the PNS, so it's reasonable to assume, I would imagine, that drugs that bind to 5-HT3 at least (possibly other receptors, as well) might cause mydriasis through that pathway. Whether or not LSD or any other bind to 5-HT3, I have no idea :D Sorry.

It's like I'm seeing colors I've never seen before! Sorry. Hope this clears it up:

Anticholinergics - Drugs that block the action of acetylcholine, like benadryl, datura, etc.
ACh - Acetylcholine; a neurotransmitter
PNS - Either peripheral nervous system or, in this case, parasympathetic nervous system. I guess PSNS would be more appropriate, but the parasympathetic is part of the peripheral anyway, so it's not that big of a deal
Mydriasis - Pupil dilation
Miosis - Pupil constriction
K-OR - Kappa opioid receptor
5-HT3 - A family of 5-HT (serotonin) receptors
