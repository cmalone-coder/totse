Here's some information for Aussie shoplifters from the perspective of a long-time employee for a Coles supermarket.

WARNING: The texts depicted below are for informational and educational purposes only, I do not condone stealing in any situation, and I do not take, nor imply any responsibility herein if you use the below information in any way contrary to its informational nature. The information listed below should not be used in anyway to assist in shoplifting, nor should it be used to undermine the security of any Cole's supermarket in anyway.

Okay, legal mumbo-jumbo over.

CCTV

The only camera's that are functional and recording are the camera's:

*Near the batteries/appliances.
*At the entrances, exits and fire-escapes.
*In the front-end of the store, point-of-sale (checkouts).
*Near candy bars, magazines, chips etc.
*Near the drink fridges.
*Near vitamins and medicine.
*Entrances into store-rooms and staff-rooms.

Common blind-spots include:

*Fruit & veg/produce.
*Deli.
*Dairy fridges.
*Baking goods.
*Pet food.
*The long back aisle

The camera's are not monitored, the store has one camera monitor in the manager's office, and is usually switched off, the camera's have the ability to zoom in/out, and can pan. The video footage is stored for 14 days locally, and is then deleted. The monitor can only monitor up to 5 camera's at any one time, and the camera's monitored most are checkouts, exits and fire-doors.



LP

*No product under $10 will set off the sensors.

*Coles doesn't prosecute for any amount under $20.

*There are no specially trained LP; managers and some filling staff are trained in loss prevention, managers are all out of the store after 5PM Monday-Friday, weekend managers, night-fill managers and "2IC's" are not trained in loss prevention.

*It's store policy not to follow people after they are off store property, and is also store policy not inform the police unless the dollar amount stolen exceeds $20 and/or the person is a known/serial shoplifter.

*RFID tags are usually the plastic rectangular ones, and are easily visible. On the inside's of large boxes (appliances and so-forth) there are tags on the underneath of the box lid. All tags are easily taken off with a knife or even by hand.

*Apart from the meat which has a dollar value exceeding $30 no meat will have any tags whatsoever, the tags on expensive meats are underneath the price/information label. Rip the plastic lining off, and whack the tray into a bag (on shit like steaks, sausages, etc.)

The staff

The staff are usually either bored teenagers who steal shit themselves (or who don't give a flying fuck if you do), or busy over-worked managers who have other stuff to worry about than shoplifters. Generally nobody particularly cares if an alarm goes off, during my time as an employee I'd only ever glance over at the sensors for a second, shrug it off and resume work.


The policies and insecurities I mentioned are the same at all Coles Supermarkets across Australia. Have fun.

Whenever I go on a 'shopping' spree I bring:
  • Inconspicuous baseball hat (it's one of those sk8r boi ones you see all the time now, stole it from some kid at school) to hide your face (and in my case my hair)
  • Large shoulder bag with a zipper top. I made it so the top folds over a bit so I can have it open and easy to get into without people seeing it. (put a few items in it before hand so it isn't obvious when you don't go in with an empty bag and leave with a full one). This is for putting stolen items in, if you haven't already guessed.
  • Baggyish pants, for putting items down if there is more security (cameras, people watching). When I'm paying for items at the counter I can actually take things there by sticking them down my pants, very discrete.
  • Exact-o knife. A very sharp one. For removing tags and security stickers.
My favorite place to go is the mall. Mine has really low security and if you go during the day on a school day it's almost empty and very low staffed.

Basically, I just pick a store, go in a browse. When I see something I like I place it behind a blind spot, wait until the staff is distracted and stuff it into my bag.

For clothes, if there is tags then take it into the dressing room to remove them. If it's the kind of place that counts how many items you take in, stuff something in your bag like you were stealing it, take it into the dressing room to take the tag off with stuff you'll pretend to put on but just put back.

CD's are trickier since almost all of them have those goddamn stickers on the top. Find a CD, check if it has the sticker on top. If not your in the clear. If not, move to a bli;nd spot and quickly remove it with your exacto knife. It just just take a couple swipes.

If you're being followed around by staff and you think you're caught, take them off guard. Go right up to them and ask them for something you know they don't have. This gives you a reason to be there and a reason to leave immediately.

If you set off an alarm, don't run! Those things go off all the time for no reason and in a busy mall half the time the staff don't even hear them. So pretend you don't either. Maintain a steady pace. Look straight ahead. Look confident. If you didn't look suspicious in the store they wont come after you. If they do, then is the time to book it (or surrender, if you chose).

In between stores, head outside and take the tags off everything. It's also good to have a stash spot (a car or something) so if you get busted it'll just be for a few items, not everything you have stolen.

Also, give it a rest. Don't go to the same store every day and steal shit unless you need to. Very suspicious, especially if you don't buy anything.

Happy shopping!
