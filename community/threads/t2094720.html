Predisposed to using opiates
Has anyone heard of being predisposed to opiate use? Not necessarily that you will become a full blown addict, but at the right dose you'll notice a legit improvement in how you function as a person. Basically, taking something like methadone not because you are a recovering junkie, but because you benefit from it in the same way a person with anxiety problems benefits from a benzo prescription.

Specific improvement would be things like smoother flow of thoughts and more control over thoughts(a sort of relief for ADD), reduced self conscious feelings and increased confidence(however not overbearingly confident), increased motivation and ambition(amotivational syndrome seemingly going away), mood swings no longer as dramatic. Theres more but I think you can get the idea from that. It seems as though if a person has a platter of mild mental problems like the ones I just mentioned, an appropriate dose of opiates may help significantly with a blanket effect on all of them.

To give you an idea of the dose range:

Methadone - Typically 20-30mg has the most ideal effect with little to no opiate tolerance. Produces a slight high but very apparent change in what I mentioned earlier(i.e. motivation, cognitive function, confidence). With a tolerance at times dose reaches 50-60mg but easily can be reduced again.

Dilaudid - Extremely similar results as the methadone. I don't remember exactly, but swallowed the dose was around 6-8mg. Frequently a combination of snorting half the dose and swallowing the other half for more immediate effects, without getting fucked up.


So far those are the only two drugs where such effects have been observed, however not many other opiates have been tried. The trend seems to be (once again with little to no tolerance) a light to mild dose of a stronger opiate, generally swallowed since the 'rush' is not what is being looked for. Something like percocet doesn't produce the same relief, no matter the dose.

Does any of this seem rational? I tried to find information on opiates as psychological medication but didn't come up with much.
