Also to be considered. A life size Tv screen showing a palm tree and beach placed next to real palm trees would be obvious, even if viewed from directly in front. The reason is that it is a flat surface which makes it obvious to our binocular vision.
How could you achieve a 3D image on a flat surface to mask your stealth tank? we cant even build a 3D television yet.
to achieve a true illusion of three dimensionality every reciever would have to accept light from a near infinate series of angles and every emitter would have to take that colour pixel and emit it at at the corresponding angle.

This sounds a bit of a mission to me. perhaps achievable with a kind of fly's eye reciever emitter design of tiny hemispheres all over the object's surface. however if the minimum diameter of an optic cable is 1mm and you'd need thousands of angles on every dome to create even a semi realistic 3D illusion, the size of every dome would be way too big to create an hi res image. Equally every reciever dome would have to be just as big, generating a darker shadow.

It would be very difficult indeed to create a 3D illusion, our pupil's are at only about 2 inches apart and yet our binocular vision is effective up to about 200 m say. Thats a tiny tiny angle that our brains calculate. If it was going to look even half realistic the domes would need to be huge.

Therefore you get a comprimise scenario. Either you have a high res flat image, or a very low res one that is poorely 3 dimensional.

Either option or anything in between would look blatantly obvious to anyone up close.

The best you could hope for would be a kind of chamelion camoflage that could keep your tank well hidden about 500 m away.
