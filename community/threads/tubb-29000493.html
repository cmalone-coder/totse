First off a KB! (kaboom) can happen in all types of firearms and all types of calibers. However the .40 (would also include the .357sig cartidge) glock reportively has a higher frequency of doing so, primary do to the unsupported chamber.

When we're talking unsupported chamber we're talking about exposed brass, specifically at the six o'clock position, resulting in most kb's blowing the magazine clear out of the gun (damaging other components, barrel, etc.)!

This I tested myself by taking a barrel from a glock 40 and a barrel from a hk 40 and sig 40, dropping various ammo types into each one and inspecting the area around the feedramp. Noticeably the glock leaves more exposed brass.

Others have indicated that kbs frequently result in poor reloads. Since the .40 and .357sig is a high pressure round, quite possibly a weakened spent cartridge adds to the unpredictability of such a event happening.
