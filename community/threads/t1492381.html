Goddamnit, mook, for the Nth time, I don't IV. But I have before, and I'll just say that, while the Heroin and Meth produced their effects, they don't compare to "natural" rewarding/pleasurable feelings, not quite. This is because A: You know you did a drug, and thus you know it's artificial, even if you're only aware of this on a subconscious level, and B: There are many, many variables that govern the exact nature of your neurochemical processes, or "emotions", to use another word. An analogy:

Methamphetamine does what it does by inhibiting the reuptake of Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline]; or blocking the chemical receptors responsible for cleansing your blood of it: In a word, it induces the exact same fight-or-flight response as an intense, traumatic event, and keeps your body from reabsorbing the norepinephrine (which would end the response; this is why meth highs last so long, as opposed to gone-as-fast-as-they-came adrenal responses) this is where the vasoconstriction, energy, clearer mind, etc, come from. HOWEVER, it's different from an adrenal response, because you endured no trauma to bring it about. And yes, meth has other neurochemical action responsible for euphoria and the like, but I'm focusing on one only. If you are, say, wounded badly, the adrenal response feels MUCH different, to say the least, than a meth high, even though the same neurotransmitters are responsible for both.

Heroin works by binding to the Mu opioid receptors in the body, and absorbs much quicker due to the diacetylization of the morphine (hence, why heroin is called Diacetylmorphine), and stimulates a release of Dopamine, a neurotransmitter responsible for pleasurable/rewarding feelings. However, again, a Heroin high feels different than, say, feeling good after doing a good deed or eating good food. I know you haven't done Heroin, by the way, but you've done opiates, which is the main experience you need, and yours specifically is sufficient enough to grasp what I'm saying here.

...Heh, sorry, got all BLTC on this. Didn't mean to. What I'm trying to say is this: Neurochemical processes, even if brought about by the same/similar neurotransmitters, vary greatly depending on your psychological state, whatever you did to bring it about, and/or your reaction to an event that brought it about. Orgasm vs. a needle full of smack are two different experiences, y'know?

Oh, and an orgasm can't really be compared accurately to anything of this Earth. That doesn't mean it's the best-feeling: It simply means that nothing out there really, truly replicates it exactly.
