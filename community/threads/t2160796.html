Everyone has to work. Realistically you are going to mix it up so that you are getting a fair amount of Fridays/Saturdays where you can still go out, but not every week. I personally think its better to make a better wage, and have more expensive fun on the days I do have off. Also, while you have your time off, and your social circle is working, you have money to afford to do things to keep you entertained.

Also, it is pretty easy to find people who also work the odd hours and form a mini-social circle within those people. You can hang with them during off-peak times and have some fun.

If you can keep your body and mind straight, and learn to enjoy mid-week fun, the pay is so much better, and job security is better when you are the guy who works the hours that most people wont.

Families do suffer though, if you are looking long term. Financially they do great, but wives hate it, or love it if they hate their spouse.

Re: Part time work
I dunno, I am a pretty lazy guy, so I cant give much positive input when it comes to hard labour. Your dads company sounds like you would have to bust your ass really hard for those few months, then pretty much do the paperwork/management in the off season.

I personally am more inclined to take jobs which spread the work evenly over the year, but like I said, im not one for manual labour.

Have you actually done what your dad does? Like, have him put you to work so you know if you can physically cut it?

If you are going to have employees eventually, you have to be able to do every job they may face, as well as teach them to do the work. Also, if they flake out on you, you gotta cover their work, which means back-to-back shifts of the shit they should have completed.

The first few years are completely on you. Until you really know the business inside and out, you cant trust employees. A bad fuck up will cost you everything. When you run the business, you may run yourself into a debt that you will have to work your entire life to pay off.

If you just become an employee somewhere, that stress is all on someone else. You arent trapped in one career. If something pisses you off, fuck it, you move on.

Crane operators can make a good 100k-200k/year, without pushing themselves that hard. And that is world-wide work. Technology isnt interested in replacing a good ole fashioned crane for the next few hundred years.

If you are willing to move around, 250k-350k is definitely realistic for 60 hours/week, with holiday time off. But, can you do it?
