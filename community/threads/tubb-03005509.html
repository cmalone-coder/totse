I'm Not Even a Serf!

Okay, here's a thought. The average medievel serf was forced to work all day in the hot sun, breaking his damn back for his master. He got to keep about half of what he produced. He wasn't allowed to own a weapon, or to do anything his master considered "sinful."
My question is, how is this different from today? Well, actually, it is different, because in purely mathematical terms, the serf has it better than you. He got to keep 50% of the fruits of his labor - the average American, after income tax, FICA, social security, and inflation created by blundering state monetary policy, gets to keep a mere 48%.
The other restrictions of serfdom apply to us as well. To be fair, we are allowed weapons. However, any weapon that would be of actual use in an armed rebellion against the state is strictly forbidden - that's one less reason to keep the people happy.
And don't try engaging in anything that prevailing culture defines as "sinful." Drugs are strictly forbidden, if by "drugs" you mean "only those psychoactive substances that can be connected with an ostensibly anti-american stereotype." You aren't allowed to get yourself a hooker - despite the fact that prostitution is certainly a more honest profession than that practiced by our politicians. Are they our leaders, or our nannies?
All of this comes down to one thing, friends - the state OWNS you. You are nothing but property to it, to be used or discarded as is convenient.
Didn't our founding fathers fight a revolution over this kind of thing?
