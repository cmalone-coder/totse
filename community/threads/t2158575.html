Oh, and you should learn to read:
5-MeO-DALT is uncontrolled in the United States. This means it is generally considered legal to buy, possess, and distribute (sell, trade or give).

Also theres also the possibility of getting prosecuted under the federal analog act because of similarities to scheduled substances.

Nope. 5-meo-DMT analogues are classified as 5-methoxy tryptamines with at least 1 N-alkyl substituent. Allyl isn't an alkyl group (neither is vinyl, isoallyll, isopropenyl, etc. :)), thus it is not covered by the analogue law (and coincidentally, neither are similar 5-esters. Don't believe me? Look up 4-Acetoxy DMT on erowid. Same principle applies with 4-O substituents [though I'm not sure if it applies to non-methyl alkyl ethers. Somebody want to help me out? I'd research it myself but I'm busy as shit. :(]. One interesting thing here though, is that unlike the N-substitutions, the 5-XO substituents actually increase in potency [and some say enjoyabililty] as the groups get larger. Shulgin even described the phenyl ether as a good idea :eek:). It's all about the phrasing of the law, which in this case simply means that if something on the N group isn't saturated then it's not an analogue. Shit, you could throw a phenyl on there and it would be totally legal. I would imagine there are at least another 30 combinations of totally legal and psychoactive tryptamine analogues just from the substituents I've described above. JP was right, pihkal and tihkal describe more possibilities than real chemicals.
