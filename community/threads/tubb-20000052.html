Re: making nunchakus
Nunchakus are typically about one foot long and one-and-a-half inches in diameter. Rope/chain length is three to four inches. Ropes are often doubled, i.e. each piece is connected by two pieces of rope close together. Making them with chains is much more difficult, as the chain actually ends in balls set into sockets so it won't twist up.

But really, if you just want to practice I suggest just walking into a martial arts school and finding out if they sell practice nunchakus made of foam rubber and plastic. They're very common, as most instructors won't let students use wooden ones until they get better (for good reason, you hurt yourself a lot!).

-Gravehead, Tae Kwon Do Guru

Bamboo is downright WICKED to beat someone with, because it flexes as you swing it. It's like a whip and a club combined. I couldn't see it as being a good wood for nunchakus for several reasons. As far as durability, it's almost unheard of for nunchakus to break in a fight as long as they're made of something reasonably strong.

Re: making nunchakus
Weight isn't a necessity to gain power behind your strikes. It's like with baseball bats- Babe Ruth used to swing something like a 42-oz. bat, while smaller guys (Mays, Williams, Griffey) use much lighter ones, yet they can all crank the ball a mile. The extra speed generated by the bat counteracts the deficiency of the lower weight. Likewise, you lose some (not all) of the whipping action with the bamboo, plus it's lighter than some woods, but the speed with which you can snap that around will leave some serious marks on anyone that gets hit (including you!).

Really, there is no best material for everyone.. the weight needs to feel right to you. The best way to hurt someone with nunchaku is to have one that feels just right to you, because your movement will be smoother and more efficient. Experiment.

if you're going to make practice nunchukau from wood, try something soft and file the edges round. For the actual weapons, plane it into an octagonal shape. The edges and points will concentrate the force and allow the weapon to cut. A final note: do not use chain, it binds, and it's noisy.

If you want to make a set of ass whomping nunchakus you have some options. The first thing you have to decide is: "Do I want to kill people or make them wish they were dead." If you want to destroy somebody, go for oak, steel, (if you can handle it), or anything else heavy, solid and hard. If you opt for the latter, I would recommend Sch 40 PVC Pipe. "WHAT?" You may be asking. Here's the thing. PVC 'Chucks are light, fast, durable, cheap and they HURT like nothing you've ever felt before. This is because they are hollow and plastic. The other plus is that you are less likely to cause permanent damage to somebody. The second factor is your binding. String and Rope are light, cheap, and not made of metal, (read: do not show up in metal detectors). Chain bindings are heavy, durable, and hard to build. It's a little more complicated than just running a chain between your handles. You need some kind of ball joint that allows flexion, (similar to your shoulder joint). I'll let you look into that aspect. My personal preference? 1 foot handles made from 3/4" sch 40, bound with braided/woven Spyder Wire. Fast, efficient, cheap, and not metal. = )
