Re: MB Limits At Wally's
The registers have limits programmed into them. Like age limits for alcohol for example.

Wally's doesn't have limits on matches, but at $1.24 a box, they come out a bit more than the $1.99 box of presidents at WG's, the other Wal.

If you review my past posts, you will see that I have always been dead set against diamonds. Recently I read mad's post on cookin and his opinion on these along with an old pal of mine, shooter, caused me to further research this brand.

The yields are somewhat significant. WG's store brand can be cut off and simply put into a jar and shaken to remove the stuff from the strips. The exception is the boxes that are wrapped in paper instead of the cardboard box. Paper wrapped are best avoided. The cardboard boxed give up about .3g per $1. Not a lot of glue, no dyes.

The old presidents have to be rubbed off. As mad recommends, just rub them off with your gloved fingers (or ungloved if you're used to working with your hands and they're already fucked up anyway). They give up about .6g per box or .3g per dollar. They have to be cut precisely to avoid all the red dye on the cover. Some red dye is unavoidable. The newly revised presidents is completely different and has not been fully batch tested yet. They give up about .7g per box or .35g per dollar. Same red dye.

The difference between the three is color and smell. They all burn about the same. WG's has a reddish brown color when dry (powder). It has more of a purple tint when wet. It has a noticable rp odor but not strong. The old presidents is brick red in color wet or dry. It has a strong rp odor. The new presidents is dark brown in appearance, but also has a very strong odor of rp.

The brick red kind has been used at the rate of .3g per g (pseudo) and produced very favorable product. When filtered and dried, it looked like ash. It was discarded. Rp can be reused if not used up. It has recently been concluded that much more experience has been gained and rp should be used in amounts that are closer to what is actually used so that when filtered it can be thrown away instead of reused. If you're not sure, always use to much. Not enough and it will cost you in the end.

One more thing. Hypophosphorous acid is not easy to make. It requires intermediate distilling skills, high temps, quality glassware and equipment and will ruin at least one boiling flask per batch.
