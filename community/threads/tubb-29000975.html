For home defense there are two options in my opinion:

1) for pistol lovers - a .25 or .22 caliber semi-automatic loaded with ratshot (first couple of rounds) and standard lead rimfire cartridges (rest of clip)

2) for rifle/shotgun lovers - a mossberg 500/590 pump action shotgun with full magazine loaded with birdshot (first round) and 3/4 inch slugs (rest of magazine)

either one will function adequately for home defense needs up to and including multiple home invaders, but will fall short at the "Organized paramilitary strike" point. Viper, where did you obtain this "enlightening" information?

How can a .38 snubbie be more powerful than a 9mm automatic? With only an inch of barrel, all of that +P powder will just make a louder, bigger muzzle flash and not help with velocity.

I don't see how a .38 would "knock someone back" any better than a 9mm. Contrary to Hollywood belief, bullets do very little to actually shove someone down. They fall because the bullet is tearing into them, shaking up their nervous system quite a bit. I could see how a bowling ball flying at someone could knock them down, but a tiny piece of lead wouldn't before just going through them. the difference of mass between the .38 special and the 9mm parabellum is only a few seven-thousands of a pound. Also, a general rule to know is that if the gun is light, then recoil will be heavier. This means that it will affect how fast you can make a follow-up shot. The 9mm would be faster in this regard.

I think that hollow point bullets are pretty much a given.

Revolvers may be a tiny bit more reliable than automatics, but modern pistol technology has made automatics pretty darn reliable in their own right also.
