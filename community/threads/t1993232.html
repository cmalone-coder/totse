Starting with Pyrotechnics

If you have interest in learning about pyrotechnics/making explosives, please read on. I have decided to try and release this world of several misconceptions about pyro. Do not expect to find any bomb "recipes", or ways to total a car with household chemicals in this guide, or even simple explosive compositions. After reading trough, I hope you’ll be able to find those yourself.

Starting out
Using google, anyone can find all the info you’ll need. Good words to run a search on are: Pyrotechnics, blackpowder, flash, compositions, salute, polumna, polumnia,
bad words, which you should not google
making bombs, cobs, pipe bomb, household chemicals

If you spend enough time searching and reading basic information, you can start making up an idea for your first project. Try to follow this order:

* decide what you want to make
* do research on it (materials, safety, etc…
* *stress* cross refence ! *stress*
* see if it’s fesible
* see if you can get the chemicals
* if so, make it
* carefully use it

If needs be, you can ask on forums for info, but only if you have done an adequate amount of research. Asking things like “how do I make gunpowder”, can be very frustrating. Make sure to check existing threads too. Most of the time, a beginners question has been asked dozens of times.

Not all forums will respond the same, some will help you immediately, others will tell you to get lost. To minimize the chances of being flamed, try and follow these simple guidelines:

Researching

The first object you’ll have to tackle is finding sites. Easy enough, there are plenty. The second obstacle, is determining whether information can or can not be trusted. It’s easy enough to copy and paste the url into a post, and ask if anybody has ever tried it before, but there are better and faster ways.

1)site lists
Plenty of forums offer enormous lists of good sites. The minimal quality will greatly depend on the members of the board were the links got posted. It’s a good idea to search trough these. If you already know what you are interested in making, use google to see if it’s on a site or not (“site:www.”url”.com “project”), this can save you a lot of time, but looking trough the actual sites will also teach you other things, and perhaps sharpen your interest on a subject, or give you ideas for future projects.

2)Begin your search in a pre-verified source.
When a forum regulars recommends you (or someone else before you) a specific site for a specific project, the info will most likely be correct.
Sites like www.wikipedia.org wil also provide quite a bit of useful information, but mind that anybody can submit. The part about making rust for thermite for example, was copied directly from the anarchists cookbook

About that “cookbook”, do not trust sources like this. Cookbooks are sources for cooks, I personally enjoy cooking and eating, as do I enjoy pyrotechnics, but that doesn’t mean I use the same sources for both. “Books” like the Jolly rogers cookbook, the poor mans james bond, or any other file with a title containing cookbook, anarchy or similar should not be trusted.

I do however suggest you read them, it greatly improves you ability to tell if someone you’re asking questions, believes that BS himself. I can’t stress enough how important it is to be able to separate good shit from bullshit. If you know something to be bullshit in advance, it’ll make the job easier.

3)Cross referencing
When you get two sites on a subject, take a look at them both. The basic info will most likely be the same, or differ only slightly. For example, one site may state ratios like (70.20.10) an other one says (69.15.16). The bigger part of the time, ratios get adjusted by the user, because they found them to work best for them. Mind that all of these people use different chemicals, figures they use adjusted ratios.

More important when cross referencing, is the additional info you get. One person gives you this little thing, another one gives you that additional piece of safety information, a third one gives possible substitutes for chems and so on. The more you read and learn the better.

4)Commercial fireworks
Studying commercial fireworks if available is also a good way to learn. Reading about firecrackers, polumnas and fountains is good, actually seeing for yourself how they’re constructed, is way better. Those devices are often safe enough to take apart, but be as safe as possible none the less. Always wear at least eye protection and gloves. Try and go for fabric gloves for durability and sturdiness.

Taking apart fireworks, will also yield supplies. You can get fuse of good quality from these, which *should* be safer than what you make yourself. Getting explosive powder out of them is far from cost-effective, and you'll never know for sure what it is you're getting out of there.

SMOKE MIX
Ingredients

--Sucrose (Sugar)
Sucrose is common table sugar. It's mixed with the KNO3 and acts as a fuel. The Sucrose burns ineffeciently, and the unused energy is released as smoke.

Making it
--Ratio 4:6 Sucrose:KNO3 of 3:5 but I haven't tried it
