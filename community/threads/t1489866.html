Branching out?
As this isnt the first serious relationship my girlfriend and I have been in, we each bring several lessons from the past. Apparently one she learned was not to be totally dependant on each otherfor in case of breaking up, its like your world melted.

Ten months into our relationship she decided it would be a good idea to rekindle some friendships that she neglected/abandoned/forgot about. (Never in my life [starting with pre-school aged, at least] have I kept more than one best friend [or girlfriend] at a time. I didnt like competing for friendships, the third-wheel effect, shit like that.) So as the first weeks of 04 she became noticeably close to two of her old (guy) friends. Their idea of a good time is basically getting smashed and playing pool or some board game. My girlfriend is in high school (as are her friends) and Im living at college a half-hour away.

The way our relationship had worked thus far was that I would come home nearly every weekend (excepting when one of us was uncommonly busy) and see her as much as possible. As of a few weeks ago she decided the weekend was a perfect time to see her friends although she sees them a few times a week she protests that she can really only hang out with them for any amount of time on weekends. Last weekend she said she would rather just come visit me for a couple hours on Friday, and that way she could see her friends Friday and Saturday nights (usually reserved to me). I came home for various family-related reasons. So I saw her Friday night (after she was done hanging out with the boys, after dinner). Saturday night I went down to CCSU to see an old friend of mine that I hadnt been talking to very much since high school because she wanted to see her friends and I thought it was better to try and keep a friend or two to take my mind off not being whom she wanted to see. (She got drunk and apparently close to alcohol poisoning, throwing up over a dozen times.) And Super-Bowl Sunday I went to her house for the game, I think because her friends were at a party she didnt want to go to, and watch the game with her parents and her (in a pathetic, hung-over, sickly state).

Ive been mountingly miserable over this. Tonight when she nagged me into bringing it up again I explained the feeling of worthlessness gained from feeling like there are several who share my place, and the feeling of neglect spurned from being neglected. When I brought up this past weekend in my defense (Why wasnt she content to see her friends for a few hours on Friday, and me the rest, etc.) she said something like because when Im with my friends were actually doing stuff instead of lying around (which is mostly what we do, but once she expressed she was discontent with it we started playing games and such). I was tired of being contradicted by this time and figured she was beyond talking to. She had been pinning me to so youre upset because I want to spend time with my friends, refusing to hear what I was trying to explain.

Im not sure how to handle this; Im having real trouble coping with her choosing her friends over me. I trust her around her friends, but natural paranoia occasionally lingers.

Further, she has placed talks about how we need to spend less time together, because shell be going to school in the fall (probably Notre Dame or LSU, or somewhere where she will only be home for vacations). I was more enjoying the enjoy it whilst it lasts and believe that conditioning is stupid technique to try and use in this case. I cannot shake the idea of her branching out being part of a self-preservatory device, that she is detaching herself. Any light would be greeted with a hug.
