The answer is situation dependent.

Examples:

The nature of the electoral process as defined in the US Constitution gives the United States Government legitimacy. It is irrelavant whether the rest of the world 'likes' our government or its leaders.

The Magna Carta and subsequent laws have given the British Government legitimacy. I could continue down the list of governments with documented governmental structures.

Now we go to monarchies. Different breed of cat but still quite similar to the British example. The monarch is in place for multiple reasons. The acceptance of the individual in that particular leadership role is what gives that government legitimacy.

Dictators/President for Life: Simply stated (by Chairman Mao) "Power emanates from the barrel of a gun". In this/these situations, legitimacy depends on where in to political system you are. If you are in government ... it's obviously legitimate.

One man's opinion.
