Try to get over the appearance issue. It's impossible to keep up a charade for long. Just let it all hang out. As long as you look presentable or better at least a few hours a day, guys hardly notice the "greasiness" first thing in the morning. He's going to be in the same boat, "morning breath" and all.

I assume you live with your parents. Will he be sleeping in your room? If not, the whole appearance thing might not even be an issue.

Space is crucial. I can't stress this enough. Even though he is there to visit with you for the summer, there will be times you need to be apart doing your own thing. Figure out ways of doing this.

Your friends are who they are. Your bf will get to know them, and if he likes you, he should be able to see something worthwhile in your friends. You needn't include them in everything, but he should be aware that they will be around. And you might want to figure a way to spend time with your friends without your bf around. Are there opportunities for "clubbing" in your area, at your age? In the USA you need to be 21 at most worthwhile clubs. So he might have to adapt to a different type of recreation.

I live with my bf now, but the situation is quite different, since we're older, no parents, and this will likely be permanent. But we moved in together rather quickly, and are lucky to compatible in issues such as space and finances.

Hopefully my advice will help because I just went through this earlier this year.

I 'dated' my partner for one year before I moved in with him (though we had known each other for years) It was long distance as well, me in the USA and he in NZ. I was the one who moved. He had already come to the USA for a month-long visit with me, and we stayed together as we traveled around California. We thought there would be no problem living together, boy were we wrong. Both of us were completely unprepared for the strain of living together, it ruined our relationship.

Everything is riding on this, you should be concerned. What you don't need to worry about though is him seeing you at your worst. That's just a part of being with someone, he'll be more concerned of what you think of his worst. You'll learn to laugh at each other's disgusting defects and humanity.

He'll essentially be giving up his life as he knows it when he moves in with you. He'll probably be prepared for this though, so it's your style that's most likely to be cramped. He's moving into your territory, your life, your social sphere, so you'll need to do your best to open up everything to him so that he feels at home, but in doing that remember you are giving up your space, your lifestyle, etc. You're doing this because you love him, remember that. Don't allow yourself to start resenting him. Be ready to make huge adjustments and sacrifices.

RESPECT!! RESPECT!! RESPECT!! I cannot stress how important this is, you both MUST respect each other. Not just lovey dovey respect, but the kind of respect you would give a complete stranger coming to stay as a guest in your home.

During the first month try to communicate a lot as you both need to discover each other's boundaries regarding shared space and personal habits. If something is getting to you do a LOT of thinking about it before you approach it. Consider all sides, and consider what you would do in a situation where you were NOT living together. Spur of the moment griping has serious consequences, so choose your battles wisely. Learn to tell the difference between what is HIS fault, and what is a product of the situation.

Be especially tollerant and flexible, take things very slowly in the first month. I'm not suggesting that in order to live with someone you have to learn to take it up the ass, but the first month is particularly vital to the future of the relationship so you just have to approach it with a bit more deference.

Good luck!
