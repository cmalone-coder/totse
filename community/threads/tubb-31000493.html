Your analogy is not entirely correct. Afghanistan was at the time of the Soviet (not Russian) invansion a sovereign state. Chechenia has been a part of Russia since the Tsars ruled.

Russia didn�t agree, and the hostilities escalated. Also inside Chechenia, as there was a fight for power among the freedom fighters from the very start.

This in itself created even more trouble for Russia and its crumbling economy, as well as for its relations to Europe, where the "Russian Mafia" very fast became a significant factor of organised crime.

Even more important for Russia and its economy, was (and is) the oil in Chechenia (sounds familiar?!).

Russia went into Chechenia with troops in 1994 hoping to end the conflict fast. Instead they found themselves in a strategic situation similar to what happened in Afghanistan.

Still, the Chechenian people and government had enough after three years, and an agreement was made that Chechenia was to become a highly independant member of the Russian Federation. There�s good reason to believe that a majority of the Chechenian people were satisfied with this. But various groups among the freedom fighters were not.
