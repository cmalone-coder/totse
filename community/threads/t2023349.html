Re: How to fight a credit card lawsuit?
Collections is a pit stop before getting sued. My strongest recommendation is that you should try to talk to the bank and negotiate a settlement or a payment plan. If that doesn't work or if your account is already on its way to collection... then they will contact you and you should settle with them. If you ignore them, then they'll go to court... depending on the amount you owe.

Tell them you are a student/kid.. whatever and that you really intend to take care of this, but you need a payment plan and stick to that plan. Even $20-$30/month will be enough to salvage this situation before it gets worse. They might even want to settle on a lump-sum... single payment, which is much lower than the original amount. Ask a parent/relative to bail you out and settle this matter once and for all.

Trust me you don't want this to go to court. I have had a roommate go through this mess... only because he thought $700 was not enough to get him sued.

As a well educated person in things like this, let me explain how banks make their money.
First, banks don't "make" anything. Their INCOME is from interest-baring accounts from customers (credit cards, personal loans, house loans, etc etc etc.) In order to let people loan this out for a set percentage of the loan, they need the actual money, right? Well, they take the money that people have in the bank (in the form of checking accounts, savings accounts, CD's, etc) and put that money towards letting other people barrow it. That's why banks pay (in the form of interest) people who have their money in the bank (usually in the form of savings accounts or certificates of deposit). Now if you borrowed, say $100,000, you will owe them the $100,000 back plus the interest (say 10% to make it mathmatically easier). Now you owe $110,000. If you don't pay the bank back the capital (amount you were loaned) plus the interest (where they make their money and pay for the interest they pay other people who "lend" them money), they lose money from a potential profit AND can't pay back whoever they borrowed it from. Of course, it's not like someone's bank account dipped when you took the loan, but you get the idea.
Hope that helps explain the economics of banking.
