Re: Pregnancy and Confusion
The statistics are grim.

Most teens who have and keep a child never, ever rise out of poverty. Unless they come from a financially well-to-do family which offers financial and moral support.

A child these days in the USA is a half-million dollar lifetime debt.

The mother won't get a full night's sleep for a good 3-4 months. A baby is a 24/7 obligation.

She'll have depression, body aches and anxiety for the next 2-3 years. And she'll be too broke to do anything about it.

All you can do is offer moral support to your friend Scott and urge him to get a decent job, since he'll likely be held responsible for financial support.

I have heard of "adoption agencies" which "match" mothers-to-be with stable married couples who would like very much to be parents. Paid expenses and compensation are often provided. This would be the most sensible route for a pregnant teen with a non-supportive family.

16? Ouch...

I'll assume this girl is a 'good' girl and isn't just considering keeping the baby to make her ex's life horrible.

The motherly feeling is quite a powerful one, even in the face of such extraordinary odds.

How is it that Scott is financially able to care for a child? His parents loaded 'er something? They do know how much kids cost, right?

I'm in a similiar situation, an unplanned pregnancy and an ex that wants me to give the child up so that he doesn't have to be responsible. Difference is, I'm 22, educated, a skilled worker, and I've dealt with a lot of hard times which (I feel) have equiped me for the challenges ahead. If I were 16 I wouldn't have the same confidence in my abilities to provide a good enough home for a child. It's not that I'm a bad person, or would make a negligent mother... but at 16 you're asking a child to not only be an adult, but a parent. IMO that's too much to ask of someone with so little life-experience.

When you do approach her it's very important that you be understanding of the 'connection' between mum and child, consider it a mother-bear situation so don't try to come between them.

It's also important that you don't attack/question her abilities as a mother. Simply point out all of the benefits the child will have from being raised in a family setting by people who are better equiped to provide for the HUGE emotional, time, and financial expense a child represents. Also point out the benefits she stands to gain, she'll be able to finish school, enjoy a little more of her young life, etc.
