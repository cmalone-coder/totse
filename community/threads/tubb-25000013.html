when speaking of the speed of light, one must differentiate between the former and the constant c. c remains the same everywhere everywhen whatever as it is the speed of light *in a vacuum*. In conditions very different from a vacuum, such as on Earth, the speed at which light travels can be slightly lower. Light goes through water at about .75c, and it slows by a factor of about 2.54 in diamond. Photons moving through a Bose-Einstein condensate can be even slower; one particular lab, using a cloud of sodium atoms cooled to nearly 0 K slowed a laser pulse by a factor of 14, if i remember correctly. Just thought you all should know.

Actually it's one of the reasons nothing with any mass at all can achieve the speed of light. You can get closer and closer, the more energy you pour into the system, but never reach it - no matter the energy input.

It one of them thar Asymptote thingies.

It's the same with length and mass too. That is, they're both Asymptotes: as you approach C length and mass increase until, at C you have infinite mass and infinite length. Which is meaningless.

Better yet, it's undefined, which is exactly what is going on in the equations.

In the equations you have a situation where these properties [time, mass & length] are calculated by what boils down to a number divided by an ever decreasing number, as the bottom number approaches zero the value for length [or mass, etc.] increases. At C the bottom number is zero.

And you know you cannot divide by zero: it is said to be undefined.
