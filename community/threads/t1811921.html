Re: Death.
Depends on how you die. If your body is totally destroyed, then nothing.

If your brain and nervous system are mostly intact, brain activity can occur at nearly the same levels it did durring life, only without sensory input, for about 10 minutes. I would like to think that you can perceive this short period of dream-state as much longer than it actually is, to the point where it effectively qualify in your mind as some type of afterlife--brain activity slowly fading out in a manner not allowing you to realize that your dream-state is ending.

Now if this brain activity does not turn out to allow for a dream-state to occur, then there is something else I've considered. Electrical impulses continue to occur in the brain and nervous system for up to 30 days after death, if the brain and nervous system are intact. After the brain cells forming your neural-net die from lack of oxygen 6 minutes(?) after death, you are left without memories. But the parts of the brain responsible for sensation remain receptive of the electrical impulses still occuring; since the organs allowing for the senses of sight, taste, hearing, and smell will no longer function without your body's life processes, you are left only with a relatively low-level sense of touch, and the feeling of physical pain is tied to this sense. Over the period of time of these electrical impulses' persistance, you will experience nothing but the horrible festering burning sensation of your body decomposing around your nerves. This would be similar to the feeling of hellfire described in the bible; having no sense of sight or hearing one would experience quiet darkness, one being forever burned by the kind of fire god created for the sole purpose of punishing the wicked--the fire that makes no light or heat or roar, but burns thousands of times stronger than the fire we experience on earth.

That all of course was very loose speculation, and I'm certainly speaking of loosely comprehended concepts I've read or heard about. I'm not schooled in neurology or anything, just some ideas I chalked together.
