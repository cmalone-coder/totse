Re: Depression
Yes, everyone goes through ups and downs. Unless you feel that you actually have "clinical depression" that keeps you from being a functioning person in day-to-day life, then you just have the "blues" or are "bummed" or "in the dumps".

I've found that to deal with it, rather than try to cheer myself up  I try and listen. Sometimes if its bad, I might even lie in bed and try to feel more bummed - because at some point you hit the bottom and bounce back up.

Make sure of a few things:

1. Diet - if you don't have a well-balanced diet, it can affect your moods.

2. Drugs - obviously fun sometimes, but can affect mood swings.

3. Exercise - a little bit of moving around can really change your outlook.

4. Don't beat yourself up if you have down periods. Everyone has them.

5. Take a multi-vitamin everyday, or at least often. Especially if you start to feel bummed. You could have a vitamin deficiency.

Take care,

Re: Depression
For children, depression is often caused by environmental factors, including family.

For teenagers, the above applies, plus issues of identity, independence, social acceptance, and chemical/hormonal activity. Dreadful.

For adults, the above applies, plus issues of finance, personal success, marriage, politics...ad nauseum.

Where do you fit?

It's hard to rule out brain chemical imbalances because most "mental health professionals" are idiots. Theyll put you on medication, by default.

Most folks want a "better answer" than this, but increasing physical activity and improving diet have a LOT to do with mental health. If I don't get my heart rate up for a sustained period of time every day, I will feel "down in the dumps" and "mopey." If I'm ill and can't work out for a prolonged period, that is very bad for me. I'll start staring at the walls and sleeping most of the day. The occurance of depression among athletes and those in the field of physical fitness is jarringly low.

Depression can be a crappy cycle. Hope you find what you need to get out of it.
