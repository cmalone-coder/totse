Re: image translation
side by side it wont work, but you can print the binary or hex code on a sperate sheet (or several sheets depending on the size of the pic. Here is why:

1 pixel (the smallest unit of display on a monitor) must be represented by a RGB (red, green, blue) number. That is 3 numbers each 8 bits to represent from 0 to 255.

R -> 0-255
G -> 0-255
B -> 0-255

I wont go into detail on why it takes 1 byte or 8 bits to represent because thats a whole new subject on the binary number system. You would require 24 bits to acurately represent one pixel. (This is, without compression, jpgs are compressed). If you picture is big and contains thousands of pixels (as many pictures do), then you will require thousands of pixels times 24bits.

This probably doesnt make cense (pun, again) to you but anyways... lets keep going.

now you have 2000 pixels times 24bits per pixel. That is 48,000 bits. A bit is represented by a 1 or a 0. So now you printer must print 48,000 1's and 0's to show what the image looks like. And chance are, it will wrap the lines at the end unless your image has very little width...

Ok, im sorry, that probably didnt help. One last clarifying statement. If I understand what you want to do with your photograph correctly, it cant be done by that method unless you have a massive plot printer, and even then Im not sure. It would be a cool art project tho.

Ill try to explain this as I see it whic may help or may confise things further.

This is for the totse people:
Take a screenshot of your desktop at 1024 X 768, on a monitor which has a 19" viewable screen. (dont confuse pixels with the actual size of the image). Then get the binary of that.

Next, fit that binary into the space that 19" of space that it took up on your monitor.

The artistic value lies in the contradiction that you are seeing the exact same thing as that jpeg of your desktop, except it's different. The same, yet different.

The problem begins when you forget how many ones and zeroes we are dealing with. He said 3.4 million characters in hex. Times 4 thats a bit under 14 million characers.

That printer needs to be VERY high resolution, because even at 1152X 864, you are still looking at only about .9 million units (pixels). The thing he wants to print has 14 times as many units. I dont know how big he wants it, but that is a pretty expensive printer.
