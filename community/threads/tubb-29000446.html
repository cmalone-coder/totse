I recommend buying a special forces manual and learning to fight from it. This alone can teach you to fight in a matter of hours. Special forces manuals should cost about $8 at a gun show.

Re: learning to fight at home
In regard to instruction books, they can help but as all others have said it is experience. During my early 20s, I had the fortune of having several very odd friends whom all got along. One of our common interests was fighting. Names have been changed because I do not wish to totally give myself away at this point.
We all fought in an abandoned storage area of my grandfather's old dry cleaning business. I did not know shit about fighting except from SOF which was the only magazine around at the time that dealt with non-traditional methods of hand to hand and/or feet.
The Backdrop: Eric, a prototypical Nazi about 6'4" and 190 with about 10% body fat was in numerous gold gloves and tough man competitions; yet he never went all the way to the top. Also, Eric had been in martial arts since I think he was out of the womb. To say the least, he could fight like hell.
Dave, was a about 5'7" and 140 and a gutter fighter who learned the hard way in Mansfield State Prison. He did not like taking it up the ass every week so he adapted fairly quickly.
My First: Eric and I doned the gloves and I was initiated. Broken nose and bloody lip aside I learned a lot fast. (He still took it easy on me) However, Dave spotted Eric's weaknesses immediately despite no proper training like Eric. He coached me and within several minutes I delivered several good body shots, much to Eric's surprise.
I still got my assed kicked because I kept thinking a head shot would do it when he would easiliy dodge them and reply accordingly. I was 5'10 and 145 at the time.
Eric was pissed and when it came time to fight Dave they were already talking trash. However, I could see Eric was a little afraid. After resting and rehydrating, Eric and Dave got it on. Dave's ferocity had to be seen to be believed. All body shots-none legal-and he quickly backed Eric into a corner till Eric called it. Eric took pot shots too by now but Dave was so good at defending against them it was of minimal consequence.
We were all really good friends after this and it was a wonderful experience. We only got to do it for about 8 more months because gramps rented the place out as a pet care center.
My point, get in the ring and learn from those who have experience. In his prime, I think Dave could have matched if not taken a special forces member in hand to hand. We expanded a little during those months with other fellows but I would stil never fight Dave. I knew my limitations.
And, no, it was not some sappy mens' group or anything akin to Fight Club. We just learned and that is all I have today.
I am 33 and out of shape but if there is anyone in northeast Ohio who has a good place (meaning no fees and instruction pro bono), I would love to get back into it again.
