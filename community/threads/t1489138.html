Re: Help. I can't like people
Avoid medication at all costs. Make it the absolute last resort, which is what it should be. This falls on you, however, because too many doctors will drop it on you too quickly. You can learn to live with this and turn it into a positive.. even if some anti-depressant could make you a little bit better than you can make yourself, the dependancy isn't worth it.

I've spent a very long time dealing with a similar situation. Whenever I saw someone I knew in high school or something, I just wanted them to go away. I don't know why. It always felt like I was supposed to make conversation with them, which I didn't want to do, and I inexplicably walked away feeling shitty. Even friends that I haven't seen in a long time trigger this sort of response, despite the other side of my brain being happy to see them again. It's like I should be hanging out with them just because we ran across each other in the grocery store.

Maybe this is what you're feeling, maybe it's something else, but since it appears we're having similar reactions to people regardless of the reason, I'll tell you what worked for me.

Avoid them.

It may seem like a contradiction to the general belief of meeting your problems head on, but this is a situation where doing that may leave you buried in negative emotion. You can't dodge everyone forever, but you can cut down your time around them to a minimum. This doesn't mean you can't do the things you enjoy doing (unless it absolutely requires more than one person, but most activities don't). Just do them by yourself, or with whatever people don't trigger this "go away" sense in you.

You may very well piss some people off or make them worry about you. You can't concern yourself with that. Eventually, you'll start to open up to the rest of the world again. It may take weeks or months (in my case, a few years, but I always had a few friends I liked spending time with, plus you're more used to being with people than I was), but it will happen. Do your thing and avoid situations you don't like.

Remember, just because you don't deal with a problem head on, it doesn't mean you're running from it.
