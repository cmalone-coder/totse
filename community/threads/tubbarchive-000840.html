Re: books that genuinely CHANGED YOUR LIFE...?
I think reading Alice In Wonderland and Through The Looking Glass, as a young child, sort of fucked me up - in a strange way. I think it permanently fucked up my perceptions of reality or something.

Other books that I think fucked me up - in various ways - as a child were:

A Wrinkle In Time - This book caused me to become incredibly OCD, to the point where I was afraid I was slipping in and out of different plains of existence depending on whether or not I licked the light switched three times or four times when I walked into the room...

Grimm Brothers Faerie Tales - I don't know why my mum thought it would be a good idea to read these to me as a child... I was convinced I could see fairies, nymphs, nombs, goblins, etc until I was almost a teenager. I still can't get it out of my head, I can only rationalize it now.

Beowulf - Inspired my studies in religious sciences and the occult.

Ray Bradbury - I read everything by him when I was a kid and he fueled the OCD that I already had from A Wrinkle In Time.

Shakespeare: After reading Beowulf, when I was nine years old, I went on a crazy olde english binge and read the entire works of Shakespeare, a bunch of Roman and Greek mythology, and Poe. Shakespeare altered my perception of what writing was supposed to be like.

Edgar Allen Poe: The Pit And The Pendulum scared the crap out of me. Also, Poe seemed to have some very strange ideas of what romantic relationships are supposed to be like. I think my future romantic endeavors were skewed by his writings about his wife.

Sophocles: I think of all the Greek mythology that I read, I found Sophocles to be the most impacting. Odepius Rex is just fucked up, and the whole thing with Antigone... Really made me appreciate my family a lot more.

Journey To The East changed the way I wrote.

After reading Catcher In The Rye I realized that you can't compare your problems to other peoples problems, and that changed the way I really dealt with people.

Steppenwolfe inspired me to go on an opium binge.

A lot of books have changed me in a lot of ways. It's sad when a book leaves you unscathed.

Cheers,
Lost

The foundation series by Isaac Asimov (Prelude to Foundation; Foward The Foundation; Foundation; Foundation and Empire; Second Foundation; Foundation's Edge; Foundation and Earth; [titles are in the series' fictional chronological order]) really changed my perception of the world around me. He expresses complicated philosophical thoughts and ideas in such a way that even the casual reader can pick up on them.

There are like 6 books (all about 350 pages) but different people suggest reading them in different orders. Totse actually has some guides on reading them in the artical archives. They all take place in the same universe and paralells can be traced back and forth across the different storylines.

Anyway, the fact that the books themselves are wonderful should be enough to make you read them... However, if that doesnt do anything for you, read them out of respect for the father of science fiction.

The Hobbit, Tolkien: Made me want to be a writer.

Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas, Hunter Thompson: This came along into my life after I got into drugs, and while I was getting heavy into the 60s. It pointed out a few things, and somehow, made me like Steinbeck. I realized that Thompson and Steinbeck were both hunting for the American Dream, just in very, very different ways. Also made me want to be a journalist/commentator.

My Philosophy Textbook: It was a monster. And it was beautiful.

The Jungle, Sinclair: We all have our follies...this turned me from a youngster who bought that bullshit line about a free market meaning a free populace into a real, thinking person. Pendulum always swings too far.

On the Road: This made my writing much more poetic. It also taught me more about...well, things in general.
