Banned From Heaven
Regular
  
Re: How did you actually change?
Good question. I'd say that over-all since I've started taking drugs (which has only been semi-recently, actually) I haven't changed significantly in the sense that I do anything differently now (besides spending my money on drugs, rather than on random shit); but I have changed dramatically in the way that I perceive a lot of things in my life (including life itself, really).

I think the most notable example of a changed perception is towards the substances themselves. For instance, before I started using drugs, I was fairly straight-edge. I drunk booze on occasion, but not really that much, and I had somewhat of an anti-drug mentality. Not in the sense that I condemned drugs/drug users; but in the sense that at the time I had made a decision not to use them, not for any moral reasons, or for any reason at all really. I'll admit that the only information I had pertaining to drugs was biased opinions from teachers at my school, who were only to happy to perpetuate bullshit rumors I now know to be completely false (I'm sure you've all heard your fair share of such myths). So, up until I was seventeen (which was this June); I never did any drugs, and nor did I have any intentions of doing them.

On my seventeenth birthday, however, a friend of mine who has always been into drugs, and who has always been a lot more open-minded than myself started talking to me on MSN. He suggested that now that I was seventeen, that I should try ecstasy with him. At first, I was reluctant, I tried to think of reasons why not to, and then I eventually determined there weren't any. You only live once, so why not?

We went down to the train station one evening later that week, and we each popped a pill. I didn't know what to expect, I was subconsciously expecting something bad to happen, but, it was incredible. I felt happy, full-of-life, better than I had ever felt. The reservations I had about myself, the obsessing over what people thought about me, just diminished. In its place was a mixture of happiness, clarity, and insight. I'd had a lot on my mind that previous week, I had to make some hard decisions. I was fretting over it, when I was tripping, the stress and anxiety ceased to exist, and I was able to make the decision more easily than I had ever anticipated.

When the trip ended, I felt like a person reborn. It sounds stupid, and hell it probably is in some ways, but I felt better about myself. This is what got me interested in drugs. One awesome trip enabled me to see how drugs can (and did) improve my life. I always used to associate drugs with low-lives and criminals. But now, I realize that there's a staggering amount of intellectual, insightful, and well-adjusted people who do them, there's obviously shitheads who do drugs, I'm not saying there isn't, but by-and-large everyone I know who do drugs are generally nice people. So, through drug-use my perceptions towards drugs, drug-users, and myself have changed for the better.

Since the first E trip, I've taken other psychoactives. I don't pretend to be an expert--as I undoubtedly have a lot to learn, and a lot to experience. So far I've taken ecstasy, shrooms, LSD, and DOI, and I've loved each and every one of them. I'd say MDMA is my favourite drug, all around, but I immensely enjoyed LSD (and still do for that matter). I love that 'head-feeling' you get where you're able to comprehend things you'd never even pondered before. The first few times I did LSD, I only got minor visual affects; but got a lot of that insight and clarity.

All-in-all, my perceptions have changed, and I have a better image of myself, but my life hasn't changed all that much. All it means is that now rather than eating, hanging out with friends, listening to music and playing Xbox sober, I do it whilst tripping.

Sorry for the length; I can't sleep and I have nothing else to do.
