Re: SSRI's and anxiety
does anyone have any knowledge on whether or not SSRI's are effective for anxiety issues?

In theory? Absolutely! Think of the most drastic and fast-acting serotonin reuptake inhibitor - MDMA.

Of course, MDMA not only inhibits reuptake, but reverses it, and has a few other side actions which enhance these effects slightly... but the basic concept is essentially the same, and fast-acting high-potency SSRIs, such as mesembrine, are known to have subjectively similar effects.

The mechanism is fairly simple. 5HT1A activity results in the release of oxytocin in the amygdala, which is about as freakin' anti-anxiety as it gets - again, think MDMA, hugs 'n all. 'n it occasionally even works in clinical trials, surprisingly often in fact, which is why they prescribe it for anxiety disorders now.

The downside, of course, is that the classical SSRIs take fucking forever to reach plasma concentrations. This is most of why you can't just double your dose and roll your fucking tits off for a day, and it also means you'll probably have about six months before it even thinks of having an effect.

any suggestions of other drugs i should ask my doctor about?

Why, ecitalopram, of course!

Currently, it's the other drug in the "high-potency rapid-onset SSRI" class, which is why it actually has a recreational use archive over at 'wid. From a more medical perspective, this also means that it will actually do something about your anxiety the day you take it, rather than the twelfth of almost never... or, since you're posting this in the second half of the year, "that day, not next year."

This basically gives it the antianxiety benefits of hardcore benzodiazapene abuse with the addiction potential of once-a-month cannabis use. 'n I think that's what you're looking for.

Of course, given your personality, any serotonin-active substance will probably cause personality changes in you. You'll be at risk for having a little less antisocial behavior. So, it's up to you if it's a worthwile tradeoff.

Other things you might consider, though they don't neccesarily require a doctor, are essential fatty acids ("fish oil" et al, required in the construction of oxytocin releasing neurons in the hippocampus), and magnesium (NMDA antagonist, gaba second messenger, and also utilized in the construction of oxytocic vessicles, if memory serves).

'n then if you're going balls-out, there's also OTC sedatives. But we won't get to that yet...
