I'll tell you what you're supposed to do in that scenario. In the name of self-preservation, you lie through your teeth and tell the raving lunatic gunman in front of you that you not only see the astronaut, but that you can describe the astronaut in full detail down to it's boots.

But what you don't do is walk away from that scenario and spread that idea that you knew to be untrue to other people, thereby joining all the other highly mistaken people and adding to an already large, organized network of other lunatics who happen to be as mentally misguided as the gunman who almost shot you dead for not agreeing with his delusion!

What you do is keep reminding yourself to encourage that little voice in the back of your head that tells you that you didn't really see the astronaut, you just considered to agree to see to it because you thought that's what you needed to do to stay alive.

quote: so what if i truly WANT to go to heaven, and I truly WANT to believe that Jesus Christ can save me,

quote: I don't believe it. because I CANT.

And that's okay. Other people don't believe it either. There's nothing malfunct in your thinking. You're just not capable of lying to yourself about what you know cannot be real. Congratulate yourself for that!

You desire salvation. Salvation from what, exactly? You desire to spend life eternal in a paradise-like setting, okay. That seems great. Who wouldn't desire that? But just because we truly desire it doesn't mean that it exists. I truly WANT to be able to get rewarded for every good action or positive thought I exhibit too, but the fact is, I probably won't, unless it's in this life.

Why live your whole life to expect what you know is highly improbable of happening, all the while knowing deep down that you don't really believe in it? That's just not a very good strategy, in my opinion. You could expend more mental energy in a more positive, life-affirming way by continuing to do what you're already doing: examining your life and striving to shape your own beliefs about the world. You can worry about the afterlife when you die, when your alleged spirit somehow magically transports beyond death all these emotional desires you harbour towards living forever.

To go back to your own analogy: Don't let the metaphorical gunman (your irrational desires) who makes you see the non-existent astronaut (God/Heaven) twist up your rationale to the point of not ever being able to recover from the ruse you played along with to save your ass.
