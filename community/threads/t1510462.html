Introduction:



The issue of gender and intelligence is a very controversial and hotly debated topic. Although there have been investigations in the past that have tried to prove otherwise, the general consensus of the psychological community is that there are no great differences in cognitive ability between males and females apart from a wider range of Intelligence Quotients (I.Q) in males.

Perhaps more important, and more insightful into out society is how males and females see themselves in terms of their own estimated intelligence. In past studies (Beloff, 1992; Bennet, 1996) a consistent trend has been visible, namely that males generally self-estimate themselves higher than women estimate themselves. But when participants were questioned about the individual components of intelligence ( problem solving, abstract thought, comprehending ideas and concepts etc.) there was no marked difference in self estimation. Between the genders. This difference indicates that intelligence in a broad sense is a trait associated with males.

This pre-conception is not confined to the individuals self estimation but extends to estimation of others intelligence. Past studies such as Hogan (1978) and Higgins (1987) have consistently shown that when participants were asked to estimate their parents I.Q the vast majority estimated their fathers to be higher than their mothers.

To put this into context, we must first define what is meant by the term intelligence and thus what an intelligence quotient is actually a measure of. The idea of what exactly intelligence is still debated but is loosely accepted to be the ability to understand the relationships between things and apply this to new circumstances. The ability to do so is split into individual components such as reasoning, planning, problem solving, comprehension of concepts and abstract thought and the ability to learn new things. Psychometric testing (intelligence testing) can be used as a tool to measure I.Q. These tests are designed to test an individuals proficiency in each of the component parts of intelligence, and comprises of puzzles and tasks such as questions about shapes to test spatial reasoning for example or small memory tasks to test short term memory. This is generally accepted as a sound measurement of intelligence.

To find out more about gender and self estimated I.Q we must also look into self concept , which is an individuals perception of their identity and self estimated ability in relation to others around themselves. One of the most influential researchers to investigate the self concepts was Carl Rogers (1947), he believed that the self is the core of the human personality and therefore that the self was a product of relationships between people. He also believed that that there is a basic human need for positive encouragement which encourages peoples tendency towards self actualisation (Purkey & Schmidt, 1987) This supports the view that people and their sense of self is influenced by conditional positive regard and interpersonal relationships, so t why is it that females estimate themselves lower than males in intelligence?

There must be something that affects females this way, the trend is even visible in young children. Raty, Kiisken, Kasanen and Nykky (2004) found in their study that children in the U.S.A as young as 2nd Grade perceived intelligence as a male trait. In this study children in 2nd, 4th and 6th grade were asked to name the pupil in their class who they thought was the best at that particular subject. When the 4th grade class were asked about who was the best at maths the boys consistently named only other boys and the majority of girls named only boys even though statistically boys and girls were equally matched in their test grades. This trend was not visible in the second grade, which shows there must be something in that children observe to make them associate intelligence with males at such a tender age.

Hogan (1978) argued that perceived gender differences in intelligence is due to females being socially rewarded by denying equality with males. This would encourage males to believe they are superior while at the same time giving girls the idea that they are inferior to the male sex, which could account for the trend of under estimation in females. But the majority of these studies were performed quite a few years ago and society has changed significantly since then. Women have come a long way in terms of rights and societies view towards a womens role in society has also changed significantly post second wave feminism.

Therefore the aim of this investigation is too see whether the difference between genders in self estimated I.Q is still prevalent today. The experimental hypothesis for this investigation is that when asked to estimate their own I.Q a higher proportion of males will estimate themselves above average intelligence than females. .The null hypothesis for this investigation is therefore that there will be no significant difference between the average self estimated I.Q of males and females.
