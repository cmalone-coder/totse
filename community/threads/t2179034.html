Re: Is there any way to help her?
Much of this could just be reiterating or disagreeing with what has already been said.

1.) Do not ruin her fun. Be involved in her life and be a friend, but she has to be able to make her own choices; let her see the consequences. If you try to lay down the law, it will just give her a chance to get negative attention. If you have friends that are burned out party girls, use them as an example of what the lifestyle can get a person. Learning from the mistakes of others always beats making them yourself; though it's not always possible, it's a good lesson.

2.) Don't get into the whole love thing unless you feel comfortable or want to get into it. I don't think that I've ever told any of my stepbrothers or sisters that I love them. It just doesn't seem "right". They know and I know, but some things are simply unspoken; actions can speak louder than words.

3.) Make time for her. It's usually not enough just to be there when she needs you, be there when she doesn't as well.

4.) Getting involved in something together is a good idea, but let her have input in it. Take the time to look through the options with her and come to a decision together. Make sure that she makes friends in whatever you decide to do; that way she doesn't lose interest if/when you're gone.

5.) Ignore any thought of sexual tension. People often don't give foster kids enough credit; she's a member of the family. You know that and can find ways to make sure that she realizes it as well.

6.) She knows how to deal with major things, so she has a good grounding to develop a strong personality. Support her and do what you can to let her trust herself. When she makes a good decision, make sure that she knows it; this is assuming that she doesn't trust in her own decisions.

Good luck. There's nothing surefire and it's an ugly situation, but sometimes great people grow out of bad situations.
