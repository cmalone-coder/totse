Re: Urgent parenting advice needed, especially if you ride a motorbike!
I'm hardly a parent, so take my advice with a grain of salt, but I do ride motorcycles, so I've got that part covered.

I (as well as almost every other rider) have dropped my bike in some fashion, and fought against the fear of not riding. I think you definitely did well in terms of asking him to go riding a few more times before making a decision. I would recommend a few things, however.

If he's not wearing proper gear (aside from the spine protector), buy him a decent seat of motorcycle leathers. As soon as you put on a set you get far more comfortable with motorcycling, it adds the feeling of protection. Naturally, don't let him become overzealous and start taking crazy risks, but it could offer him some good comfort.

Secondly, get him to take the Motorcycle Safety Course or equivalent for England (correct?). If there's anything I recommend all of the time, it's a safety course. They give so many benefits, it's unreal. Possibly including (at least in Canada) insurance benefits. It actually pays for itself in 2-4 years, depending on your rates. Not to mention a good training in evasive maneuvers, emergency braking etc.

At least you don't really have to worry about him speeding off on a 50cc bike.
I'm fairly sure if he hopped onto a 600cc sportsbike he would be far, far more shaken up and less likely to ride. Good choice on the beginner bike.
Oh, yeah. Don't let him feel ashamed of dropping it or anything, regardless of fault in the accident.


Just let him know that almost everybody drops their first bike (occasionally multiple times), and he's hardly an anomaly. If he decides (as I'm almost certain he will) to continue riding, just make sure he learns from the incident.

Treat all drivers as if they're out to kill you.

Never, ever trust the driver that isn't you. Seems a bit paranoid, but it's absolutely true. There have been times I'm cruising in a lane and someone to my right decides (without looking, because they don't see a car in their perepheral), they want my lane. If I wasn't watching, the likelihood of getting clipped (crashing and getting seriously injured) was much, much higher.

You sound like you're dealing with this very well, though. Please continue to make sure he rides with his gear. Bare minimum:
Helmet, gloves, jacket, boots.

Best of luck,
BloodiedMind

Rockers, wearing leather jackets and riding heavy motorcycles, poured scorn on the mods, who wore suits and rode scooters. The rockers considered mods to be weedy, effeminate snobs. Just a note about that whole "speed" thing.

It's quite likely he was going a bit fast on the bike anyways, but you do have to consider bikes are harder to judge speed with (especially directly in front/behind.).

That's why drivers seem to pull out infront of you a lot more on a motorcycle than a car, there's no points to judge speed/placement off of. I'd love to find the article on it, but I honestly can't, but that essentially sums it up.

So he could have really been doing the speed limit or a bit over and you could have just misjudged it. My father gets on my case all the time for 'speeding off" when in reality I'm just accelerating to 50-60 kp/h and short-shifting to reasonable sound levels. It just -looks- fast because I'm on a sportsbike (Even though it's 1987, still looks fairly nippy).

So give him a bit of doubt, get him some proper gear if he decides to ride (maybe it'll encourage him) and get him to a proper motorcycle course.

I can't say about scooters, but most motorcycles made now have incredible brakes (Sportsbikes especially). Even though most cards have 4 brakes, the two that bikes have often are enough to haul their speeds down quite quickly, so he likely will be able to stop in time under most circumstances.

On my bike ('87), I can stop far, far quicker than most modern cars (supercars excluded). I can only imagine the potency of current bikes.

In the UK you can drive a 50cc when you are 16, then up to a 125cc when you hit 17 and until you pass your test. You are then restricted for two years.
