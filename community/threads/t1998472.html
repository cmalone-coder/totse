Re: Ratio conversions
Well that really depends on the specifics, such as if it is just a mixture or if something is being synthesized. Sometimes people give the ratios for a mixture of a liquid with a solid already factoring in that the liquid will be measured a different way. Something like 85 grams of AN and 15 ml nitroglycerin for example

If we're producing something that requires a synthesis, then your best course of action would probably be to find the specific gravity of the liquid and use that to find how many grams are in each ml. You would want to be looking at molar amounts really.

Again, it would be easier to answer with some more specifics.

Re: Ratio conversions
Let's hypothesize that I know a 100-mL graduated cylinder filled with a particular granular charcoal from a reliable chemical supplier masses 32 grams more than the empty cylinder because you're friend let you use his scale real quick. If in the future I needed to use 10 grams of charcoal in a preperation, then I could fill the cylinder to just about the 32 mL mark and be relitively accurate with the measurement. Volume is a property of solids and can be used to measure them.

Granted it's not as accurate as a good scale, it is still far more accurate than just guessing, even if it is a rarely used technique in pure and applied chemistry.
