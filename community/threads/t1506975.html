if you don't know what to say to #3 here is my advice.

inform her of your partying style (especially things she will find appalling) and how you've noticed it differs from hers. say you don't think this will work in the long run. point out that you respect her decisions, and maybe fake a bit of self-criticism about your own habits. sum it up by saying that you like and admire her, but just don't think you're compatible because of your differences.

don't give this as a speech. say one thing at a time and allow time for responses and questions in between. don't be detached when speaking, but don't be overly emotional. describe your thoughts as though you were explaining a math problem you're figuring out as you go along. if she contradicts you, hear her out, and then respond with "i hear what you're saying, i just don;t think i agree." allow her to end the interaction on her own terms. if possible, smile/hug her/say goodbye nicely when you part. however, don't attempt to nurture her while you're breaking up. this will come off as condescension and will make her feel small.

this way is not the easiest for you in the short run, but will save her the most hurt. it;s a way of breaking it off without making her feel like it's her fault or she did anything wrong. it sounds cliche, but no one wants to think that they were dumped because of their own personal qualities not being adequate. also, according to what you said, it's pretty honest.
