Right, I'll answer this once and for all so it stops being posted on here..

The dilemma posed in the question "can God make a rock so heave that he is unable to move it" is a false dilemma. It is false because it is erected on a false premise. It assumes that "omnipotence" means that God can do anything. Yet, as a theological term, omnipotence does not mean that God can do anything; He cannot lie, He cannot die, He cannot act against his nature, He cannot be God and not be God at the same time.

What omnipotence does mean is that God holds all power over his creation. No part of creation is outside his sovereign control. Therefore, there is an answer to the rock dilemma. The answer is no. The answer is that God cannot build a rock so big that he couldn't move it. Why? If God ever build such a rock, he would be creating something over which he has no power. He would be destroying his own omnipotence, which is impossible for him to do. God cannot stop being God; He can not not be omnipotent.

God's power is restricted by what and who he is.
