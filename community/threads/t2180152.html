Re: SSRI Discontinuation
My doctor refuses to acknowledge that paxil has withdrawals, so I wont be getting much help from him.

They do have withdrawals; not just the return of original symptoms. They're not addictive in the usual way of dose-escalation etc., but they might as well be 'cause you'll feel like shit for a good few weeks.

I guarantee that benzos will help, especially for the anxiety. Just take as much as you can while still remaining functional enough to live -- you're not gonna be going in to work/school while on proper withdrawals, so you might as well make it bearable.

5-HTP was a good suggestion, but I think l-tryptophan might be better as I heard it lasts longer and 5-HTP gives me horrible nausea. I'd also hesitantly recommend St. John's Wort, since it is an SSRI but doesn't have known withdrawals (meaning it presumably doesn't alter serotonin receptors like pharmaceutical SSRIs) so hopefully wouldn't slow down the recovery.

Obviously any feel-good drugs like opiates will help. You might want to get on an MAOI for that period; maybe an unselective one because selegiline can heighten anxiety.

You may want to look into anticonvulsants and GABA-augmenting drugs for the anxiety and sleep issues (maybe also GHB for sleep).

Re: SSRI Discontinuation
Quote:
Originally Posted by vilbe
would you say SSRI's cause a dependency?


Yes, but not in the sense that something like opiates or benzos do. They don't provide an immediate pleasurable feeling, or really any direct pleasurable effects at all, which removes many of the psychological aspects of dependency, but any sort of change in dosage causes a mild but still very undesirable general feeling of shittiness. If you don't taper off properly when you stop taking them you'll feel like a bag of shit mentally and physically for 2 or 3 weeks, at least I did when I stopped Zoloft. It's not much worse than how crappy you feel for the first week or so when you start taking them though, that sucks ass too. I'm fairly certain that it wasn't just me not agreeing with the Zoloft, the psychologist who prescribed them told me that feeling sick when the dose changes is normal.

SSRIs, or at least Zoloft which is the only one I have experience with, in general are kinda shit, they take weeks to start working properly and just as long to stop and once they do start doing what they're supposed to and you don't feel like you have the flu or something all the time you just feel a bit more numb to all emotion, positive or negative, or at least that's how it was for me. I was much quieter, less excitable, and while it softened bad experiences or thoughts, it also seemed to equally reduce my capacity for being extremely happy or otherwise positive, even when I had actual, tangible reasons to feel as such that did not involve ingesting other drugs.

It could have just been placebo but I swear I noticed a difference in the distance between the "peaks and valleys" of emotion that everyone experiences to some extent when I stopped taking it. The fact that it takes 2 or 3 weeks for them to start or stop working fully makes it kinda hard to tell though. Still, feeling crappy all day because you forgot to take a pill that doesn't even make you feel good or really do anything worthwhile is a fucking pain in the ass.

Maybe SSRIs are great if you're the sort of person who's on top of the world one minute and contemplating suicide the next, but if you're even close to normal they'll probably do more harm than good to your overall mental state, not to mention they don't mix with several much more worthwhile substances. Someone who experiences extreme emotions like that must already have an neurotransmitter imbalance of some sort, so SSRIs might be just what they need, but to be honest I think a lot of "depressed" people who are prescribed SSRIs would do better with a serious, introspective trip or two on whatever hallucinogen they feel is best for them.

Definitely not for everyone, but a truly visionary experience can be just what you need to gain some perspective and get your real priorities in order. At least in my case what was diagnosed as depression was more of a natural uncertainty about my long term goals which manifested itself as a general lack of motivation in all aspects of my life (this was years before I started smoking bud so don't say "amotivational syndrome").

A couple very successful experiences with hallucinogens did a lot more to improve my day to day mood and outlook on life than Zoloft, and I haven't taken any sort of dissociative or psychedelic in several months, unless you count weed. Identifying and working to correct the sources of your "depression" is a lot more constructive than trying to directly alter your brain chemistry every day to blunt whatever psychological hardship you might be experiencing.

Fuck, this ended up being much longer than I had originally pictured, I'm stopping now so I don't spend all afternoon writing about my experience with SSRIs and why I think they're shit for the vast majority of people because I could without trying, even if I hadn't been taking amphetamines for the past day and a half.

tl;dr: Fuck SSRIs, most of the people on them are unhappy for actual reasons and need to correct those before they consider the possibility of a natural neurotransmitter imbalance which can be corrected with SSRIs. And they make you feel like shit for weeks when you stop, start or change your daily dosage.
