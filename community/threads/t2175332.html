We'll start with socialism:

Socialists mainly share the belief that capitalism unfairly concentrates power and wealth among a small segment of society that controls capital and creates an unequal society. All socialists advocate the creation of an egalitarian society, in which wealth and power are distributed more evenly, although there is considerable disagreement among socialists over how, and to what extent this could be achieved.

Socialism is not a discrete philosophy of fixed doctrine and program; its branches advocate a degree of social interventionism and economic rationalization, sometimes opposing each other.

When this boils down to it, this does not mean that the state will own and control everything as a standard. That's called nationalism (think Nazi Germany) and is an extreme take on socialist ideology. People whine about socialism but don't really know what it is. It's helped set up:

Now, about communism:

Communism is a socioeconomic structure and political ideology that promotes the establishment of an egalitarian, classless society based on common ownership of the means of production and property in general.[1][2][3] Karl Marx posited that communism would be the final stage in human society, evolving into a classless, stateless society of "pure communism".

Note this.

Anything where there's a state or class system or promotes such is NOT communist. You cannot have a communist government, it's a fucking paradox.

Communism is progressive and as such it cannot be outlined in any form yet, because it hasn't happened yet. It's the hypothesized final stage of material production and social organisation where scarcity and oppression are to be at their lowest. You can only make sweeping guesses as to what this society would be like based upon social and material circumstances that are currently rooted.
