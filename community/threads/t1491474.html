Re: Age and Sexuality
quote:Why is it that damn near any person you talk to will hear about 6-year-olds being "boyfriend and girlfriend" and think nothing of it, but hear a 14-year-old declare that she's not of the accepted sexual orientation and suddenly she's too young to make that kind of "decision"?

It's not that society is actively trying to discourage people from expressing a lifestyle choice (or genetic predisposition or whatever it happens to be) which differs from the norm -- it's just that in the absence of any concrete certainty and given the fact that people as young as 14 aren't necessarily physically, psychologically, emotionally, and sexually mature yet, it makes sense to assume (even on a purely statistical basis) that they'll probably turn out like the majority of people (e.g. straight).

quote:You can be normal at any age, but you can't be weird until you're 18? How does this make any sense?

Well I for one never said that a person could be normal at any age -- in fact, most people who study psychology or psychiatry agree that the notion of normalcy is an artificial construct -- it doesn't exist aside from the fact that we think it exists or that we might want to achieve it.

Most people under the age of 18 aren't necessarily sure of who they are or what they are, and I think that a lot of the friction we see between 'adults' and 'rebellious youth' is because our bodies and minds tell us we're mature and 'adult society' doesn't.

And why not think so? Young men and women, some as young as 14, were married and starting to have kids and property and businesses as recently as two- or three-hundred years ago. In some modern societies, this still happens.

And it's only the need for a formalized education that really keeps young people bound to their parents (and to the system) until they're 18 -- I think any notion that ALL minors are immature is false. But escaping the stereotype placed on the majority of your peers (e.g. that you're under the influence of fluctuating hormones) isn't exactly easy to do or to demonstrate.

quote:Most kids know they're straight at a young age. Often when they're 12 or under. I knew when I was 10 that I liked girls as well as guys. It was just something I accepted and went with. It was just another part of who I was, nothing too important.

I'm actually glad that you're both comfortable with your sexuality and that you've got the maturity to express it -- you're miles further toward being emotionally healthy than most of your peers.

quote:Then I found out when I was 12 that this was one of those things that people would look at above everything else. And people would either hate me for it or say that I wasn't ready for a decision like that. Now that I'm 14 (that's 4 years "not knowing who I am"), I still take shit for being too young. What the fuck is this obsession with telling people who they are? Carbon, any thoughts?

It's a shitty system, I'll admit. And I'm not saying who you should be, or even that I know what you say you are ... all I can point out is that until you reach the age where society starts to relax its stranglehold on your rights and priveleges to live the way you want, some people won't take you seriously and others will doubt your judgement.

Take me for example -- I'm supposedly a gifted individual and that I've got the rational sense to make some good decisions. By all rights, I could probably have voted in an election as early as fifteen, but the law here says eighteen. My loss, I guess.

But I'd point out that it's only four more years (and quite possibly less) before people begin to take you more seriously and to respect your right to decide things for yourself. That's adulthood for you.

Four years isn't a long time, during which you can enjoy your decided-upon sexual identity while some others your age are still trying to figure things out.
