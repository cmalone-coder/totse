Re: For all you "anti-abortionists"
Abortion should be an option, but there should be no arguments against adoption, period. There are downsides to carrying a child to term- physical changes, emotional alterations, social stigma in the case of a teenage girl- but those apply whether keeping the baby or giving it to willing parents. Money isn't an issue- all hospital bills related to childbirth are paid for by the adoptive parents. Whether or not it's a "hassle" is up to the would-be mother- it's not for you to blast adoption just because you think it could be a pain for a pregnant girl.

Switching gears... asking "what if your mother aborted you" is a flawed argument. My mother was 29 when she had me. Her and my dad were poised and ready to be parents. The same holds for the majority of the people here, I'm betting. Even the ones who were unexpectedly conceived probably had parents who were in a position to take care of a child, even if it was a bit of a struggle and they hadn't planned on it happening just yet.

A 15 or 16-year-old kid is in a completely different situation. Yes, I think adoption is a better option than abortion. There won't be a child to take care of in either case, but bringing the pregnancy to term will definitely teach that girl some lessons. More than anything physical, conscience is most likely to affect someone who has an abortion; an adoption will leave that conscience more or less free.

That girl, however, will have to deal with a lot of shit due to pregnancy. Social stigma is one thing; as preferable as it might seem to those a little older to be unashamed when you make a mistake and carry your burdens openly, this is major shit to a teenage girl. Pregnancy-related illness is another thing. School- taking classes may not be as difficult as holding a job, but you can expect a girl will be taking a significant amount of time off in order to take care of the birth. If she's smart and/or a hard worker, she can most likely get through her classes ok, but that takes a level of maturity many high school students don't have. That could easily result in her falling a semester or full year behind in her classes.

These are just the things that will affect the majority of those high school students who find themselves in the situation of an unplanned pregnancy. There may be other issues specific to certain situations that would make seeing the pregnancy through, even in order to give the baby up for adoption, a less-welcome possibility. This also assumes adoption is acceptable to the mother; some would not be able to bear giving up their child to anyone else. It's either abortion or keep it to them, and it would wind up fucking up at least two lives- the mother and the father- to try and raise the child.

I'm sure someone will come around and question my logic. How can I dare say that abortion is acceptable simply to make life easier for the girl? After all, social problems and having to catch up in school are not things which are nearly impossible to overcome, and it could be argued graduating high school later will give a girl a wiser outlook on life coming out.

The downsides of bringing a child to term are the major ones a girl will face- I say them as observations, nothing more. As I said, I believe adoption to be the better option in theory. Theory doesn't always play out so well practically, though. Abortion might not be preferable from a big-picture perspective, but it's her own small piece of the big picture that a girl has to consider first and foremost when making this decision. I have no problems with saying adoption should be upheld as positive, but to say someone is dead wrong for getting an abortion is overly moralistic and short-sighted. Unless you know the specifics of a situation, you are not qualified to determine what the "right" choice is.
