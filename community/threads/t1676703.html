Re: anti social
Being anti-social and being socially-anxious are two entirely different sides of the spectrum. Being anti-social is being willing to do things that are malevolent just for the sake of saying "fuck socialization", or things like "fuck everyone else". It's ALMOST, but not quite, like being sociopathic. Being socially anxious is being afraid to socialize, usually because you're afraid of being judged by other people.

I am socially anxious (meaning I am somewhat afraid of people). When I was first diagnosed, the doctor didn't give me benzos. Benzos are considered drugs of LAST RESORT among qualified psychiatrists. They will usually give you anti-depressants first, since they have properties that suppress anxiety. If that doesn't work, then they'll try benzos.

Actually, I'm also diagnosed bipolar. I'm taking anti-seizure medication, and it actually works better than either the benzos or the anti-depressants for me at controlling my anxiety. The way they work is to prevent areas of the brain from spontaneously over-firing, so this could explain the anxiety-control properties (as well as the mood-control properties that are why they're used for bipolar patients).

Basically, in anxiety disorders, some areas of the brain start firing in response to stimuli (which you might not be aware of) and stimulate production of adrenaline. You can then either become anxious or become violent (fight-or-flight response). Any drug for anxiety simply prevents the mechanism from firing as easily, meaning you have less adrenaline in your system, and therefore feel better.

Yes, SSRIs "screw with" serotonin. SNRIs "screw with" norepinephrine, and dopamine reuptake inhibitors "screw with" dopamine. They make more of it available by causing your body to not break down the chemicals involved. Not all of the medications do the same things -- in fact, there's a wide variety of ways the medications work.

In fact, the main component of treatment for anxiety MUST BE therapy of some sort. Cognitive-behavioral therapy is probably the most effective form of psychotherapy currently available for anxiety patients, and its main idea is to teach people to recognize when they're being irrational and adjust their thoughts and behaviors so that they'll feel better. Group therapy can sometimes be even more effective, once the patient is ready for it -- seeing other people who suffer from anxiety can make it easier to deal with it. However, the medical treatment can, in the worst cases of anxiety disorders, be instrumental in preparing to use the other forms of treatment. Some people aren't capable of correcting their thoughts, attitudes and behaviors themselves without medication because the thoughts and feelings have become so etched into their minds that they can't even question them because of their intensity. I have problems even going out my front door if I don't have my medication -- and yes, that problem occurred BEFORE I was medicated.
