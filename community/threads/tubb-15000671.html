Motivation
I hope someone out there has the answer, because I'm definitely not smart enough to answer this myself.

Forget whether or not god and an afterlife exist for a moment and just pretend that we know for certain that there is no god and there is no afterlife. When we die, that's it. No more you.

Now, non-existance doesn't sound so bad to me. It's not like you'll be around to cry about it. You won't even know you've died. You'll feel that sharp pain in your chest or notice that that bus is coming at you awfully fast and then that's it, nothing else.

This is what I have come to personally believe happens when you die, and from some of the other posts on this forum I know that I'm not alone here. My question here is how does this belief affect the others out there who see things this way? Death basically erases everything you spend your life building. All the money/power/cars/women/etc. you've built your entire life trying to accumulate means nothing. You may have beautiful and loving children to carry on your family name, but what good is that doing you? You may have been the one to finally bring lasting peace to the world, but do you really care what state you leave the world in when you can't even care?

Shouldn't I be trying to maximize my pleasure during the time I have? Screw everyone else. The universe could implode the moment I die for all I'll care. Forget about the preserving the environment, because I'll be long gone before it becomes a serious problem. I should just kill and steal and screw other people over as long as it benefits me and I can get away with it.

How am I supposed to remain a happy and functioning member of society, if in the end death will just negate everything? I guess I'm just a very selfish person, but I cannot help but see things this way. If I don't exist, then absolutely nothing else matters. How do others out there who believe death is the end cope with this? I've been taught all my life that I should do "good" and help others, and I almost instinctually lean that way. But, rationally I can't see how doing "good" is directly benefiting me.

Man, after re-reading this post I think it's pretty incoherent. Sorry for that, but it's late and out of frustration I seem to have just dumped it all out in one big pile. I hope that others can get through it and have some insightful comments for me. Please help me sort this out.

ytrweq and dark_magneto

To say that because you will cease to exist after you die means that what you do now is meaningless... this is misleading.

You are drawing the state of "nothing" (that state you will be in after you die) into your concept of life (that state you are in before you die). Your purpose should not be influenced by the impending arrival of your nonexistence. They are two seperate paradigms with no relation to each other. So, we don't say "what I do on this planet doesn't matter one bit" because it does matter. Not after you die, sure. But NOW.

"The act itself should be enjoyed if only so that it is useful for this reason." Here is HOW your purpose should be carried out. With happiness. Why? Because we understand the state of happiness to be superior to a state of sadness. We like it more. BUT, this doesn't mean we HAVE to turn to hedonism. Do those things which will make you happy - and remember, hedonism won't necessarily make you happy.

Just because doing good things makes you happy can be seen in a selfish light, doesn't make them bad things. THAT you are the kind of person who enjoys doing good deeds is the thing THAT is good.

Why is it good? Because it makes you happy. Why do good deeds make you happy? Because thats the way you are. Don't worry about it. Just bring up some more nice people.

Notice that if you can fully appreciate the rough philosophy outlined above you cease to be able to bring judgement against other people's actions. You do end up with the ability to say "I don't like that" which gives you your motivation to change those people's behaviour.

This, to my mind, is how to solve your "Motivation" problem.
