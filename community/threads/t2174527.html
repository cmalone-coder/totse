Re: Is monogomy unnatural?
If marriage is a tool to further consumerism, then divorce is it's ally.

As for whether monogamy is unnatural, well I don't know how you prove what is natural and what isn't. You'll have to define it first. If you define it as something like "the default setting for a certain aspect of the functioning of a particular creature" then, I'm not exactly sure. Anthropologically, I'm fairly certain that not every ancient culture in the world had a policy of strict monogamy, so if that was your definition it would be hard to justify.

It's hard to say what is and isn't "natural" and I'm not exactly sure that we, as a product of nature, aren't natural in our behaviours regardless of what we do. So it's a fairly useless term really.

With things like cloning and homosexuality, lots of people claim that these are unnatural behaviours. So I suppose their definition of natural would be "occuring outside the human race" or "occuring naturally i.e. without human intervention."

So I think the real problem here is defining natural, and how exactly you place humanity outside that definition in anything we can possibly do.

To answer your question, monogamy occurs in some species to a limited extent, and in others not at all. So I suppose the term "natural" might be used to measure the behaviour of our species against that of other animals.

In assessing "natural" behaviour, you have to look at the condition that the particular species in generally raised in or adjusted to. If the conditions change, the behaviour probably will too. So I'd be inclined to say that there is no "natural" behaviour at all, only behaviour which is adjusted to a set of conditions which the particular species is accustomed to (e.g. culture).

I don't know why people feel the need to commit. I suppose it can make a healthy environment for raising children, but as often as not it fucks them up for life. Yeah I don't see an argument for marriage. Sorry about the length.

Re: Is monogomy unnatural?
Is monogamy instinctual? Probably not. From a biological standpoint breeding is supposed to be conducive to genetic diversity, and having a single partner for life doesn't really work with the whole goal of reproduction. Polygamy is generally the dominant mode of reproduction in nature.

However from a sociological standpoint, monogamy might not be a product of our primal needs and urges, however modern society contains a plethora of examples where our instincts are ignored in favor of cultural constructs. In my eyes it doesn't really matter whether or not monogamy is 'natural,' whether or not it is part of our instinctual functioning, because we live in a modern society and are raised in a culture of which monogamy is an integral part. Of course the idea of having a single partner for life is an idea that is constructed by society, but that doesn't make it less valid or necessary. In this day and age people tend to be molded by the culture and society they're raised in just as much, if not more than their instinctual make-up. So maybe we don't have a visceral need to get married and have a partner for life, however our subconscious is shaped by more than our instincts, and the learned desire for monogamy, for consistent companionship and normalcy, is just as powerful and valid as the instinctual desires for the contrary.
