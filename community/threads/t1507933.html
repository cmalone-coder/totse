On the Marginalization of the Heart
Birth.

You are born, wah-wah-wah, aww look at the cute baby, christ wash the placenta off his face, etc.

In the early years of life you can be the center of affection, or at least these was one time the norm. You are a new life ushered into this world with love and appreciation. Your parents love you, the dog loves you, people visiting love you, it's all great and happy and sun-shine like. Now, with extraordinarily high divorce rates, poverty lines being wickedly disproportionate, etc. this is not necessarily the case. This will, however, not be the focus of what I'm trying to say here because this may or may not lead down into a chicken or the egg kind of conversation as the parents are victims of this process I'm going to attempt to outline. The events will be described in an americano-centric view point because they will be one of experience rather than scholarly descriptions, and this is the vantage point that I am writing from.

America: land of nothing's free, and the home where nothing's safe.

After this brief experience of, at least, some sort of family life in which you aren't responsible for much because you don't have motor skills, communication abilities, deeper level processing, etc. you are enrolled in school. Suddenly you're not so loved, you're teased at every turn it seems, but this isn't the point either. There is a trend throughout the educational process that will spill into your life thereafter that places importance on some things, and insignificance on others.

It starts early.

What's the first thing you learn? The pledge of Allegiance.

I pledge allegiance to the flag, and the united states of America; and to the republic for which it stands. One nation, under God, indivisible with liberty and justice for all.

Then you repeat this every single day for the duration of your education until you reach, if you ever do, secondary eduation. The fact that just about every statement in this pledge is pure unadulterated bullshit doesn't matter at this point because you don't understand it; you're fuckin' 5. Nobody really reflects on what it intends, and I'm not saying that the words themselves has a subconscious effect on us all, because we memorize them to the point of having no influence on us before we understand what's being said.

The point is uniformity; unquestioned, authoritative, unyielding uniformity.

As we age this attitude is pervasive, and applies throughout our educational process. We all learn the same material in similar fashions, we're all issued the same social nonsense, and we're all given the same institutes and gatherings to uphold as worth while. The grand football games of highschool, the awkward dance socials of middle school, the ubiquitous threat of detention throughout the proceedings. Throughout all of this many social subjugations are taking place, but within the ambit of this essay I'd like to focus on the heart as it's pushed further and furhter into the margins until, by the time you can understand it, you aren't capable of experiencing it.

Well, that's not a very accurate way of putting it I suppose. By the time you are old enough to express love maturely, you've been educated in a fashion that, in this world, in order to be productive, you have no use for love; it's detrimental to success. This is a realization you make on your own, and it's rarely a truly conscious decision, but it seems to be the norm.

Throughout the highschool, it seems, we're taught, essentially, the same nonsense every year. The english classes go over grammer, and, at best, assign a new book that either no one will read or is of the skill level, and depth of something not worth reading; math classes exchange concepts for formulas, but they're all based on the foundation of having a "trick" to getting at the answer, without understanding of the logic behind it being necessary; history goes over the same, euro-centric, events every year under different names, but the material doesn't appear to change. The pervasive theme is speed, efficiency, everybody getting the same answer, and nobody really understanding why it's the right answer.

The result: at the end of highschool nobody seems to be aware of the fact that they've only been equipped with the ability to small talk (since everybody's taken the same material, thus having some sort of common ground), and the ability to do mindless, repetitive tasks.

Afterwards you have two options (well three if you count military, but the conformity and uselessness of love should be so abundantly clear in this walk of life that I shouldn't have to expand on it.): 1) Go to college, or 2) get a shitty job and be poor.

In both of these options the kind of employment available, either immediately and at low pay, or delayed gratification and higher pay, have, at their heart, capitalistic success as it's only goal. There's no passion for the task, there's no motivation, outside of money, for even seeking employment. So we live to work, rather than working to live.

So you wake up, go to work, return home with work on the brain (or go to your second job if you didn't go to college), with hopes of a promotion, a raise, what-have-you, and time for yourself is marginalized more and more to weekends, which, if you're a good little capitalist, should be spent doing work anyway. Then, of course, with what little liesure you time you have it must, simply must, be spent upholding your image of success. Constantly striving for a bigger and better house; keeping the lawn trimmed, proper and pretty; buying the latest style of success; equipping yourself with the latest technological advancement, cell phone with internet, pda with gpi, whatever; and holding "social" where work is functioning as the heart and soul, a morale booster at best, and shame shame shame if anybody outside of your high level of stature is seen there doing anything but serving food.

Add to this the feminist movement meaning that women are striving twice as hard to acquire this same state of self-imposed imprisonment, and you have a world where the heart has no place.

I'm not against the feminist movement, and I believe "equal pay for equal work" and the like is wonderful. Women and men have equality laced through them. My beef is that the brass ring that they should have an equal shot at acquiring is, at the current moment, horribly alienating and robs life of meaning.

Who has time to create meaning in life through the solidarity that can be found in family? Love has no monetary value; lust does!
