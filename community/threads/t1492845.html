Re: Opening
Some people have social skills, some don't.

When I'm talking to a stranger it just comes natural. You shouldn't feel pressured to say something. I usually comment on something that's happening around us. That way, it's a neutral subject that you both have in common.

For instance, last weekend I was walking into someone's house for the first time. I didn't know anyone there except for the person I came with. There were four guys playing Spades so I sat down, introduced myself, and started talking about Spades which lead to cards which lead to gambling and the conversation went from there.

Your name should NEVER be the meat of your discussion. Introductions aren't alway appropriate either. I have a rule that I only introduce myself when I'm shaking someone's hand. (That goes for business situations or if your meeting a friend of a friend.) Think about it, if you're at a party and someone does something stupid you just make a comment, you don't say your name and THEN make the comment. Maybe that's a stupid example but whatever. I often find myself talking to someone for a period of time and finally asking their name or saying, "By the way..."

Also, when you are meeting someone. Shake their hand FIRMLY. Even if it is a girl. I don't know if you guys have weak hand shakes or are just afraid of hurting me but my hand isn't going to break from a firm handshake, dammit. The only guys that can get away with doing the kissy cheek thing are dudes I'm close to or knew from childhood/school. Don't do it to every girl you just meet, it's too personal. I find older men don't do this, it's just the younger ones. Why? Is this a generation X thing?

I find it extremely easy to spark up a conversation with someone in line at the grocery store but some people can't. I guess it's just one of those "things". Confidence and general knowledge in a wide array of subjects would be a good start to being a friendly stranger.

I used to have that problem...I couldn't initiate a conversation with anyone...Now I work in a hotel as concierge!

The trick to it is learning to be comfortable with starting conversations with anyone...Male or female.

I started out a while ago by just talking to guys in pubs...Becoming comfortable with talking to strangers and making a good conversation out of it.

With women you just have to be able to do the same thing...Don't go in hoping to get a number or a date...Go in hoping to get a conversation.

That's where the key lies...Once you have a conversation going, you can work on directing it to your needs.

For example, just go up and say something like "I've never seen such a hideous pair of shoes" then point at someone, giggle and introduce yourself.

She'll be a little wary at first but just talk to her as though you're being friendly and she'll open up a little more.
