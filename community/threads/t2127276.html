Can we have a serious Mac/PC discussion?
Okay, well I was spending a lot of time on 4chan's /g/ board (technology), and there's a lot of hate almost towards all OS's (even linux), but I use a Mac, so I only really cared about the mac arguments. I've come across these points from people who have likely never touched a mac in their life, and this is what I have to say about them. (I'm not really being a fan boy here, I like windows XP as much as my mac, but Vista got a lot more hate then leopard did.)

1) Yeah well you can't play games on a mac
Well, I don't really need to. I'm not a gamer, and I don't imagine you would get a Mac if you were! I still play C&C3 on here (Command and conquer 3) and it runs just fine! My friends plays Crysis on his ubuntu box, and many other games. He's a huge gamer and doesn't only use windows.

2) Mac's are way to expensive, and you can't even upgrade it!
Okay, so I have to spend about $1600 once . You spend let's say, $1000 on a PC, then you install vista and realize all your hardware is crap so immediately you spend another $500 fixing it up, let's not forget the occasional upgrades every year or two if you want some more RAM. With my mac, I don't have to worry about upgrading because it does what I want with the spec's it has. If I wanted item X Y Z I would build my own PC, (if I had the chance to build my own PC it would cost way more then a Mac)...so yes, at face value a Mac may be more expensive but it will work.

3) Mac's just...WORK!
This is a bad thing, right?

4) You can't do anything useful on a Mac, go make another slideshow!
Actually, this neat screenshot here shows the config file for the imageboard software I plan to be hosting on my site. (Since Apache was already installed on here.) So, um...yeah actually I can program in many languages. (I've done some Perl, CSS, PHP, HTML/XML from my nano editor ^_^)

http://i30.tinypic.com/2qcdvn9.png

I also have XCode, but I'm much to lazy to start it all up.

That's pretty much the only things that I've heard people complaining about. But, let me tell you the problems I do have with Mac. (Yeah, I dislike some features)

1) It's not customizable, unless you want to spend $20. (Shapeshifter by unsanity is a program that can change the look of things...but eh, it cost $20!)

2) Occasionally something might go wrong and if you view the error report it's a shit load of assembly. Literally, EAX's jumping off the screen everywhere. I've only had this happen about 5-6 times though. (I've had the comp for about 8 months)

3) It comes with a bunch of shit I don't care about, like iCalander or something. Some people might find it useful, but eh, not for me.

That's all I can really say. In the end it all comes down to preference, why do people argue so much about the OS's, and when they do- why do they make such retarded arguments?!?! It'd be like me saying windows crashes all the time. (It does crash, but not excessively. XP though, vista I'm not to sure about.)

If you have any question about Mac's, I'd be happy to answer from a user POV. Just, don't flame. I'm admitting that Mac's aren't perfect, thanks if you read the whole thing.

Re: Can we have a serious Mac/PC discussion?
How about the annoyingly custom hardware in Macs?

If my cat pisses on my Dell laptop's power supply, I can buy a new universal one for under the price of a DVD and carry on working.

Also, if a component - say, the motherboard - fries and dies on a Mac tower, you can't just go out to the shop and buy a new one. I imagine you have to buy the replacement OEM part from Apple direct, which isn't going to be cheaper than a white-box item from a retailer.

The inflexibility of Macbooks also really put me off. Whilst looking for a laptop, I did consider one but the offerings by Dell gave more flexibility for less money. A lot less money. I can whip out the CD drive with the press of a button and replace it with a second battery, space saving blank or a hard drive caddy. I frequently use this feature to "customise" my machine for the needs of that day - when I want to use it all day long for typing, in goes the second battery. Or if it's going to be lugged around in the car, used as a GPS, the blanking plate is used. Very handy.

And if I manage to kill the optical drive? £15 later and the click of a latch and I'm up and running again.

Macbooks also lack a docking station, something which I couldn't do without in a portable machine. I have a printer, big monitor, keyboard, power adaptor and a bunch of other stuff all connected up to the dock - just drop the laptop on the dock and bam, everything's connected up.

HP and IBM also have a similar system of interchangeable modules and docking stations - come on Apple, if you're so advanced, do it!

Cost and availability of parts is also awful on Macs. I dropped my Dell down a flight of hard wooden stairs (I'm an idiot, don't ever watch videos and walk at the same time), breaking a hinge. Cost to fix: £10 and 15 minutes work. Nice, easy-to-access screws, and the whole process is documented in the service manual downloadable from Dell's site. I've taken apart Macbooks and it's not pretty. They're made to look clean and screwless, not easily maintainable.

Heh...I wonder if a Macbook would have survived that 15 step plunge of death...

Macs are more expensive than PCs. I buy most things, including my computers, second hand as I don't need the most up-to-date power available. About a year and a half ago, my Dell D600 (1.6ghz P-M, 80gb HD, 1gb RAM) cost me £200 ($400) with two new batteries and a case from Ebay. I just had a look on the 'bay and a 1.4ghz iBook seems to run for just about twice what I paid - and that's a year on. It also doesn't have as much memory and the screen is lower resolution.

Note that I don't game on a PC - at all. I have a chipped Xbox for all night 4 player drunken Mario Kart matches and Halo when people are over, but I don't play on my own.
