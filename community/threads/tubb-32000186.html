quote:
Originally posted by CallMeBob:
personaly i think its an urban legend but if it was real it would be cool

Man, that's pretty wrong. Lucid dreaming isn't an urban legend, but it is a skill that must be mastered over time.

Lucid dreaming simply involves realizing you are dreaming in the middle of, well, a dream. This can be done by doing what are known as "reality checks", in which you make a habit of examining your surroundings (like reading words in a book), then looking away, then looking back to see if anything has changed. Now, because nobody does this as a habit, you will have to make it one. Ask yourself, on a regular basis, whether or not you are dreaming, and find some evidence to back it up.

Also, you can record the events of your dream right after the REM phase in your sleep, when the memories are freshest in your mind, and recording them in a dream journal, or something of the like (Don't bother telling yourself you'll remember the dream later, either, because 80% of the time, very few, if any, of the important details of your dream remain). Soon you will be able to recognize these elements while dreaming, and will be able to take full control.

But of course, the excitement caused by the knowledge that one is dreaming can sometimes cause them to wake up, in turn, essentially blowing what you've worked for, not to mention losing sleep in the process. So, if you feel you're about to wake, just start spinning around; it works for most lucid dreamers.

Keep questing for that Holy Grail.

What I suggested is a meditation like technique. The thing is, if you are exhaused, you will easily slip into sleep. Meditation lingers around the border of stage 1 and stage 2 sleep. Dreaming during stage 1 is not really dreaming, more if a vivid imagination. Dreaming during stage 2 is where your subconscious mind takes over, this is the most common part of sleep as well. Eventually after being in stage 2 for anywhere from a few minutes to a few hours you will enter REM. REM is hard to classify as a sleep stage, since it has unique properties, though it is closer to stage 3. Stage 4 and 5 are simply-put: hibernation. If you want to actually say "I'm going to lucid dream tonight" than I suggest trying what I do. If you simply want to lucid dream, but dont' care when, do what everyone else says. Catching yourself in the middle of a dream is usually more vivid than doing it my way.

I have had several spontaneous lucid dreams and can say wholeheartedly that Lucid Dreaming is indeed a real occurence.

I have read many documents,books on the subject, and there are just as many views and methods of obtaining the lucid dream state.

Basically, for some reason, you become consiouss of the fact that you are dreaming.

The first time this happened to me was after I had been really sick and fatigued and I got up to ride my bike to work at about 2am. I ended up getting woozy on the way there and passed out on the side of the road in a ditch. When I came to about a half hour later, I rode back home and went to sleep.

That was when it happened. I dont remember how I knew I was dreaming, but I do remember I was consciouss of presence both in the dream and in my body lying in bed at the same time. This I later found out was called "bi-location" where you can be consciouss of being in two places at once.

This happened throughout the night where I would remember I was dreaming, be able to control it(flying, punching through doors and then rematerializing them,etc),then get snapped into another dream where I had to remember I was dreaming all over again.

I ended up getting to a scary part where I didnt know If I was asleep or awake, and I could feel my body paralyzed in the "real" world. I ended up being able to throw my arm up and slap my self in the face till I was actually able to wake all the way up.

Since then I have had a few spontaneous occurences but never to the same degree or lucidity as the first one. I have tried to develop it as a skill but with limited success, as I dont stick with things for very long before I give up.

Some people believe that if your lucid dreaming, your only one step away from achieving a full blown out of body experience (OBE), wich I believe I have been able to achieve with slightly better success.
