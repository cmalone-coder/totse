Ok, *I* have a legal question...
Yes, I know I'm usually answering these rather than asking them, but heres one I can't seem to find any law regarding.

83 year old female, living in Georgia. Her daughter, 58, is disabled and lives with her. Their AC is broken and does not work. Daughter has respiratory condition of some kind which is exasperated by the heat. She has a service contract with Sears (whom she may or may not have bought the AC from, I do know a service/repair contract exists with them). Repeated calls have been made to try and have the AC fixed, and the last call said it may take 10+ days for completion.

She posed the question to me, is there any law which states elderly or disabled have preference and priority over non-elderly and non-disabled in such circumstances?

Currently searching: Sears repair/service policy, geriatric law, Alabama and Georgia Human Relations Law

Any thoughts?

Re: Ok, *I* have a legal question...
I'd approach this from a tort perspective. When accepting the contract Sears may have assumed a responsibility to ensure the normal operation of the AC. The essential question here is whether Sears have a duty of care to the owner to see that they are not caused undue discomfort by a delay in fixing the AC, or whether the duty of care extends just to fixing the AC within a reasonable time.

Since the contract was entered into, presumably without communication of any particulars of circumstance, I'd say no, Sears have no specific duty of care. However if they fail to fix it when they stated, or within reasonable time, they are liable for damage caused by their failure to act.
