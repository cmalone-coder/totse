Re: Homework drugs
Good nutrition, rest/sleep, hydration, excercise, etc, are generally best for any measure of human performance. You can try to take supplemental measures to combat a deficiency in these areas, but the fact is you're still somewhat working against yourself if you're denying yourself the necessities that should always come first and foremost if concerned with performance.

There are other things that can help, though they're best when you already have the above working for you.

Smart drugs, or Nootropics, along with nutritional supplements to maintain or heighten levels of various non-essential (but useful) biomolecules (ie: various nutritional suplpements such as Taurine, GABA, or some source of choline, choline being the chief neurotransmitter in 'intelligence' or signal integrity between neurons), would likely be the best and least damaging way.

I'm not saying Adderall is 'bad', but relying on it for performance in routine things like homework and stuff just seems like a bad idea, as far as the long term is concerned. While Nootropics and other nutritional supplements claiming to improve brain functioning are generally quite subtle, some are unnoticeable, but some nootropics/nutritional supplements strike me as a bit better for maintaining academic performance in the longer term than speed.

Piracetam is a good starting point, also get some choline in there, Alpha GPC Choline is best, but it's expensive... a combination of Lecithin and Choline Bitartarate is much cheaper and a decent compromise. ALCAR, or acetyl-l-carnitine, is another favorite. I find it improves motivation and energy and clarity, and it's something I plan to take forever (good in an anti-aging regimen, maintains and revitalizes cellular mitochondria, including those within our neurons). If you're taking a large amount of ALCAR (2 or more grams, which I recommend) you should also take a powerful antioxidant like Na-RALA, or just RALA (r-alpha-lipoic acid), because ALCAR in higher doses can increase oxidative stress, but if you take some form of alpha lipoic acid there seems to be nothing to worry about (they actually are quite synergistic or beneficial if taken together). Those are my favorites right now, those four supplements have become something of a mainstay, that I can't ever see dropping in the near future.

There are a whole bunch of other smart drugs such as Vinpocetine, Hydergine, Huperazine A, Galantamine, Centrophenoxine, Idebenone, and a bunch of others. Of those, all I can say is Vinpocetine is good for a mental boost of clarity, but it seems using it long term might be counter productive to intelligence in the long term (as various stimulants are). Hydergine doesn't seem to do much at all on it's own, for me, but taking it with piracetam is syngergistic. Centrophenoxine is quite good, and synergistic with piracetam. It's also a good source of choline, being a good second to Alpha GPC choline (it's a bit cheaper and has somewhat comparable results as far as quality goes). The rest sound interesting to me, but I havent gotten around to trying them.

As far as nutritional supplements go, I hear a good regimen for maintaining brain health/function is as follows:

250 - 1000 mg of ALCAR (I take 2000mg, split into two doses daily, taken along with some form of alpha lipoic acid.)
200 - 300 mg of Phosphatidyl serine
500 - 1,500 mg of GABA
1,000 mg of Taurine
and as a daily regimes of base nutrients
B-Complex 50 mg
Vitamin C 1,000 mg
Vitamin E 400 mg
Magnesium 400 mg
Zinc 25 mg
Selenium 200 mcg
Boron 3 mg
300 mg of EPA
300 mg of DHA (both essential fatty acids)

None of this will necessarily make you smarter, but it'll make sure your brain has mostly everything it needs in order to continue to work well long term. These are also things our brains begin to lack more and more as we age. Therefore, it's also a very good anti-aging regimen. A person worried about mental decline due to age could employ such a daily regimen to stay near the top of their game.

I've started on such a regimen and I'm only 20. I suppose it's kind of a proactive approach, as I haven't noticed much or any age related decline. Still, it's something I'd like to avoid, and many of these things are things our body is quite able of making (beneficial) use of (or at least it seems to me) and that our bodies are sometimes insufficient in producing. Especially as we age.
