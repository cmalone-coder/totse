The ills of drinking: My experiment.
So my experiment is going to be over soon; or, at least, I will be attempting to end it soon (a year early no less.. that's how difficult it gets). For those of you that don't have any idea what I'm talking about, this probably won't mean much to you -- and to those of you who know what I'm talking about, this will probably mean less to you than the fact that the experiment is (purportedly) ending. The experiment was to get drunk, in company or alone, for 4-6 days of the week for 5 years and see how it would affect someone. Does it make you a good writer like the others? What are typical drunks like and is it really like people assume? Will I turn into Nick Nolte?

Therefore, although this is far less valuable to you than to myself, I've started to think about those things I've learned and experienced, and I'm trying to make sense of it all. I'm not sure whether I'll be able to elucidate this all until a long while after this has ended, but I'm guessing that there's no harm in trying. So here goes... first some bad things:



*** As predicted, the behavior is self-perpetuating -- but to a degree that I had not expected at all, which is why I'm going to have a hard time ending it. Everyone's heard the cliché about walking in the shoes of another, but you can't walk here and understand it after a few weeks, or even a year. If you are not affected by this particular condition, you do not understand it. At all. Spare your platitudes about personal responsibility and self-respect and caring about those around you. They mean nothing, and are perpetuated by people that don't understand it in the first place.



*** It does, in fact, affect those around you. But not how you would expect. You tend to guard those who tried to make you into a [decent] person, and then flood those who you've run into along the way toward an inevitable death with your problems. I always thought that it was rather strange that families never seemed to do much of anything about it, but the fact is that they rarely know the severity thereof. It is our instinct, I believe, to protect those who at least attempted to protect us throughout our lives, however ineffective their pursuits might be(come). We don't want our parents to think their efforts failed nor do we want to appear as failures to them, and so we hide. Which I guess isn't much of an epiphany, but it is especially important in this kind of scenario.

On the other hand, those people that you care about in the present or near-past --regardless of how you feel about them-- will suffer immensely. This is my main reason for trying to end it early. I don't need to explain this further.



*** I think just as well --albeit in a different way-- but my ability to succinctly elucidate my thoughts in conversation has been severely... damaged? I'm not sure if this is because it affects one part of the brain more than another, but I have been increasingly unable to say what I mean on-the-fly, even when I'm trying to express something insanely simple. I stutter, I slur, and I confuse one word and part of speech for another - and that's not being under the influence.

This is, as far as I can tell, a result of long-term abuse, as it only began happening after the first two years or so, then progressively became worse. I guess, looking at the situation objectively, that this could be attributed to other factors in life: stress, information overload, change of subculture, etc. -- but I'm pretty sure that it's a matter of a change in actual brain chemistry. Whether this relents after the fact has yet to be seen, but from anecdotal evidence I am concluding that the damage can be repaired in most cases.



*** I've done terribly stupid things that I would not have done otherwise. The fact of the matter, though, is that when I've done this I've almost always been under the influence of something else as well. While I think it's pretty plausible that yes, people will do very stupid things under the influence, a lot of people will not do so under the influence of merely one thing -- but will rather be led to do so under an amalgamation of chemicals. I understand, however, that each person's chemistry is different, so I really can't say how true that statement is. This isn't an objective experiment, but I would hope that's understood by now.



*** By all of my observations, people with this problem are lonely -- not in the sense that they lack friends or family, but in the sense that they feel (and often are) disconnected and alienated from this world at large. This is something I had suspected, of course, so I might just be interjecting my own expectations into the matter.. but I'm pretty sure it holds true. There is a culture that goes along with this, and you genuinely do connect with people in a way that was an impossibility beforehand.

This also tends to transcend class, the fact with which I still have a problem. The wealthiest sit with the poorest in this and drown together as a result (though the rich will last longer). I'm not eager to admit that many things transcend class, so take this as an admission of being ideologically biased before now... but don't expect me to back down from everything else.


On the other hand, some good things:


*** I have become a far more compassionate person. When you have a severe problem, you are less likely to wholly fault others for their own. This could change after the experiment genuinely ends, of course, but I honestly hope that it doesn't. I want to understand the complexities of human behavior, not judge them offhand simply because I do not exhibit the same behavior.



*** With the right level of juice, I can write a stream of consciousness that has never been paralleled before by me. This actually surprised me, because I had always considered the "altered state" argument to be a romantic mythology. So while there are, in fact, elements of truth behind it, I should also say that the inspiration is not only fleeting, but stagnating; that is, the longer I have continued this cause, the less frequent my inspiration.



*** I become LESS introspective, which from my previous experiences was counter to my intuition. Yet, it's true: I think far less about myself and far more about other people. Granted, I don't think of "the other" as frequently now as I do "others," but that seems to make me more humane rather than less.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


I have much more to say about this, more than I think I could write here... but since this is going to be reaching a different stage soon, I'll wait until then to share more. I am, after all, still experiencing things as I have for the past four years, so it wouldn't be exactly fair to blurt out everything before a conclusive end is in sight.

Thoughts on a postcard.

I can't remember if my teacher told us he always wrote drunk or always sober, I can't remember. But at any rate, he drank like a fish. I think it cost him his first marriage, I can't really remember.

"Where I'm Calling From" is a bunch of short stories written by him, might be of interest.
