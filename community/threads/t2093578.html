I never trouble myself to get rid of the little 1 unit-sized bubbles at the top of my rigs when I shoot, and I've never had any problems with embolisms. The idea that the smallest bubble will cause an air embolism is a myth; the actual amount of air it would take for that to happen is quite substantial (1/2cc or more) and depends on certain other factors such as body positioning.

Never the less, it is good practice to get rid of all the air bubbles before injecting. Injecting numerous little air bubbles over time can cause issues if they accumulate.

When attempting to register a vein, here's a tip:

Immediately after piercing the skin (but before you pierce the vein), pull back a little bit on the plunger. This will create a "vacuum", or suction, within the chamber that will immediately draw blood into the syringe when the needle comes into contact with a vein.

This way you don't have to jab your arm, hope to hit a vein, and then try to register. Doing it that way when you're new to IV'ing is a good way to fuck up your arms fast. Try it the way I mentioned and I guarantee you you'll save yourself alot of time and frustration.

I don't ever tie off or use a tourniquet (my veins are visible enough), but if you do make sure you release it after you register. If you can't release it after registering without knocking the needle out of the vein, then make sure to do so after you inject. Not releasing the tourniquet, especially if it's a tight one, is a good way to collapse your vein. You'd be surprised how many people don't do this...

You're attempting to IV a pill, right? Don't apply heat and make sure you filter well. I'm assuming you don't have any micron filters so my advice would be to backload. In other words, take a syringe and put small pieces of cotton/coffee filter inside of it. Next, pour your opiate solution into the rig with the filters and replace the plunger, pushing the solution through the filters out and into a spoon. This is the next best way to filter anything next to using a micron....
