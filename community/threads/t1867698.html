That would be the .22 Sniper SubSonic. It is a form of .22, but it uses a shortened .22LR case to accomodate a 60 grain projectile.

The .22 SSS is a heavy bullet that travels fairly fast, and has the .22's characteristic flat trajectory as a result.

Unlike a standard .22 or .22LR, though, it is a heavy projectile and as such is not blown off course by the slightest of winds.

And NO, a standard .22 WON'T kill you unless you hit in a vital area like the throat. Even aiming at the heart isn't very reliable, because a lot of the area is covered by ribs, and a .22 will bounce off without even breaking them. Also bear in mind that bulletproof vests have a trauma plate over the heart, so don't even think about shooting there with a .22.

The .22 SSS has enough power to be effective out to about 200m.

If you want a cheap sniper rifle, get a .223 or similar.

By the way, the .22 SSS is NOT a very effective sniper calibre. it is only used because it travels below the speed of sound, and is therefore able to be silenced very effectively.

I think some of us are being a bit optimistic about the killing power of .22LR's and .22WMR's.

-------

An adult human male weighs about as much as an adult Whitetail deer, and there are lots of recorded kills on Whitetail deer. If we look at the smallest calibers commonly used for Whitetails, we see 30-30 (at short ranges), 25-06 (at medium ranges) and .270 (at longer ranges). People in the Central US do often use .30-06's and even occasionally .308's, but the 30-06 is a tradidional-type caliber, and .308's are popular for shooting cheap military and commercial reload ammo. You don't usually need a 30-06 or a 308, because even they can shoot considerably farther than a .270, whitetail deer are woodland and deep meadow animals and the longest shots you'll ever see are maybe 200 yards. A close shot might be 50 yards.

.....Now....

Nobody who expects to take anything home shoots at a deer with a fucking .22LR, or even with a .22WMR. A .223 is the LEGAL MINIMUM for whitetail deer in several states, and it is considered a poor choice because of its relatively low power, combined with its small-diameter bullet. A 30-30 beats it easy in killing power at close range. You cah shoot someone in the head with a .22LR from 300 yards and (if you hit them) they would be injured, but they are NOT going to just drop over dead. I haven't heard of anyone taking a .223 deerhunting, even though it is legal--to instantly knock down a full-grown 150-lb deer with one chest shot requires a lot of luck with that caliber.... If you're in deep woods, you use a 30-30. If you're working a wooded edge, you use a 25-06. If you're working a meadow pass, you use a .270.

-----------

If you want to find out really how much "knock down power" a gun caliber has, all you need to do is look up what sizes of animals are successfully hunted with it regularly, and at what ranges. .22LR's are used for rabbits, squirrels and various close-range (&lt;20 yards) backyard pests. .22WMR's are used on foxes and coyotes out to around 200 yards. .17HMR's are working well on coyotes out to around 150 yards. You could shoot at a bull elephant with a .22LR and certainly hit it, but it will turn around and stomp you to death today, and die of septic infection sometime next month.
