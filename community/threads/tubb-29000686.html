They also don't modify it because that will ruin the art, Yet in ancient China, Practioners of a certain martial arts would improvise it according to certain circumstances such as their body frame. Martial arts in ancient times were created according to the needs of the practioner. We all know the story of the Okinawans and their karate; They were oppresed by samurai armies and most of the time these samurai dressed in armor so therefore the karateka had to emphasize on skills that sacrificed speed to build up strength in order to pierce their opponent`s armor. In modern times attackers no longer wear heavy armour and these ancient skills had to be modified for effective street combat.

Personally I believe that there is no such thing as the ultimate martial art. One practices an art, finds it's faults and improvises it by adding skills of other arts. Just like Daniel Insanto said: "Absorb what is useful".

It seems shameful that monks in ancient times preached against greed and thought their arts without monetary gain only to find people today exploiting these fine arts.

I suggest you find a good martial art school that charges a fair membership rate, learn all that you can from it and improvise by learning skills from books and videos of other martial arts.

What type of person are you refering to when the say "Street Brawler"? A Bouncer or what not?

In my mind a Street Brawler would be someone with no formal training, but alot of experience. The thing is most of this fighting experience would most likely be against other non-trained opponents.

If I had to bet on either a UFC fighter or Street Fighter, I'd have to consider some things:

1. What Style does a UFC fighter have? UFC is just a fighting format, and has many different types of fighting styles, ranging from Muay Thai, Judo, boxing, Gracie Ju-jitsu, and even Tang Soo Do(one known exclusive fighter). Lets also not forget all the Hybrid fighters(Which probably is the majority).

2. The conditioning of both opponents. Although its nearly impossible to judge total conditioning just by looking at someone. However you can see how "cut" a fighter is, which would give you a general idea of what kinda of shape their in. You would also have to consider differences in weight.

3. Fight Experience. This can be a huge factor. If a UFC fighter whos only documented wins are over Frank Shamrock, Dan Severen, Tito Ortiz, Vanderlei Silva, and Rodrigo Nogueira, thats still a pretty impressive resume, and would hold much more weight with me then a Street Brawler who claims to have defeated hundreds of other street fighters.

Basically what I'm trying to say is that deciding who wins in a UFC vs. "Street Brawler" match is not cut and dry, and totally depends who the hell is in the ring(or where ever).
