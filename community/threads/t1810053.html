The various texts which make up what we know today as "the bible" are exactly that, various texts, of varying text types. e.g. Revelations is in a style of writing common in the period (1st Century AD), Apocalyptic. Psalms is like a collection of songs. Books like Luke's Gospel and Acts are written as a historical account. Other text types include Oracle's, Personal Narratives, early legal and constitutional documents, letters from leaders in the early church and so on.

There is also more manuscript witnesses for the validity of the New Testament than any other piece of ancient literature. (I can post the evidence if u really want it). And this is accepted among secular historians.

This means that what we have today is the same as what was written originally. As for the legitimacy of its content...e.g. the events contained in the New Testament, both archaeology and historical documents bear witness to many of the events contained in the New Testament (i can post this evidence also) . THere are a number of internal factors (e.g. the major lack of discrepency between the texts of the New Testament. Also, the people of the time it was written would have known the texts to be fraudulant, if they were, and thus the whole following would have collapsed.

So yes, i know (not believe) that the bible is a trustworthy document. Yes it contains accounts of miracles, some of which seem explainable. Others do not. It provides alot of answers to alot of questions. On other questions, it appears strangely silent.

If you really want to look into the reliability (or lack thereof) of the bible, look at Josh McDowell's "Evidence that demands a verdict" or "More than a Carpenter", or something by John Stott, or "Who rolled away the stone?" or something like that. Hell, even go to any university library and look through the ancient history section for the Middle East.
