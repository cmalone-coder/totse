A note on criticism.
I have encountered two types of people that generally piss me off when it comes to attitudes toward writing: 1) those who think anyone can write and therefore those who actually do write well haven't made a lick of effort at the endeavor; and 2) those who believe writing is a pointless, narcissistic practice devoid of any social or cultural value in the long-run.

Concerning the former attitude: if you believe that anyone with half a brain can write well, then by all means do so. If it's such an effortless thing to do, you should have no problem writing the next great novel--or even getting published in a reputable fashion at all. Unfortunately, the truth is that writing most definitely is a difficult thing to do well, regardless of your supposed genius that encompasses all worldly things, and takes a lot of practice and self-criticism to improve. Writing is like any other worthy endeavor: it takes massive amounts of labor to do well, and even then you're likely not doing it well enough. It's a skill that has to be honed over a lifetime.

Concerning the latter attitude: almost any experience you have, apart from the tactile world, is directly shaped by writers. Any television program, song, book, news snippet, political speech, movie, video game and so on is ultimately reliant upon a writer or a collection thereof at some point in production. Writers, when it is all said and done, forge cultural values in a literate society--though granted writers are subject to the social and political constraints of their time and place. Writers shape all media experienced by you or anyone else, which is profoundly important; they simply disappear into the background noise of the product at which they labored. In short, tell them 'go fuck yourself' and keep working at it!
