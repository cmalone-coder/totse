Galaxies and Black Holes
A fairly common concept is that galaxies have at their center a gravitational mass which is commonly refered to as a "black hole" or "singularity". I do not disagree, mainly because I can't think of a better idea.

Here is the problem. Discussions on the age of the universe give our solar system an age of 4-6 billion (US) years. The Milky Way is given an age twice that and the universe as a whole three times that.

The treatise I read, claimed that our solar system had to wait to be formed because supernova expolsions were required to create the elements eventually winding up on Earth. Also the spiral nature of the Milky Way had to develop before our solar system could form.

I don't get it...we go from the big bang...to matter...to black holes/singularities...to formation of galaxies.

The sequence is wrong. Black holes are formed by the collapse of neutron stars greater than 3 solar masses. But the super nova of such a large mass star required to make a neutron star which would eventually collapse and make that size singularity required to form a galaxy, would have blown all the matter off to never never land and not allow the formation of a galaxy.

Your thoughts/comments are appreciated.

This is my understanding of the subject. I read a lot about it, and for sources I suggest New Scientist and Physics World. Both are magasines, but both also have exceptionally good websites as well.

Big bang kicks it all off. matter and anti matter produce. Krackov's law and ( I forget his name's) Law show how there is a slight imbalance between matter and anti matter anhililation. This leads to the physical universe we have today ( There is a nice new proof of this that was published in Physics World and Nature, to do with the spin of nutrienos, that even a simpleton like myslef understood.)

This matter condensed into the stars, and eventually, the planets that we have today. As the stars go through there fusion processes, they convert lighter atoms into heavier atoms. Alpha and Beta decay handle the elemets that are not naturally formed in the stars ( Most odd numbered elements)

AT THE SAME TIME, stars are also collapsing and forming black holes. While it maybe true that there are black holes at the centre of a good percentage of galaxies, the centre of a galaxy is not needed to form a black hole.

I think where the confusion arises in this subject is that all these occurences DONT have to happen at the same time. It's only when considered in a strictly linear timepath that you get yourt head messed around.

I also wouldn't attach to much empathis to the gravistar theory. As I understand it, it was a theory that was thrown out unexpectedly by a group who got odd results in their maths. It was jumped on by a lot of people looking for a different way of thinking about the black hole problems, but the matths has been re-worked a lot, and shown that the basis of the theory was flawed.
