Re: Unlocking Vehicles.
Not thick at all really. I'm not sure what gauge it would be, but not much thicker than about half a dozen pieces of construction paper should be fine for most purposes. The trick with these things is practice. Different doors have different locks. It's a good idea to cut a wedge near the far end of the piece to pulll levers if you need to, but in my experience with older american cars you usually just find the right place in the door and push down. You can get a good feel for what the inside of the door looks like if you poke around long enough, but that slip and pop, no sweat, you see on the movies is for the pro's or guys who've done it to the same type of car a million times before.

Re: Unlocking Vehicles.
I've never actually taken the door panel's off to look at then so I can't give great detail. I'm not an expert, I was just a little bit of a trouble maker when I was a teenager. Anyway, from my experience it's usually within 5 inches or so to the left of the lock knob, although I have done an old Dart that had it about three inches to the right.
Like I said, they're all different. The best thing to do Is slide that bad boy in and poke around. There is nothing in ther REALLY fragile so don't be a flincher about it. Poke, prod and pull. Watch the knob. If it moves, you're in the right place. Look to push and if there is no gove there, try and find someplace to hook and pull.
