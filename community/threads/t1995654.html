Re: Smoke bomb equation
The exact ammount of specific products depend on more than just the ratio of KNO3 to sucrose. IE at higher pressure on product is more likely to form than another... or at high temperature, etc...

All you can really do is have the breakdown products of sucrose, IE CO2, H2O, C, and have the decomposition products of KNO3, IE K2CO3, possibily KOH, etc... you can't get anything exact without analysing the reaction products, including the gases produced.

Find google groups and search the rec.pyrotechnics group... they have many many years worth of archived searchable threads... you should be able to find some approximations there.

Well.. I wrote a lengthy responce to how i got to this, but ill cut it back to the bare min... as totse was down for a min and it was lost...

8KNO3 + C12H22O11 + 2O2 > 8CO2 + 11H2O + 4N2 + 4K2CO3

The 2O2 on the reactants side is from the ambient air... its assumed:
2KNO3 -> 2K N2 + 3O2
The O2 goes to oxidizing the C in sucrose...
The 2K reacts with ambient oxygen from the air forming K2O which reacts with some of the CO2 from the sucrose forming K2CO3... However this is flawed as no K actually forms... KNO3 decomposes to KNO2, then with loss of NO forming K2O or something like that... so yeah not actually what happens.. but the teacher might believe it... or you can just say this is sorta what could happen... and this reaction is way to complex.

Re: Smoke bomb equation
Also, don't forget that it IS an exothermic reaction, so the heat is a product instead of a reactant. You probably put it on the left because you know you have to light a fuse to get it started, well, that's the initiation energy. Obviously, a lot more energy than that is released from the compound as it decomposes. So, it should look something like:

(a)KNO3 + (b)C12H22O11 ---> (c)CO2 + (d)H2O + (e)K2CO3 + (f)CO + (g)N2 ...et + Heat (delta H)

(where a-g are coefficients)
