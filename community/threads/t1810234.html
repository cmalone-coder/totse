You're all wrong.

Let's hear what Thomas Huxley, who coined the word "agnostic" has to say about it.

Quote:
quote:Positively the principle may be expressed: In matters of the intellect, follow your reason as far as it will take you, without regard to any other consideration. And negatively: In matters of the intellect, do not pretend that conclusions are certain which are not demonstrated or demonstrable.
In short, if you have no evidence for a proposition don't claim to know anything about the truth of that proposition.

I am an agnostic atheist. I have no hidden knowledge and I am not a theist. There is no contradiction there.

Of course, I recognize that no definition is absolute so prefer to try to figure out what people mean rather than what they say.

No longer in Camelot it is a silly place
I'm agnostic after having been an atheist for a long time. My beliefs are pretty simple:

-I think it is not within my ability to know if there is or isn't a god. By "god", I mean a higher force, or grand design, or supreme being, or whatever might be responsible for the universe. Something outside our understanding and outside what we know from science. Basically I believe it is unwise to be 100% sure of anything. Many things I'm 99.9% sure of, but never do I consider myself able to be absolutely certain of.

-I am almost certain that all human religions are largely invented and that none are the true pipeline (so to speak) to whatever the real truth of everything is. There is much wisdom in most religions, but I have a feeling that is there is a god, he/she/it doesn't write books and construct religions for the believers.

Basically, I don't assume that it's within my power to know "what's really going on". And I don't have much use for faith. When there are an infinite number of things one can have faith in, what are the odds of being right? I have hunches about some things, but no certainties.
