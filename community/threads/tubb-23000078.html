CDMA uses the full available spectrum. Individual conversations are encoded with a pseudo-random digital sequence
CDMA is a digital spread-spectrum modulation technique, not a form of encryption.

CDMA digitizes the conversation and tags it with a frequency code. The data is then scattered across the frequency band in a pseudorandom pattern.

The receiving device is instructed to read only the data corresponding to a particular code to reconstruct the signal.

Therefore CDMA is not technically encrypted it is just a method for sending and receiving a digital transmission.

TDMA, or Time Division Multiple Access, is a digital alternative to the analog AMPS, or Advanced Mobile Phone System, cellular service.

TDMA works in a method similar to network packet-switching. It allows a cellular operator to divide the signal into short segments, allowing different conversations to "take turns." This allows three times as many people to use the system than amps.

Listening in on a digital phone call will be similar to decoding a digital pager, with the use of something similar to POCSAG, ie a digital scanner with computer interface and software.
