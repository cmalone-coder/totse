In my opinion, the rp/i is the most creative as well as the most customizable method of reduction. I am biased though. The main drawbacks to consider are:

1. It can require long cooking time.

2. Red Phosphorous is hard to find. Matchbook strikers is the only source I know of.

3. Compared to the anhydrous ammonia method, precursors must be relatively clean (the lower the rxn. temp, the more forgiving it is in this respect.)

Some of the benefits:

1. Everything is OTC and easy to find in medium sized to big cities. (US)

2. The actual reaction environment is very docile. Nothing that blows up. (Corrosive acid is about it)

3. Wide variation of end product, but very repeatable when same conditions are met. This allows the creator (thats you) to adjust various elements of the reaction to tailor the final product to suit your own taste.

4. Can be set up in a lavalamp (and other relatively safe heat sources) and left unattended for long hours (once stability is established) without worry.

The last part has become a topic of interest for Swigotta and I have already acquired and modified equipment necessary for his first set of experiments with safer, "set it and forget it" methods that seem to have gained popularity as of late. Data is already being gathered on this theme.
