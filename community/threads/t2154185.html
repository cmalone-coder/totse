While blunting of personality is considered to be one of the negative symptoms of schizophrenia, it does not (nor do any of the negative symptoms, while the positive symptoms of schizophrenia usually do) comprise a break with reality sufficient to be considered a psychosis.

Technically, even hallucinations do not rise to the level of psychosis, provided they do not interfere with a person's ability to interact with and think in what an outside observer unaware of the hallucination would consider a calm and rational manner. They're usually listed as psychotic, rather than neurotic, symptoms however, simply because most people when being chased down the street by an agressive polar bear will run like a bitch rather than sit around debating the plausibility of there being a polar bear - which, when there isn't a polar bear, would represent a break with reality.

The surest sign of psychosis is disordered thought process far beyond mere emotional disturbance. Think of the bizzare ramblings of the more extremely schizophrenic individuals on your local mass transit system, and how disturbingly much less sense (or maybe "more nonsense") they seem to make than, say, the nonsensical mumblings of the extremely drunk.

It's that disturbance of thought which most reliably creates a break with reality, and insures that the supplemental symptoms, such as hallucinations, or anxiety (e.g. "paranoid fantasies" et al, the fear comes before the rationalization)... are never successfully checked against any sense of realism.
