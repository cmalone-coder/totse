Re: What are the signs of testicular cancer?
There are 2 main types of testicular cancer:

if you are around 40-50 years: painless firm enlargement of one testicle.

if you are 40 and younger (especially around 25-30), there may not be any enlargement at all, may be a small fluid collection (hydrocoele- "HYDRO-SEEL") and usually by the time anyone begins to suspect anything, there is spread to the lungs (coughing up blood, weight loss).

Here's what you need to know: testicular cancer is RARE. Hydrocoeles (benign collections of fluid in the scrotum) are EXTREMELY common, usually have nothing to do with cancer, and in most cases, they are easily operated on if one so wishes and they go away forever.

If you do have a hydrocoele, and you or your doctor is suspicious of it, you can get an ultrasound of the scrotum which will usually show an underlying cancer if there actually is one, or they can take a sample of the fluid with a needle for testing for malignant cells.

And apart from hydrocoeles, there can also be a hernia causing enlargement of one side of the scrotum. So in most cases, it's all pretty benign and common stuff.
