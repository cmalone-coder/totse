DLPA Info Needed
DLPA, also known as D,L-phenylalanine, apparently a pain reliever with two forms of the amino acid phenylalanine.

Now, I've searched the net. Searched here on totse, Blue Light, Erowid, Opiophile, medical journals, etc. and all I'm getting is very basic anecdotal information. There is very little contradicting info though, so that seems to be a good sign.

The reason I'm so interested in this is because I have chronic back pain, and I may be losing my insurance at the end of the year. I'm currently prescribed Hydrocodone 10mg/500mg TID (three time a day) for my chronic back pain caused by an accident that broke several bones in my lower back (coccyx, sacrum, a common extra lumbar bone mutation I have, etc).

Anyhow, I'm going back to college next semester, and there is no way I can do this without adequate pain relief. My original plan (if I lost my insurance) was to purchase poppy pods and have a cup of poppy pod tea a day, and then use the seeds to plant my own, but that would get expensive very quick, and it would take too long for the pods to mature (Besides, I'm not very experienced with growing papaver somniferum). Personally I would rather have poppy tea than Hydrocodone (It may not give me the same "buzz" but it helps the pain better and it lasts a lot longer.)

Back on topic. I recently heard of DLPA, and yesterday I bought some at my local health food store. Basically, from what I've read, DLPA acts in a similar way that L-Dopa acts on Dopamine (a precursor) and SSRI's or SNRI's act on Serotonin and Noreprenephrine. As far as I can understand (I only know so much about pharmalogical matters) it acts as both a precursor to endorphins and also retards the enzymes that break down the endorphins and Noreprenephrine, slowling their reuptake.

I am currently taking 750 mg DLPA twice a day, however, most information I've read says it takes at least a week before you can notice any noticeable results. So basically I'm asking if anyone has had any experience with DLPA, or has a better understanding on how this amino acid works (if it does at all).

Thanks again all.

If you're on opiates for chronic pain, you will DEFINITELY want to be getting more dietary magnesium. My recommendation is 500-750mgs daily (three a day, spread out), you'll find them at the store for like $4/100pills. Just look up "NMDA antagonists and opiate analgesia" and you'll find that they prevent tolerance, something that you will find out fairly quickly. Magnesium is the body's natural NMDA antagonist, and is probably the cheapest one you can get.

In a study that compared four forms of magnesium preparations, results suggested lower bioavailability of magnesium oxide, with significantly higher and equal absorption and bioavailability of magnesium chloride and magnesium lactate

http://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/magnesium.asp

Probably because Magnesium oxide was virtually insoluble in water and only 43% soluble in simulated peak acid secretion (http://www.jacn.org/cgi/content/abstract/9/1/48).

A general rule: The less basic (and more soluble) your counterion, the more bioavailable the stuff is.

Good thinking on the ghetto magnesium citrate; does the stuff dissolve easily?

Yup. Although, its supersaturation dynamics are some of the strangest I've ever seen in an ionic compound. When a solution is boiled, driving off a large quantity of water, then cooled, it has a consistency much like that of dried tree sap, or maybe almost like rubber cement. From some certain concentration, until it's anhydrous, it's a very bizarre, sticky, solid mess. Once dry, it has to be powdered, and produces very light fluffy crystals. In this state, it dissolves readily in water. Another interesting property of the stuff, is that it has little to no flavor. I expected it to taste strongly of nastiness, but I found it surprisingly palatable.
