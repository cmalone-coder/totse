Re: Prove to me MDMA is bad thing
I don't think many of you know you're talking about.

There are plenty of studies demonstrating the lasting neurological damage as a consequence of MDMA use. However, the majority of these studies involve injecting laboratory animals with obscene amounts of the chemical for days to weeks on end. Most MDMA users take a considerably smaller mg/kg ratio than what is administered to the lab animals and not for such a consistent and dramatic stretch of time. Also -- most of the MDMA users you and I know aren't mice. Mice with their differing physiology are affected by the drug in ways humans are not, vice versa.

While the findings in lab animals can and do provide useful and relevant information, these findings do not serve as a completely clear and accurate representation of the relationship between MDMA and humans specifically. Ethical implications limit what and how we research. Much of what we're left to work with is anecdotal. This posits a problem because anecdotal experiences cannot be controlled for. It's also worth noting that it's not yet fully understood how neurological changes translate into changes in behavior.

There does exist a definite correlation between depression and MDMA use/slower cognitive functioning (compared to the average non-drug user) and MDMA use in humans. While I don't doubt that much of it is indeed causal, it's not fair to attribute all of it as such. Keep in mind the type of people drawn to drug use; those who have used other drugs in the past, those stuck in an unhappy disposition, people who place less emphasis on the value education, etc.

What it boils down to is moderation. No doubt there is much positive utility (interpersonal, intrapersonal, spiritual, therapeutic) to be extracted from responsible ecstasy use. It's all a matter of finding a right balance. Although, it's glaringly obvious sometimes how in-denial many of you who identify themselves as pro-drug can be. Which is a shame because, ultimately, I feel this does more harm for the cause than good. Less people are willing to take the radical seriously, even if he does have legitimate claims to offer.
