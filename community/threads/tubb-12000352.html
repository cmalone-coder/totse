there's a prog called clear disk that u boot up from floppy that overwrites all the binary to 0's i'm no computer expert but i think that would do the trick.



Your right in principle but in practice as the hard disk is used to position of the write head on the drive changes slightly this means that when data is over written there is a shadow left by the bytes that were previously on that position.
This effect can be counter acted by having many passes over the disk and writing random bits rather than 0's. Any good disk wiping prog will do this. The problem isn't to wipe stuff that no longer has FAT entries but to delete all the files so their disk space can be wiped. This is due to windows mania for keeping logs and redundant copies, more over these copies often don't represent redundancy within the system since missing any of them is critical. Bollocks to that.
