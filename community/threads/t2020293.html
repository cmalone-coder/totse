Absolute Zero: Why won't it work?
This is based on the assumption that you have heard the theory that for Absolute Zero to be plausible, all contact with any other matter to the device or w/e that is causing the temperature to reach absolute zero must be severed.

That is: If you have a device that can, theoretically, reach absolute zero, for it to work it must have no connection with anything that has a temperature.

Alright, now here is what I am thinking.

First of all: I know nothing about vacuums, gravity and light. So if this is completely flawed, just tell me and let this thread die. I am a complete noob at shit like this, and I am not very smart. T_T

Here we go.

If a vacuum was created on Earth to house an 'absolute zero machine', I am guessing it would probably not work because of gravity, light, etc. The same could go for device in space.

If there was a way to produce anti-gravity, would a device to cause absolute zero to occur work in it if all light or w/e was blocked, and it was in a vacuum?

Re: Absolute Zero: Why won't it work?
Whether or not the thing your trying to cool is touching anything or not, it is impossible to reach absolute zero.

However, if for some reason you could cool something to absolute zero if there was nothing at all interacting with it, your method would not shield this thing from all possible interactions in the universe.

Even if you could create an anti-gravity device, which is currently believed to be impossible, and shield it from all light, there a still a number of things that would interact with it from neutrinos to WIMPs( if these even exist).

And if you could somehow shield this thing from all these outside influences, which would be theoretically possible, there are things that are ubiquitous throughout the entire universe which would be impossible to stop. Things such as virtual particles, dark energy and zero-point energy, which could possibly all be the same thing, are always present everywhere in the universe with no possible way to shield against, so they would always present some form of interaction with the object.
