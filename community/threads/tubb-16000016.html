My Pop, The Warlock
The 60's (or so I've heard) were a rather tumultous era, and my pop was not unexcepted into the phenomenon. He is actually pictured, with his first wife, in a book intitled: The Summer of Love. He one day plans to write a book of his experiences in the Haight Ashbury during the Summer of Love intitled Tales of Love In the Haight. Shnazy grip, huh?

Well, to get down to the point, there was an awful lot of political and social upheaval, and religious. Afterall, the 60's were following the 50's, when everyone was being convicted of being a communist and people were desperately trying to prove their humanity through their religion.

"Look. We're good, White, Christians."

Now they have a dreaded Irish Catholic as president, and well, you know the story. So, there was my pop, in the thick of it, joining a witches coven.

Growing up with a pop as warlock is... um... well... interesting. Our bookshelves with filled with books and panflits on Wicca, herbs and other flora, numerology, palm reading, telepathy, ghosts, etc... all over paranormal shpiel.

We had four family shows: Americas Most Wanted (I have actually seen family on that show), Married With Children, Are You Being Served, and Unsolved Mysteries. When Unsolved Mysteries came on, my pop would turn the volume on the television set way up so you could hear that creepy music throughout the whole neighbourhood and we'd come running from every corner of the Earth.

My childhood was filled with incantations, wild stories, and ceremonies. So, my pop and I had a falling out some years back. I won't go into a huge thing about my lousy childhood and all, but I'll tell you, I moved in with him when I was fourteen and within four months his bitch kicked me out. I was living on the streets, and I haven't forgiven him to this day. But, about a week ago he shows up, out of the blue, unnanounced (of course) like he thinks he's the Goddamn Messiah or something, and gives me this book: Teen Witch.

I have to admit. I was a bit offended. I hadn't spoken to the man in over three years and he gives me this dorky book with some over-sexed looking teenagers in front of a full moon on the cover of it. I mean, I'd grown up with this bullshit. I can read palms, I can create concotions, evoke spirits, all that jazz and he gives me this lousy book as if it's supposed to make up for three years of silence.

The book, as of now, is sitting on one of the old bookshelves. I flipped through it the other day, and despite it's almost mirthfull cover and title, it's not too bad. It has some usefull information, so I'm thinking of setting up the old shrine and all. May be that's just his way of breaking the ice. He never was too good as saying sorry.

Re: My Pop, The Warlock
First things first: I'm having a really hard time believing in the truth of this story, for one reason: you constantly refer to your dad as a "warlock". This is not a term used by witches. "Warlock" is an old Scottish word meaning, "oathbreaker", and is regarded as a dire insult. Hollywood latched onto this term, because is sounds scary, and because the stereotypical "witch" is female. Anyone who had actually studied Wicca, as you say your father did, would know this, and would not use that word to refer to himself. Witches are witches, male or female. The only exception to this I've seen is Raymond Buckland's reference to a male witch as a "Wicca" and a female witch as a "Wicce".

The only people I've ever seen refering to themselves as "warlocks" are slimy (usually, but not always, young) men with no lives who are desperately in need of attention.

Ok, now that that's said...

I can absolutely sympathize with your feelings. Your father has done you wrong in every way. I too left home at age 14 and it was very hard to survive.

It was also very callous and insensitive of your father to just show up at your door with a book, sans apology or even *recognition* of the pain you suffered. He sounds like a man with some major issues.

He's also just not a very good witch, in either sense of the phrase. Witchcraft expressly forbids forcing your religion on someone else. If I were to have children, I would naturally want them to grow up to accept my faith, because it makes me happy, but I would give them the opportunity to learn about many spiritual paths. This is the way most Pagan parent do things. It is also considered unethical to initiate a child. Only adults can make the informed choice to accept initiation.

Unfortunately, this is another one of those situations where you have no control over anyone's actions but your own. I came froma very abusive household. A lot of this abuse was centered around the Roman Catholic Faith, which I was raised in. I don't blame the Church for this abuse, but I blame them for some of the ridiculous, bigoted policies they hold.

Paganism has helped and healed me a lot. Whether it is right for you or not is purely your choice. Only you can decide that. I say, if it feels right, go with it.

Regarding your relationship with your father, I can only reiterate that the only personyou can control is yourself. So, you can't keep your dad from being a jerk. You *can* choose whether or not you wish to alow yourself to be mistreated. You have the right to exclude him from your life, if this is what you need for peace of mind.

Personally, if I were you, and he showed up at my house, I'd not open the door, especially now that it is obvious he thinks he's done no wrong. (and especially because I've heard "Teen Witch" is a lame book!) But, I burn bridges.

You might also want to consider some therapy, to help you cope with your feelings of abandonment. If economic factors prevent you, there are many agencies that offer a sliding scale pay system.

I hope you find a spiritual path (or lack thereof) that makes you, and nobody else, happy. You will know in your heart what's right.

blessed be

Lost_Girl
In point of fact you can be Wiccan and Jewish. (For that matter you can be Wiccan and Eastern Orthodox Christian or any other religion with out a conflict between either belief system.) The key is to not violate the Wiccan "Read" ("An' it harm none, do what thou wilt.") you can chouse to fallow a single god and adhere to the tenets of the faith that has grown up around that god as long as "you harm no one, including yourself". Wicca is a path of respect and learning, a way of seeing the world around you. Part of that path is learning about "God" in whatever forms you find in the religions(and prepceptions) around you and chousing the one that sets a resonance in your soul. For some people that means they chouse a Pantheon of gods just as the Greeks, Romans, and many ancient people did. For others that means studying Judaism. For both it means extensive study; to learn what your belief is, and what it is not. (For a "Pagan Wiccan" this is a bit more intensive as that person has to read a lot more. There are a lot of people out there and just because this person does it one way does not mean it should be your way.) Judaism is a rich an wondrous heritage as is Wicca even if you feel compelled to chouse one or the other let it be an informed choosing.

My heart felt advice:
1. Study and learn all you can, do not let any one book tell you how to believe. (Even in Christianity the bible is made up of several books even though we tend to see it as one.)

2. Do not let public opinion sway you away from you heart. Strength comes with knowledge; time and study will show you things you never knew, but that is no substitute to listing to your own soul.
