I was going to e-mail you to see what was going on... wish I had. :\ Sorry.

You sound... eh.. for lack of a better word, scared. Scared of what, I don't know. You enjoy the regular companionship of a relationship, but you don't want to get too close. The question is, why? Some people fear the idea of real, permanent commitment, which is what we've been trained to see relationships as, even if we know intellectually that's not realistic. Some people who haven't had the best home lives or, especially, had parents that didn't get along that well can get skittish because they don't want the same thing to happen to them. For others, it just never feels quite right.

If you've done this from day one with all your boyfriends, I'm not sure you're ready for a relationship at all, with anybody. You've been in them, and you have the knowledge of what goes into one, but that doesn't mean your maturity level is high enough to take advantage of what's in your head (which you seem to think already).

That's not quite what it sounds like, though. You said it didn't happen immediately with Ted (by the way, what kind of a fucking name is Ted?), and, although you didn't specify, I doubt it happened this way with your last boyfriend either. Something is triggering this in your head. If it happened when you moved in with this guy, that would certainly suggest you start to break down once things reach a certain level of seriousness, regardless of the fact you two definitely went way too fast.

What really sucks about this is that there's nothing that can get you ready for it besides time and patience. Being in a relationship may help, if you're with a guy that can withstand the shit you do and not fire back in kind or say the hell with it. It could also hurt, if you find yourself in a situation like this one, where the apathy and the nasty sarcasm cause definite negative effects. But if you forego them altogether until you're sure you're ready, how can you ever know when that time comes?

You're lucky enough to be a girl that can pull a guy whenever she feels the need, but you can't let that turn into a negative. You were on the right track when you said that a relationship works in its initial stages in part due to the fact that the people aren't always together, and thus have time to miss each other. You haven't had a real boyfriend in six months, but you've still gone out on the town, checked things out, hit it now and again. In short, you haven't given yourself a chance to miss guys as a whole.

If you think you're getting the idea of what I'm saying, you're right. I honestly believe you may need to take a step back and, hard as it may be, reach a point where you can exist without guys. Seriously, when was the last time you had any significant period where you weren't involved, getting laid, checking someone out, had someone checking you out (that you noticed enough for it to stick in your head), or thinking about how badly you needed to get some?

I think part of you wants to be with someone, but the other part wants to be able to say to a guy, "I don't *need* you." No one's going to care about that besides your boyfriends, though, so those are the ones who catch that half of your personality. The problem with feeling the need to prove something to someone, though, is that it means you really haven't proven it to yourself. When you have 100% confidence in your ability to do something- anything- you lose the *need* to show everyone else you can do it. You may still want to, you may still enjoy it, but without that need, it's controllable. That's not a point you're at right now. That means you have to prove to yourself that you can manage without guys, without companionship, on your own in a way that I don't think you've experienced in a long time, if ever.

What do you need to do? I don't know. How can it be accomplished? I don't know. How long will it take? I don't know. Is it worth it? I think so, but that's a decision of each of us has to make on our own. The more involved you are with the opposite sex, the harder it is to disentangle yourself from them, even if every fiber of your being says it's for the best. That's not a problem I've ever really had, so I dare not assume how difficult it might be- just how necessary.

I know there's a strong pull to get out and do shit while you can. Still... remember, you might die tomorrow, but you probably won't. There's nothing wrong with going and enjoying yourself now, while you're young, but it's worth it to keep an eye on the future, because chances are you've got a lot of life remaining. If you can devote yourself to something that will improve you dramatically, regardless of how long the road is, you may find that it's still just a fraction of the time you spend reaping the rewards of your actions.

But to each their own. If I'm even mostly correct with this, it would seem you have some decisions to make in regards to what you want to make of yourself. And if I'm mostly wrong, then I'm just a long-winded asshole. Sorry for wasting your time.
