Re: Wines for both cooking and drinking
It's really hard to recommend "brands" for wine, especially on a budget, man. I really don't know that much about wine, but i know how to choose a fairly decent bottle which i'll like; suits me, actually.

Just have a look at the bottle, aside from all the wanky "chocolate undertones with subtle truffle" bollocks, there should be some indication of how dry the wine is, which you can use to match it to whatever you're producing. A sweeter white wine might work better with some sea-food, for example. Never buy table wine or "cooking wine", they're just shit, and you wouldn't wana drink them with your meal, so why bother...? Get some semi-decent cheap stuff; $8-10 kinda range, either Californian or imported (lots of the 06 Australian/New Zealand wines are loveeeeely) or some South American wine...?

Chilean and Argentinian wine always seems pretty good to me, but bear in mind i'm usually buying a budget bottle, rarely spending over £5 or getting older than an '05 bottle. That said, i've drunk expensive wine, and clearly I don't have a "complex" palate, cos good cheap wine is hardly that inferior to the "average" expensive stuff.

Your best bet is to buy a few bottles and just sample as you cook!
