You have to hit rock bottom in order to know who you are.
These are the words of Tyler Durden. I know this because I've read the book and I've seen the movie "Fight Club."
I know who I am right now. I am Jack's overwhelming sense of failure. You know what? I am getting ahead of myself. Let's go back a bit.
Tyler and I were servers. He was a banquet server who pissed in a soup. I worked at either family restaurants or neighborhood pubs. (I've never contaminated anybody's food. So, if I ever waited on you, you're safe.) I was suddenly without a job.
This is when my fall began. This did not affect Tyler. He is a fictional character that I am using as a literary device. I am Jack, the artsy fartsy writer boy.
Tyler gave out a homework assignment. Go out and start a fight with a complete stranger. I declined. I'm trying to find a job, I don't have time for such nonsense. I stopped at four restaurants that day. Two were not hiring. One was not hiring me. The fourth, well, I got an interview.
It was at a pub on the Westside. The manager stepped up to a bar to interview me. His eyes were bloodshot. His shirt stained with sweat. His breath stank of bourbon. He kept nodding off mid-sentence. I should have left. No person with any self respect would even consider working for this man. I was all ready to say, "When do I start?" I am Jack's desperation.
He was half-way into offering me a job when he stopped and said, "Wait a minute. You ain't got tits." He vomited and passed out, leaving me at the bar alone. I sat there. Thinking. It was all making sense. I can't get a real job because I have no experience, or skills, for that matter. Now, I can't get a job waiting tables because of my gender? I could hear my psyche free falling through the air, whistling all the way down, until it landed with a crash on the rocky surface. I felt Tyler's hand on my shoulder as we looked down at my shattered dignity and the lifeless body that was my self-worth. He just smiled and said, "Congratulations."
I know who I am right now. I am Jack's overwhelming sense of failure.

Woody Rau. re-printed without permission.

Trippy, I think you missed the point of that movie. The revolution had nothing to do with anything. The reason you don't question Project Mayham is that its goals are irrelevant. Its purpose is to satisfy the desires of Tyler Durden who, though he has not quite been able to give up his self-definition based on relationship to society, has decided that said relationship need meet only his own standards and not those of society, in spite of the seeming paradox. Unfortunately, rather than complete the synthesis and abandon social identity altogether, Tyler goes into complete relapse, and then doesn't even die. What a shame.

At least that was my take on it. But sometimes I read things into stories that just reflect whatever I'm thinking at the time, especially ones as ambiguous as Fight Club. It's also been a while since I saw it. If anyone has read the book, that would probably shed some light.

Anyway, there were a lot of funny moments in that movie, regardless of its points. I think that was its best quality.
