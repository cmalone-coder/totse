Aikido is NOT a fighting art, it is purely defensive in nature. I hear some schools of yoshinkan aikido teach atemi (strikes) but I have never encountered anyone who practiced them. Aikido is very choreographed and not cut for the street. When people say martial arts are not about kicking ass they may as well be talking about aikido. Aikido is about all the reasons people study martial arts besides fighting, physical exercise, grace, coordination, beauty, cultural heritage, spirituality etcetera. You have to train it for years to be able to pull it off on the street. It is nearly useless as a self defense or combat method.

Re: was it akido?
I train in Aikido as well, and I agree that if you want the classic American "Damnit I wasnt results NOW" thing, then Aikido isn't for you. However, If you really know your Aikido I've seen some people who could take on 5 ripped guys at once and waste em all.

If you plan to discuss Aikido, however, its important to discuss the different styles of it.

The two most active in the US are:

Ki Society style: focuses on energy and meditation, yes you can kick ass with it but it will take you 70 years. On the other hand, you may find yourself at enlightenment.

Aikido Schools of Ueshiba style mine) Focuses on the movement and force of attack and how to deal with it. Adresses Aikido as a fighting art. Some people really go at each other in practice and it is seriously impressive after more than 5 years or so of training. Here you can also pick up some of the meditiation and philosophy as well as weapons, however, they aren't officially taught.
