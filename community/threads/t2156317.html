Downer Endings
I guess this can apply to any type of literature, but movies and TV are probably the main area where this is relevant.

Anyways, what do you guys think of depressing/downer/nihilistic endings? It seems to have become an obsession among movie-watchers, and whenever a serious movie has a happy or neutral ending, you have asswipes complaining that the ending wasn't dark enough. What's with the obsession with depressing endings?

I personally don't have any inherent problem with downer endings, but there's a time and a place for it. For instance, people complained about 28 Days Later because Jim survived. His death wouldn't have added anything to the film, so why on Earth would you want the main character to die? Recently I was on the message board for The Fall. A few morons wanted Roy to commit suicide in the end, despite the 2-hour journey that was all about him recovering emotionally. Not only would his death have added nothing to the film, it would have destroyed the significance of what came before it.

Downer endings can work perfectly sometimes...it just has to fit the overall tone. Like, the Dawn of the Dead remake was all about how fucked the survivors were, so the downer ending was perfectly fitting (though I do appreciate them offering a neutral/happy ending before the credits). I also don't think The Blair Witch Project would have worked half as well if it had ended on a happy note. Same with Saw...if everything turned out well and Jigsaw got arrested/killed, it wouldn't be very chilling at all.

So what do you guys think? Am I the only one that gets sick of people wanting a depressing ending for every single serious movie that comes out?
