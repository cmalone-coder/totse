The Nice Treaty
The Nice treaty is a treaty just passed in the European Union to allow 12 applicant member states to join the Union. The treaty required all of the member states to ratify it before it was passed. Ireland was the only country constitutionally obliged to have a referendum on it. The first referendum took place in 2001, which was voted no. This year, the Irish government called for a second referendum (somebody pass the democracy) which turned out to be a yes.

Why? Because the government allocated �500 million to a Yes to Nice campaign and kept all the No propoganda off the TV. Come on, people, 500 million?! We could have built a hospital or two, or raised the amount of aid given to 0.7% of the GNP (asked by of all European countries, but we're in this falsified economic downturn supposedly so we give something like 0.4%).

If you bothered to read that, you may wonder why we had to have a referendum. It's because the treaty does a lot more than expand Europe. In fact, this treaty isn't even necessary to expand Europe. The D�il (House of Commons or whatever they are in your country) said that if the people voted No to the treaty, that it would be a No to Nice and a Yes to expansion, which every No voter welcomed.

This treaty does:
- Militarises us. It is a violation of our constitution (we are militarily neutral which is why we got a referendum) but the treaty puts military matters into the EU hands.

- Makes an undemocratic share of power proportionate to population size. France, Britain, and Germany will have twice as much power while Ireland gets a measly 2.02%. And 1/4 of the time we won't even have a commissioner representing us.

- This is carefully worded so they don't say it but Europe is going to become one big superpower. Look at the signs: The power is population divided as if we were all one big country and, quote:
