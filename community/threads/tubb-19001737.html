The theory presented is :

That won't work in any vaugely human-sized body of water. For starters, electricity follows the path of the least resistance. If the water does complete the circuit the power would simply skirt the edges of the cells from the negative to positive end, and would never electrify the water.

Even before this happens, however, it should be noted that fresh water with no salt is actually a rather poor conductor. It takes many thousands of volts and/or a good amperage behind it to get electricity though any useful amount of fresh water, and you aren't goting to get either of those from a bundle of AA batteries.

Re: Battery Bomb: does it work?
It only takes one amp to kill a person with straight-through zero resistance. You need lots of voltage to push those amps through human skin, even if it's wet.

American households have two (?) phase 120 volt power, usually topping out around 100 amps. Most circut breakers and fuse boxes in residential settings are set at 75, 65, or even 50 amps. This will be your limiting factor. Even if you bypass the breaker you will likely set the wiring on fire with a full force no-load setup like this.

There are usually three (?) phase 240 volt outlets in at least once place in the house for plugging in laundry machines, electric furnaces, and refidgerators. I know not what sort of amperage is behind these.

See "Avioding Electrocution" at Dan's Data: http://www.dansdata.com/gz013.htm
