Re: Are we really free?
Freedom, when used alone, is such a vague term. But a vague term with many positive connotations. This is why it is used by people like Mussolini in speeches to persuade people.

Persuasion, being a form of power, leads me to my next point: there is really only freedom from or to things. This fits in to that whole sociological study of power started by Nietzsche, which really flourished with Michel Foucault. The French word for power is "pouvoir", which means "to be able". So as you can see, power and freedom, especially freedom to, are closely linked.

But I think what you are talking about when you ask your question, which is incredibly vague, is something along the lines of Jean-Paul Sartre, who would answer, and I would agree, that we are truly free to choose, but not to not choose because even not to choose is to choose. Yet with this freedom to choose, comes the burden of responsibility. People often state "I was just following orders", but they chose to follow orders, so there's no excuses... of course, this is what I thought when I read the title, your actual post seems to be more political, lol.

I'm just rambling openly here, so excuse me if I've gotten off topic.
