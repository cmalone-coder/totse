What totse is all about...
From the things Jeff has said or written about the original ethos of the site, it's meant to be a place where anybody can put their ideas across and share their knowledge and information, not a place where their input can be approved or denied based on grounds of 'worthiness'. If there's any screening process I'm sure it's just there to weed out advertisments or pure spam.

When I originally set up totse the web did not exist. Text file BBSes were one of the few ways that ANYONE could get their message across to the masses. (Well, the masses of techno-geeks anyhow.)

Now that anyone can set up a web page / BLOG / MySpace page / YouTube video there really isn't much of a lack of ways to get your message out there, although some hosting companies take a dim view of the type of material we have here and sites that carry it often get shut down.

The original purpose was to get as many ideas out there as possible to force people to think for themselves and not accept whatever they were spoon-fed by the mass media.

Well, now the web IS the mass media, and there are a million different views on a million different web sites with millions more added daily.

So what is the purpose of the site, now that the mass media mirrors totse?

So what is the purpose of the site, now that the mass media mirrors totse?

Full-on sales pitch, and I have to pretend as if someone besides Jeff Hunter posed this question:

The purpose of this site is to serve as a gathering place, or a nerve center, if you will - of like-minded human beings hell bent on expressing their individual ideas. It is a two-sided coin. One one side, it is somewhat of a relic in that it houses a ridiculously large database of text files on just about any given subject of interest (or disinterest). These articles defy the usual shelf life of internet missives in that it constantly ebbs and flows right alongside the course of public awareness, revolving around the alternative philosophies of different people, from different places, going in different directions. While still preserving the ideas of yore, of trends long passed and conspiracies long solved - it serves as a reminder of the constantly changing states of minds that pass through quickly but are logged seemingly forever in cyberspace.

On the flip side, it's the fresh, brand-new face of a collective consciousness that is very much alive and teeming with it's own brand of community awareness. It lives, it breathes, it feeds on the ideas expressed within the confines of it's forums. Most noteably, it has a way of making passers-through covet the privelage of belonging to a community that, anonymity be damned, seems to remain as cohesive and unpenetrable as the fiercest of cults. The guise of anonymity encourages a level of online disinhibition that allows it's posters to speak freely and openly, exposing the attitude and perception of today's young visionaries.

The mass media only attempts to mirror Totse, but Totse has the advantage of mirroring the very finger that sits on the pulse of the current generation's views on The Real World. That kind of thing is so raw, so visceral, so not-made-for-public consumption - that the mass media fails to capture the true mindset of it's younger, thinking generation. The media en masse is forever tethered to the obligation of presenting a shiny, happy, neatly-wrapped package to the rest of society that gives a somewhat glossed-over version of what people are thinking. Totse embodies the real, the ugly and the malformed views of people who insist on having a voice.

And it doesn't make any apologies for doing so.
