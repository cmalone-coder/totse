Re: Drugs to help me be less nervous.
If you want to feel less nervous, I can think of few methods that match the effectiveness of drugs. Feeling nervous, or experiencing fear, is an event that triggers a certain state; you're right to look to drugs since they remove you from the state itself.

From personal experience, I can recommend Adderall (dextroamphetamine) and Xanax (alprazolam). A stimulant, Adderall speeds up cognitive function and heightens the concentration of neuotransmitters in your brain, as well as triggers the 'fight or flight' response. This often manifests as increased confidence, willingness to socialize, desire to speak and happiness. It operates differently than Xanax, a benzodiazapene used to treat anxiety and panic disorders. Xanax effectively removes the ability for you to worry about things, invoking a dreamy, confident and care-free state. I would rate both as being highly effective.

Re: Drugs to help me be less nervous.
Xanaxs, klonopines, pot, alcohol.

Weed never made me feel less nervous in public, no matter the amount or quality. When you're borderline agoraphobic the last thing you need in a public place is a drug that makes you paranoid.

I'm a former benzo fiend and a fairly experienced drinker and I still believe that low-dosed amphetamines/stimulants have helped my anxiety more than anything. 30-40 mg of Adderall with a 150 mg Bupropion chaser takes the garbled clusterfuck of problems and worries in my head and lets me approach it piece by piece, which is a lot more effective than trying to cope with ten different trains of thought at one time. I also feel more confident in what I say in conversations, which basically eliminates my social anxiety completely. If ADD is actually a real thing, I'm pretty sure I have it.

I don't get that effect from all stimulants, though; caffeine usually makes the mind-racing worse and makes me feel jittery and spastic when taken in even moderate amounts (~200 mg), for example.

OP, it will benefit you greatly if you can break down the key parts of your anxiety and understand what causes it. Generalized anxiety is different from social anxiety, which is different from full-blown panic disorder, and so on and so forth.
