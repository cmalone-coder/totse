Okay, let me try to address my concerns with the available theories thus far. In regards to the "Dots Theory," what you people claim may play a role, but assuming that dots will transform when you're hallucinating is one thing, and could very well be true. This does not allow one to conclude, however, that the dots should always be represented as spiders. There are many things the mind could jump to when seeing a dot while hallucinating, other bugs not being the least likely option. And yet, you never see fields of ants or flies. Also, the dots theory may fail to explain how some of us see entire armies of spiders rather than individuals.

In regards to the "Primal Fear Theory," you need to read up a bit more on psychology and philosophy (Kant) to see that this is unlikely. You're assuming that we are genetically hardwired to fear spiders. This is very, very improbable. Very few things are included in the human mind instinctually beyond the reaction to pull your hand out of the fire when it burns you. For a trait to become so widespread, it would have to start as a random mutation in some of the population. Then, it would have to have evolutionary value, so that those with the trait of the fear of spiders would survive, while all others died. Then, it would have to be dominant, so that it could continue to be passed on in every situation, even after society progressed to the point that lacking a fear of spiders was no longer a death sentence.

However, when the "Primal Fear Theory" touches on our fear of the dark, this is completely different. This may indeed be instinctually hardwired into the human mind via genetics and evolution. This is almost certainly why all human beings are diurnal; it served such great evolutionary purpose at one point that it became the dominant genetic trait for our entire species.

Thinking it over, I can't offer a much better solution. Keep in mind that my understanding of the brain structuring in this idea is very limited, and I'd love for someone with a bit more knowledge on the subject to take a crack at it:

Okay, can we assume that there is a specific region in the brain responsible for storing visual memories? Probably. Can we assume that this region may be further subdivided into areas that store visuals of certain different types, and that the structuring of this region may be relatively common between all human beings? I don't know, but let's say yes and keep going. Perhaps then, there is a certain area of the brain that is destined to hold the memory of what a spider looks like. Obviously, this data must be stored somewhere in order for us to recall the concept of a spider when we next see one. I'm just taking that a step further on the hypothetical that there is a specific physical location in the brain where all human beings store the concept and image of a spider.

I don't know exactly how diphen works in the brain in order to cause the delerium hallucinations that make it infamous on sites like totse, but we can assume that, like all other recreational substances, it acts on a specific set of chemicals in the brain. Lets say that these chemicals were fucking around in a physical location in the brain right near the location where we store the concept of a spider. Perhaps somehow, just through chance, diphen happens to trigger the visual cue for spider in most people. If the area that diphen acted upon was positioned slightly differently, perhaps we would receive images of bunny rabbits instead.

Yeah, my theory is pretty shaky at the moment. I'll do a little research and see if I can back it up or find another alternative, but like I said, I'm not buying the "Dots Theory" or the "Primal Fear Theory." While either or both of these may have some merit in describing contributing factors, neither (nor the combination of the two) has the ability to adequately describe this phenomena.
