Backyard Ballistics This forum is dedicated to the safe teaching and responsible handling of high and low explosives. You will not find any information on how to make "bombs" or any such device(s) . Backyard ballistics will teach you the science and the art of pyrotechnics and how to manufacture and use explosives/pyrotechnics safely and responsibly. Backyard ballistics is also a haven for rocketry and pneumatic cannons. All information contained within this forum is for educational purposes ONLY. Well, I started out working with neutron induced chain-reaction explosions, which was then later related to the critical mass of fissionable materials. This was a tough point in my life because I/we couldn't figure out how to sustain the reaction at a constant level. I, then, with the help of several lab partners, had recieved several large (one half - one pound) samples of U-235 (natural), but then realized that the material could not, by itself, hold a sustaining reaction as easily as U-238 could have, but I still had to use the 235. After a few months, I/we decided to go for it, and a partner got around 300 lbs. of 235. There was a deadline brought to me, so I had to turn the 235 into 25% enriched 235, irradiated, produced by electromagnetic seperation. I really can't get into the details, but we finally ended up detonating 108 tons of Comp. B, while standing about 3025 ft. away. That is my basic starting point.
