Re: iso propyl in the microwave ok?
lol, ok, well I suppose I exaggerated a bit. The way you do it is right, short bursts. But if left on steady it very well could heat up to the solvents flash point, which means all those vapors as well as the liquid will ignite. It's the vapors that getcha though. Flammable vapors in the right combination can actually DDT, which stands for deflagration to detonation transition. In other words, it starts to burn, and since the ratio is JUST RIGHT the acceleration of the burn gets so fast that it exceeds roughly 340 m/s and it is then classified as a detonation, though the actual velocity of detonation can be much higher than just 340 m/s.

And when microwaving solvents the REAL risk is stray ignition sources, which is what happened to me. I won't say what I was doing, but I was nuking some MEK, and didn't even think about the pilot light on my stove being on. Well the fumes built up, leaked out, and found their way right to the pilot light, and I saw the flame travel from the oven to my microwave, and though there was only a whoomph, the fireball was fucking huge, and it blew the door open. The liquid inside stayed lit.

It was really no big deal, as I was kind of expecting SOMETHING to happen (again with flash point) so I was prepared for it, and it didn't damage anything, and I blew out the pilot light and went right back to work.


But for evapping ISO you need to just put it on top of a running TV or computer monitor or some other electronic device, and the radiant heat from that device, with a fan, will quickly evaporate it.

Re: iso propyl in the microwave ok?
You've never seen someone put out a lit match in a cup of gas? It's the vapors, not the actual liquid that burns.


Think of it chemically. In order for something to burn, it must have oxygen present right? Well, there is no O2 in gas, so it won't ignite. BUT if you were to hold it above the gas, while the vapors come off, mixing in with air, it will light, and then the reason it STAYS lit is because it's just the vapors coming off the gas as if it were not lit, but the heat makes it evap faster, burning faster and more strongly etc.

Each gasoline molecule must be in contact with an oxygen atom in order to burn. Just like flash powder, too little oxidizer, and it doesn't really burn, OR if it is not mixed well enough. It's all about surface area and intimate contact.


Fire needs oxygen. There is no oxygen at the bottom of a cup of gas, hence, no fire.

Now if you were to take gasoline and mix it in the proper ratios with air it would explode. Hmmm, maybe there is a way we could harness that power for locomotion.
