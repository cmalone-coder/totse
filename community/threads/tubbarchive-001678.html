Re: Required Reading for Humans (Second Edition)
Mook's List (prepare to be shocked and awed):


  1. Ulysses by James Joyce
    Probably everybody on this forum has heard one of my rants on how amazing and profound this book is--it's easily my favorite of all time, and this is mostly due to the fact that it's probably the most complex book ever written. I am a reader who likes being challenged by the books that he reads, and Ulysses is, to me, the ultimate literary challenge--I know it is a real possibility that I may never fully understand it, and that is why I like it so much.
  2. The Catcher in the Rye by JD Salinger
    A lot of people view this as a "required HS reading" book and dismiss it at that. I cannot protest against this view more vehemently; Holden Caulfield is one of the most identifiable characters, and probably one of the greatest modern heroes, in literature. Catcher in the Rye is one of the few books that has brought me to tears, though as of my 2nd reading I still can't pinpoint exactly why. Third is the charm, as they say.
  3. As I Lay Dying by William Faulkner
    Faulkner has a way of making the characters' situations at the end of the story so FUBAR you're left with your mouth gaping. This is no exception. As I Lay Dying is probably the most emotionally developed novel I have ever read--this is made even more impressive by its length; for instance, the character Jewel only has one passage, but it is instantly clear in 3 pages exactly what his emotions are on the plot, no questions asked.
  4. Heart of Darkness by Joseph Conrad
    I am a teacher's aide for our school's AP Literature teacher; this year she told me her class wouldn't be reading Heart of Darkness because too many kids complained that it was "too long." This just goes to show high school kids are morons. In the first place, Conrad has the most beautiful prose of almost any author, in my opinion. Second, this is one of the most nuanced and abstract novels that I have ever read--like I said above, I love challenge, and Conrad packing a highly developed theme into some 90 pages, almost completely through descriptive narrative, is a challenge.
  5. ...


More later.
