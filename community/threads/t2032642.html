Suggestions for Jeff and moderators (long)
Vorsicht! Long post! :eek: If you do not have time to read this now, don't. But at some point come back and read it all the way through. There are many things in here that concern everyone. To aid you, I organized my post into bullet points and divided it into the following sections: Upper administration (Jeff), Lower administration (moderators), and What changes can be made (or something like that).


So I make lists. It's how I get things done and it's how I organize my work. While on vacation I compiled a list of things that were annoying me on the forums. I am going to try and be as blunt and honest about everything as possible because I don't want there to be any misunderstandings on how I feel. I am speaking on the behalf of members and moderators who've emailed me in the past about many of the issues to follow.

I do NOT want to make it seem like I'm trying to overthrow anyone or kick anyone out. I'm not out to get or attack anyone. I'm just somewhat disappointed with how things have turned out lately and I feel that the only way to get things back the way they were is to be entirely honest about how I feel. Because I did not want to leave anything out of this list, I emailed my feelings to a few moderators ahead of time since they had previously come to me about many of these issues - my goal in that was NOT to leave others out of this. Anyway, here goes:

Upper administration:

Sorry to pick on you Jeff...


As of lately it seems that anyone can request to be moderator of anything (in the admin forum) and Jeff will approve. I don't think that we should just add mods whenever someone asks and let them stay indefinitely. We might as well just make everyone a mod of everything if all they have to do is ask. Currently we have 89 moderators, some of whom are moderating up to 4 forums.

Usually when I've added mods in the past I at least make sure the addition is OK with the current mods of that forum. Then I decide whether the addition of new mods is actually necessary. Currently there seems to be little to no screening process outside of the complaints and faint suggestions that the current moderators put forth (if they can get their words in before the person is made a mod).

Jeff adds unwanted forums arbitrarily without acknowledging the wanted ones (it was more than a month after cellular mods - which wasn't requested - was added that Hello World finally appeared). I don't have a problem with new forums at all, I actually think it's a pretty good idea sometimes. But such changes are usually made without asking the current moderators whether they think it would work much less telling them that the changes are going to be made.

Jeff isn't around enough to make the wanted and needed changes to the BBS and hasn't given anyone else access to make such changes (adding and removing moderators and occasionally adding or removing forums). I've researched the issue on vbul and it is actually pretty easy to make new admin groups that have limited permissions - It is not necessary to grant someone full administrative privileges in order for them to perform the said functions. The permissions that would be especially useful would be adding and removing moderators and forums. In fact, there are a number of threads in this forum right now that are asking for moderators to be added or removed - some of which have been here a while.

Totse is constipated. We always say, "oh, well we can always undo the changes" when we add new forums or mods. Unfortunately, nobody ever wants to remove them when the time comes. People whine and complain about undoing the said changes because they A) don't want to be demodded, B) Don't want to see their friends demodded, or C) don't want to get rid of their forum (because it will result in them being demodded). People come to rely on the changes. Eventually we end up with too many mods to keep track of and too lots of low traffic forums. We have a total of 89 moderators (if I counted correctly) which is 1 moderator for every 400 users (about). Some forums have too many moderators. Spurious Generalities and Games People Play both have an inordinate amount of moderators - SG might actually need that many, but that's debatable elsewhere.

Acidmelt and I have been almost single handedly running the forums for the past few years (outside of major technical issues which Jeff usually fixes). I have been posting constantly for the last seven years. Honestly, I can count on one hand the number of threads I've seen Jeff make outside of NOTT and M&A. I know a person's posting record has little to do with how much they visit the forum and how much they care about it, but over the last few years (especially before the vbul upgrade) I've gotten a remarkable number of emails asking whether or not Jeff even knows totse still exists. Sometimes I don't know whether I should be telling people yes or no.

On that note: Jeff, I'm not complaining, I'm requesting - My request is that you acknowledge the massive amount of time, care, and dedication that Acidmelt and I both share for his forum by including us in more decisions and allowing us to make our own as well. I know you're probably got a very busy life outside of totse but we don't. If you let us, I am more than sure we can run things smoothly.

Archives need to be approved, edited, deleted, or denied by more than one person. A long time ago JC recruited me to find a list of moderators and active posters who would be good for this sort of thing. I found about a half dozen or so. Since then, JC has disappeared and the list became outdated. I'm sure that we could find more than triple that if we were to count heads again today.

Lower administration:


Mods are over-moderating (I know, I complain about this all the time, but...) - I don't know whether people are power hungry or whether they just like to break the rules, but I've seen plenty of moderators edit posts (that didn't need to be edited), close threads, and threaten users simply to make a statement. That statement is: I'm the moderator, listen to me or I'll put you in your place. - That's not how you moderate. A moderator is not special or more significant than anyone else on the forum. The only reason we have moderators is because we feel they know the most about the subject matter and what does and doesn't fit into the forum. Because they have that knowledge and experience, they have the capability to make changes when needed. They are a poster first and a moderator second. I know sambob already posted about this, but I figured I'd say it here too since it was bothering both of us.

Nobody's been getting the "New moderator talk" because there have been so many moderator additions in the last few months. I've seen people asking questions as simple as "How do I know when to close a thread in my forum?", "Should I move this to another forum?", and "where is the close thread button?" Which tells me a few things: A) Nobody told them how to do their job, B) they don't post in the forum enough to know what does and what does not fly (the latter of which raises the question: why are they a moderator?). I started writing a guide to supplement Wintermute's moderating guide before I went on vacation. I'll post that as soon as I'm done, but in the mean time people need to stop worrying about doing a bad job and just do it. Don't be afraid to ask questions either. If you don't know, I'm sure someone else here does. On a lighter note: People seem to be getting much better about this. No longer as big of an issue.

Some moderators are moderating way too many sections. Some are moderating up to 4 different forums on the site (high traffic forums, at that). Frankly, I'm just not convinced that anyone is fit to handle that much work. Even if you do have the time for it, it is quite easy to burn out on the content. After moderating the same forum for a few months, it's a lot harder to have that same touch that you did when you first started. You post less, visit the forum less, and when you do visit it's only to make everyone remember that you're still around - usually by doing a lot of moderation all at once. I've seen it happen to a LOT of people (Snoopy said I could use him as an example of this). I'm not saying that it happens to everyone, but I would rather see a person moderate 2 forums really well than 4 forums shittily.

A lot of moderators are suggesting themselves or others for moderating forums that they rarely post in. Yes, they often times know a lot about the forum and it's often a good addition, but as I said earlier in this list: One must be a poster before they are a moderator. You can't start moderating a forum after never having posted there (or only posted rarely) and expect instant respect from the posters there.

There's a lot of bickering, flaming, and general disrespect occuring in this forum (Mods and Admins). I honestly don't care if people hate eachother (or me), just as long as we can all work together. I don't really mind if people dislike each or don't get along very well, but when they tangent in the middle of another thread about something else in order to do it, I think it's out of line. It seems that after a certain point, every thread turns into: A) some sort of argument, B) spam (no, not ads, just useless and irrelevant posting). I'm not really sure whether this is even worthy of complaining about, but someone raised the point that nobody would allow regular posters to do that in their forums, so why should mods be allowed to do it to each other in M&A? Thread hijacking happens in M&A more than anywhere else on totse and it always ends up in an argument while everyone ignores the initial concern of the thread.

Things that can be done immediately to help correct some of the problems above:


Some forums simply have too many moderators. Spurious and Games People Play especially. There is also a slew of posts in M&A requesting moderator changes (people quitting, mostly).

We need to have a more thorough process for assigning moderators to forums. We must decide whether these forums need that many moderators as well as whether the moderator is qualified for the forum. Currently there is no process for that, it seems. As one of the other moderators mentioned to me: "I could moderate any forum I wanted, all I have to do is ask in M&A and BAM! It's done regardless of whether I post there or know anything about it."

When new moderators ARE added, the person who suggested adding them should give them a rundown of how things on that forum work, when to close/move posts, and most importantly: ask questions! I also suggest that we post an official moderation guide in this forum or use Wintermute's.

Changes that should be considered for the future:


Since Jeff is not able to be at totse 24/7, the forum might benefit from having someone who is around more often to do things like add and remove moderators and forums when necessary.

A team or system should be set up for editing incoming articles and managing old ones. I don't know whether this would require new software or what, but I think it is worth investigating.
