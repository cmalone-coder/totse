It says, more or less, that a well regulated militia is necessary to the security of a free state, so the government shall not infringe on the right to keep and bear arms. Some people get hung up on the "well regulated" part and say, "well, we've already got one of those; It's called the army." But, a militia is by definition a group of armed citizens not part of a regular army. There was considerable disagreement among the Founding Fathers as to how the militia should be regulated. Hamilton suggests in Federalist 29 that the officers of the militia should be appointed by the states. Others argued that the government should have no part in the regulation of the militia. Certainly "well regulated" means the militia will be disciplined and effective. This does not necessarily mean regulated by the government. I'll get back to this in a minute. The other key part of this is the word "free." They could have just as easily written "necessary to the security of a state," but "free" was deliberately included. That's because the Founding Fathers of the United States of America realized that an armed citizenry is the most effective check against tyrrany by the government (Hamilton was notable in his disagreement, but this was rooted in the fact that Hamilton did not support a standing army. So, any oppression going on would be done by the militia which Hamilton didn't believe could happen because the militia men were just everyday guys.)
