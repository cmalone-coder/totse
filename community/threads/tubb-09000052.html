From a more scientific standpoint:

The Theory of Relativity:
Given: An object with mass with respect to a point.

As the object's speed approaches that of the speed of light, it's length approaches zero, its mass approaches infinite, and its time approaches infinite.

Therefore, from the point, you will "see" an object moving close to the speed of light get shorter, more massive, and it will age less in the same amount of time.

From the point of view of the object, its time passes as normal and everything around it passes more slowly.

If light did not go the speed of light, it would be hard to see, and here's why:
A photon has no mass, but it does have a lifespan, and that lifespan is extremely short. Less than a second. How does light get from stars thousands of light years away??? To the photon, its lifespan is as short as it should be, but to us, it lives forever because relative to us, its time is infinite.

To clear another thing up: It is not practically impossible to go the speed of light, it is impossible.
Unless you know of a force that equals infinite. An object with an infinite mass takes an infinite amount of force to change its velocity. Therefore as you get faster, it becomes harder and harder to go faster. You can approach the speed of light, but never reach it.

To touch on time travel, consider time a dimension, that as we know it, we travel in, in a straight line. To travel in time, we would not just *blink* and appear where we wanted to be, we would have to accelerate our time vector. Therefore, if one were to watch a clock from a "time machine" we would see its hands slow down and go backwards until we appeared where we were aiming for, at which time they would again slow down and speed up to 'normal'.

To accelerate a mass you need a force, to accelerate an object through time, you need a force that affects time. As we know it, time has a momentum(sub time) that we cannot affect. Too bad, but an interesing idea is it not?
