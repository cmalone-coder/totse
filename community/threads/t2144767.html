2010 Camaro
http://www.popularmechanics.com/blog...s/4274421.html

So far, it will only come with the Alloytec 3.6 and the LS3 engine, with 306 and 396 hp, respectively.

Style-wise, I don't think it looks quite agressive enough to be persuasive, being that the car is so large and tall. It looks similar to the RX8 from the front, but the looks just don't cut it from a side shot.

The gauges and interior from afar look decent, although nothing to scream about. I see that GM is still in love with that damn plastic, though. Jeez, can't we get some rubbery plastic instead of the hard stuff? At least I don't hurt my elbow when I rest in my Fiero.

Performance sounds pretty good, and almost on par with the Charger SRT-8. I wouldn't look down much on the 3.6L since it should have VVT when it makes its way into the Camaro and GM has already got the block over the 1000 pony milestone. I really, really hope GM doesn't try to sell the Camaro with the Ecotec in it unless they are going with a twin-charge setup. Even then the car weighs too damn much.

Although it is nice to see that the Alloytec and LS3 has fuel economy measures built it, I am saddened greatly that there is no focus on smaller and faster cars. In the 70s and 80s when we had fuel crises the automotive manufacturers buckled down and started producing minimalist cars. The Fiero was quite sporty for its time, got almost 40 mpg, was quite cheap to build and to buy, and had the cleanest emmisions of any American-made car in 1984. Even in the early 90's, Oldsmobile's 4-cylinder rocket was able to get nearly that much milage and had far more power than the stock Ecotecs that GM uses today.

I suspect that we won't see any neo-Metros (lol pun) until after the Volt is firmly established as either a winner or loser. There won't be any ultra-efficient cars to come from GM for another year or so because they aren't going to give the Volt a few months from the factory before there are announcements for a more efficient car, especially with consumers clamoring for milage. GM probably already have plans to make small cars to American standards, and hopefully even import smaller Opel engines with turbos slapped on them. Jeez, just give me a sporty, RWD car that has great gas milage and good performance....

Actually the peak figures*/** (which might or might not be conservatively rated) of the three currently available engine options are the following:

1) 300hp & 273ft lbs for the direct injected version of the 3.6L HFV6

2) 422hp & 408ft lbs for the 6.2L LS3 V8 (It's rival, the Challenger, is claimed to be 425hp - but is also much heavier)

3) 400hp & 395ft lbs for the 6.2L L99 V8



I want them to drop in the LS9 in either 550hp Cadillac, or 638hp ZR1 trim, because it has been confirmed that it will be offered for sale Down Under, and 422hp just doesn't cut it down here..

*No, I don't like comparing cars using peak figures either, but for this purpose it's the best method we have, and the statistics are always available. It's rare that a manufacturer will give you dyno graphs of their cars.

** These aren't third party SAE certified figures as of yet, but I doubt that what you posted were - it's way too early to be seeing SAE numbers.
