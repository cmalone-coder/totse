This quote, however, is full of failure. First off, if you've been taking Adderall to focus, and you have a legitimate ADD problem, it is highly unlikely that stepping down to methylphenidate with keep you functioning at "finals of my last semester at college" levels. Secondly, methylphenidate has terrible side effects and drawbacks compared to Adderall; you don't focus as well, you feel more jittery, you're not as social, you don't feel like God (ever), you're not affected by the medication for as long, you don't eat all day and then eat at night (I dunno, this made me sick), you still don't always sleep right (and you can't stay up to cram), and most importantly you feel depressed, exhausted and generally like total shit after it suddenly stops working sooner than you needed it to. The comedown off of methylphenidate, on a daily basis, was actually starting to make me feel manic, something Adderall has never done. And I mean, if it was any good, why isn't there a street market for its recreational use (sans studying)? It's because Ritalin blows balls.

My only advice to OP is: take stupid noob's advice. Pick a date, a time and a fucking rationale for quitting, and stick to them. Addiction is shitty, and if you don't wanna check into rehab or counseling, you've gotta man up if you're gonna beat it alone. When you do quit, set aside several weeks to let everything get out of your system, and avoid any possible people/places/scenarios where you could even come in contact with it. Lock yourself in your house, pick a new hobby, read a book.. whatever it takes to get your mind off the withdrawal and psychological dependence.

By the way, it sounds like you might have had an incident of amphetamine psychosis, which is a very bad thing. If that's the case, even I might agree that you need to cut back; the problem arises when, in the throws of addiction, maintaining a normal dosage is guaranteed to result in imminent relapse. Should you (honestly) think that you can't take your minimum daily dosage without regression into abusive patterns, do yourself a favor and cut it out long enough to allow your physiology to get over your habit.
