A lot of English ales have a very nice rich strong flavour. They aren't necessarily dark so don't think that anyone drinking a light-ish coloured beer is a poncy lagerboy! Also don't discount 'bitters', they are strong pale ales - I imagine 'bitter' is fairly unheard of in the US - could someone verify this?

My personal favourite ales are:
Yorkshire Terrier (you won't find this outside of... Yorkshire... so no help to you I guess)
Spitfire
Newcastle Brown Ale
Old Bob (you won't find this outside of East Anglia)

Some common ales/english beers that you should be able to find in a good off licence in the US are:
Guinness
Spitfire
Newcastle Brown
IPA
Old Speckled Hen
Bishop's Finger
Old Peculier (yes, I did spell that right)
Boddington's

People dont use the term bitters, but plenty of pale ale is made in the US, more than in the UK actually, and MANY MORE varieties.

In fact the American style of pale ale (APA: American Pale Ale) is MORE 'bitter' than the IPA's and basically any kind of bitter prefered in the UK. But just like many Americans dont know the term 'bitter', I bet most peple in the UK don't know that! :P

And as for UK beers in the US, Spitfire.. less likely. New castle yes. Some others are, Greene King IPA, Welss Bombardier (in a bottle if possible, as the nitro cans are terrible!), Tetleys (not flavorful, but popular).

Some more favorites of mine are, Young's Double Chocolate Stout, Samuel Smith Oatmeal Stout, Dorothy Goodbody's Stout, and many others.. but I feel like I should mention MURPHY'S stout as its very popular in the US too.
Some of those I would have to disagree with. Boddingtons for example, though they call it 'pub ale' or 'bitter', its well, not. ITs smooth and mild tasting,very little hop flavor, and despite the low ABV you can taste the alcohol - weird!
