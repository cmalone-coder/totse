Determinism Vs. Libertarianism
Do you think that all our moral decisions are pre-determined, and we have no real choice in them? Or do you think that consciousness involves something more than a chain of cause and effect, and we are free in our actions?

I fully side with determinism. Whether our thoughts are the product of genes or our experiences or both, they must be predestined. I'm not saying they could practically be predicted, because that would obviously be incredibly complicated, but everything we do must be pre-destined. The experience of choice is an illusion - there's a lot of evidence to suggest that our decisions are instantaneous, and the reasoning 'behind' them is made up afterwards.

What are your thoughts? I wanted to make this a poll but I couldnt work out how

I don't see why people can't reconcile this. Just because the decisions we make aren't predetermined doesn't mean we don't make decisions. Why would the experience of choice be an illusion? You consider your options and make a decision, and that decision determines what you will do. That the process of consideration is deterministic is irrelevant; it still occurs. The only 'illusionary' aspect would be the idea that either outcome is somehow abstractly possible, but that's just a way of conceptualizing the process. What matters is that before you make your decision you don't know what you will do, and though you yourself are hypothetically a product of deterministic systems, you still make choices.

However, there is also evidence to suggest that this is not the case, and we must consider the fact that indeterminacy does not equal freedom, as someone else pointed out.
