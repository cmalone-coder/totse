Re: Shaped charges
"......That would mean the best place to put a detonater (or fuse if you're using a primary) would be behind the point of the cone, right? ......"

Right. At the top of the charge and perfectly centered. In general, when designing your shaped charge, how well it performs its intended role will be dependent on several factors....

1) Design. A badly made shaped charged performs as well as it was designed (meaning gold=gold & shit=shit). The cone (liner) should be perfectly aligned with the body of the device and the explosive should be packed to a uniform density with no air pockets (a liquid explosive would likely have a perfect uniformity regarding density).

The detonation should begin at the exact rear center of the charge.

The quantity of explosives used would be based on the height of the cone/liner... meaning the thickness of the charge (from the top of the cone to the top of the charge) should be equal to or greater than the height of the cone.

2) The Explosives Detonation Velocity. The more powerful the explosive, the better. A detonation velocity of 6,000 meters per second or greater is recommended, but anything with a det velocity of 3,000 mps up to 6,000 mps can be used... the shaped charge just won't penetrate as much (or as well) than if you used a better explosive.

3) Containment/Confinement. An explosive with a det velocity of 6,000 mps + won't need any extraordinary confinement. A tin can should suffice. But anything with a lower det velocity than that will need strong sidewall confinement, such as a steel pipe.

4) Liner. As was said earlier, copper is considered the optimum cone/liner material, but mild steel, aluminum, and glass is just as serviceable.

Preferably the cone will have an apex angle between 30 and 60 degrees (45 degrees is a good compromise). Cone thickness should be uniform and the shape should be proportional (round) from the top (apex) to the bottom (mouth) of the cone.

5) Standoff. As was stated earlier, a standoff should be used for maximum performance. Optimum standoff distance between the base of the charge/cone and the target can be from 3/4 to 1 1/2 times the diameter of the cone/liner.
