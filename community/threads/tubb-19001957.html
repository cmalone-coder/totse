To boil, or not to boil. My H2O2 experiment.
I've been making AP a lot recently, and I've been wondering how boiling the H2O2 affects the yields. I just started an experiment to see. I'm making 2 batches of AP, side by side. Both batches used 30mL of acetone and 16mL of sulfuric acid. I know I'm adding a bit more acetone and acid than reccomended, but I have ample supply. One batch uses 200mL of standard 3% H2O2. The other uses 20mL of H2O2, boiled down from 200mL of 3%. So far I have only gotten to the point of cooling my acetone/H2O2 mixture. I'll let you know what the results of this little experiment are in a day or two.

Notes:
-I poured the acetone into the H2O2 while it was still really hot and I'm pretty sure some of it reacted. It turned a little bit white. I guess I have dicycloacetone peroxide. Remind me not to do that again.
-It took quite a long time to boil the H2O2 down.
-The boiled down H2O2 has a yellowish tint.
-The batch with the boiled H2O2 kind of sizzled as I added acid.
-The heat spike that happens with the addition of the acid happened much more rapidly in the batch with the boiled H2O2.

Re: To boil, or not to boil. My H2O2 experiment.
If you don't have a strong acid, do not bother. The H2S04 I use is sold as 'Masterjack' drain opener. In the fine print it says it's sulfuric acid. DrainO brand is NaOH, a strong base. If you mix the H2SO4 and the NaOH, you get a bunch of gas, so it might make a good pressure bomb. Anyway here's a test to see if something you have is actual H2SO4: put ~1 teaspoon of sugar into a small glass and pour a few mL of your substance on the sugar. If it's sulfuric acid (or some other powerful dehydrating agent), the sugar will turn into a big blob of carbon after a minute or so.
