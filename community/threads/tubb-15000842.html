quote:
Originally posted by bkc:
I started to read this, but got stuck right here. I don't understand this and find it difficult to keep reading when I don't understand the premise. Does everybody get this? Want to explain it?


It means that throughout history man has always looked to metaphysically oriented philosophy (god precisely) for the meaning of life. Over the 17th and 18th centuries philosophers had debated this very idea, and existentialism is born of the idea that it is actually impossible to know truth about man, and that metaphysics (god) is really a human invention. Thus humans are "free" from judgement in their actions, and responsible for them in the sense that no "master plan" is accessible from which to dictate right and wrong modes of living. Hence our existence, the FACT that we exist, is primary, and our essence (how we define reality, through god or whatever) can only follow this primary fact. Hence while prior it was thought that "man is created in the image of god", Existentialism sees the idea of god as created by man, as a justifier of man - his essence. Existentialism means we are free of any obligation in our behavior to religion or other systems of thought that seek to predetermine man's path in life (in a fatalistic manner).
