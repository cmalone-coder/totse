Sniper rifle question
I have often noticed a specific trait of Sniper Rifles that seams to be a sort of accuracy rating of the weapon, but have yet to figure out what it stands for or how it is Measured.

That rating is measured in "MOA" for example "1/4 MOA"

Can any of you explain this rating system to me?
 #2 
2002-07-11, 12:39
Zero the Inestimable
Regular
  
Re: Sniper rifle question
Minutes of Angle are divisions of degrees. The MoA rating of a firearm describes, probably when held in a vise, the maximum deviation from the centerline of the bore that a group of shots will have.

In real life, it's more of a likely-to-have, as ammunition, windage, and skill of the shooter will all vary.

~Zero the Inestimable

Re: Sniper rifle question
Like wise, I agree with Zero.
When you've got a well made, floating barreled rifle, thats quoting 1/2 or 1/4 MOA, the biggest problem comes from ammunition, becuase, while you might have a 1/4 MOA rifle, a lot of ammo, especially military loads, unless you've got a VERY patient Quarter Master, who'll get you nice stuff, is more likely to be around 1/2-1 MOA.

As a guide, think of fireing 5 shots at a single target. a 1 MOA setup will place all 5 holes within 1 inch at one hundred yards. equally, 1 MOA will place 5 shots in 2 inches at two hundred yards, and 3 at three hundred. Most snipers wouldn't want to shoot at much more than three hundred if they can, so your looking at a guarenteed head shot at 350 yards if your a competent shot, at 1 MOA.

Ammunition shouldn't be a problem (what?) Snipers are issued M-82 Match 7.62 BT Ball Ammunition. Match Grade means all of the ammunition in that "batch" are made from the same lot of primers, casings, ball, powder everything is the same and the quality is consistent as should the consistency of the end result.

I'm not sure what England uses for sniper rifles but the USMC Scout/Snipers use the M-40A1 built on the Remington Model 700 Action. Aiming for the head is ineffective, look at when some one is moving..... the arms legs and head are constantly moving the center mass area of the body (upper torso) moves very little, making it a much easier target. I do know in the Army Sniper manual the engagement of targets below 800yards is prohibited unless absoulutely necessary.
