Re: CB, Scanner, or Shortwave?
The difference between air band and regular stuff is that Air band is in the frequency range of 108 to 130mhz (roughly) and some cheaper scanners will not cover this. The other difference is that aircraft radios use Amplitude modulation (AM) whereas conventional police radios use Frequency modulation (FM). If you try and recieve an Amplitude modulated signal with a frequency modulation reciever you will just get static or barely audible speech.

Re: CB, Scanner, or Shortwave?
Perfect scanner, at least what I have, and it works great, as I listen to the air band every now and then as well. Radio shack pro-94. It covers 29-54, 108-136.975(aircraft band) 216-225 (I think military and 220 MHz ham band, 406-512, 806-823-police trunking bands, 896-956, and 1240-1300, all in MHz. It covers about everything I can possibly think of using a scanner for, clandestine or not. It should be cheaper, too, since it is a few years old. Give it a look, and that Yaesu sounds good too!

Oh, and to answer your question negative zero, there is a big difference between am and fm. Am is Amplitude modulation, which means if you look at the vibrating wave as a line, it would increase or decrease in modulation, or height. This would be much like a guitar string.

Fm on the other hand, vibrates more like a slinky if you rapidly move it on one end, the wave travels as a compression across the slinky. The wave height is the same but the frequency changes, hence the name frequency modulation. Both acheive the same thing in the end, to produce a voltage high enough to be amplified and put through speakers, a TV, whatever.

So, anAM signal modulates, or "swings", whereas an FM signal stays the same, since it is only a carrier signal whose frequency varies. If you yell into any AM transmitter, the max amount of power comes out only when the signal is modulated the highest, at the top of the yell. In quiet, the station only broadcasts a weak carrier, although this is more true only in CB and ham radio. You can yell into an Fm transmitter as much as you want, but that will not increase how far you can talk with it.

That would be my best way to describe AM and FM. Also, I should add, AM or FM can be used on any frequency, although the clarity will generally increase as the frequency increases; An AM station has to use a lot more amperage to drive its amplifiers than an
FM one only because AM radio sits in the shortwave area of the spectrum, where the frequency is rather measured in kilohertz rather than megahertz, while FM operates in the 100 megahertz range. Intead of having to ocscillate a signal that have a wavelength of a thousand feet like AM radio does, an FM station only has to oscillate a wave about 19 inches long. That could be equated to thinking of the energy it would take to make a thousand foot rope vibrate compared to a 19 inch one.

Plus, FM can be stereo, while although it is possible to make stereo AM, it just doesnt have the clarity of FM.

By the way, I have a radio that can transmit AM, FM, and SSB. That does prove that Am and FM are signal modes, not just where the common radio station uses the format.

It's a cool hobby, and who knows, Howard Stern had to have his start somewhere. Well, then again, look at Dave Roth, but I'm just gonna stop there.

Hope it helps.
