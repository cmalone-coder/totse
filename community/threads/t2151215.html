If you take benzos, your problems will disappear for as long as you can escalate your dose for, then you'll go through withdrawal and be left in a worse state than the beginning. You don't learn anything on benzos, so you'll revert to how you were before as soon as the dose wears off.

I went to the doctor a while ago in search of benzos when addicted to about 5mg clonazepam/day, but was refused. Pretty much all doctors would refuse these days, although I don't live in a country with a health system based on profit.

SSRIs are currently the only drugs that can lead to long-term solutions for anxiety/depression. They don't kill your memory like benzos, so combined with CBT can actually help past when you're eventually taken off them.

Neither are really long-term solutions more than for a few years, but SSRIs actually have a purpose in dealing with the problem instead of than just giving you amnesia, along with destroying any education/work you had planned.

If you think they're totally ineffective pharmacologically, would you say the same about MDMA? You could compare SSRI effects to low-dose ecstasy, and not many people would deny its antidepressant/anxiolytic benefit.

With all that said though, it's not a bad idea to get some bennies for occasional use (so you don't avoid things) before any other treatment starts working, as long as you can control yourself. If you begin on SSRIs, it's really not that hard to get a few weeks of valium/whatever until it starts helping.

Re: SSRIs vs benzos
I'm just leery of the side effects (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_serotonin_reuptake_inhibitor#Adverse_eff ects), and I think for good reason.

From that list, I'd say only the bold ones below last longer than the first few weeks for most:


* anhedonia
* nausea
* drowsiness or somnolence
* headache
* clenching of teeth
* extremely vivid and strange dreams
* dizziness
* changes in appetite
* weight loss/gain (measured by a change in bodyweight of 7 pounds)
* may result in a double risk of bone fractures and injuries
* changes in sexual behaviour (see the next section)
* increased feelings of depression and anxiety (which may sometimes provoke panic attacks)
* tremors
* autonomic dysfunction including orthostatic hypotension, increased or reduced sweating
* akathisia
* liver or renal impairment
* suicidal ideation (thoughts of suicide)
* Photosensitivity (increased risk of sunburn) (Use protective clothing, such as long sleeves and hats, and sunscreen to decrease the risk of sunburn.)

Some of those are specific to individual SSRIs -- some are sedating, some are more 'activating', some can lead to weight-gain, others to weight-loss. You can probably avoid some side-effects by choosing the right drug.

Also, you should probably ignore the part about liver/kidney impairment.

Remember they are anti-anxiety/anti-depressant meds, so if they made most people suicidal and depressed past a few weeks, they'd be pretty crap.
