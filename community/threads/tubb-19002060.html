����I'm so clever.



����I made a very interesting discovery today. It's something to flagrantly obvious that you think that someone would have written about it by now.
����Teflon and KNO3 do not play well together. This is what I gather, at least. I had an accidental ignition of a pot full of KNO3 and sucrose smoke mixture today, and with completely pure ingredients and a perfectly safe electric hot plate (same as I always use) I can only surmise that it was my new non-stick pot that caused the premature ignition.
����The smoke mixture (20 ounces of it) ignited very shortly after it first began to melt. I imagine the KNO3 oxidized some element of the teflon as soon as it went molten. The entire pot shortly flared up and I lost my entire batch. I first considered the possibility of an impurity or other outside ignition source. The pot was clean, though, and I was using the same ingredients I always do. The next part tipped me off: In the pool of molten KNO3 in the bottom of the pot was a blob of melted teflon - Energetically skimming on the surface of the KNO3 and burning away.
����Interesting.



����I was left with this hardened cake of teflon and KNO3. The stuff solidified quite quickly, leaving this strange porous material behind.
����The biggest loss of the afternoon was my trusty electric hot plate, which may or may not still work after this little adventure.



����Long story short: Don't mix up smoke bombs in a non-stick pot.
