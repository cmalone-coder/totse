The thing is, many people don't really know what the word 'addiction' truly means. People too often confuse addiction with just, you know, non-compulsively liking something. If Marijuana is addictive, then other normal things such as playing outside in the sun, riding rollercoasters and eating ice cream are per definition also addictive.

TRUE ADDICTION is this:

When you do something such as drinking alcohol, smoking crack or huffing ether for the first time, you will just like it the way you like the above mentioned non-addictive yet 'fun' activities. Do it twice, thrice, etc... and you'll still like it. After a certain number of uses (depending on the substance), it will start getting sortof old. I mean, you still love doing the drug, but the novelty or the 'magic' people always get when they use a substance for the first couple of times will have worn off. By now the user has become pretty familiar with the effect it produces. This is true for every drug/activity, be it marijuana, ice cream or cocaine.
But this point is where the train of events diverts.

When you look at traditionally non-addictive substances such as weed, the user will at this point either quit using the substance for a while, instinctively/naturally taper off its use, or just feel less excited about it, will think about it less, and will start focusing his/her thoughts on other things. Either way, the user's natural urge to continue using will fade away, perhaps completely. It is however likely that the user will once again end up looking forward to smoking weed again if it has been a while. The abstinence/tapering down period can last quite a long time. Many people who occasionally toked during high school and stopped using during the period emcompassing their early careers, the first years of mariage and the birth of their kids , sometimes end up smoking marijuana again during adulthood 'for old time's sake' or to bring back old memories. Never at one point during their lives did their use ever turn problematic or disrupted their lives.

But for substances generally found 'addictive' such as cocaine, cigarettes, etc., The user will not taper down when the initial discovery phase has ended. In fact, it feels as if the inital novelty never seems to end. The user starts feeling conflicting thoughts and emotions the more he uses. Even though the rational voice of his mind seems to say "I don't really like doing this drug anymore, I'd like to stop using it and do something else", there seems to be a sneaky, devilish and incredibly subtle voice/feeling/urge coming form his bowels that whispers "But on second thought... I kinda feel like doing some more." The presence of this 'little devil' (as many addicts like to style it) is most noticable either during the time between the peak of the high and the comedown, or when the user happens to be in the same setting where he usually does the drug (IE, smokers often relapse while driving their car). Addicts will even try to mask their own addiction to themselves: they will make up excuses to themselves in order to 'get permission' from their internal voice of reason to do the drug. "I'm in a stressful period of my life right now, I'll taper down when things are less hectic." "I've worked my ass off all day, I think I've at least earned myself the right to light up one cigarette."

The conflicting thoughts are a trademark of 'true addiction'; What addict has never had the following happen to him: You're sitting in your chair thinking about using coke. You feel bad about using, yet you can't resist the urge to do 'just one line'. You bust out a pile of coke, make a line from it, snort it, feel satisfied and tell yourself "I'll just settle with this" and you put the pile of coke away. Put the coke in the drawer, close the drawer. Then suddenly the 'little devil' is there, you can feel him nag at you: you want more, you need more. "Fuck it," you say, and before you know it the drawer opens, the pile is on your desk, you make a line and it goes back up your nose. "Damn it", you think, "I shouldn't have done this," and back to the drawer it goes. The drawer never stays closed for more than 10 minutes. Open, closed, open, closed, open, 'till all the coke has gone. While you're high, you're sitting there writing notes to yourself that tell you to stop, but when the last line starts lose its effect, you don't even bother reading them anymore. That's true addiction. Your asshole teacher claiming that weed is addictive is a fucking insult to people who wished they never touched the real addictive drugs or wished they at least had the common sence not to do them so much that it got them addicted.
