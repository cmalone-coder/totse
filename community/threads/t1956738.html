Re: Effects of a nuclear blast
You're forgetting thermal radiation, which would fry you almost instantly if you faced the blast. If not, don't worry, the overall temperature of the atmosphere should cook you anyway. Again, if you face the blast, even very far away, it would get you blind for the rest of your life. In a high-grade underground shelter, these effects are not of a problem.

Fallout radiation would become the major concern a few hours after the detonation, contaminating the area for years, and would probably kill you a few days later out of radiation poisoning, still assuming you're out in the open. In a shelter, this would condemn you in living in your shelter all that time, assuming buildings didn't collapse on it after the blast.

The blast itself would destroy virtually everything except superbuildings worth several millions of dollars. On humans, it would cause severe ear and lung problem, assuming you're still out in the open, but don't worry, the other effects will take care of you and you won't suffer these problems for too long. In a shelter, you would feel an earthquake that would be all.

Re: Effects of a nuclear blast
m3divinewind, I guess you have very limited knowledge of nuclear weapons in general.

1. Extreme heat: look above, see thermal radiation.

2. "you guys are forgetting that if a nuke goes off in a place where there are densely built, tall buildings, the force of the bomb would obliterate those building and crush anything underneath.": Err... no. In your everyday nuclear war, explosions either take place in altitude, or on the surface, not underground. Dense buildings at ground zero would be most likely vaporised away by a thermonuclear explosion, some may stand the direct shock of a low-yield A-bomb.

3."the sheer impact of the bomb's explosion": It's called initial blast, and negative effect. Look above.

4."If you somehow manage not to get crushed by debri and rubble, you'll soon (within hours, maybe a few days) start to feel the effects of the radiation (which goes way farther than the actual explosion).": If you're close enough to have buildings collapsing all around you, thermal radiation will kill you way before radiation.

5."it'll only expose you to the radiation (which'll last for a few centuries)": Wrong. Radiation at ground zero last for about 3 or 4 decades, at most. In a global thermonuclear war(which is most likely to happen if ever a nuclear weapon was detonated on US territory), radiation would and should be enough to wipe out most of humanity and maybe would last a lot longer.

6."and the horrible sand storm outside.": There is no sand strom in a nuclear detonation.

7."modern nuke": The newest nukes are almost 15 years old, and generally don't go over 20 megatons, and those almost don't exist anymore, thanks to START 1 and 2.

8."which would be enough to throw the climate of the area completely out of whack, block the sun, and start a local (and soon global) ice age": Don't mix muclear detonation and nuclear winter. There is hardly enough dust in a nuclear cloud to ever change anything in the climate. In a nuclear war, there might be a slight decrease a global temperature but nothing enough to "throw the climate of the area completely out of whack".

9."So, aren't you glad your tax dollars (50 trillion or so) go towards a good cause?": There is no way the US government can collect 50 trillion dollars in income taxes.It would mean the the average American citizen would have to pay about 180 000 dollars in taxes each year.
