counterfeiting made simple
[disclaimer: Do not take this post seriously. I am not a counterfeiter. I have never broken the law in my life [except for minor traffic infractions]. This article is for vendors to be aware of the next wave of desktop counterfeiting and to safeguard themselves from it. I am not responsible for any illegal or immoral activities on anyone else's behalf.]

materials:
* a high-resolution (2400X2400 minimum) printer-scanner-copier with good color and contrast balance
* 25% rag paper (but ordinary printer paper can be used)
* a clean and intact bill of any denomination ($20 and under, as a personal policy)
* a sheet of 'slightly thick' paper
* a sheet of black construction paper
* an iron
* clear tape (non-shiny surface; this is critical)
* a ruler
* a razor blade
* baby powder
* a few well-circulated $1 bills
* ~30 minutes to prepare, 2 hours to condition

So you're sitting around wondering where to get some more cash for some herb because all you have is a $20 bill and you want a half-ounce of the danks? Here's an INCREDIBLY EASY method to make your money multiply with minimal effort:

1) find a bill that is crisp, with squared corners and little to no 'grime' from being in circulation. the new $20 is a good candidate for this, but if you can find a crisp/clean bill pre-1992 (can't remember the year specifically, research this yourself) it will be lacking a watermark and holographic ink and thus will be a good candidate

2) slightly dampen the bill and iron it flat, making sure to smooth out any wrinkles. don't press down with the iron too hard while moving it or the bill can 'warp' and be harder to copy. the bill should feel like a brand-new crisp bill.

3) find a piece of semi-thick paper (construction paper will do) and center the bill on it, making sure it is at least parallel to the paper horizontally; rotation matters, centering it does not. mark the corners around the bill with 'L' shapes and use a ruler+razor to cut out a bill-sized rectangle in the thick paper. this is your placeholder. discard the cut-out portion and keep the sheet. this guarantees that the fronts and backs will match up perfectly.

4) put the bill inside the rectangular hole, and with a small amount of tape at each corner on the opposite side of your first copy, secure it. there should be no gaps between the edge of the bill and the placeholder. if there are, it's okay, just be sure to align the same edges up between prints.

5) mark one side of your placeholder sheet 'F' and one 'B'. put the bill+placeholder in your scanner bed and put the sheet of black construction paper on top of this. the construction paper will keep the bill from becoming 'transparent' and patterns from the other side ending up on your print. put the scanner on the 'best' quality setting, and experiment with different contrasts and color intensities until you get a print that looks as close as possible to the original. leave the copies on the 8.5"X11" sheet, or else they're useless.
copy; do NOT scan. almost all printer drivers have a built-in bill-identifying function that will recognize patterns on the bill and halt the printing. this may also alert the feds via internet. you have been warned.

6) once you have the front perfected, run anywhere from 2 to 10 copies of the front of the bill. write down the settings you used, and turn the placeholder over so that the back of the bill is down with the black construction paper on top. run a few copies until you have the back perfected, and write down your settings.

7) figure out which side of the paper gets printed on in relation to the paper tray. also note which way is 'up' and which way is 'down'. if you're a stoner, this is easy to fuck up, so draw and arrow on one side of a piece of paper and scan an empty page.

8) put your already-printed 'fronts' of the bill in the paper feed according to your results from #7. put the 'back' side of the bill+placeholder in your scanner bed with the black construction paper on top, and run your 2 to 10 copies.

9) inspect each 8.5"X11" print, checking for detail, quality, contrast, etc. next to the original bill. keep ONLY the candidates that look convincing. destroy anything you don't use, and dispose of it elsewhere.

10) using a ruler and a razor, cut out each bill reproduction to exact specifications. crumple and smooth the bill a few times, iron it flat, and repeat. to make it even more convincing, fold down a small piece of a corner or two and iron it down, then smooth it out, etc etc until it is convincing enough. study a currently-circulated bill for the wear patterns and try to mimic them.

11) if you used rag paper, and your product looks to be 'close enough', skip to step #12. if you used printer paper, or if your bills just seem 'off' to the touch, put small amounts of baby powder on the bill and crumple/smooth/ruffle/smooth the bill until the powder isn't visible, but makes the bill feel more convincing.

12) put the new bills in between some real bills in your wallet and sit on your ass for a while. this is to de-scent the bills and make them smell like ordinary money.

passing off the bills;
plan when you're going to pass the bills carefully. know which places are likely to check for a watermark, use the pen test, or look for the 'USA' strip that's in a lot of new bills.

if your school cafeteria is usually packed during break, the cashiers will typically be in a hurry and not suspect anything. keep the bill in your pocket, wadded up a bit, and only present the money when it's time to pay. do NOT look around to see who's watching; keep your eyes lowered. when she hands you the change, calmly pocket it and walk out with whatever you just bought.

have a friend help you out, if possible. avoid passing bills from the same batch at the same place, and give it a few days between attempts. basically, be creative in how you pawn them off, and if you get caught, it was money a friend owed you and plead the 5th.

in conclusion, counterfeiting is easy and profitable.
