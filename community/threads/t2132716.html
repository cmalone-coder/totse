The psychological aspect will last a long, long time. This varies greatly from person to person and depends on a number of variables, such as coping mechanisms and willpower.

The hardest part of it is teaching yourself that when you feel stressed/angry/tired/bored, there are other ways to correct the problem besides smoking a cigarette. Unless you find some alternative methods of relief for yourself, it's going to be very hard to balance the scale between giving in and smoking or abstaining and feeling like total shit. There is a middle ground.

I came off benzos a few times (major psychological withdrawals/cravings) and for me it was always easier to supplement the times of my use (stress, anxiety, depression, frustration) with other things that had a tendency to relax me, like, in my case, music and creative writing.

I also tried keeping a journal log of frequency of the cravings, their severity, and how I reacted to the craving at that time, whether it was constructive or not. I found that as I went along in my recovery I could look back over these entries and note times of increased craving, times of decreased craving, and both the external (shitty day at work) and internal (feeling overwhelmed) factors that contributed to each respectively. This helped me a lot in better preparing myself mentally to either continue doing what was working for me in related future situations or learning what I need to work on to reduce negative reactions.

Sounds fuckin' complicated, I know, and sometimes it is. But psychological addiction is a motherfucker and tends to be a lot more complex than some people think.

I'm constantly finding myself light up when I don't even want a cig, just cause I'm waiting on something, or because I just finished a task.

There's also the fact that it's impossible to escape seeing tobacco constantly. When you're quitting, it's best to not even think about the drug, but a LOT of people smoke. I'm really not trying to argue either way here. I've never been addicted to heroin, benzos or anything considered a hard drug. These are just some observations from someone on the brink of quitting cigs.
