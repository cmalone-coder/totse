Re: Knife Throwing
I have the cutest little set of Fury wedge-point throwing knives that I normally use. They normally come two to a sheath and set, but I have about six sets of them. They're four inches at their longest but still satisfyingly heavy and they fly very well.

I know most people's opinions on using small and light throwing knives in combat, but these were dirt cheap and they fly well, and have done wonders for me to practice with. I can throw better scores on the dartboard with my little Fury knives than most people can do with normal darts. They're safe to chuck around indoors, too.

If that's not your tatse I'll second the dollar store steak knife recommendation. Once you learn to throw those pieces of crap with accuracy you can throw anything.

Set up a chunk of plywood or strand board on the back of your garage, draw the target of your choice at it, take six paces back, and throw knives at it until you get the hang of it. There are at least three ways to throw a knife that I can think of, and two that I am proficient in.

The first and hardest, though generally the fastest, is the dart method. You hold the knife blade foward between your thumb and first and/or second fingers and sling it at your target. You can't use this effectively with any knife with a large or shaped handle, fat blade, or hilt. It's just about the only way to use throwing spikes, however.

The second and most commonly seen is the flipping method. This will work with quite literally any knife that has a point on it once you get the hang of it. I prefer to hold the blade of the knife to give myself the spin. All you need to do is judge the distance within a yard or so and wing the knife such that it lands point first. It's best to have several cheap knives when you're starting out, as you will throw lots of handle shots, flat shots, and edgewise shots that don't stick in the target. This will annoy you immensely if you have only one knife and you have to go pick it up every time you miss.

I prefer to hold the point of the blade, with the flat face parallel to the ground. You often see people throwing with the blade vertical, but I find this very difficult to do for the beginner and rather counterproductive. When you first start, concentrate on the spin of the knife rather than the power and work on making it land point first. Once you can do this, try putting power behind the knife, and once you can do that try doing it from different distances.

Learning to throw knives is lots of fun, but in order to do it effectively you will need to practice a lot. Best of luck to you, and have fun.

~Zero
