Re: Is there any hope for Africa?
I honestly don't know. One of the most progressive countries on the continent (Ghana) is where it is today thanks to a truly benevolent dictator during the 80's. With democracy so fragile and riddled with corruption in most African democracies it seems like a benevolent dictator is the most reasonable thing to hope for. At any rate, I don't think there is anything the West can do to help the political state of some of the failing countries; any lasting solution in places like Zimbabwe, Angola and Rwanda will have to be brought about by either the people of those respective countries or the leaders of other African nations, as anything sponsored by the "colonial powers" seems to inherently arouse suspicion. The most depressing thing is that thanks to an epidemic of corruption Kenya (once one of the most rapidly developing countries) is again on the slide, especially where violent crime is concerned.

With regards to "hope", we'll be more equipped to answer that question after the upcoming elections in Ghana. If the elections are free and fair, and the country continues to grow in material wealth, then I'd definately say there's hope.
