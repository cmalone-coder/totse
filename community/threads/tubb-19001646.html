I imagine it would work, but at what cost?

Perchlorates are quite expensive compared to nitrates (potassium nitrate and sodium nitrate for black powder, anyway). If you have potassium perchlorate you're much better off using it with a better fuel like aluminum powder.

Perchlorates are far more stable than chlorates (though less stable than nitrates). They don't react badly with sulfur as far as I know, but don't quote me. Permanganates, sulfur, and a hygroscopic material (KNO3) would be a bad idea. I don't suggest anyone try that.

Re: bp
Warweed, I suggest you see a licensed counselor. Anyway, here's the way to make BP like a pro:
Take 75% potassium nitrate, 15% air-float charcoal and 10% sulfur and ball-mill it for at least 6hrs. This will leave a relatively fine powder which is good for some pyrotechnic uses, but to make granulated BP, you need to SLIGHTLY moisten this powder and press it (I'd say in between 2 boards with bricks on top), then wait until this is fully dry and crush up the resulting "brick" of BP.
