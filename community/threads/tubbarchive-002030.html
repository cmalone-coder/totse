War against the unseen insurgent is the modern war. Most of our weaponry was designed for a full on confrontation with the Soviets, you're right, but we are now changing our tactics and technology to better suit said war on terrorists, insurgents, assassins, and others.

Precision here is the key. Despite however high tech our cluster munitions and anti-tank weaponry may have seemed, I think that they are in some parts of this war, how exactly they are intended to be used, are rather obsolete. In my opinion, the most helpful weapons in our arsenal (aside from rock solid intel and good op-sec) is the designated marksman.

In a lot of ways, this is an image war (so much of this is due to advancements in communication technology). Much collateral damage is now largely unnacceptable. The insurgent will seek to maximize the amount of collateral damage that we do to support his campaign. That team of riflemen with their ACOG sights who can shoot their rifles to MOA accuracy will zap an insurgent from blocks away.

Of course, in reality it doesn't actually work like this, but many units in the US Army and Marine Corps are moving more towards a doctirne of that nature. In the unit of a marine friend of mine, everybody has 4x ACOGs on their M16A4s. In fact, I was talking to another friend of mine in the 10th mountain, and in his words a lot of infantry units are now feeling more like "starship troopers" than what peoples traditional idea of the Army is. ALICE is a thing of the past, everybody uses MOLLE gear now. Everybody has AIMPOINT optics and team leaders have ACOGs. Even the SAW gunners have thos Elcan optical sights on their weapons. These "leg" infantry units are becoming more and more like those high speed SF units. Why?

I have explained before that this is a result of the clinton era defense budget cuts. With dropping recruit numbers and more people doing rear echelon jobs for college money the infantry had no choice but to do more with less.

The solution at this point seems to be to have a highly trained and technically assisted infantrymen. I was struck after watching a documentary on the military channel about green berets in Afghanistan how similarly their operators, who were blending into the local civilian population, were acting exactly like police officers (albiet without much of the restraint that most of the LEOs stateside show).

Make no mistake, the modern war is very different from many peoples concepts of what the modern war would be. In my opinion, world war 3 has already been fought. That was the war on communism fought of course by image, propaganda, information warfare, and proxy. World war 4 is the current war on terrorism, which is no more of a "war" than the "war" on communism or the "war" on drugs is. It's actions of image, propaganda, information warfare, proxy. The same kind of war that political campaigns fight during an election. Actual combat occurs much less on this battlefield.

Which brings me to what in my opinion is the biggest failure of this "war on terror". We can't fight it. It's like trying to slice seawater. We have to play it like we played commmunism. This "war" will be won by image, propaganada, information warfare, proxy, and the intense efforts of our highly trained and technically assisted military infantrymen when the situation calls. Put simply, we are moving closer towards the "dagger in the dark" than the "1000 swords at dawn".
