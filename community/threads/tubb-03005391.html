I can't tell you everything, but I can give you some insight.

We took over fighting the Viet Cong from the French shortly after the Korean War, it was supposedly all to defeat the 'communist influence' in that region of Asia. Viet Nam was a French Colony up till then.

In the beginning it was just supposed to be advisors telling the South Viet Nam military how to defeat the revolutionaries. From there it went to supplies and material, then troops, then a shitload of troops, then one day everybody realized that it had shifted from a South 'Nam vs North 'Nam fight to a US Military vs Russian/Chinese supplied communist forces total fiasco.

Then about 1967 it started going very badly downhill, too many guys getting killed for what? Then came the Tet Offensive, and it appeared we were caught flat-footed and totally dumbfounded. Nixon swore he wasn't going to be the President who lost the only war the US would ever lose so he escalated everything, bombing Hanoi and mining the harbor, going into Laos and Cambodia, leaning on 'Uncle Ho' real hard so they would come to the 'peace table' so he could bail out and save his ass.

Then about 1971 he started selling the idea about getting the US out of the war, "Vietnamizing the war" he called it, what it really was you could call the "give the South 'Nam military a bunch of guns and ammo and say "see-ya". Then in the end it just all went bad, those last few days watching all those people who would hang on to the undercarriage of planes and choppers just to get out, it was bad to see.

You are mostly correct. The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (Joint Resolution of Congress HJ RES 1145 August 7, 1964) is when things really started the ball rolling for the United States. Global is correct that by 1967 things were going downhill...however, Johnson was still the President. The Tet offensive, which began on January 30, 1968, was a defeat for North Vietnam militarily but a blow to Johnson's weakened standing at home. Faced with political challenges from Eugene McCarthy and Robert Kennedy in his own party, Johnson also worried about what would happen to his own health if he ran again. On March 31, 1968, he announced that he was limiting the bombing of North Vietnam and was seeking negotiations.
