{Long post below; be warned}

It's worth pointing out that because anywhere from 1 to 10 percent of the population are homosexual or have homosexual tendencies, but perhaps as much as 50% of the population might have bi-sexual tendencies, this isn't an uncommon question. In fact, I'd wager it's a question that most people ask themselves through the course of their lives.

I'd like to suggest to everybody that the question "am I gay?" is perhaps a little black & white in its aim -- we should maybe be asking the question "exactly how hetero- or homo-sexual am I?"

Anyhoo ...

quote:I am 16 years old, I have had a couple boyfriends in the past years, but I think I have figured out something.

Let me begin by saying that at the age of 16, it's okay if you still need time to figure out what you want out of life, and what things you enjoy and what things you don't.

I had a high-school classmate who eventually came to the conclusion that she was a lesbian, although she was into her early twenties before she came to that conclusion.

If at this point in your life you've had a few boyfriends but you don't know if guys are any good to be with, or guys are even like-able, this is perfectly normal. We're not exactly mature examples of manhood at that age.

quote:I have this best friend and we hang out almost everyday at the park and the movies and different stuff. I just realized that I am the happiest, most lively person when I am with her. We can talk about anything together.

Women are known to be able to develop closer mutual friendships than men are. If your feelings aren't sexual in nature or have no sexual color to them, I'd say it was perfectly normal to feel this way about another woman.

quote:She's 17 years old, and broke up with a boyfriend about a month ago due to him cheating on her. She says she can't trust guys for a long time.

Another common reality, although it's worth pointing out that if she's willing to date guys, she's probably not a lesbian or bisexual.

quote:I think I have feelings for her, but I don't know. I will be coming out of the closet if I do. What should I do? Should I bring up the "lesbian" thing in a conversation and see how she reacts?

Bringing it up in conversation might work. If you were to go the route of expressing your feelings for her, but she was actually straight as an arrow, it might affect your friendship. That and the fact that at her age, she probably still doesn't know exactly what she wants either.

quote:Also, is being lesbian a bad thing? Is it something to be ashamed of?

"Nobody has the right to make you feel inferior without your consent." (Eleanor Roosevelt) You should never feel ashamed about who or what you are just because it doesn't fit in with somebody's idea of perfection or even average-ness.

My primary bit of advice is this: "wait-and-see." You'll know by the age of 24 or thereabouts if you're genuinely, irretrievably homosexual. Bringing that label on yourself too early might cause you some problems, especially if it turns out not to be true.

While we all harp on about how modern society is inclusive and accepting, however, it's worth mentioning that some people are still apprehensive about homosexuality and it has been known to cause problems in social circles, employment situations, etc.

I mean, if you do eventually come to the conclusion that you're a lesbian, you can come out to your parents and the closest friends you trust, but for those people who don't have any business knowing what your sexual orientation is -- let them think whatever they want to think and thereby not pre-judge you to be an abnormal person or whatever.

The less liberal elements of society don't have to know and, perhaps, shouldn't know. Less trouble for you. If it comes down to it, I know it would feel distasteful to have to cover up your true nature because of a few troublemakers, but part of emotional maturity (at least, as I came to experience it) is realizing that your assessment of yourself is all that matters.

While the opinions of other people really don't matter, if they should be of some secondary importance you can feel free to lie and know that you're just dealing with their insecurities for them.
