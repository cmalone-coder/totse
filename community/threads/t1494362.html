Yes, plan your shots. Camera angle is very important as is correct lighting. A good photographer can do wonders with a half decent camera and the correct lighting and camera angle. The right lens can be important too..., but you can get by with a fixed lens as well.

Photography is alot like painting- just having the right equipment will not make you a great painter. A good artist can make great pictures with a cheap pencil as well as with the best oils and brushes.

Learn all you can about lighting and experiment. Over and over again!

One thing for sure- if you want good sharp pictures taken at passionate (and unposed) moments you need lots of light and a fast shutter. Watch out for distracting shadows.

My Olympus E-10 is used in horse jumping competitions (where catching the horse at exactly the right position relative to the obsticle is vitaly important) with great results. All Digital cameras have a time lag problem (amount of time from when you press the shutter until the picture is actually taken). The E-10 (and other good cameras) have a minimal time lag. Still you must train alot to get an instinct for when to push that button- a little sooner then you might think. Especially when you are also involved in the action. You need to know your camera and your subject in your sleep in order to get the best results. Train, practice and train some more.

Also learn picture editing in PhotoShop (for example) to help you get the results you want. You can do alot there often easier then with a camera and lights alone. But you still do need good basic pictures to start from.
