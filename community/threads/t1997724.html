Step 1-Get the recipe from someone who actually knows what they're talking about and not really pay attention while they're giving you tips.

Step 2-look through aformentioned recipe exclaiming "that's ridiculous! What's the point of a cheese sauce that only has half a pack of cheese in it?!?!?"

Step 3-Start to assemble your own version of the ingredients on the recipe, modifying and altering the weights as you see fit.

Step 4-Also, take out any of the recipe items you dont like.

Step 5-Start to cook the sauce, and ignore the recipe directions that say "cook on a low heat" and blast the motherfuker on number 6 the hob.

Step 6-Pour in as much cornflour as the recipe says, start to stir it, get impatient due to lack of ..anything happening, and pour in the rest of the packet.

Step 7-Turn your back for a minute to make some coffee, and then turn back to the stove, get mobbed and attacked by the giant foaming mountain of cornflour paste that came from no-where.

Step 8-After cleaning up the mess, and banishing the cornflour monster back to it's saucepan, proceed to grate an entire pack of Edam cheese into the sauce, knowing you know way better than the person who gave you the recipe, who distinctly said "dont use too much cheese! And dont pour it all in at once!! and use a crumbly cheese like cheddar!!"

Step 9-Stir up this bubbling floury cheesey concoction, waiting for it to turn into a delicate and slightly runny cheese sauce to which you can add mushrooms.

Step 10-Realise it's too thick. Add more water and milk.

Step 11-add more cornflour because the milk and water made it too runny.

Step 12- Add "just a little bit more cheese" because the volume of the sauce has just doubled.

Step 13-realise that nothing is going to happen and that your magical cooking isnt going to suddenly work. Then quietly retreat to a corner to attempt to eat the cheesy,pasty, gooey thing you've created, and look over the recipe and the measurements just to remind yourself what a moron you are.


======================================


Here is, apparently, the best way to make cheese sauce.


Ingredients:

3-4 big spoonfulls of butter.
3-big spoonfulls of cornflour.
Salt (to taste).
black pepper, white pepper and ginger- 1/2 teaspoon.
2 cups of full fat milk.
1 cup of a crumbly mild cheese like cheddar.
5-6 shakes of parmesan.
Handfull of broken wallnuts.







(note, I know the cup measurement thing is confusing. I've got cups that hold about an ounce of high powered coffee,and cups that can hold around 4 pints of tea. In this case, a 'cup' just means an average sized normal cup. Use your own judgement.)



Instructions:

Melt the butter slowly, remove from heat.
Stir in the flour and seasonings, and add the milk. Stir all till well mixed then cook on a low heat, stirring constantly.
Stir till thick and smoooth and then add cheese and stir again till smooth.
Sprinkle in parmesan and walnut halves and stir in well.

2) In regards to 'fashion', they're just trends. Peoples tastes change over time of course, but in the short term... chefs make their menus based on what they feel people will like. Chefs watch other chef's menus as they develop, and if they see something more 'spectacular' or interesting than what the have, they adjust and copy. Also the top restaurants in the world are copied by competition, and they are copied by their competition, and it goes on and on. It works the same way as it does in the fashion industry. At the beginning of the year Giorgio Armani, or Gucci, or Ralph Lauren create some designs, then Armani Excange or Polo get cheaper versions, then the popular teen likes like Abercrombie & Fitch or The Gap looks at those stores and copies them, and so on and so on until even clothes at Walmart and Target have similiar fashions, unique colors of that season, etc etc.

Also with food availabilit in terms of what ingredients are best that year is a factor. Like some years, the weather results in poor tomatoes throughout the summe, or some years there is a lack of good mushrooms in abundance, and so on. For example 2005 was a GREAT year or bordeauxs. One of the best years in recent memory for sure. As we get further into 2007 and eventually into mid-late 2008 when the 2005 bordeauxs really start to become drinkable), you will see the top chefs using more of them in cooking when they decide on their menus. This trend will trickle down until even big chain restaurants are using more red wine in their recipes. Of course, as it 'trickles' down, restaurants will go from Mouton-Rothschild red bordeaux to Yellow Tail merlot (YUCK), but thats how trends work.

Right now a trend in sauces, is to not use the traditional, heavy, thick roux and reduction based French sauces. I couldn't even say why, but I've noticed it, and I'm not even cooking in a restaurant anymore!

3) Alcohol, especially low ABV spirits like wine and beer (welsh rarebits baby!!) are quite easy to cook with. Anything can be improved with a little wine - just a little though if you're just adding it for flavor, red wines especially can really ruin a recipe if you add just a DROP too much! But for a sauce with a lot of wine, just heat it slowly and make sure it gets hot enough to burn the alcohol away. Of course all of these sauces are meant to be cooked to a boil so that shouldn't be a problem.

At high heats many strong spirits (80% abv or even fortified wines sometimes or barleywines and meads) will flame up, but no for long. Its very impressive looking but it is't going to like... turn your entire dish into carbon. :P

4) Cream is easy to heat as well, just heat it SLOWLY.

And most importantly, I think this will help you in all of your cooking

RELAX.
Take a deep breath.
Don't let excessive language in the recipes directions confuse you, familiarize yourself with the recipe but don't let it overwhelm you.

One thing that helps me is to read the out loud and VISUALIZE the things in the recipe (especially the directions) while you read it. This way you won't have to go desparately searching for more mixing bowls, seasonings, vinegar, chicken stock, WHATEVER - in the MIDDLE of the cooking process.

Prepraration is that important!
