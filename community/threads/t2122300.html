I started to do one of my long writeup & 80% thru it,
it crashed.

Search my threads for the info.
Best get old cars & whiteware & old electric moters & transformers & lawnmowers to get the metal.

You need lots of them, get them from Whiteware dealers & ask around, drop circulars in peoples letterboxs asking to take away metal rubbish, cars, fridges, washing machines, TV's, microwaves, dishwashers etc etc.

Its best to have some way of transporting it, like a Car & trailer, do it legit as if you get fined for something, then you just wasted a LOT of work & got nowhere.
Don't chance it, its not worth it, double check.

The more you do the better, use gloves as scars on the hands aren't nice & don't go away.

You will need to be able to burn the insulation off the wire so you need a good safe place to do it without getting busted by police or Firefighters. Its better to ask permission first or even notify them.
Being caught out isn't nice, trust me.
It involves lots of smoke that will black out a city block area & has 6 foot high flames.

You are going to spend lots of time doing it, but that time flys by.

You need-
Gloves, sledge hammer, screwdrivers & plyers & levers (from the cars tool kits is nice)
A small sledge hammer & normal hammer.
Punch pins to drive stuff out, small electric motors have a spindle thru them that do a good job.
A workbench with a sturdy Vise.
A small electric drill with a phillips screwdriver bit in it, a old 12volt one connected up to a car battery is bloody handy & saves lots of time.
Crowbars & garden loppers to cut wire & stuff.
Anglegrinder with earmuffs & glasses. You need to be taught how to use one as they can harm you to the point of not being worth doing scrapmetal, or resenting that you did.
Sacks to put the metal in.

You should use Earmuffs as it WILL deafen you over a short time, I have ringing in my ears.
Also you will then have no reason to not bash hard where you want to, when you want to.
Time goes faster as you get into your little 'bubble' & work like fuk.

You need to clean all the iron off the Ali & copper, that gets pretty fiddly towards the end of the scrapping

After scrapping,
A Microwave is worth NZ$8+ (tranny is 1kg,
Copper part from magnatron 90gms, (needs work!)
Ali fins from magnatron 30gms,
Burnt off wire 30gms+,
Theres two strong magnets that are worth about $2 each for a bunch on Ebay etc.
Little transformer 50+gms.

A sack of Ali is worth NZ$20-30. extrusions from fridges,ovens,washing machines, bits of castings, ali wire from electric motors, cooling fins, tubing etc.

A starter motor is worth about NZ$6 or less.
Copper wire & Ali housing & brass contacts.

A TV has copper wire around the back of the TV screen yoke. 1LB copper.
Another around the edge of the screen for the degauser (which can sometimes be Ali wire)
Transformers & wiring loom.

Caution with handling TV screens, they WILL EXPLODE if they are not devacuumed.
Simply wear glasses/gloves, take off back of TV, unplug the plug on the back of the screen, find little melted end of tube, stick end of screwdriver in there & pop off the little melted bit.
Air will rush into the screen so don't have anything close to it - like your hand -as it will suck the blood out of you.

I nearly blinded myself by smashing a old screen that I thought was safe, really heavy sharp glass shards for 10 foot around me.

So, yeah ask away...

http://www.metalcorp.co.nz/pricelist.php
NZ scrapmetal prices

I started saving up Magnesium, its found on the outside of electric motors sometimes.
It looks like a dull Aluminium but its lighter & has different 'ring' to it if you hit it with something, it rings longer too.

But then I found the prices are less than Ali, so it would be easyer to just chuck it in with the Ali anyway
( Ali Alloys mostly about 10% magnesium or silicon)

Then today I find out that over the last 6 months its gone up are far bit, not worth as much as Ali but...

But it might be fun to put some on a fire outside & let it burn real bright for a while...

Sometimes when I have pulled apart something I have found the reason why it was dumped. (& the last sound it made!)
Like washing machines, theres a 'burnout' on the printed circuit board.
It might be a faulty part like a resistor or Capacitor, or a break in the board or a bad soldering joint etc.
Its a lot more common than I thought.

One had a burnt out motor wire (F&P Smart drive)
www.tomw.net.au/blog/2006/02/submarine-resear...
All that would have taken was one hand tightened plastic nut & 4 metal screws with a hex head on it to fix it.
But I'd already scrapped the rest.

You might be able to sell the whole unit, as people use them to generate electricity thru windpower.

Those machines have a large diameter electric motor, direct drive to the impeller part inside the machine.
Each one of those has 1Kg of copper wire in it.
It needs to be unwound or the wire cut out.
I found I could unwind two at a time (on top of each other) while its sitting over a peice of bar placed in my benchvise.
Or hang it by the wire & let it unwind itself, you need to jiggle it when the wire gets caught on the plastic.

You can chop the drive shaft out of the plastic bin with a axe, use glasses & gloves, theres plenty of chances to get hurt here.
Then burn a bunch of the alloy housings to get the Ali out.
The shaft is magnetic Stainless steel but theres a small nonmagnetic collar in there too.

--------------------
Ferrous means steel/Iron, anything a magnet sticks to.

Stainless steel> two sorts
1/ Nonmagnetic,
A magnet will not stick to it because theres so much Nickel in with the steel.
High nickel content makes it resistant to corrosion & tougher.
Nickel is worth lots of money so that makes the stainless steel worth money.
The $/Kg value is not high But because the metal is heavy you can get a good amount (weight) for a small volume.
I have been suprised the amount of money I have got for a small sack of nonferrous SS.

2/ magnetic, not much nickel, this metal goes in with all the plain scrap iron. (NZ$50 - $150+ ton)

-----------------------
If its not too late you might be able to find the replacement part & fix the washing machine, then sell it for even more money.
Oh, I found that the washing machine hoses with Stainless steel braid around them are 250gms SS each, thats with the ends (brass & or stainless fittings) cut off & the liner removed. So 4 hoses = 1Kg stainless steel.

------------------------
Car engines are hard work, you need to be able to strip down several to its basic parts, like.
Head,
Gearbox
Motor housing
Manifolds

Then attack several at one time.
ALL the metal needs to be taken off, every stud, insert etc.

To take out the studs (double thread ended bolt) you can first use plyers sometimes,
Or then a monkey wrench (on the unthreaded part)

Or then screw onto the thread a nut, then another, then use a spanner to tighten them both together & then put the spanner on the bottom nut & undo it.
Giving the end of the stud a sharp tap with a hammer will dislodge the steel away from the Ali (they sometime weld together a bit)
Do the hitting bit After threading on the nuts (it expands the end of the stud & you then can't thread the nuts on.

If a bolt or stud breaks off inside the Ali casting you can chisel away the Ali between the stud & the outside of the casting & then punch the stud out with a chisel or punch.

If it goes right thru the casting you can sometimes punch it right thru the casting with the punch again.
Or using a electric drill & bit, drill out the stud or weaken the alloy around it with the drill, then punch.

Rubber seals & round tube (locating) inserts can be picked out with a cold chisel or a round rod/punch.
Valve seat inserts can be punched out using a cold chisel from the 'Inlet' side of the seat. (bitchy)

Its easyer to use a valve removing tool to get the valve/spring/boss/collet out of the head.

Its possible to put something under the head to hold the valve in place & then use a metal tube or even the end of a ring spanner placed over the boss, then hit it with a hammer & the collets will drop out.

Trouble is, is that its all under pressure & that spring will shoot out & hit you, normally in the eye, quite violently.
So be bloody carefull with your eyes.
Use proper safety glasses.

Then you need to take out the valve guide/insert, they move on one direction easyer than the other & you need a punch to do it, a electric motor armature spindle (the rod going thru the motor) is good for this.

So is a glove on your left hand 'couse if you hit it the bone in your finger/hand cracks a bit & by fuk that hurts, I can't even tap it hard & it hurts for days now.

Heads are about 8Kg+ each

In the block there are metal tube sleeves (the bores) that need to be taken out.
I have a big angle grinder with a cutoff disc in it to do that, the block gets split in two & the sleeves fall out.
The other way is to bash it with a sledge hammer or axe the thing apart.
Be carefull, missing causes accidents.

------------------------
Brass, NZ$5 Kg
Theres brass everywhere, electrical connectors, castings, copper pipe fittings are brass, brass taps, radiator tube fittings etc etc
Most of its small stuff but it all adds up.
I had 7Kg in a shopping bag @ $5 Kg = $35

--------------------

Lead,
Leads worth fukall maybe NZ$.50 - $1 Kg

I was suprised how little considering how much effort I went to to get the old lead gas pipe out of a house once.
Most of the lead is lead weights off car wheels, lead headed nails, roof flashings etc.

I met someone who pays a lot more (NZ$2Kg) for lead.
He has some lead molds & makes divers weight belts & sells them to a diving/second hand store.
The divers loose them all the time so they always want them.
Fishing sinkers too, I've seen molds in hardware stores so you can make your own.
I got a peice of wood & drilled 3/4inch dia -1inch deep holes in it, then put a old bike spoke across the holes, then dangled fencing staples (horseshoe shaped nails) over the spoke into the holes.
Then I poured molten lead into the holes & made sinkers.

-------
Car batterys have lead in then & they pay per battery.
Be carefull with the car batterys as they have acid in them.
That acid will eat into material & then when you wash it all the little holes appear.
That will cost you good money $$$, for little return.

I tip them upside down on gravel or such, so the acid drains out, then I pour water over the battery & kick it back up again, clean battery with water & leave it there to dry.
The acid will fuk your eyes too. Be carefull.
Wear safety glasses

Scrapmetal dealers fullful tenders of metal.

Like one company in ? China/Asia/anywhere else wants "200 Ton of grade2 copper scrap for electroplating refining".

They say they will pay "X amount of $ for it to be delivered at X date"

The dealer looks at his stock & says "Thats a good price, we have 172 ton of copper & could almost fulfull that Contract"
"except we now would need an extra 38 ton of copper"

So the dealer starts calling up other scrapmetal companys to ask if they would sell some of their stock to him at a slightly above market price, so he can 'do the deal'

Now this is where you can make some extra $ by saving up lots of metal, once you have , say 100Kg copper.
Call them up & say you have that amount ready & ask what you could get for it.
Say that you are getting more in soon & will be adding to that 100Kg & if they like they could call you back if the price moves up or if they want to get that amount suddenly.

They think, {Ok, its there, I don't have to pay for it right now, but its essentialy 'In stock' & I can use it to make up a contract, so I will pay a certain %$ more than the current market price} & you make extra $$, they will also pick it up for free.
