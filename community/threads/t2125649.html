Re: Sleeping issues
I second the motion on Melatonin; it doesn't pack the sedative punch that a tranquilizing agent would, but it seems, at least to me, to aid a great deal in reaching of state of natural fatigue, as Dack9 said. It's also non-addictive and fairly cheap. If you can find them, get the Melatonin lozenges; they dissolve under your tongue in less than a minute and usually come in fairly appealing flavors like orange or lemon.

If this won't do the trick for you and you do feel the need for a sleeping medication, you need to pinpoint the problem. If you have trouble simply falling asleep but can stay there once you do, than a short-acting benzo like Xanax or Ativan would likely suit you. Xanax is occasionally prescribed for sleep, but most doctors lean more towards Ativan if they're settling for a benzo-fix in the matter. The good thing about short-acting benzos is that they hit you quick, are more sedating than the longer-lasting ones, and will wear off well before morning so you don't wake up groggy or buzzed. The downside is that they will stop working several hours after you're asleep, and if you have trouble staying asleep as well, they're likely cause an even worse rebound affect that can make it even harder to fall asleep again once you wake up.

If your problem is staying asleep, then something like Ambien or Lunesta would probably work well. Some doctors try temazepam, but I wouldn't even bother; it's extremely mild and really not suitable for anyone but the very med-sensitive type. Klonopin or Valium would also work here as the longer-acting benzo agents, but to me, Valium in prescribed doses simply isn't sedating enough; you'd be lucky to get 20 mg a night, and even then it'll probably be lack-luster in putting or keeping you out. Klonopin tends to be more sedating, in my experience, and a typical nighttime dose of it for insomnia is usually around 2 mg, which should be suitable for someone with a low tolerance and is at an average weight. However, especially with Klonopin, and possible Ambien as well (I've never taken Ambien), some people report the effects can last well into their morning, making your get-out-of-bed routine a bit more of a drag than usual. If it does help your sleep, though, that's a small sacrifice to make, IMHO.

Of course, if you try one of these benzos and it does the trick, that's going to be positive reinforcement to indulge in them more often, namely anytime you want to get some solid sleep. A once-daily insomnia dosage of benzos in therapeutic range usually isn't very risky as far as physical dependence, but the psychological factor will certainly come into play, and I speak from experience when I say that that is a bitch with benzos.

I'd also suggest trying to approach the cause of this stress and anxiety through internal reflection rather than external medication, but that's for a different forum, so I'll spare you the nagging.
