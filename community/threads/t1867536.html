".....Theyre cast in cingle pieces from shiny hicarbon steel, which is shity......"

High Carbon steel IS what you want in a good blade, whether knife, sword, or other... though I will agree that cast steel (in the traditional sense), and stainless is inappropriate for swords unless forged and heat treated properly. Blades made with cast steel using the stock removal method or stamping will be low quality.

".....You need to start a sword with low carb steel, then fold it by continuous hammering over several hours, introducing breaks into the matrix of the steel....."

Simplistic... and wrong. Why people actually believe this type of crap is beyond me.

Using your description as the word to go by, then you are saying that a sword made from mild steel (low carbon content) that has been "folded" several times will create a blade of better quality than a sword made from high carbon steel using proper forging and heat treating techniques, or even a blade that was made by stock removal and properly heat treated.



BULLSHIT.

Unless you are adding actual carbon content while "folding", you will end up with nothing more than a mild steel blade that will bend quite easily when you hit something that provides moderate (or more) resistance, and that cannot be hardened or tempered worth a shit... if at all.

Feel free to say "I wasn't talking about mild steel..." if you want, but you did say low carbon content and that's what it amounts to.

".....introducing breaks into the matrix of the steel. otherwise a problem-dislocation- in the steel can cause it to break....."
