Re: question about solvent and solutes
Actually, non-volatile things dissolved in water do not vaporize with the water.

However things like oils that have very high boiling points but are insoluble in water can be brought over with the water during a distillation. This is a very different concept though.

So you are safe boiling off a solvent and not loosing any product. You wouldn't want to overheat you stuff though.

Re: question about solvent and solutes
The NaOH combines with the HCl to form salt (NaCl) and water, and this liberates the freebase. The freebase pseudo is a solid, not quite an oil. It will work in experiments too but to make it into a salt that can be crystallized etc you just dissolve the freebase in dry toluene and then gas it with dry HCl gas, or you can dissolve the freebase in acidic (HCl)water and then evaporate the excess HCl and water to get the psuedo HCl.

Note that acidifying until the water tests neutral is not enough to make all of the pseudo freebase turn into the salt. I don't knoe the pH at which it is all converted so you just have to over acidify it a health amount and then evap.
