One technique that is amazingly effective is actually a defensive maneuver. Basically all you do is wait for them to throw a punch, and you use one hand to catch the inside of their wrist and the other to jam the elbow against the joint. Once you do this you can go a couple different directions, one you can take their arm, force them toward you (sidestep out of their way) and trip them as they go past you. Or you could break their arm or put them into a more secure hold, whatever. You'll probably have to put in a little effort with a friend to get this to work well, you'll need to develop a little speed and timing. Be careful when practicing this tho, you can easily break someone's arm this way. But it's very effective for the amount of effort you have to put in.
I used this on a guy who was somewhat larger than myself, i did the trip on him right into a wall. The guy was very bloody and very unconscious, and all it took was an angry punch and a quick reaction.

Gravehead (and anyone else), here's a similar trick to what your instructor did that you can practice. Hold your arm straight out, palm up, and ask a friend to bend it, outmuscling you. Most people will be able to do it. After that, stand there with your arms at your sides, look at your feet, and picture yourself standing in water. Slowly visualize the water coming up through your feet, up your legs, torso, and finally your arms. Put your arm out as it fills, and when you can see the water clearly shooting out your fingertips, nod (or otherwise signal) so that your friend tries again.

As long as your concentration is solid and they don't do anything that would physically force your arm to bend (like squeezing your bicep while pushing up on your wrist), your arm won't bend. It may go up and down, but it will stay straight. This is something our sensei did with us a couple of times in class, with people who had never done any chi exercises before, and we all pulled it off with some measure of success. It works.

judo, using lots of throws. it is similar in parts but that's the same of all martial arts. if you study brazilian, gracie or any other style of jiu-jitsu (other than traditional) they will teach you all about kicks, punches and other modern strikes. there are very few throws in jiu-jitsu. there are many takedowns, but the most simple is to just get a hold of them and let your let your feet go. you don't have to really know what you're doing like you would if you were to bother trying to sweep his legs or anything, just let your weight bring him down. then, once on the ground, the key is to let him wear himself out. even if you're on the bottom, don't struggle, don't panic. even if you know no jiu-jitsu remain calm, and simply cling to him, keeping limbs and head away from any strikes he may try to land. it's almost like an extended bear hug. he'll try to get free and use up very precious energy doing so. meanwhile, you are using less energy and need less strength to simply hang on. in a fight, my first objective is always get to the ground. if you do know this martial art, this is where the fun begins. while he's worrying about getting loose, you're busy figuring out which limb of his to break first. most are simply joint locks and joint manipulation and take more knowledge than strength. if there are no easy openings, throw a few kidney shots. simple rabbit punches will do, because any kind of shot to the kidney will make you reflexivly (sp) drop your arms to protect it (although you would think it, that's not true about every part of the body). if you don't know anything and still end up on the ground, and therefor don't know what does what, then simple common sense will help. the face (especially the nose and even ears) are great targets. even a weak shot to the nose will make his eyes water so he can't see. the only thing he can do is pull his hands up to wipe his eyes, leaving his body exposed to punishment. and you may not think about it, but just let someone smack you in the ears a few times and see how long it takes before you start to have to put your arms up to block it. if it's a real fight, that was started by the other guy, i'd have no reservations about kneeing him in the nuts. just make sure you get him flush in the nuts or you'll just make him mad and he'll then try the same to you. kidneys are always vulnerable, especially if you can hit them from behind. and if you're on the ground, with him on top, the best thing to do is wrap your legs around him and use your heels to kick them. very effective. pressure points also work well but are hard to nail the first time and in a fight, the firt time might be the only chance you get so i don't bother wasting time thinking about them...
anyone still readin at this point? anyway. where was i? oh yea. chokes are my favorite submission move right now. there are many chokes that can easily be learned, ones that involve using the persons own clothing against them. you can do a choke from the front or rear, anything from a basic naked choke, to my personal favorite triangle choke using your legs. other good targets on opponents are the back of his knees. a few heel drops on them and that will put an end to him trying to stand up. and even under the arms is good becasue of all the sensitive nerves there. hey, you gotta work with what you got. anyway, that's all i'll post for now, i don't know if anyone will even read to this point. if you do and agree with me or have any question or anything, then feel free to comment or ask. i have had the privilige or training in this martial art for years and would be happy to help.
