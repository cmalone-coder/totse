Details?

My room is pretty much separated from the rest of the house and I have my own bathroom. I keep a lot of fish and I think at the time I had 9 aquariums going in my room. This was a great excuse for the light and noise.

Anyway I used a 24x12x12 to grow the seedlings out with additional CFLs as well as the double flourecent on the aquarium. I then moved them to a 48x24x24 and grew them out again with the same lights and flowered them at about 24inches with a bit of careful training.

All i did was completely seal the aquariums with black card and told my parents it was an important breeding project and not to touch it. I also had a hole in the lid for a fan as well as fans actually inside the aquarium for circulation and another extraction fan running through a carbon filter although its effectiveness was debatable because I also had a fuck tonne of plugin air fresheners and the windows open constantly.

Probably a bit complicated for your average grower and kind of only works if you have a load of aquariums in your room and a genuine open passion for aquatics as an excuse (I work in the industry and also study it at college.)

I actually think that the aquatics industry and research could possibly be used for some kind of front and reasons to buy certain chemicals, money and large greenhouses/lights/power consumption in a large scale growing operation or other drug production. But that's just a random musing I realised whilst daydreaming in a lecture on cultivating aquatic plants and again in another about developing aquarium pharmaceuticals.

Edit: The strains I grew were northern light and bubblegum (It doesnt look, smell or taste anything like bubblegum) both are low growing indicas and although it was a mixed pack from a trusted friend I could not really tell the difference between the strains. All 4 plants were flowered at 24ins indoors and others did not grow very much past 3ft outdoors in bright sunlight. They are also very hardy plants although they tend to flower a bit late in the year if outdoors it shouldn't be a problem in milder climates.
