Re: Time travel
Mind if I ramble a bit?

Personally, I theorize time travel is possible. I even have some very crude theories on how to accomplish it, but I have no idea how to go about them without killing myself in the process.

One possibility that I think has been mentioned is that even if one could travel back in time, one could change nothing. This is because by going into the past, all actions would contribute to the creation of the present you originally experienced. Example, you tried to stop a bank robbery, but you accidentally tripped and fell into the security guard there, giving the robbers the chance they were waiting for. It's related to the predestination paradox, and that theory is the bane of any would be temporal interlopers.

Personally, I think that it's unlikely. For this kind of set up, the transmission of mass and energy would have to be set up outside of time as we know it somehow, by some master engineer of the universe who has a bizarre decorating sense. The same thing happened when scientists tried to explain the orbits of other planets while using the Earth as the center of the model: the charts showed a series of loops called epicycles, and it looked bizarre.

This 'closed theory of time' is especially popular amongst some physicists because it complies with the laws of thermodynamics, specifically the part about energy neither being created or destroyed, only converted.

I disagree.

I subscribe to the 'open time theory' which allows time travelers to make changes. This is because of a flaw in thermodynamics in general: It always involves a closed system in testing or proofing a principle, but nobody yet has learned to develop a closed, isolated system that can exclude gravity.

And Einstein proved that Time and Space and Gravity are all intimately connected. Furthermore, research into high voltage electromagnetics links electricity with gravity... all it takes as a few intuitive leaps of logic to come to the conclusion that time travel is possible.

But what of the paradoxical situations that may arise, hmmm? Well, let's look at a classic example. Say I charge up my Temporal Displacer and send myself back to 1930 or so. I go around and find my grandfather before he and my grandmother can have the baby which will eventually become my father. Everyone in my family tree that follows after them will disappear. Wait... that would include ME too, right? Uh oh.

I have two possible theories about what happens next, each depending upon the nature of the means used to travel through time.

1. If I used a method that kept me still attached to the line of causal events that resulted in my existence, then like wildfire, a chain reaction of new events will follow that link probably faster than light, eventually reducing me to nothing but random molecules and photons. The problem with this happening is that most people think that the result will destroy time and space because then I can never go back to prevent myself from existing in the first place. Or second place. Or... either way, or anyway, that you look at it, it boggles the mind and ergo, the mind thinks that the universe can't handle it either. I disagree. The instantaneous action that results in the moment I alter time so that my existence from that point on is impossible still occurs in the 'me-less' timeline, but in the form of random quantum uncertainty on a macroscopic scale. Ever heard of spontaneous combustion? Think of it like that, but resulting in the effects of a general result, not specific symptoms. It doesn't matter whether Gramps is incinerated or disintegrated or shredded or beheaded, something will spontaneously come about to result in his discontinued existence.

2. If the method used completely seperated you from the causal series in space time that lead to your existence, you're still around after Gramps bites the dust. However, if you try to return to your original time after this happens, you will find that the world you knew will be somewhat different... especially in that nobody knows who you are.

Strictly speaking, the further back you go in time, the more dangerous it becomes to try and change things. Not only for yourself, but for anything or anyone you happen to want to continue to exist (friends, family, favorite TV series, et cetera). I personally think that once you go back beyond your age in years, anything you do has a better than %50 chance of destroying your 'present'.

I am surprised that no-one has yet mentioned methods of time travel. There are a few theroretical ways that it could be done.

1. Wormholes
These represent one of the most plauseable methods of time travel. There is one key issue that would need to be addressed, how to move a wormhole. This is important because this method relies on "time dilation" which is a derivitive of the therory of reletivity. It is in essance that as you speed up time slows down, so in order to apply this to wormholes it would be nessesary to move one end of the wormhole relative to the other. This would mean that the moved end would be further back in time than the other, this time gradient would allow for movement though time as well as space. Another way of achiving a time gradient is using special reletivity by having the two ends in gravitational fields of differing stengths.

2. Space-time bubbles
Another method is to create a bubble in space around yourself and detach it from the universe. You could then in theroy re-attach to any point in space-time. The main problems with getting this to work are bending space in the right way and detaching from and importantly re-attaching to the universe.
