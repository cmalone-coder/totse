Worth putting some money into a '98 Volvo V70 AWD?
I've been driving for 2 years, and I finally have a car that I can call my own. It has around 220,000 miles on it, it's been in two accidents, the check engine light has been on for over a year, there's a oil leak in the turbo and god knows what else wrong with it. I'm not particularly knowledgeable when it comes to cars, I understand the basics of how an engine works but I probably couldn't identify what does what if you opened up the hood and asked me. I'd like to change that, and I figure the best way to is to actually work on a car.

So, in your opinion, would it be worthwhile for me to spend a little money and "soup up" my car? Apparently there's a decent selection of aftermarket parts for the V70, but I'm not really sure where to start assuming it's not going to be a complete waste of money. I'm not planning on really doing much racing, I just want a car that performs better than average, and to learn a thing or two in the process.

Re: Worth putting some money into a '98 Volvo V70 AWD?
Buy a "For dummies" book and learn how to do basic car mantinance and what everything does with that.

Other than that, good luck with your car and future cars.

Re: Worth putting some money into a '98 Volvo V70 AWD?
Let me start by saying yes, put some money into that Volvo. AWD + Turbo + Massive trunk? Hell to the yes!

But, for starters I think you have the wrong idea about "souping up". Do not, under any circumstances, "soup it up". I don't mean modify your Volvo to make it faster, I mean fix the car up first. What you should do is try to mechanically and aesthetically restore it to factory condition. Start by fixing everything that's wrong with it, at least if you can.

You should know that if you do attempt to do the work yourself, then be prepared to not have your Volvo on the road for a while. Also prepare to make some mistakes, you'll probably have to take it to a mechanic with your tail between your legs once or twice. Getting someone to fix your mistakes isn't great, but it happens to the best of us.

I am interested in knowing the details of its crash damage; where, how bad, and how well was it repaired?

Generally, Volvos are pretty good cars and are worth taking care of. It is true that you can turn them into real fire-breathing monsters as well. Which engine is it exactly? I understand the S/V70s had straight-5s. I've seen videos on youtube where these fairly normal looking Volvos cruise down the highway with engines that sound similar to the legendary turbo 5-cyls of Audi fame. It didn't go quite as fast, but then that's like saying your Porsche 911 can't go as fast as that Mclaren F1...
