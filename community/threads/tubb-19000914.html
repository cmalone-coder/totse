Re: Thermite
Yes, listen to Culexus, he is right.

If you have been attempting to make thermite with Iron(II) oxide i.e. Rust, then it won't work. You need Iron(III) oxide, from burning iron, probably the easiest way from attaining it is by burning iron filings or iron wire.

The reason for this is because in Iron(II) oxide (FEO), the iron has an oxidation state of +2. Whereas in Iron(III) oxide (FE2O3), it has an oxidation state of +3. This means that when it is reduced by the aluminium, more energy is released because more electrons are being transferred between the aluminium and the iron.

Re: Thermite

I think I see the problem here.

The magnesium ribbon won't work because it's contacting only a small portion of your thermite and burns fairly quickly.
Same with the pile of magnesium filings- the heat is there but if you don't have enough it burns too fast to get the reaction going.

Thermite isn't like sugar & nitrate/chlorate, or any other host of combos. It has the reactant properties of rock (figure of speech). You're talking about two metals that need to be brought up to a very high heat (and kept there) before it can become a self-sustaining inferno.

You need an igniter that keeps the juice going long enough for the metals to begin reacting on their own.

An acquaintance uses Al powder and Mg filings (1:2 ratio) thoroughly mixed with Potassium nitrate (1 pt Al/Mg mixture to 1 part Potassium nitrate). He spreads it over the top of his thermite till it's about 1/4" or a little more deep.

Since a fuse won't get that going he puts some sugar/chlorate in the middle of it with a fuse sticking out.

I don't know why he does it that way when *I* think it would be easier to just drop the Potassium Nitrate and mix the Al/Mg with the sugar chlorate.

That's supposed to burn hot enough (and long enough) for the thermite to get going on its own.

Even though the info is second hand, hope it helps...
