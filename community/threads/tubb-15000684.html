Religion = A crutch
I'm curious as to why more people don't see this. All I see religion as, is a crutch for the weak minded.

Example:

A bird dies, and two young boys and a girl see it. Jimmy says, "Chances are that bird died because of that wound on its wing. Perhaps it was attacked by a venomous animal, or just bled to death. However without full examination, the birds true death will never be ceratin", then the other boy, Billy, says, "No Jimmy, it was simply the birds time to go!". Both boys turn to the girl, Lucy, and stare waiting for her response as it will settle the argument.

However Lucy doesn't know why the bird died and is debating between whichof the two answers is best. If Lucy agrees with Jimmy, that there is an obvious sign of possible death, but truly no evidence, and the bird must be examined properly to determine the cause of his demise, then it is clear Lucy is willing to take time/effort, and use her mind to dsitinguish between the thousand possiblities to come to a comclusion. However if she agrees with Billy, that it was simply 'his time to go', then it is clear Lucy does not want to examine evidence, or put work into her decision, because it is easier to believe things just happen because they're supposed to, rather then for a sometimes lengthy, and difficult to come by reason.

OBVIOUSLY there are some holes in the story, but its not some iron clad statement meant to change the ways of religious people, its just soemthing to get across how I feel.

I don't understand, how people can simply sit back, and accept everything in this earth, as a work of god. Don't you ever seak proof, or evidence?

And don't you realize religious folk have a sort of circular logic, that simply turns your question around instead of answering it? For example if I say, "Has there ever been proof of gods existance?" instead of replying with the true answer, of NO, they will say, "Has there ever been proof that god doesn't exist!?"

Now, thats just stupidity. If I find a person dead on the street, and soemone says "Hey you killed her!" it's logical that my defence would be "Wheres the proof I killed her?" the prosecuter however cannot come back and say "Wheres the proof you didnt!"

By all logic the burden of proof should be on those making the accusation. Or in religious cases, those introducing the ideas. How can soemone simply say "Here is what happened, and I am right simply because it is just as impossible for you to prove me wrong, as it is for me to prove myelf right!" Thats 100% illogical, and that portraiys many religions very accuratly.

The way I see it, religion is a crutch. When soemone does not possess the mental capacity to realize that the world is full of questions we will never be able to answer, because what we need to know is so far out of reach, they turn to religion because it lays out all the answers. Well, it lays out whats said to be the answers.

Death fits into that a lot. Some people can't acept that when we die we will simply not exist. We will just no longer be here. So they turn to religion, because it even explains our 'after life'.

But Im sure this will all be turned around, wityh that circular logic. For example thigns like, "If you're not dead how do you know there is no afterlife!" well just the same, I can use the same logic to say unless they're dead they dont know it does exist. As well, Im sure I'll be told that non-believers are too weak to accept the fact that the 'answers' are all right here, and they just cant realize/handle it.

But I digress, and await responses out of simple curiosity.

It's more than stupidity, it's sheer idiocy. It's an argument from ignorance. People that believe in things because they can't be disproven are blind to all reason and brainwashed beyond repair. If God didn't exist, per the painfully obvious indication, there would be no way of showing it.

What would be the criteria for falsification of the proposition that there is a God? In other words, if there wasn't a God how could one show that?

Well formed, objective statements about reality, usually have some criteria of falsification. If the statement "There is a God." doesn't have any criteria for falsification, then there are reasons to think that the statement is not a well-formed, objective statement about reality.

I will expand a bit on my above point.

What I'm asking is this. What would it take to prove that there was no God?

"If you can show "_______", than that would be evidence against their being a God."

If they can't fill in that blank with some criteria, then the statement that there is a God seems to be without content since no matter what the evidence was, they would still believe in a God.

The credibility of a religion depends on the justifiability of its truth claims. There are three basic criteria we commonly employ to determine the validity and truthfulness of a worldview. These criteria affirm that in order for a religion to be true it must be: Logically consistent - Factually Verifiable - Existentially Livable. It is meaningless to say that reality is beyond logic or that religion is a matter of faith and devoid of reason for this very statement presupposes logic.

I could say that sock puppets are conspiring to take over the world when not being observed or recorded and there's no way you could prove me wrong. You can't disprove something that has no proof to begin with. There is no proof to dispell. The same reason they shouldn't be believing it is the same reason it cannot be shown to be false in the first place, that reason being no proof.

Here's an example to illustrate what I mean:

Jimmy says that he has an invisible pink elephant in his room. If all non-visual tests fail to reveal the elephant and require such ad-hoc rationalizations such as "He's weightless and doesn't leave footprints.", then it becoomes evidence against it's existence.

So ultimately, religion is a baseless claim because it has nothing to base it's assertions off of. They are human constructs. If the religion was actually based on something that a testable hypothesis could be built on, then it would be credible. Since their is no criteria for falsification and the religion was founded of a basis of beliefs and not data, then they seem to be without merit.

It's the easyest thing in the world to tell someone that good things will happen to them after they die if they submit to your rules and if they don't, bad things will. That is a propaganda tactic. It pressures you into believing it with social pressure, fear, propaganda, and threat. Subsequentially, since we have logically evolved since then and now know much more about reality, we can shed these ancient superstitions in pursuit of the real truth and not just some ill-contrived baseless conclusion cooked up several thousand years ago.

While not a religious person myself I can sympathize with people who were raised in a religious environment, I think it would take a lot of confidence in whatever new viewpoints he/she had arrived at to shrug off a belief system that saturated their early lives. This is probibly why even some very rational, scientifically minded people will go to great lengths to justify their belief in a higher power. To describe religion as a crutch would, in my opinion, be fairly accurate, especially if it is used to support secular views, ie:anti gay, anti abortion, but I would like to make it a point to separate organized religion from personal belief systems. While religion may be a crutch, a belief system (that is to say, things we choose to believe rather than things things that are proven to us empirically ) is an important part of a persons psyche. A couple of you mentioned your ages, I am 32, and have endured my share of personal crises that sometime had me wishing I had a god to pray to. You will find that as you get older (fuck, I swore I would never talk like that, but WTF this is a religious discussion, rules of engagement are suspended), defining your place in the universe will become more important. "I am (name) the __________." , that blank will eventually become important to you. Religion is tempting even to a rational mind because it sorts this out in a manner that does not require any further explanation, just a commitment of time and belief, and provides a baseline for further refining ones place in the universe. ie: Christian Onion Farmers of North Dakota (heh,heh) Obviouly a member of this group would define himself primarily as a christian, then by his career, then by his geography. By defining himself as a christian first he solidifies in his mind his place in the world and may go about farming onions without worrying about any nasty theological debates. The poor non believer on the other hand searches endlessly for ways in which to define himself, some end up adopting religious views from foreign countries, usually whatever is getting the most press. For every 5 occidental Taoists I meet, maybe one has delved deeply enough into taoism for it to really be a religion to them, the rest are just looking for a little structure for their personal beliefs and a way to be a little different. My end point being, while religions can constrict the viewpoints of their followers and are often abused to support secular viewpoints, the need to define one's self is very powerful, a little unfounded belief can go a long way towards separating a person from the herd or finding community, depending on whatever a person needs in their life at the time.
