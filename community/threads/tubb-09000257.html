Re: Bible Contradictions
Free will and omnicience is one, I'll elaborate.

If God created us and knew precisely everything we would ever do to a 100% accuracy, he by definition controls us. Not controlling us would be creating us in a way where we had free will, as opposed to where we're stuck fulfilling his omniscience.

Free will would mean God looking in the future and saying "there's a 50/50 chance on this, but it's ultimately up to the human to decide--so I can't say what he'll choose", not "there's a 100% chance he'll do this, so he can't choose anything else".

The first version makes at least a bit of sense and solves this problem, but it violates God's supposed omniscience since he would be limited to knowing only many things, not all things. Omniscience, omnipotence, and other omni-traits are incoherent, logically contradict their own definitions, and can only be defended by people with a pre-existing belief that makes it obligatory to defend them, no matter the cost in mind-bending gymnastics.

An easier, simpler, just plain better explanation is that these traits were made up by humans to have a more impressive God, which subsequently gained them more converts and fed the belief even more in a never-ending feedback loop.

Contradictions in Christian theology deriving from attributes of God:

Omniscience: knows everything, including what he'll do; therefore can't do anything he hasn't already foreseen and isn't omnipotent.
Omnipotence: can do anything, including things he won't be able to undo.
Omnipresence: makes the existence of places where he isn't present (Hell) logically impossible.
Transcendence: makes any other claims you make on the attributes of God worthless, since they cannot be known.
Sovereignty: depends on the ultimate version of 'might makes right'. No objective standard for determining how the judgement of one being is above the judgement of another exists, although we can make determinations based on reason. If this attribute is correct, we would have no recourse if God ordered us to kill innocent children except to follow his orders (like the ancient Israelis apparently did in the Old Testament).
Holiness: 'holiness' is by definition that which is divine. As such, it's worthless, circular reasoning and nothing more.
Mercy: no comment.
Righteousness: see Sovereignty.

The very fact that that God sees the future accurately makes it clear that there is no "free will" present from the start. If God is omnicient then he knows every decision you will ever make. If he didn't, then he wouldn't be omnicient. Having complete knowledge of what will happen is predetermination by definition. If something knows exactly what will happen, then it has to already have been determined, otherwise they wouldn't know. "Free moral agency" would actually make it possible to choose something freely, without it being completely known in advance (which by definition makes it predetermined).

If Christians claim free will exists, and they do, since Christianity rests on its existence, the burden of proof is on them to show how it exists. However, they fail, because they cannot show how free will is more likely than the illusion of free will given the data we currently possess. If you make no specific claims on the nature of God (like deists, agnostics and atheists), you avoid this problem. Once you do, however, you open your position to scrutiny which it can't survive unless you decide to accept its "truth" entirely on faith.

The very ability to know what humans will do to a 100% accuracy in the future means there are no truly random, unknown factors which could affect the decisions humans make and screw up the prediction. In other words, if anything can know everything there is to know about the universe, it operates completely deterministically. And when everything is determined, free will is a complete illusion.
