Re: simple question about evolution
In evolution, things evolve, hence the name.

If a monkey evolves to walk upright, and evolution sees it as an improvement, then it continues down the line. On the other hand if a monkey evolves arms 6 feet long, and evolution doesn't see it as an improvement, then that line dies out.

There were a million tangents that a thousand different branches divided off of, but the only thing that continued was the ape/monkey branch and the humanoid line. So many things died out just because it wasn't their time, but the improvements continued on.

Re: simple question about evolution
Prometheus is caught in the old (19th century) concept of social Darwinism. It is mostly speculative. Money has nothing to do with natural selection.
Does anyone remember Reverend Malthus and his dismal prediction about organisms multiplying until they absorb all the resources? If you were like me, your biology teacher left you hanging with the conclustion that we all die at that point. Actually that point happened 3.5 billion years ago. We (all earthly organisms) have been living on a Malthusian edge since then. All organisms tend to over-reproduce and if left unchecked would convert there entire environment into copies of themselves. But, long before Africa could become packed with pachyderms, some critical resource would become a bottle-neck to the viability of the offspring. Now, it becomes a matter of jockeying for some reproductive advantage.
No organism can replicate itself exactly through an infinite number of generations. We call this imperfect copying "variation". Over many generations (thousands) some of these variations within a population may increase, on average, the number of viable, fertile offspring produced by an organism. Even a tiny advantage will eventually spread through an entire population. The old version "disappears". Note this is not genocide.
To avoid being tedious, lets jump to East Africa about 3 million years ago. There is a fairly robust population of forest dwelling primates that pick fruit, scavange, and occasionally consume animal protein. Right in the middle of this environment the continent is slowly spliting (the rift valley). To the east the environment is slowly changing to high plains. To the west it is becoming lowland forest. The eastern population has few trees in its environment so the front appendages are no longer needed for climbing. Variants in the population that make these tree grippers into more flexible "fingers" are selected. Over the millenia similar variations occur and the eastern population becomes the bipedal homo. The western group splits and variation and natural selection lead to chimps and bonobos.
Hope this helps.
