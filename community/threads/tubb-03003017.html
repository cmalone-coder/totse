you're right, you can't tame them per-se, their instinct is to be alpha, dominant. as long as you show dominant behavior (ie: lots of playing with you always placing a hand or limb on their shoulder, or putting your head on top of theirs, or as they get older and their instinct to challenge grows, holding them prone and lightly biting the top of their muzzle) to them from birth, you have no problems. they will not try to challenge your *alpha* position in the *pack*. staring at them is confrontational, and even a submissive wolf feels threatened by a direct stare, and is going to defend itself. dogs don't have the instinctual drive that wolves do, you don't have to treat them with such dominant behavior. they already know their place, they've been bred for ages to be domesticated. that may be the reason people feel that wolves cannot be tamed or domesticated, to most people a wolf is a dog and can be treated like one. but they do make loving loyal pets when they are raised correctly.
