Re: Anti Oxidats / Vitamins, controversy
There are no direct studies into the neurotoxicity of orally dosed MDMA in humans, and there aren't likely to be any because of the ethical issues involved in administering potentially neurotoxic substances to humans. Studies of frequent users can't control for many other variables, so MDMA can never be reliably isolated as a causative factor in these studies. That said, reviews of MDMA's neurotoxicity do seem to indicate that use in humans can cause cognitive impairment; there's just not enough evidence to reach firm conclusions.

"Despite large methodological problems the bulk of evidence suggests residual alterations of serotonergic transmission in MDMA users, although at least partial restitution may occur after long-term abstinence. However, functional sequelae may persist even after longer periods of abstinence. To date, the most consistent findings associate subtle cognitive, particularly memory, impairments with heavy ecstasy use. However, the evidence cannot be considered definite and the issues of possible pre-existing traits or the effects of polydrug use are not resolved."

Gouzoulis-Mayfrank, E. & Daumann, J. (2006) Neurotoxicity of methylenedioxyamphetamines (MDMA; ecstasy) in humans: how strong is the evidence for persistent brain damage? Addiction, 101, pp. 348–361.

"Evidence for the occurrence of MDMA-induced neurotoxic damage in human users remains equivocal, although some biochemical and functional data suggest that damage may occur in the brains of heavy users. There is also some evidence for long-term physiological and psychological changes occurring in human recreational users. However, such evidence is complicated by the lack of knowledge of doses ingested and the fact that many subjects studied are or have been poly-drug users."

Green, R.A., Mecham, A.O., Elliott, M.J., O'Shea, E. & Colado, I.M. (2003) The Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacology of 3,4-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA, "Ecstasy"). Pharmacological Reviews, 55, pp. 463-508.

Given that MDMA is more neurotoxic in higher-order non-human primates than in rats, I'd say it's a pretty safe bet that it is neurotoxic in humans. EDIT: BlackPope, MDMA has been proven neurotoxic in many lab animals other than rats. In fact, it's proven neurotoxic in pretty much any animal it's been administered to, although most of those studies use intramuscular/intravascular methods instead of oral. /EDIT
