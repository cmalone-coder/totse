Re: Drug Test To Get Prescribed Suboxone
Congrats on your decision Blaze! I've been on Sub for a few years now (On and off) and as a rule of thumb, they do want to see a positive opiate screen on your first urine/drug test to make sure you're not just trying to get a script to sell/abuse. It's like this at Methadone clinics too.

Also, you need to be in moderate to moderately severe withdrawals in order to be given your induction dose of Suboxone (Which should be under your doc's supervision to make sure no precipitated withdrawal symptoms occur or worsen). This usually means you need to be off any short acting opiates (Heroin, IR Oxycodone, Vicodin, IR Morphine, etc.) for at least 24-48 hours, allowing your body to enter withdrawal during your initial visit so that Suboxone (Buprenorphine) does not throw you into PWD's (Precipitated withdrawals), but actually help alleviate your existing symptoms instead. If you are coming off of long acting opiates (Methadone, ER Oxycodone, ER Morphine. etc.), you need to wait up to 5 days (Off of the drug(s)) before you can be safely inducted on Suboxone.

So my advice would be to take a Vicodin or Percocet or snort a bag of H two days before your Suboxone appointment, if you haven't done so in the past 2-3 days already. As bad as this sounds, the doctor will 9.9 times out of 10 deny you a script and induction into a Suboxone maintenance program if you do not show up in withdrawal or more importantly your drug screen comes up negative for opiates (Because of the fact they will think you are doctor shopping/getting a script to sell). Though obviously, you will want all of your other future drug screens to be negative for opiates and positive for Buprenorphine (Suboxone); and my doctor does this every time I see him/get a new script AND most doctors DO check to see if the Suboxone is in your urine and that all other opiates are not; some even refuse to give you another script if you show up positive for any other opiate or negative for Suboxone, but most would give a fair warning because of the nature of addiction and relapse being so fairly common.

Anyway, hopefully I have answered your questions. And if you have any other questions about Suboxone or what not, please feel free to ask. And if you still have my AIM screen name, you can always get me there (Haven't changed it).

If you need anyone to talk to about your future plans and what to do from here, I've been there and will be more than happy to help! You're such a strong person for making this choice and I know you will succeed!

Take care and be safe!
