It's always been my opinion that you should share everything with your partner. I don't mean right off the bat, but once you've developed a trusting loving relationship with someone I believe they deserve to know everything about you.

Of course, I also believe that it's unhealthy to be ashamed of your past, even mistakes. We all make mistakes, and we learn from them, and most of the time we become better people. I would only be ashamed of not learning the lessons from my mistakes.

And I also believe that as a partner you should be particularly understanding and accepting. You shouldn't ever hold your partner's past against them, it's in the past for a good reason. Also, if you want to have that kind of open trusting relationship you have to be easy to talk to, approachable, trustworthy. You have to be the kind of person they feel comfortable sharing all of their secrets with.

I think Cindy and I are talking about different things, but Im not sure. Let me see if I can clarify my position. I believe honesty is very important to a relationship. However, I believe that when people talk about total honesty or total disclosure they apply that principle to areas it should not apply to. And I believe that if someone freaks out because he/she finds out that their significant other was just being kind when they complimented or avoided an insult, and they think that means they cannot trust that person, that over reaction is rooted in insecurity.

A parent does not tell their kid everything. Some topics are avoided, and some are lied about, for good reason too. There is a difference between a parent making up something to avoid telling their 6 year old that uncle Bob is leaving aunt Mary because Mary is a cheating whore, and a parent telling their kids that she cannot afford piano lessons if that is not a valid reason. There are also harmless lies (Santa) and harmful lies (your daddy is not coming for visitation this week because he does not love you).

How do you tell the difference? Common sense. If the lie is something that helps the relationship, and does not cause harm, and being honest has no benefit other than honesty itself and will cause harm, then use common sense. Those who see someone who lies in those cases as someone they can never trust; they are paranoid and insecure and have no real grasp of reality.

There is a difference between that and games etc. I do think that honesty is important to a real relationship.
