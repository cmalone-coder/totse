Re: RCA built in router?
That could cause problems and thus, I dont recommend it. You would have to plug that cable into the uplink port on your hub for it to technically work.

For it to realistically work, you have to do the above and be sure that your cable provider will assign you more than one IP. Each machine hooked up to that hub will require a real IP address as there would be no router doing NAT (PAT) for you. I am unsure of what happens if you are only allowed one IP from the provider, I assume only one machine would have connectivity at a time.

I DO NOT recommend that kind of setup anyways. When all people are using the connection, you will have many many collisions, thus slowing down the connection and you will have a harder time securing things up.

I DO recommend a router.

Re: RCA built in router?
Just plugging the modem into a hub with other computers will work, with a few tweaks. I've got my DSL modem hooked to the uplink on my cheap-ass Linksys hub. Three 'puters are plugged into the other ports of the hub. My main win2k machine VPNs the modem to connect. It's running NAT software to provide service to the other computers, so I only have to pay for one public IP. If that machine is down, the other computers can still connect (one at a time) to the modem. Even with the hub chopping up the bandwidth like that, there's more than enough room for 1.5Mbs 'net flow...

Not sure if this'll help you, though. The way I have to tunnel to the modem is one of the less common connection methods, but then I'm on this bizarre PPP over ATM protocol. If I could afford it, though, I would have a switch and a dedicated router or a *nix gateway. Good luck.
