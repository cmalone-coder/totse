Its basically impossible for anyone to imagine just how immensely large Graham's Number is, and a googolplex does not compare one iota to Graham's Number. If your interested, try to understand how Knuth's up arrow notation works (and the related topic of the hyper operator) by reading about it on wikipedia and planet math and other sites. I say read about it several times on several sites, because even though it seems like a simple concept, it can be really hard to grasp just how quickly (and I mean really fucking quickly) it grows.

Then look up the expression for Graham's Number in the Knuth up arrow notation (or equivalently hyper operator notation) and see if you can possibly comprehend what it means, because I know I can't come close even though I fully understand the notation.

You can also try to read about Conway chained arrow notation, which leads to a nice definition of Graham's Number. In my opinion though, this is much harder to understand, not nearly as intuitive, and also does not make the number seem as large as in the hyper operator notation, (even though it, of course, is as large).

The main point of this thread is that things in nature like the mass of the universe actually do not compare to large numbers such as googolplex and Graham's number. For example, in that number you wrote, you have a 1 followed by a large number of zeros. If you were to consider a similar number, being a 1 followed by many zeros, with the number of zeros being equal to the number of atoms in the whole universe, you would not even have a googolplex, and a googolplex is absolutely nothing compared to Graham's number. Even googolplex^googolplex would be absurdly tiny compared to Graham's number.

However, you are completely correct that we can make numbers as large as we want, like in your post graham's number times graham's number, would certainly be larger than just graham's number. However, the reason that graham's number is considered so amazing is not just its stupendous size, but the fact that it was actually used in a mathematical proof of a very reasonable nature, in an area of math known as Ramsey theory.

That's why googolplex is not really that amazing since even though it is large, it was constructed for the sole purpose of being large. Whereas with Graham's number, it came up in a math proof (as an upper bound of something) and only later was it realized how large it really was.
