They have previously used previous customers full names and attributed false quotes of endorsement to them. They apparently operate dozens of sham sites that are all for the same company. They have supposedly stolen articles from other websites for their own use, mainly posting them on their dummy sites to increase their rankings. There's basically a whole lot of shit wrong with the IAS scheme. I can't comment on the validity of the stolen works claims, because that's "he-said/she-said" kind of stuff, but the made up sales pitches and the like are common knowledge, and the people I heard the other stuff from are people I trust. So take from it what you will.

I used to order from IAS, but after finding out about all of the above, I've just written them off, and choose to patronize more honest vendors.
