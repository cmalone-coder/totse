Buying my first firearm
I just got a call from my Mom, apparently my paycheck from my last two weeks of work is waiting on me at home. In addition to that I have a business prospect right now which can make me a bit of money (at least enough for a poor college student) quickly. My 21st birthday is next week as well, so I'll soon be able to concealed carry if I buy a pistol. All that being said, I'm finally in a position to purchase a gun.

Even though I'm getting some cash, I can't go all out. I'm planning on budgeting about $250 (the profit that I will make on the business deal) for a new or used gun.

Right now, I'm not sure what I want. I'm looking for either a pistol, carbine, or rifle (I know, pretty wide range.) I plan on buying more weapons in the future, but I want to make a first purchase the will fill my needs until I can afford to buy another gun.

I'm buying the weapon for personal defense against thugs-- both the criminal and the possible jack-booted ones of the future. I'm looking for something that will give me significant penetration, even against vermin with kevlar hides.

As far as pistols go, I'd really like to get a CZ-52. It fires the 7.62x25 round. I like this round because it's available as in FMJ as cheap military surplus. It's supposed to have great penetration against soft body armor. A CZ-52 would cost me about $150 for the weapon, magazine, and holster. That's a pretty good bargain. It's a full size service weapon, but it's pretty thin so it carries a bit better than other service pistols. I have two problems with the weapon, those being range and availablity of ammo. The range problem is inherent to pistols. It would be nice to be able to pick off targets from a longer range, but I know I won't be able to do it with a pistol. While the ammo is available mail order, it would be nice to be able to head over to Wal-Mart and get what ammo I need. Aside from this pistol and cartridge does anyone have suggestions for a good CCW pistol and cartridge that will have significant penetration against up to, say, III-A NIJ standards? I'd love a 10mm converted to BOZZ .224 or a Five-seveN with the SS190, but I can't buy either both for cost and legality reasons.

As far as a carbine goes, I know Marlin and Winchester and I think Remington make some nice lever action carbines firing revolver and smaller rifle caliber rounds. I wouldn't mind a lever action in .44 mag, as long as I can attain the bullets in FMJ form. Also, I know that revolvers configured for .44 mag can also fire .45acp, is this the case with a lever action? A carbine is of course unsuited to concealed carry, but I think it would make a great home defense weapon; although it will lack the range and power of a rifle and also lack the portability of a pistol. It would probably be pretty good in CQB though.

A rifle would be a completely different beast than the carbine and pistol. It would be more effective at long range and crappy at short range. Good for defending a position from far away, but not so good up close. Looking through some ads online, I can get a Czech CZ/VZ-24, which is based on the Mauser 98 action and fires 8mm Mauser, for about $100. I hear ballistically the 8mm Mauser is similar to the .30-06. I guess that would mean one would need level IV armor to prevent penetration from a 8mm Mauser round as the .30-06 requires level IV? Anyone know what ammo availability and price is like for this? I'd imagine surplus ammo is not too expensive. I've also seen a few Enfields in .303 British in my price range. From what I hear this is a very competent round. Anyone know what availability and cost is like for this round? Two rounds I like, but for which the gun might be a bit more pricey, are the .270 and .243 Winchester. Both cartridges are more or less a .308 necked down. I'm just not sure about whether this round can be found in FMJ or if what sounds like it might be JHP (websites often just say "slow controlled expansion") is the best I can do in those calibers. The power of a rifle round and the range are what I like about rifles, even though they're useless for very short range. Maybe if I could affix a bayonet that problem could be solved...

Anyway, that's just what I'm thinking right now. I just found out about the money, so I haven't had the time to check the local pawn shops yet. If anyone has any thoughts, can make a good argument, I'll listen. Thanks for reading through all that shit. By the way, I'm not crazy, I won't go on a shooting rampage against police or anything, but it can't hurt to be really prepared for thugs/civil unrest/zombies/jack booted thugs/etc.

Re: Buying my first firearm
If you're looking for a pistol for concealed carry or self defense you really don't want over penatration.

Quality self defense ammunition chambered in 9MM will penatrate on average 10-15" and expand up to .60" give or take a tenth of a inch.

Sure getting enough penatration is ideal, take for example a slower moving 147grain 9MM. It'll over penatrate but is not moving fast enough to realiably expand. Therefore smaller temporary and pernament tissue cavity = less loss of blood pressure.

A 9MM round that is growing in popularity with law enforcement is the Federal 124grain +P+ i'm tempted to pick up a couple boxes.

You seem pretty concerned about penatrating body armor, why?

quote:
than a .45 that expands to .80 but doesn't penetrate enough to hit vital structures


Imagine the ammount of blood loss. That's the number one killer unless you're lucky enough to neutralize your target with a upper spinal cord or head shot.

Start off with a pistol, something that fits your hand well, is a natural pointer in your hand, easy to opperate and something you can see yourself concealing well and get your permit to carry.

Eventually get yourself a nice rifle. They are not only fun at the range but i replaced my home defense 12g shotgun with my ar15.

One shot stops are a practically a myth regardless of caliber. Of course shot placement is key. Practice with your gun putting 2-3 quick and accurate COM shots. That's your best bet. Grab a set of snap caps (dummy rounds) when you pick up your pistol and practice dry firing at home. This will get you acquainted with the trigger pull. A lot of shooters loose accuracy in the trigger pull.
