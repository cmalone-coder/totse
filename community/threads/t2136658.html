Re: How to end the war on drugs. DRUGGIES REPRESENT!!
Let's just say you did manage to educate the majority of schoolkids, so that they knew the truth about drugs by the time they can vote. How many voting age people actually go to the polls? As an example, in the 2004 presidential election it was a 60% turnout. Number of votes cast: 122 million. So the population of your electorate is around 200 million.

How many of these are going to be the young people that you have educated? Adults are simply too closed minded with regard to drugs to believe anything other than what their government has been telling them their whole lives. Big generalisation, but whatever.

So let's assume that your plan is going to rely on educating teenagers with the truth, using email and social networking sites as your biggest platform. Be honest, if you weren't a drug user and someone sent you a fucking long email claiming that the things you'd been told your whole life weren't true, would you believe it? No, you'd see the (e.g) 'FW: Legalise drugs!' in the subject line, dismiss it as some bullshit chain email and delete it.

If you wanted to make any significant difference through voting, you would have to get every single young voter in the country knowing the facts before they were even of voting age. Even if you did somehow manage that, and got them all to the polls, who's to say that they would even all be pro-legalisation? I'm sure there's a significant proportion who is strongly against drugs, regardless of whether they know the true health effects or not.

I'm not very well-versed with American politics so correct me if I'm wrong (almost certainly), but before a vote is even cast, doesn't it have to be proposed by a senator or something? I can't see a politician in Washington proposing to legalise any drug, let alone all drugs. Weed in California is still *technically* illegal, even though no-one really gives a shit there. Attitudes towards drugs vary from state to state, so it's never going to be legal across the country. Politicians do what gets them the popular vote and keeps them in power, and attempting to legalise drugs is not going to go down well with the majority of the voting public.

I'm not trying to shit on your sandcastle. I'm all for legalisation (along with effective regulation), I'm just being realistic. Even in Holland, weed isn't legal, so you've got a snowball's chance in hell of it happening in the US, let alone with any other drugs. The best you can hope for is federal decriminalisation of weed, but even that would be a huge score.
