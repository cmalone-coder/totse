Re: How do I start?
Google it and find a decent weight program that has you evenly distributing exercise over all your body parts, broken up into 4 times a week. Do it for a month and check your progress. From there you can tweak a little. Just start lifting, running and being consistent.

You won't see abs within a month. Just so you don't get disillusioned. Having abs requires you to have a very small body fat percentage. Go for bulk to begin with. Increase the amount you eat by a couple of thousand calories.

Quote:
Originally Posted by Tamerlane
So could you guys help out a bit? Thanks.
Okay.

The crap I was rambling about above is "high-intensity cardiovascular exercise." The specific cite in cal/hr was for a few martial arts, forgot which.

There's lots of technical articles, but let's start simple : the basic concept of high-intensity cardio is that you should be on the ground and out of breath in under a minute. This can be done with sprinting. This can be done by working over a heavy bag. This can be done by doing pushups really, really fast.

Once you're laid out on the floor panting, you rest a bit, and then you do it again. One really, really neat trend which I support is finding as many different things that can floor you in a minute, and do a different one after each recovery.

Half-hour of this, and you'll lose a few pounds of water weight alone. Be sure to have an electrolyte mix handy. Just in case you sweat yourself into something like seizures/cramps/cardiac arrest. Luckily for you, cramps usually come first.

Does it work? Fuck yes. Will you lose weight swiftly? So long as you don't add 2,000 calorie milkshakes to every meal, fuck yes. Will you be in a crapload better shape? Fuck yes. Hell, you'll probably have better total fitness than some of our weightlifters, because size doesn't always mean that you can make a ten mile run.

Will this give you strength? Well... no. You'd actually have to lift weights to do that. 'n while Parallax will probably tell you to go buy "starting strength" - which isn't a bad book for beginners, even if its local marketing campaign makes it overhyped - I'm just going to lay down the much simpler concept : you gain strength by lifting really heavy crap. You keep gaining strength by using your new stringth to lift even heavier crap.

Yeah. That's really all there is to it. We could go on for hours about maximizing your insulin spike, or what balance of fats is optimal for anabolic growth, or a thousand other things... but it's probably a good idea to save crap like that until you've actually gotten on the ground and started actually doing strength work. Find heavy stuff. Lift heavy stuff. That's all there is to it. Muscle growth, that is.

So... high-intensity cardio intervals, lifting heavy stuff. There ya go, everything ya need to fulfill your request in the best possible fashion.
