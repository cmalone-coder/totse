Help With A Problem
If you're not interested in hearing about the details of my injury and how I treat it, but wouldn't mind answering a few questions, just skip to the last paragraph.

I herniated two disks on the L4/L5 & L5/S1 vertebrae about fourteen years ago. Since then, I've had multiple failed surgeries to repair the herniations, leaving behind scar tissue that only exacerbated the irreparable nerve damage adjacent to those regions.

After every conceivable procedure (nerve blocks, epidural steroid injection, sacroiliac joint injection, facet joint injection, several bouts of physical and aqua therapy, chiropractic, acupuncture, Craniosacral therapy, even Reike), had been attempted and met with abject failure, I finally began my journey into the world of over-prescribing of a seemingly endless cycle of opiates, anti-inflammatories, muscle relaxers, sleeping pills, blood pressure and cholesterol medicine, etc. I think I've had every opiate in the book at one time or another. My current regimen of opiates consists of 120mg of Oxycodone, 40mg of Methadone, and 100Mcg of Fentanyl daily. I'm not as crazy about the Duragesic as the previous narcotic: 96mg of Hydromorphone per day, and instead of 40mg of Methadone, I was taking 200mg daily. Believe it or not, the change was prompted my the death of Heath Ledger. One of the nurses warned my Doctore that my opiate level was way off the chart and if I ever OD'ed, it might raise some legal eyebrows.

Truth is, because of my tolerance, the meds just barely controlled my back/buttocks and radiating leg pain. When they finished titrating down to my current dosage, I had to curtail my activities to some extent, which already didn't add up to much anyway. To top it off, I got hit by an even more intolerable infirmity about two years ago and it has steadily worsened.

It's called peripheral neuropathy and it stems from the nerve damage in my back. The disease started as a tingling in my toes that slowly turned into a burning sensation akin to leaving one's hands in the snow until the commencement of an icy, burning sensation. As bad as that was, it slowly spread to engulf the entire surface of both my feet and has been progressing up my ankles.

Walking becomes even more tortuous as the day turns goes by. It starts out as soreness (akin to standing in one place all day), but that's just the prelude to the next stage. I call it walking on rocks, which soon turns to into the feeling of walking on a few bones, to the final stage, which I liken to walking on nails, definitely the most painful of the four stages. As one might surmise, I stay off my feet as much as humanly possible to avoid the hell that is my existence.

But recently, I was going through the process of changing doctors to one a little closer to home, to combat the steady rise in gas prices. Upon meeting my first potential candidate, and now I'm getting to the crux of this mini opus, I described my symptoms. He calmly said, "Have you tried medicinal marijuana?" The question kind of caught me off guard. Being offered marijuana from a doctor whom I had just met was truly a surreal experience. Heck, I was just glad that he had agreed to take on my previously mentioned, opiate-heavy, med regimen without even reading my file (my previous doctor hadn't faxed it over yet, but I brought in my prescription bottles as proof that I was on the meds I mentioned earlier).

I told him I hadn't tried MJ. I said, "Would I have to smoke it? I've never even smoked cigarettes." He dropped the subject, saying to keep it in mind if my neuropathy became any more unmanageable.

Well that was three months ago and I have another visit coming up soon. I did some research and learned that MJ is commonly prescribed (in states where it's not prohibited) for my condition and I was thinking about giving it a try. So, here are my questions:

Does anyone use MJ for severe pain, and is it effective?
Is it possible to ingest it (in brownies for instance) and get the same effect?
Does MJ harm your lungs (or body) like cigarettes?
Is it true that you have to build up a tolerance before MJ is effective?

Thank in advance and sorry for the shoddy grammar. I had another sleepless night in hell, so if anyone wants me to give a shout out to Satan, let me know.

Re: Help With A Problem
Marijuana is one of the most effective substances to deal with pain, especially neuropathic pain that is not reponding well to opiates. What level of pain are you at before your first opiate dose of the day, and what level are you at after the dose starts working (x/10)?

You can use oral marijuana for pain relief, but smoking is the preferred route b/c you can titrate your dose easier, the onset of pain relief is faster, you need less marijuana, etc. Oral MJ is most often used in cases where the person either cannot smoke b/c of lung problems or when the person cannot smoke for some other reason. However, oral MJ lasts a lot longer than smoked MJ. That doesnt mean it provides better pain relief though. When I would use MJ for pain (pinched nerve, migraine headaches w/nausea) I would tend to use oral MJ and smoke some when I needed more pain relief. The oral MJ provides a baseline (like a long-acting opiate) and the smoked MJ is like a breakthrough dose.

Marijuana can harm your lungs but nowhere near anything like cigarettes. Smoking anything period can harm your lungs from inhaled smoke. With marijuana the damage is not clear, a study a few years back found that the incidence of lung cancer in marijuna-only smokers was only slightly above the incidence in the control group (nonsmokers), whereas smokers of both marijuana and tobacco had lower incidences of lung cancer than smokers of just tobacco. Even though smoking marijuana makes you cough, this isnt a direct indicator of lung damage. Basically, you would have to smoke a lot of marijuana for a very long time to be at risk for lung damamge I think.

You certainly dont need to build up a tolerance for MJ to be effective. Where that statement is coming from I think is that some people dont get high their first time smoking. Getting high doesnt always correlate with pain relief, I've gotten pain relief most of the time long before I got high (within 3-5 inhalations usually).

A good thing many pain patients like about marijuana actually is that there are fewer side effects than with opiates, and when you start to use marijuana for pain relief you will probably have better pain relief (especially with regard to the kind of neuropathic pain you have, though yours is not completely just nerve pain I think from your description of it) with the combination of marijuana and the dose of opiates you are used to than you would if you were to take more opiates. Basically if you work MJ in with your pain management regimen, it should (theoretically) allow you to stabilize your opiate dose rather than continue to increase it without compromising pain relief. MJ can also be helpful to use with opiates b/c it can sometimes combat opiate side effects such as nausea, appetite suppression, and sometimes can even help with sedation (depending on the variety of marijuana you'll be getting, sometimes the MJ can be stimulating sometimes sedating).

Would you be getting marijuana from a local dispensary, or would you have to go look for a dealer? If you would be getting it from a dispensary, you should definately give the MJ a try. It could improve your quality of life tremendously.
