End of Warfare (as we know it)?
So, over the past 60 years, warfare has changed drastically. Vietnam, Korea, Iraq (Bush1 and Bush2), Somalia, Israel, and other conflicts have all been in-between a well equipped, well trained force with an abundance of resources against a force with ill equipped soldiers using guerrilla warfare tactics.

So has the Allies vs Axis style of warfare ended? Where two countries or two teams of countries face off, as seen in WWI, WWII, and the other various European wars.

What do you think? Is guerrilla warfare the new warfare? Technology versus hit-and-run tactics?

Re: End of Warfare (as we know it)?
I don't think the days of two sides standing off against each other are over whatsoever, we're just not seeing it now. Should the political climate lean that way, I don't doubt that we could see another war of this type. Guerrilla warfare to me just seems to be the most efficient method of fighting back a small unarmed opposition has, especially wqhile on the defensive, and we certainly don't see the world's main aggressor (the USofA) picking fights with anyone of any real military power at the moment. The mobility and elusiveness of this type of opposition make it hard to predict when or where the next attack will come, giving them the element of surprise, which is a benefit when launching a strike of any type. The hit and run style of a guerrilla resistance make it easier to successfully pick away at the edges of a large force while maintaining relatively low losses. This is simply the best option they have for fighting back with what they've got...
