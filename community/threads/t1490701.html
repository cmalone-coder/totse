Re: Periods -
You can't bleed to death. The amount of bleeding though disturbing, isn't a direct problem to your health.

Depo shots stop of significantly diminish your period for 3 months (I believe). After that, most women experience the hemmorage style period, simply because the body has not released the built up menstural blood, and it sort of makes for 3 periods at once. I am not suggesting you get depo, but maybe someone on here who has utilized depo can tell you how they dealed with the mess.

Some women do bleed more than others. Birth Control should not effect that. Where you to talk with your gyno, there are probably hormones or some form of perscription which you can take which will reduce the production of menstrul fluids. As long as you are not planning on having a baby anytime soon, I am sure if this is available you can get it.

This is a totally out there possiblity, but you could talk to a doctor about getting ahold of the third in the RU (abortion pill) series. It is the drug which gets the females uterus to contract and relase the fetus and the surrounding contents. It is considered the equivilent of a period, and starts you off on the next month. If you could have your period each month, the flow would likely be a lot less. I dont know if that would have major reprocussions/is even possible, but i just thought i'd throw that kind of idea out there.

If wearing tampons is sore, either you are not wearing them correctly or there is something you need to see a gyno about. Just a question, but can you have sex without pain? If you can, you are probably putting them in at slightly the wrong angle. For me if the tampon is like 1 degree off, It's unbearable, so that could be a problem.

Two pads one on top of the other might cause some problems with side spillage, which is where the leakage is. If you are going to wear two, you might want to invest in two packs of supers, the big gal ones for the bottom and a regular size for the top. This way, when it leaks over the side of the regular, there is something else to protect your undies.

Your anemia could have an impact on the unregulated-ness of your periods. Many components that build up menstural blood will be shared with those which build up regular blood, so your body will try to hold on to the components for your bloodstream rather than for your period.

If no one can give you some really good advice, i would say just go to the gyno. There are many school/pphood type clinics which offer gynocologists for a lower cost. If you call one of them, they may be able to tell you if they could do it without contacting your insurance/ telling your family or a social worker.
