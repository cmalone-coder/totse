Re: Love, and what it means to you.
I've just broke up with someone very very dear to me who I loved... I think the loss has made me realise what love is... This is not an exhaustive list, but it's certainly how I felt about her... This type of love is different from the love of your family, but will obviously share some characteristics:

Love is the desire to spend time with someone, life's most finite resource. You could go to a restaurant, or sit and just enjoy their company, and still feel like you have a good time.

Love is the desire to make someone happy, no matter what it takes.

Love is an undying dedication to that person.

Love is a sacrifice, but worth it if they love you too; You care so strongly that if anyone wronged them, they've wronged you too. You'd die for that person, just so they could live.

Love is selflessness in its truest form; no reward is required from the person you love, except the hope that you've made them smile.

Love is being there for that person, in the highs and the lows.

Love is making someone feel wanted, someone feel special, and being proud that they are yours and yours only.

Love is vulnerability; You leave yourself open to so much damage on the trust they won't betray you. And that is why true heartbreak is so devastating.

I could go into a lot more, but... I can't think of any off the top of my head right now. I personally think it's rare to find someone you feel truely that strongly about; I guess only time will tell for me.

I hope this helps; this is what true love is, to me. Unless you've experienced it, you'll shit bricks at trying to comprehend the feeling of love, and loss.

Best regards,
Zaknafein.

Re: Love, and what it means to you.
Love is any of a number of emotions and experiences related to a sense of strong affection. The word love can refer to a variety of different feelings, states, and attitudes, ranging from generic pleasure ("I loved that meal") to intense interpersonal attraction ("I love my girlfriend"). This diversity of meanings, combined with the complexity of the feelings involved, makes love unusually difficult to consistently define, even compared to other emotional states.

As an abstract concept, love usually refers to a deep, ineffable feeling of tenderly caring for another person. Even this limited conception of love, however, encompasses a wealth of different feelings, from the passionate desire and intimacy of romantic love to the nonsexual emotional closeness of familial and Platonic love to the profound oneness or devotion of religious love. Love in its various forms acts as a major facilitator of interpersonal relationships and, owing to its central psychological importance, is one of the most common themes in the creative arts.
