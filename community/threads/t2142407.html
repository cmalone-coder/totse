What is this acid lyse and carmelizing you speak of and how would one go about doing it?

Two different things... 'n I'm not surprised that "lysis" turned up cell lysis in protien extraction, but that's not what we're doing here...

Starch (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch) is basically a catch-all term for multiunit carbohydrates. Basically, it's a crapload of sugars polymerized with what is usually an esterified bond.

Attack it with acid, it tends to fall back apart into simple sugars. In fact, one can demonstrate this to one's self pretty easy with a slice of bread 'n a glass of lemonade, which makes for very convenient eating. ;) Take a bite of bread, a sip of lemonade, 'n feel the bread just instantly melt.

The downside of this is that diphenhydramine is also an ester, between whatever godforsaken dimethylalkylamine it is and diphenymethylenol. The reason there's still hope to destroy the starch through acid lysis is that - as demonstrated by the bread & lemonade example - lysis of complex carbohydrates tends to be almost instantaneous in fairly gentle conditions which other things could withstand with ease.

Carmelization, otoh, is a fancy way of saying "KILL IT WITH FIRE!!!!" - at least when it comes to the sugars. It's the same caramel that's on your caramel apples and other candies, and the process is fairly simple - when you heat a sugar, it turns brown 'n undergoes chemical changes. I'm guessing these involve carbonization with the elimination of water, but point being, it's not quite the same sugary substance it was.

This is extremely popular with disabling methylcellulose, which forms a bitchin' goo on contact with water. I have no idea what it would do with corn starch, but it's worth considering - and like I said, whole boxes of corn starch to experiment on are fairly cheap. ;)
