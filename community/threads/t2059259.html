If things are getting 'dificult' after a month and a half, just think real serioulsy about gradually moving on.

One of the biggest mistakes we (both 12 and 55 y/o folks) make is to try too hard to make a 'relationship' work.

"What can I do to make her like me more?"
"How can I make him stay interested in me?"

It is precisely the above kind of mindsets that now puts the divorce rate at over fifty percent.

If she doesn't like you more... move on
If he can't stay interested in you... move on

Breaking up is hard to do. No one knows that better than I do. It hurts; it hurts a LOT.

But honestly... what are your options? You break up now, or you break up later.

If it doesn't click, it just doesn't click. If either has to 'work' at making it work now, it will only get harder later on. No question, it is a lot easier to put out a bit more effort now, no matter what the sacrifice may seem like, but in most cases it is just prolonging and enhancing the ultimate agony.

Look at me: I had misgivings early on. It was just easier to let her move in than to break up. It was just easier to let her go ahead with the wedding plans than to break up. It was just easier to let her control most of my life than to divorce her.

Long story short... 18 years after I said "I Do"... I said "I don't no more"

She left with nearly $1/2 M. I filed for bankruptcy (no kids, alimony or anything)

Sorry for the rant... but the bottom line is really quite simple:

Do NOT try to make something 'work' just because it will make you feel better today, or make you think you will hurt less tomorrow. The things you do today will, or at least can, affect the rest of your lives. You cannot make someone love you if they don't. They cannot make you love them if you don't. If you love someone and they love you, for god's sake just go for it. With that at your side, you face a somewhat better than the 50/50 chance of it working out. If not, I still do get a referral commission from my divorce attorney, so let me know, eh?
