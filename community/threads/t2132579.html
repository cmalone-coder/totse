There's a reason why you think your communication skills are shit: people are fucking boring. Most people are looking for their own personal jester, someone to crack jokes and offer petty observations and commentaries on life's nuances to entertain them. At the same time, most people are also craving attention from those around them, and they get this attention by being entertaining to others. It's like a trade extroverts have worked out; they trade jokes, bullshit talk, and confirmation for attention, and vice-versa.

Frankly, I hate dry, forced conversation. If you aren't interested in someone, I don't see why you should push it just to avoid silence. Silence is nice. I don't think I've never been annoyed by silence.
However, if you are insistent on carrying on a conversation with someone, even if you don't particularly find them interesting, I can only think of one piece of advice: ask questions.
* By asking questions you control the conversation. The topic depends on what you ask, so if you want you can steer the topic towards something you know thoroughly.
* People generally like talking about themselves.
Start off with just generic bullshit questions ("What do you do? Do you sing/play any instruments? Have you seen *movie*?") and the more questions they answer, the more specific your questions can be ("Oh, you like Donnie Darko too? Did you know they're making a sequel?"). Just remember that he who asks the questions controls the conversation.

Or you know, you could date one of those "LiEK OMg!! grrrlllzz". I personally don't talk good, and I like to surround myself with people who talk a lot, so that all I have to do is ask the occasional question and let them go at it.

Both of these statements have more meaning than you might think. One, is that associating with and surrounding yourself with those type of people "Like he said this and she said that and OMG can you believe..." those people you know who I'm talking about goddamnit is a good way to get the practice. If you're not one of those people who has good communication skills, then practicing on those type of people is very crucial. Because it somehow happens to even out in that the shy people have interesting and relevant things to talk about but don't want to for whatever reasons (bad communication/social skills), and the people who have ridiculously good communication skills talk about celebrities' lives and other retarded shit.

And the part you need to learn is not being interesting, that comes with your personality and your style, what you know about, etc. You just need to learn to make small talk and continue conversations even if you don't want to, and all the things that go along with that. Pretending like you're interested is a key skill, and that involves also asking questions even if you don't give a shit. You also need to make eye contact, give good body language, and practice timing and delivery, especially speaking loudly and clearly. However there is no cut and dry formula for these things. We can't say, "Say that if she says this and then wait 2.5 seconds and react this way" because planning conversations out doesn't really work like that. You can't transition from one topic to another too roughly either. So learn the finer intricacies of conversation, to the point where you'll be able to talk about stupid, boring shit with people you don't really enjoy talking to. Then, imagine how easy it will be to carry a conversation with your friends or someone you're crushing on.

The other part that's meaningful here is that you don't have to do all the work or be constantly talking to have a good conversation, or to be considered interesting. Like this poster said, he often just interjects some things and asks a few questions. If you haven't noticed, some people love talking (I am once I get started), so sometimes all it takes is a question or two to get them going. If not, then it takes more on your part. But you can't expect to have a good convo if you're not into it because it takes two people for that. Anyway the point is that you don't have to always be talking to be "cool" or even just not awkward. Just say shit when you have shit to say and stay within your boundaries. Don't talk about things you know nothing about and don't say anything you know is stupid. Other than that just chip in when you can and try and let some of your personality and charm shine through. I think you might be a little caught up on this concept of being a good conversationalist without noticing that it's not absolutely necessary to have a good social life.

Shit, people could end up loving you because you're not so damn outspoken and you don't start spewing random opinions about shit you don't even know about. That's where listening will really be important. You can start and continue conversations without even listening to people(honestly, try it sometime). Stupid people won't even notice as long as you give off the correct body cues and facial expressions, etc. But if you listen to what people are actually trying to say, you'll be able to truly connect with someone you might not even expect. Note: your conversational partner has to actually be saying something for this to be true, so don't apply that to everybody.

Anyway that ended up being kinda long and disorganized but I hope you take something from it.
