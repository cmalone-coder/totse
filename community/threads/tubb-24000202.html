Re: AUTO/\MANUAL?
I like manuals, just cause it gives me more control of the car, and it gives me something to do. Usually an automatic adds about $500+ to the price of a new car and sucks up around 20% or more power until the converter locks up.

I like other people having automatics because it makes me money. Especially since this is a somewhat rural area and people tow shit and play in the mud pretty regularly. I can rebuild an automatic in about 7 or 8(book time is 9-13) hours and can make around $150 or so if I do it at work. If I do it at home it takes me a little bit longer, but I make around $250 or so after parts.

Manuals will usually last forever because any abuse that will damage it will tear up a clutch or flywheel long before it gets bad enough to hurt the transmission. About the only thing they can't handle is being a moron with the shifter and forcing it into the wrong gear at the wrong speed.

To show you how much transmission shops rip people off. It takes a good tech under a days work and about $150 in parts. Yet they'll usually charge around $700+ for a typical automatic. They'll usually have a $7 an hour guy removing and replacing it, then a tech making $15 or so in the back rebuilding it.

Re: AUTO/\MANUAL?
It used to be that a manual would get a shitload better mileage than an automatic would. With the advances in computer control now, most automatics don't lose as much power and can sometimes beat a manual on gas mileage.

Even 18 wheelers are going to automatics alot more now. I've talked to some people that say with an automatic the trucks can get around 7.5-8 mpg as opposed to 6.5-7. It might not sound like much, but when you figure they run 12,000 miles a month the difference can be several hundred dollars. Several hundred dollars for hundreds of trucks for a large company is quite a bit of money. A few years ago when I was a diesel mechanic, an automatic was out of the question because of power loss, weight and mileage.
