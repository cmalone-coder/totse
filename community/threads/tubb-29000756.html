Both young men were Honours Students in the Department of Econometrics and Business Statistics.

The Monash University gunman was a member of the Springvale Pistol Club. An almost exclusively Vietnamese club.

As such, range officers and instructors, probably DO give range commands in Vietnamese.

BUT.

Before a firearms licence can be issued, one must pass an exam in firearms safety.

In my own experience this is usually conducted in English.

I have serious doubts that any police examiner would consider an interpreter here. (However, if I am wrong in this last matter, please correct me).

Victorian State firearms laws became some of the most stringent in Australia under the Labor administration of former Premier John Cain, and were further tightened under the Kennet Government.

I am somewhat more conversant with New South Wales State firearms laws. However, since 1996, there has been much greater uniformity in National firearms control policy.

Under New South Wales law you must first obtain a firearms licence. This involves a detailed examination of the applicants knowledge of firearms safety.

The applicant must also prove "genuine need" to own a gun.
(Membership of a Target Shooting Club, is recognised as "genuine need" under present State laws).

Once all of the above proceedures have been satisfactoraly completed, THEN, you may apply for a licence.

This takes on average a period of 5-6 weeks to process, including the mandatory 28 day "cooling off" period and all Police records checks.

Once you have been granted your firearms licence, you may apply for a pistol licence. This usually involves attending at least 7 lectures and supervised range instruction, spread over a period of 7 weeks.

Having passed this training, you may now apply to have your licence upgraded to a category "H" licence. Once again, this will take an average of 5-6 weeks to process, and pass registry.

Now that you have your licence, MOST clubs will insist upon imposing a calibre restriction, (usually .22 rimfire), on the type of pistol you may use during the "probationary stage" of your club membership.

You will now need an official "permit to aquire". Once again, this will take an average of 5-6 weeks to pass registry.

Under New South Wales law you must still undergo a mandatory 28 day "cooling off" period for EACH susequent aquisition.

Under Victorian State law,however, second and subsequent firearms permits are issued after 7 days.

Let's look at the time frame here:

1.) Minimum of 5 weeks to licence.

2.)Add 7 weeks for pistol training.

3.)Add another 5 weeks for first permit.

4.) Add one week for EACH of 6 subsequent pistols.

This makes for theoretical MINIMUM of 23-24 weeks to become legally licenced to possess 7 handguns!

Even if this guy put in his application the moment he set foot on Australian soil, I find this whole scenario hard to credit.

Again, this case seems to offer more questions than answers.

The Springvale Pistol Club, to which the perpetrator belonged, is described as a "wall-to-wall" Vietnamese Club.

The perpetrator has such a poor command of English, that one cannot help but wonder if their is some "ghosting" going on at the Springvale Pistol Club, whereby, an English speaking member of the Club, (possibly an office bearer), cuts through the mountain of paperwork for new applicants, and possibly fronts up for them at the Firearms Registry. Giving them all they need to pass the criteria.

If, as I am begining to suspect, that some senior officials of the Springvale Pistol Club are corrupt. Then I think that the Club, it's officials, and their relationship with Victorian Firearms Registry Officers should publically investigated.

PS

If anyone here can find out a little bit more on this matter, I would appreciate it if they would post any links, or refer me to any news articles that they may have come across.
