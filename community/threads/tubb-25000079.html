Here's what's what [finally, someone didn't beat me to the punch ]:

*I stand corrected: it seems, Tryp, that you posted while I was composing my reply. Boo! beat again* SoO some of what I wrote will be redundant because Tryp. has already said it. I'm not going to go through and remove it all: too dreary a task at the moment.


There are two basic types of fundamental particles one is the Lepton the other is the quark.

Leptons are either neutral or carry a charge of one whole electric unit. They have a spin value of 1/2.

There are three charged leptons, all are have a negative charge. The lightest of them is the electron [the other two are the muon and the tau]. Each of them have an associated neutral charged lepton [neutrino. thus electron - neutrino, muon neutrino, etc.]


[I'm not even going to go into anti-leptons here.]

As for the part they might play in cold dark mater, I'm think you are confused since leptons are relatively low in mass. Even the tau, which is 3,700 times the mass of an electron, is still not all that much when compared to the mass of a quark.

I think you must be thinking of the Higgs particle, no? Top quark, perhaps?


On to Quarks:[and I don't mean a bar on DS9]

Quarks have a spin of 1, and either a charge of 2/3 [the Up quark] or -1/3 [the Down quark] [There's also the Top, the Bottom, The Strange, and the Charmed quarks. But the original question only asked about the atom and that's only the Up and Down quarks. Otherwise, this could go on forever - even by the standards of my posts ]

Quarks combine to make up Baryons and Mesons.

Baryons, which are protons and neutrons [and corresponding anti-particles], are made of three quarks, but the total charge always has to equal either +1, 0, or -1 [which would be proton, neutron, anti-neutron, anti-proton].

In addition to spin and charge, quarks also have a property called 'color', which I'm also not going to go into.

One last thing I will mention are gluons. These are massless particles that transmit the force that binds quarks together, which is also known as the strong nuclear force.
