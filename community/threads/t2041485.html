Re: Well, that's the end.
You've decided that the distance is too far for you to continue to date, and at the same time you desire for her not to return to her old habits.

You can't really have your cake and eat it too. If you want to remove yourself from that part of your life, it's not fair for you to not want her to drink or smoke again.

I do find myself and others turning to drugs in times of stress, but also don't assume that just because she starts again she is going to hurt herself, or even that she started again because you left her. She may have just stopped doing those things out of respect for you and will return to them since you are no longer a part of her life.

If she has alcoholism issues, then that's another matter. She can't be addicted to pot so I wouldn't worry too much about that. But I'm not here to start a fight about the effects of drugs.

You must realize that she is going to be bitter, obviously. You have left her and she is unhappy, and from the sounds of it you guys had a really good, long relationship. She may also feel that you are leaving her just to have a good time in college.

My advice would be to continue to talk to her and express interest in her friendship even if she does not return the favor. You may want to put some distance though, or else the old feelings may come back in one of the parties, as it has for me in the past. Keep it friendly, and keep it constant, and more likely than not she will move on past loving you and see you as a friend.

If not? Well, that's life. Like another poster mentioned, it's not going to be easy. Whether or not she will hurt herself is really her choice at this point, because you have no more say in her life. But again, don't start thinking she's abusing herself because she takes up drinking or smoking again, she may have just stopped out of respect for you.
