Try on these shoes...
I've created a hypothetical scenario, and I'd like to know what you fellas would do/feel/think if you were in these shoes...

(Don't read more into the story than there is, just do your best to put yourself into it)

<UL TYPE=SQUARE>

You meet a beautiful, loving woman. Your ideal in every way. After a year or so you marry, 12 months later you have your first child.

Driving home from a visit to friends one evening you fall asleep at the wheel, your car goes off the road and rolls several times. You and your child are okay, but your wife is badly injured.

She spends months in the hospital, comatose. When she finally wakes up she remembers nothing, not you, not your child, nothing. Because she doesn't remember she no longer loves you, she doesn't want to go home with you. Everything is unfamiliar to her, she treats you like a stranger.

You give her space, and even though your heart is breaking you do your best to wear a smile in hopes that you can work your charm again.... it's like starting from scratch, only harder because this time she's different.

Because things are different, pressured, and stressed she begins to develop the idea that yours isn't a 'good' relationship. She can't remember the good times, so all she has to base her feelings/choices on is that she has a husband she doesn't know, who isn't exactly himself because his whole world has completely fallen to pieces.

And all this time there's that nagging thought in the back of your mind, it's your fault. You crashed the car.

6 months later things haven't changed... it's like she hasn't even considered the possibility of rekindling your relationship. She's still the same beautiful person you fell in love with, which just makes it that much more painful. She cannot, or will not let you get close enough to even stand a chance.

And you still love her with all your heart.

</UL>

How does this effect you?

What do you do?

Give up?

Move on?

Keep hoping?

I'm hoping this has nothing at all to do with the help-me-decide-how-to-kill-myself thread, and is instead really a worthwhile thought experiment.

quote:How does this effect you?

I'd feel guilty. I'd feel like a complete failure at being a father and a husband, because (egalitarian ideologies aside for the moment) I'd feel like I failed to protect my family and the woman I loved. I mean, thank god our child wasn't hurt, but that was just blind luck -- all 3 of us could've been killed or worse. I'd be worrying about what life would be like as a single parent -- whether or not I could hack it as a widower/father, along with how the hell was I going to go about explaining the situation to our child.

quote:What do you do?

"If you love something, let it go." Truer words were never spoken. If I loved my wife enough to promise to honor her, I'd never think about forcing her to do anything she didn't want to do. If it was in her heart to leave, I'd have to let her.

It would probably destroy me to do it, but I wouldn't have any choice in light of my feelings. I'd tell her that I loved her, figure out someplace she could go, even help her pack her bags. It's impossible to love somebody and at the same time act selfishly

quote:Give up?

More like "come to an acceptance of the situation," as that's what it would be. On one level, the woman I cared about would be gone. As if she'd actually died in that crash. It'd be like suddenly having her long-lost twin replacing her, and although it might look and feel the same, it wouldn't be her.

quote:Move on?

Maybe in time. You don't just fall in love with someone, get married, have a child together, and have those feelings die overnight. Maybe for the sake of our child, especially if my wife didn't want to stick around to take care of it, I'd find a good friend or somebody new after a time who could also be my wife and the mother of my/our child(ren). But that kind of person usually comes around only once.

quote:Keep hoping?

'Not my place to hope for that much. I couldn't keep telling my kid, "Don't worry, Mommy will be coming home some day once she figures things out." It'd be cruel to our kid and cruel to myself, to be perfectly honest. I'd try my best to accept the situation as-is and not hope for even a partial return to the way things were.

Good topic, Sarc. Got my brain working.
