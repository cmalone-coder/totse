Re: Headspace?
AFAIK, this only applies to HK rifles, namely the 20, 30, 50 and 90 series. While the placement of the barrel in relation to the reciever is important (only let an experienced gunsmith remove and reinstall the barrel), headspace is as follows: Its the distance between the bolt head (in battery) and the barrel face. You only have about 1/32 or 1/64 of an inch to play with.
Headspace is important because of the 'roller-lock' mechanism used on these HK rifles (including mp5/94). Too little and you won't be able to pull the bolt back. Too much and the bolt will unlock prematurely during semi-auto fire.

I'm not sure if this applies to any other type of rifle, but for what its worth, thats what "headspace" is. If you want more info, check here: http://www.hkpro.com/Internal.htm

Headspace is the relationship between the breach face and the case neck area of the barrel. If there is too much head space the neck of the case could expand and in extreme cases it could blow up the gun. Too little and the bolt may not close.

Re: Headspace?
well I read this I might as well let everyone know what headspace is.

headspace is a measurement of the catridge seating in the chamber position right before the bullet is fired the amount of "play" forward or backward when the bolt is closed on a catrige. Target headspace is the point where the bolt face is in a distance with the barell where the bullet is compleatly seated but imposible to shift forward or backwards without adjusting the space between barell and bolt face.

Excessive headspace can lead to everything from misfires to a full-on destroyed reciver (yes it would "blow up" after the trigger was pulled)

Not enough headspace will cause less than total chambering 95% of the time the weapon will not drop the hammer when the chamber is not 100% closed meaning an unfiring gun, however some guns that will fire with a bolt less than 100% closed (such as older guns or any open-bolt gun like an uzi or mac-10)will either destroy the brass or "blow up" upon firing.

weapons to watch headspace in are weapons with non-factory or gunsmith adjusted distance between barell and bolt face. rifles where the barell are screwed on are most common to be requiring to be adjusted properly with a new bolt. AR-15's M-16's bolt action rifles ect.

weapons not typically to be concerned with headspace have universal standard barell space and bolt space, 1911's 92fs' revolvers ect.

headspace is measured in go/no-go plugs shaped like cartriges they are slightly above or below cartrige standards meaning a rifle that holds a "no-go" headspace guage needs to be re-adjusted and NOT fired a rifle that chambers a go guage and not a no-go guage is capable of safely firing ammuntion although there is a broad range of being "ok to shoot" so a properly headspaced gun maybe adjusted even more closley to a specific grade of ammo for the ultimate accuracy the gun can produce although this will limit you to using only a certain grade of ammo in your gun (no more cheap soviet union ammo for your high tolerance chamber)

well I hope I got the point across. questions?
