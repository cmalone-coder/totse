Re: Potassium Permaganate
Resolution isn't hard. All you need is an optically active acid. Like tartaric acid for instance. l-Tartaric acid is available pretty fucking easily. Racemate freebase is dissolved in a generous amount of anhydrous methanol. Whenever the freebase is salted with an acid that is optically active, and the optical isomer of the acid matches the one of the freebase molecule, it will no longer be soluable in methanol.
So neutralize the methanol with l-tartaric acid. This forms d-methamphetamine l-tartrate, and l-methamphetamine l-tartrate. Place this solution in the freezer, and the l-methamphetamine l-tartrate will precipitate. Filter this out, and continue by concentrating the mother liquor. After a fraction of the mother liqour is gone from concentrating (let's say, ummm, a fourth), freeze again and filter out anything that precipitates.
Once the mother liqour is concentrated, dilute with water and basify to pH 12. Extract like usual and crystallize with acid of your choice (I prefer HCl, most potent of the salts by weight).

Also, if it was made from ephedrine, pseudo, or PPA, dextro isomers will dominate the product, because of the stereo specifity of the synthesis.

Guys listen up: Decarboxylation of phenylalanine would yield you expensive (and inactive) phenethylamine. You guys are thinking of the reduction of phenylalanine. (you don't want to get rid of that carbon) It is tougher than it looks BTW. The KRz synth is bullshit or so I've heard.

A school level of understanding of the synths would be around third year or so, mind you I forced myself to learn before I ever entered secondary school. You need to know terms like washing, dean stark adapter, re crystallization, trituration(not titration) reflux, and the theorys behind vacuum distillation. Oh and not neccisary but what is a Kugel Rohr apparatus?

A good book would be James Zubricks O. Chem lab survival manual. I read it after I learned everything on my own and found it to be very basic, it would help someone to start. Any other "practical" organic text that could be found in a university library(or college in the US) would be a great help.
