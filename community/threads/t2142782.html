Today I had a shocking relization about ugly cars
So as I was riding along in my mother's Audi TT 3.2 Quattro I noticed everyone was staring at us, even with an M5 E60 in the lane right next to us (Which I happened to drooling over ), and even though there was a 5L V10 4-door supercar right there, everyone was looking at the not-so-fast, flashy, sub-supercar TT.

And that got me thinking about the looks of cars. Now, surly we all know that the BMW M5 E60 might be one of the ugliest things ever to set foot (err... tire) on this planet, it is one of the best, as well as one of the biggest engineering marvels. Yet the designers couldn't be fucked to make it look good. And for a long while I thought that it was just because they were either

A.) Lazy
or
B.) Out of money

But then I thought, "What if they did this on purpose?" "What if they aren't supposed to be pretty, for the purpose of being cool?" Any car that's new, flashy, exciting, and has lots of angles and sudden changes is a car that people will usually buy to show off. Let's face it, it's the truth. And I'm not talking about muscle cars, those will always be cool no matter what, I'm talking things like the Mercedes SLK-280. It's a piece of steaming shit, I know, I've been in one. It's underpowered, and it feels loose in the bends. However, the looks say otherwise.

So, I've come to the conclusion that car manufacturers don't care if a car is ugly if it's supposed to be a car. They design it to be flashy and gaudy (gawdy?) if they want to sell them by the masses, but not necessarily as a car. Things like the Audi TT, the Corvette (Well, not the Z06 or whatever that new one is, just the base model) and, I think most of all, the Ford Mustang. It used to be a car that anyone who was cool owned, but now the people who own them are middle aged men/women who are trying to look cooler than they are.

Let's look at the Volkswagen Jetta, or the Honda Civic (Although that's taking a rapid slide towards where the Mustang is classified in my book), or the Mazda B-Series. Why do I include these cars? Let's look over it.

The Jetta. It's luxurious, reliable, decently quick (If you don't get the shitty 4-cyl), and it gets decent mileage. Let's think now, it's not very pretty. I mean, you could live with it, but let's be honest, it isn't a beauty. But it does what a car is supposed to do, which is take people and things where they're supposed to go in a relatively efficient and quick way.

Now let's look at the Honda Civic. It does the exact same thing as the Jetta, it's pretty much the same, although it's on a rapid slope to becoming a shitty marketing icon like the Mustang is.

And now the Mazda B-Series. I should start off by saying that I usually hate pickup trucks. Well, I don't hate them, I hate the people that buy them as big as they can so that have a larger pen()r. Now that's a shitty waste. A Ford F250 for the daily commute to work? Please. Now the Mazda B-series is a pickup for someone who actually needs a pickup. It's not huge, it's not stupidly flared, it's just a good solid pickup truck, doing what it's supposed to do.

This has somewhat flared from my original topic, so I guess what I'm trying to say is that a car that's not gaudy and flashy and stupid is a true car, something that people buy as a car, and not as an extension of their penises. I'm not saying that the ONLY cars that aren't like this are good daily commuters like the VW golf, things such as the M5 (as was mentioned before) is a car that just wants to be good at what it does, and not be all flash and no substince.

Thoughts?
