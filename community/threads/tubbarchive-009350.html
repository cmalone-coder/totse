Seems that copper oxychloride decomposes at relitively low temps, about 140, though this may just be a cracking of the molecule. It is used primarily in 'sugar blues' with a strong oxidiser (KClO3), lactose, cu oxychloride, chlorine donor, resin fuel, and dextrin to bind. These all burn relitively cool, and thus color saturation remains good. Medium burn speeds also make it attractive. I cannot quote any exact ratios for you.

Part of a quoted post from the old APC, by Crazy Swede: "...Many modern formulas rely on copper oxychloride (Cux(OH)yClz) which sometimes can produce very good blue stars without any problems with compatibility or hygroscopicity. But because the material can vary in structure and copper content, some brands will work nicely while others might produce lousy results. Actually, not less than 18 different structures of copper oxychloride are known! The most common seems to be Natural Atacamite (Cu2(OH)3Cl) and Brunswick Green (CuCl2 x 3 Cu(OH)2 x 4 H2O).

Some of the best and cheapest blues are based on using a mixture of both copper oxychloride and basic copper carbonate. Some people believe that the reasons for success in this combination are the additional chlorine from the copper oxychloride and the flame expanding property of the basic copper carbonate (from the carbon dioxide that is produced when the carbonate dissociates!)."
