Re: Medication or counselling?
I really can't stress how important I think it is that you do start making the decisions about getting some help. Going to your GP is tough, I know, but I think you'll find once you've been it'll be a huge weight off your mind, and you'll feel positive that you did something about it. I had counselling for a few months before I even went to my GP, and I have to say that although I was very skeptical and cynical about it, it really did help in the long run. It was a lot harder work than I expected, and sometimes very emotional, which is hard when you're with a total stranger. My university offers a free professional counselling service for students and staff, maybe your school / Uni offers something similar? If not you will be able to get referred by your GP.

They way people react to antidepressants can really vary. From my experience, and from what my counsellor said, most people that go on them find them to be helpful, and wonder why they didnt go on them sooner! The initial side affects can be annoying (for the first week or two), (for me, nausea, loss of appetite, anxiety). Once adjusted to them though, the only real side affect I've noticed is a general apathy... they can make you feel a bit numb. The low moods are reduced, but also the highs, which is really frustrating. Like I said, this is personal experience, and I'm sure it varies for other people lots. I've had a relatively positive experience with them.

You said you don't want your mum to find out any counselling / meds. Mind if I ask why this is? Obviously I know nothing of your relationship with your parents. I see no reason why they'd really have to find out about any treatment you decide to have if you don't want them to. Have you managed to talk to any family members / friends about all this? For me, that was by far the hardest part, I felt embarassed and almost like I'd let everybody down. But once you can recognise that depression is an illness, not a personality flaw, and you can talk to someone close to you about it, it makes it a lot easier to get help and support.

I'm no doctor, but I hope my experiences can be helpful to you in some way.

Much of what your saying reminds me of how I felt a few months ago. It wasn't until I'd had a couple of months of counselling and had started on Prozac that I could actually really admit to myself that I was clinically depressed. Everything in my life is fine really; I'm in Uni studying a course I should find interesting, have some good friends, get on with my folks, a lovely boyfriend, and I thought I was just being a whiney little bitch when I felt so bad.

One thing I've really learnt about depression, mostly by talking to people in a similar situation to me, is that you WON'T necessarily feel like crap all the time. I always found that the terrible lows would often be followed by highs, which could last a few hours, days, or even a week. And it was during these times that the self doubt would kick in, and I wouldn't be able to believe there was anything really wrong. In a way, thats what counselling helped me the most with. When I first went, I was expecting to be told to maybe take some of the classes they offer (they do like, low mood management, self esteem classes and the like) and maybe get a couple of sessions with a counsellor. Despite turning up feeling ok, I was offered the longest course they can give (though its a fairly limited service.) In some ways, that totally freaked me out, but I also found it really validating in a way.

As with the Doctor, they take this kind of thing extremely seriously, especially if they learn that you're thinking of harming yourself (I wasn't) - thats one of the main things they'll probably try to find out if you go to them. My GP said it would be unusual to put someone straight on meds, (plus I was really against the idea), so he told me to go away for a couple of weeks, have a think, see how things went, and go back if nothing changed. Which I did, resulting in my giving the meds a try. At the end of the day, its entirely your decision what course of action you choose with it, and theyll be likely to talk through all the options with you.

I'm sorry that you don't feel like you can talk to anybody. I can sympathise, it is extremely hard to do. Perhaps once you start some kind of treatment you'll find it easier?
