Re: compressed universes?
Okay. If there is an infinite (or very, very, very, very large) number of these Universes which are super-compressed and exist within the matter (and maybe anti-matter) of this Universe, then of course since chaos in space reigns, their laws of physics would equally be chaotic. But 'randomly placed'? I think that is not giving enough credit to this machine we call the Universe (or should I say multiverse, although that would again be dealing with a multi-dimensional theory, and we're not there right now). Now, of course one might say that the very nature of chaos is random, but after reading some literature on it, one comes to the decision that there indeed is order and purpose in the phenomenon.
It is highly unlikely that life evolved only on this planet in our Universe. This has been determined. Then there must, I think, be life in each of the super-compressed Universes, although probably not the sort of life we are used to.

Anyway, quantum teleportation is fascinating stuff. Although at this point it seems that only microscopic things can be teleported, the idea of doing this to a macroscopic object is exciting, however poses some ethical problems (ie. this would require one to destroy the original and reproduce in different spot/would the consciousness also be transferred etc.). Why this? Perhaps when the teleportation of macroscopic objects becomes possible, re-assemble can be made on a microscopic level, thus allowing further examination of the micro-universes. There are many holes in this theory that I know of already, but it's an interesting possibility nonetheless...
