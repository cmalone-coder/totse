Where the hell did you get that figure? Let me break it down for you (I know a bit about genetics)

Let's assume that your dad has a recesive gene for Hutchenson's (first nasty recesive single gene disease I could think of). Now that would mean that you have a 50% chance of getting that recesive gene. Wether or not you have it, you remain healthy because there your mother does not carry it. This applies to your sister too.

Now if you and your sister have a kid, that kid stands a 25% chance of inheriting that gene from you, and a 25% chance from your sister. That means there is a 43.75% chance of becoming a carier (one gene) a 6.25% chance of getting the disease (gene from each) and a 50% chance of not having it at all.

Now there are lots of types of defective genes out there, thousands of known genetic diseases. Odds are that you carry the genes for several of them. With each defective gene (or genes for multi-gene diseases) you multiply that figure (actualy you multiply the chance of not catching it, but that's not the point).

Point is that the odds get worse the more blood you have in common. With first cousins the odds of getting a genetic disease that way (single gene calculations are much easier) are 75% of not having the gene, 23.4375% of being a carrier, and 1.5625% for having the disease.
