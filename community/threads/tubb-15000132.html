Re: clearing up Catholic contradictions
I have answered this. Basically, it can be summed up like this.

Humans cannot enjoy both free will and a world free from sin. Once God chose to create human beings with free will, it was up to humans as to whether there was sin or not. God created the possibility of evil, but it was humans who actualized
that potentiality.

God might tolerate certain short-range evils in order to achieve long-range goods that humans cannot foresee.

Imagine a bear in a trap, and a hunter who
wants to liberate him. The hunter tries and fails to win the bear's confidence, so he has no choice but to shoot the bear full of tranquilizers to knock him out so he can be freed of the trap. The terrified bear thinks the hunter is trying to kill him. He doesn't understand that the hunter is acting out of
compassion.

God does the same thing to us sometimes, and we can't comprehend why he does it any more than the bear can understand the motivations
of the hunter. We simply have to trust God.

Imagine the tranquilizers as the test, and the trap as the filth of the world. What we think is harming us may actually be doing us good. God's reason for his action is his own.
It can't be defined by anything else than this: His. The tranquilizers either break us, or teach us a valuable lesson. They destroy those who do not learn from them and it is they are doomed to repeat that failure until it is corrected. The others, those who learn, associate the trap with the horrible sting and pain of the darts and so, will turn from the trap and live the life that God has created for them.

Yes, God already knows the outcome of the trials which we face, but what good is it if he doesn't let that person experience it? Just because he knows doesn't mean that he interacts directly with his creation. Its like playing a fighting game with an invincibility cheat. You know the outcome, your victory. but you still wantt the other player to experience it. (REAL BAD ANALOGY: comparing theology with video games)


i hope that this clears things up a bit
