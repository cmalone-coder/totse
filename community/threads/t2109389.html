The unfortunate truth about Heroin is that, like another poster mentioned, it's with you forever. I will only go back to the Harrison Act in history, but from that time we (humanity in the West) have learned a great deal about Heroin/Opioid addiction. Cold turkey does not work. Sure, some people get off and stay off forever. The vast, overwhelming majority, do not. Rehabs and most hospitals (who either make you go cold turkey off Opioids with no meds or with symptomatic medication i.e. Quinine for leg cramps, Ibuprofen for aches and pain, Ativan/Klonopin/Valium/Librium for anxiety, Trazadone for insomnia, Compazine for upset stomach, Loperamide for diahrea, Clonidine etc etc etc) have almost a 100% failure rate in terms of lifelong abstinence from Opioids. There are physical changes to ones metabolism, brain and other parts/functioning of the body and mind as a result of Opioid addiction, many of which are not completely understood yet. Maintanence with Methadone, Buprenorphine, LAAM, Morphine, Dihydrocodeine/DHC118, Hydromorphone, Heroin, etc is the best approach. Any Opioid maintanence therapy is better than none. Tramadol maintanence would be more successful than cold turkey rehab.

Good luck if you can quit for good. But keep in mind what Bill Burroughs wrote in Junky, "No one can hit the skids faster or harder than a self cured junkie".

Meta, I had read a few articles that theorized iatogenic Opioid dependant people (i.e. people who become dependant and/or addicted to opioids due to medical treatment and/or a medical condition) do not have withdrawals and cravings as severe as those who are addicted and dependant. Using Heroin to kill legitimate physical pain as the primary driver in your use is a lot different psychologically than chasing that high. Also, people who were tapered and taken off Opioids for chronic pain report lifelong 'cravings' and pseudowithdrawal syndrome to a much lesser degree.
