"..... Using the knife, pry out the rubber window sealant that is in place in the frame all around the window. ....."

For old houses and crappy windows, that may work. Only place I've seen rubber seals on windows was on some trailers. And most competent people who have windows using silicone glazing compounds to seal and hold their windows in place also use glaziers points (little metal triangles that are jammed into the wooden window frame that stick out in front of the glass to hold it in place). That stuff can't be put back. We won't even get into those who use a glazing putty that hardens in place.

Since his assertion is that they're rich, they will likely have energy efficient windows/frames. That technique will not work on them. The frames cover the glass (almost always tempered) far enough that removing the glass is a dead option.

"..... It will not trigger any alarms that are on the window frames themselves, because the frame of the window does not move at all......"

Many "well to do" homeowners that actually have alarm systems professionally (and competently) installed also use foil on the glass. If the glass is broken or removed, the foil is broken, which triggers the alarm. That, however, is easily checked for.

This technique also doesn't cover noise & acoustic glass break sensors, many of which nowadays can be adjusted for the amount of noise needed to trigger them.

"..... unless you are up against motion sensors ....."

Depending on how they're setup, they can sometimes be defeated. Photoelectric beams can also be a problem (but still defeatable).

"..... They will be baffled as to how you got in......"

The homeowners maybe. The cops.... for all of about 10 minutes or so.
