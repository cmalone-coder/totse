Re: growin some shrooms!
Pftek is very easy, depending on how much you want to grow there are better methods but for personal use and for your friends 8-10 cakes will do you fine.

The sterilisation and inoculation of the cakes is the most important stage and I cannot stress how important a sterile environment is. Any contamination and that entire cake is fucked which is a lot considering one cake could yield up to an oz+ dry with successive fruiting. Also try not to use too much of the spores inoculating each cake because as long as there are spores they will spread rapidly. Rather than using an excess of spores make sure that a small amount of spores are evenly distributed around the cake to ensure that the mycelium spreads fast.

Another useful tip is that two normal buckets work great for an incubator. Fill one bucket 1/4 of the way with water and stick a cheap 25w aquarium heater set at about 20c then put the other bucket inside the water filled bucket. This will push the water right up the side of the bucket to the rim (and probably spill it over the top) as long as you keep this topped up it will ensure that heat is evenly distributed around the interior bucket.

A drop in temperature and light will trigger fruiting and it is at this point where a totally sterile environment is unessesary. It is still important to keep the clear fruiting container clean and sealed (A fine cloth is good keeping contaminants out and allows for gas exchange.) Also make sure that you change the perlite or other fruiting substrate after each successive fruiting. The only problems I had at this stage was that towards the end of the last (4th) Fruiting the laterite began to mould, although at this point the mycelium was strong enough to withstand this invasion but the mushrooms also became contaminated with small flys.


Dunking is also very important and submerging the cakes which are very light and must be submerged in a bucket of cold sterile but de-chlorinated water and containing no other chemicals for around 12 hours (but no more.) This will increase yields and will also help greatly to trigger fruiting when some cakes can be reluctant to fruit.

Good luck, shroomery.org

alright, well as an experienced shroom grower, I can't write down everything I know, but here's some tips to get you started. First off, do NOT try to cake method. It has low yields, it isn't that much easier than doing a bulk grow, and overall is just a crappy method for people who have a brain. I suggest taking rye grain substrate, simmering it until only a few grains start to burst, and then pressure cooking them. Best way imo to pressure cook is to take a tyvek paper that you can get for free at a post office or even order for freeonline. Poke 2 holes in the mason jar lid ( a larger hole in the middle and a smaller inoculation hole to the side. Put the tyvek paper on the jar opening and then put the lid on that of that and then put some cotton into the lid holes. Screw on the ring. Cover with 2 layers of aluminum foil and put a rubber band around the foil to keep it in place. Pressure cook at 15psi for and hour and a half.

After the substrate jars cool, I would suggest making a glove box. All you have to do is get a large tupperware box with a lid and cut holes for your hands. Go to a publix or place where they have tube baggies that you put flowers bouquets in. Attach those to some rubber cleaning gloves to the flower plastic and figure out how to tape it together to make a glove box.

Take all your materials such as spore syringe, jars, paper towels, lighter, alcohol, and lyso and put it into the glove box and close the lid. First put alcohol on a paper towel and wipe your hands down first then the rest of the box. Then spray a bit of lysol into the air. Finally, take your spore syringe, flame the tip until it gets red and squirt out a little bit of excess. keep an alcohol swab on the needle after flaming. Remove the foil layers from your jars and inject 1-2 cc's of spore solution into the jar. Retract the needle still holding an alcohol swap to it and repeat for other jars. Shake up the jars not too roughly.

Put a layer of tinfoil back on top of the jars but not too tight, you will want a bit of air exchange. In a week, you should start to see white fuzzy stuff. Sometimes contamination can happen, but it is rare if you follow this technique to the dot. Put the jars in a warm dark place. When there are nice sized clumps of white, shake gently to spread them throughout the substrate. After the initial white stuff appears, it should only take about a week to fully colonize.

You can go many ways with the next step, but the method I find most cost efficient and produces good yields is take a large chunk of colonized substrate, and put that into some new fully prepared and sterilized substrate jars. You also need to do this under a glove box.

With the rest of your fully colonized jars, pick a proper fruiting container that suits your space needs. I use a lasagna tin baking pan with a high dome lid. Line the bottom with black plastic from a garbage bag or something and make sure you throughly wipe everything with alcohol.

This step does not reauire the glove box but try to do it in a small bathroom and lysol the shit out of the air. Open the colonized jars and with a sterilized spoon, spoon about a 2 inch layer of white stuff evenly. Cover that with an inch layer of casing mix. Casing mix I usually buy online. Shredded straw and coco coir are the best mix in a 50/50 ratio. Sometimes this has to be pressure cooked for a short time, but I've used both with success. Put in inch of that layer on top and smooth it all out. Spray with distilled water until the casing layer is moist and glistening, but dont over do it.

Cover the whole thing in saran wrap and then put the clear plastic dome on top loosely. Put in a warm dark place with some airflow or lift the dome once and day and fan it to give air exchange.

As soon as some of the white stringy mycelium appear in even areas popping through the casing layer, remove the saran wrap, keep the dome lid on, and place the whole thing in a light but shady spot with a lot of air flow. Keeping an optimal humidity and air exchange is the key to getting nice yields and huge shrooms. Spray the dome lid once a day but keep it loosely on. Also fan at least once a day but preferably 3 times. Trick is to not make the environment too wet, too dry, or not enough fresh air. Placing your fruiting chamber outside would be ideal but there are too many contaminants in the wind. Btw, keep the casing layer most but stop spraying once you start to see pins.

Once pinning occurs, only spray the lid once in a while and keep it well ventilated. If you notice white fuzz growing at the base of the stems, it's getting too much moisture and not enough air. At this point you're oh so very close to harvest....Pick the shrooms by twisting from the bottom just when the veil breaks and the shroom starts to open.

You'll be harvesting throughout the days because they will mature at different rates. When the first flush is over, I suggest adding like a half-inch of casing layer and saturating that with water enough so that it is glistening but not soaked. Cover it again with saran wrap and lid and put it in a warm dark place. When you see new mycelium breaking the casing layer, repeat the fruiting steps. I suggest only doing this twice. The next thing once you've had your second harvest is to make a 3 inch hole the shape of your container in the ground and kinda lay it like sod. Cover it with some extra bark, or even better those woodchip fertilizers and water it. Especially after a good rain, you'll get some MONSTER shrooms. This is primarily due to the fact that nature provides the most ideal temperature, air and humidity conditions. You can create a self-sustaining outdoor shroom garden this way as well.

Since I've gone this far aready, to properly store shrooms, they have to be bone dry and crispy.
Eat em fresh but the best experiences (10g wet = 1g dry), but an easy way to dry em without a food dehydrator is put it next to a heater on layers of paper towels. If you really want them to last, I'll share a little secret with you guys......You know those keyboard dusters? The air that comes out of them displaces oxygen, which can break down active chemicals. So take a baggie with your dried shrooms, press all the air out, fill it with keyboard duster air, and then press the air out again. I've kept shrooms this way just sittin in my closet for a year without losing potency. Throw in a little packet of dessicate as well.

Bulk grows using horse poo are sort of hit and miss imo. They are easier but you need more space basically and is messier
