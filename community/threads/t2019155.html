Why did love evolve?
I'm speaking of romantic love. I ask this and I'm told that it's so we can raise our offspring better, but didn't earlier humans, earliest humans, and our ancestors live in huge packs? Why would the father and mother need to love eachother? Protection is already provided by the rest of the pack. Monogamy seems pretty stupid. I mean, you have sex, your seed spreads, what's the use of continuing to be with one person? I'm not speaking of modern times, where, at least in the Western civilization, a mom and dad live together with their offspring for a while. That's a pretty recent invention. So, why did romantic love happen?

All animals take care of their own offspring, at least all k-selection animals do. Even in the incredibly large herds of wildebeest, the herd offers protection, but babies stick with their mommy. Then mommy tends to stick by daddy, because daddy is stronger and offers better protection.

I'm not really sure what your question is, but it doesn't really matter. If it's about mommy loving daddy, most female animals stick by a male animal for protection. If it's about parents loving their kids, all k-selection animals (small litters, with a long infancy) stick with their parents.
