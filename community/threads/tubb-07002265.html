Quarter "Scam"
this "scam" I thought up works, but I just need your feedback to make it a little better, and gain suggestions on how to make it more efficient and profitable. this is what happens...

I live in Central New York, so the Canadian border is only about a 2 hour drive. I bring about $500-$600 US dollars with me through Customs and into the nearest foreign currency exchange. I typically, depending on the exchange rates, receive about $1000 CAN. I then drive to the nearest bank. At the bank I use my $1000 CAN to buy $1000 in CAN quarters. (As long as I place a coin order a week in advance, there is no limit to the number of coins they can supply me with). I put the quarters in my car, and drive back across the border. Back in the states, I spend the quarters as if they are 'normal' US quarters. (The closer you live to the border, the more accecpted foreign coins are). It takes me about a week to filter/exchange the CAN quarters into the US coin supply.

In case your wondering where the profit is, I started with $600, got $1000, and exchanged $1000. My profit is around $400, less gas costs.

Here are the questions I need feedback and answers to:

1. I can get rid of $1000 in quarters in a week, but I want to raise it to, say, $5000 a week. How could I get rid of $5000 in quarters a week, without anyone realizing that they are CAN?

2. I'm around 20, so I still look young. Everytime I cross the border, becasue of my age, they like to stop and search my car. They find the quarters, and get really weird at me. Legally, I can bring $10000 US across the border undeclared. My problem is: They are going to stop me regardless, just because I am young. If I declare $5000 in quarters, they will search me and interrogate me. If I do not declare it (legally I dont have to), they look in my car (cause of my age), and find $5000 in quarters in the back seat. That dosen't look....right...to them, more interrogation. How would I go about bringing $5000 in quarters into the US? I've thought about shipping them back to me from Canada. I've calculated the costs, and it still will be profitable, but not by nearly as much. Suggestions?

3. I've had success in filtering in the CAN quarters at banks, and at the stores I shop at. I'd like to try vending machines. Some work - I put in 4 CAN quarters, hit the coin return, and 4 US quarters come out (60 cent profit instantally from $1). The only problem is that for a week after I clean a machine out of US quarters, the only thing in it will be CAN quarters. Is there anyway to manipulate CAN coins to work on US machines? I understand the majority of CAN coins are magnetic, so I'm not sure if it possible. I suppose I could just visit more machines, but I'm sure there is an easier way.

Thats pretty much all I do. It works with $1000, which brings in on average about $400 every 1 to 3 weeks, depening on how lazy I am...

Anyone have any ideas on how I could make this more profitable, efficient, etc? Feedback is greatly appreciated!

Oh yea - if there is anyone who lives near the Ontario/New York border who would be willing to help me out, with their time, and money, I'm sure we could arrange a 'profit-sharing' plan...

how picky is a bank? it all depends on how close to the border you live. if you try it in niagara falls, ny or bufallo, they probably wont give you any problems. but if you try this in albany or nyc, they probably will catch on quickly.

you can't put many canadian quarters in a roll at all, or too frequently. a few reasons are: i estimate only about .5 of the change I get is Canadian. this should be true of the banks change also. if they begin getting a CAN to US rate of 1:40, they might think something up. whats that come out to? a littl over 2%? that wont look to good, and they will catch you quickly. another reason why you can use many in a roll is that a canadian quarter is not as tall as a US quarter. if you have one can quarter and 1 us quarter, they look the same, but when you get a roll of CAN and a roll os US, the difference in height is close to a half inch. can quarters are 1.22mm thick and 18.03mm in diameter. us quarters are 1.75mm thick and 24.26mm in diameter. the differences are too noticable with one in each hand, but as i said, when you get a roll of each, the differences in height, weight, and diameter are obvious (the fewere can coins per roll, the better, and the safer - the longer it takes too..). the banks will wonder why half of their quarter rolls are extremely short, open them up, and sort of...catch on. not good. yet another reason why you cant put lots of CAN quarters in a roll is the fact that they are magnetic and US quarters are not. i have heard of some banks that run a magnet over the rolls before they will accecpt them. I have come up with 2 soluctions around this. 1 - find a way to demagnetize quarters for a short period of time, or perm. 2 - replace the whole scam with nickels or pennies. sure you wont make as much, but no can pennies are magnetic, nor ar nickels struck after a particular year (its either 1982, 1985, or 1995 - cant remember which off hand). doing this with pennies would take a considerable ammount of time, and you would ultimatley lose money in gas. plus machines dont take pennies, so your places to dispose of them are very limied. nickels, i estimate would be easier to get rid of, but then again, you make only 1/5 the profit as you would with nickels. you would probably break even, if that. (whats the point in wasting your time if you dont make money and just break even?)

for this scam, you can use clear rolls either. the reason is that from the reeds, can quarters do not compare with us quarters. can quarters minted from 1979 to 1999 containted 99% nickel and from 2000 to present contained 92% steek, 5.5% copper, and 2.5% nickel plating. us quarters contain a copper core, and an outside layer called a clad, made of 75% copper and 25% cupro-nickel alloy. as you can tell, the American coins contain much more copper. this is why a canadian coin will appear silver on the reeds, whereas a us coin will appear copper or reddish in color. when they look at the roll, they will see mainly red reeds, with 4 or 5 silver reeds inserted. see enough of these during your visit, and it will raise suspicions with the teller...

This is an interesting idea. I bet it would have been a lot easier 10 years ago, but now there are better machines and I have no idea which ones will reject Canadian coins. I have had some pop machines return them. Here are some ideas to try:

The post office machines are the only ones that I can think of that take pennies. Not all PO vending machines, but each post office should have one in a 24 hour lobby. You can test all the Canadian coins to see which they take. Also, check the change return to see if it is the same coins or different ones. If different, it will be easy. If the same, you need to buy some stamps (which have cash value) and get change. Putting in a quarter for a 1 cent make up stamp would be ideal.

Another idea is subway sytems. The one I am familiar with is the Metro in Washington DC. If these machines take Canadian coins, it should be easy to launder a ton in one day. You can get change for buying cards with cash value (that don't expire) and you can add 5 cents at a time after you get the minimum amount to increase your change. Also, you can sell these cards in the stations without much trouble. It is not uncommon for someone to buy a card they don't need and offer it to someone else in line for the purchase price or a small discount. In addition, there are several machines at each station and lots of stations that you can access with an all day pass. (If you are going to do a lot and don't want them to get wise when one machine is 10% Canadian money.) It is not uncommon for someone on the trains to have a bag on a dolly that you can use to hold a bunch of coins. Just be sure to plan ahead to be able to take a small amount out at a time in a way that no one will see all of them.

Banks that I go to no longer take rolls of coins. They have machines like the coinstar ones previously discussed that have a return for rejected objects. You need to test these machines with some Canadian and mostly American first to see which will take US coin.

If these types of machines take Canadian coins, then it would probably be very profitable. Otherwise, I don't see how you can launder enough with cashiers to make it worth your while. Most of the newer pop machines will reject them.

If anyone has some Canadian coins they could use to test some of these machines, please post the results.
