Capital Punishment
So you've probably heard the statistics: more than 1% of Americans are in prison, it costs $66 billion dollars a year to maintain the prisons, which is $88 per person per day. Obviously there's a problem with the prison system.

Do you believe in capital punishment? If so, do you believe we should enforce it more?

It seems like the right choice to me. What with the rapists, sex offenders, mass murderers, and otherwise dangerous people in the world, it seems like the right thing to do. The $86.08 of potassium chloride needed for execution is a much better financial investment than letting someone sit in a cell for the rest of their life, living off the government's money - our tax dollars. Then there's the chance that they get parole or break out of prison, thus probably returning to their old habits.

Otherwise, a bullet only costs a few cents.

So if I was in charge of the prison system, I'd enforce the death penalty to a near-totalitarian level. A criminal sentenced to two years of prison time, by today's standards, would just be euthanized. Not only would that save us billions of dollars, that would intimidate other criminals to not do the things they do. Thus, crime would hypothetically go down. Altogether, the country could be a better place.

What would you do?

I sometimes think that the biggest difference between Britan and America was the enlightenment period.

I mean, as Vince says, we got the same shit over here as you got over there, but it's the little differences. And I think the biggest and most striking of the little differences have their roots in that brief burst of philosophy, where England sat around for a few decades thinking about it's actions, while America was busy killing Indians or enslaving blacks.

For example, our legal system. It's heavily moulded by Mill's 'On Liberty', and I think is far superior to the American legal system. It really is far more centred around letting the guilty go free rather than imprison the innocent. I have seen loads of silly courtroom news stories from the U.S. about people getting imprisoned for really non-utilitarian things, like burning a flag. I'd like to think that would NEVER happen in England. We have stupid stories of judges making weird decisions that piss people off too of course, but far more often the stories are about the judge letting a man go unreasonably, rather than imprisoning him unreasonably, which is a big fucking difference.

England is world renowned for it's 'soft' legal system. What an Englishman may get for murder is what many other countries charge for drug charges, and of course, there is no capital punishment. (We are not though, overrun with crime, and I would assume have lower crime rates than America.) I'd like to think that this softness is actual a symptom of the system being just. It means that there is far more room for reasonable doubt, and so they must be more concerned with avoiding false imprisonment.

There is plenty of other stuff from the Enlightenment too. Such as England's tenancy to be almost fully atheist. An interviewer over here, Jeremy Paxman, was trying to embarrass Tony Blair, asking him if he prayed like George W Bush with his government, or if he prayed with Bush, and he laughed a little and said "of course not". In contrast I hear Bush prays every morning, and God advises him, and so on.

Then of course you have another great figure of the period, Darwin, which explains itself. Evolution is fact here, not 'theory'.

I mean, they are relativly small things that have worked their way into the English psyche, and not the American one. I'm not saying it's a perfect country, or even a better one, I just mean that we had a small period of being philosophiclly brilliant, and it has made us a bit more sensible and thoughtful in matters of philosophy, such as empiricism, logic and justice.

Anyway, so maybe it's John Stuart Mill or Jeremy Bentham running through my veins, but I can't sympathise with capital punishment. It seems barbaric and regretable, and fails many many ethical tests. The legal system needs to be built on letting ahundred guilty men go rather than imprisoning an innocent one. Capital punishment cannot then work. An doing so as the OP said, to save money, raises even more problems.

If you want to be a moron about it, shake your fist and nod your big meat filled head while drooling "lock em up, fucking murderers an rapists n fuckin bastards! kill em! kill em all! Let em byrn!" but when it comes to a true ethical reflection on the actions of the state, the people you are killing are not murderers and rapists, but people. Who here has not been a criminal at some point? and were you not still a man? Did you deserve to be treated as a criminal, rather than a man? Would it have been just?
