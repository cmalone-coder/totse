Re: A few dumb questions
To question 1)

Only difference ABS has when compared to non-ABS brakes is that while non ABS brakes will skid if you brake too hard, causing a loss of control and a decrease in braking force, ABS brakes will in effect automatically stop you just before you apply too much force.

When driving a car without ABS, you can

a) The 'fuck this' technique. Slam down on the brakes as hard as possible and hope you don't skid into a tree.

b) Cadence braking: Pulse the brake pedal (might be called pumping) very rapidly around where you want it to be. I have my doubts that joe bloggs can get anywhere close to a decent result from this

c) Threshold braking: squeeze on the brakes slowly... you should feel (and hear, if you go too far) the braking force diminish at the point when the tyres start slipping. back away from this point. Try to get as close to it as possible without going too far. You will probably get about 90% of what is optimal.

Note: I have never tried any of these techniques. Use at your own risk, they could be completely wrong.

To question 2)

Don't know.

To question 3)

That is cost cutting in action. All, or at least most, of the wires in your car are part of something called a wiring harness. The wiring harness is basically all the wires. Pretty simple really. Every option on your car may need more or less wires, e.g. if you don't have a 6 stack CD player in the boot you may not need that wire running to the boot.

However, it would be expensive to design and buy the machines for every individual possible wiring harness for each combination of options. Instead, they have a single wiring harness which assumes every option is ticked, and then in the event that an option isn't ticked, its place on the harness simply is left unplugged.

Re: A few dumb questions
try taking the car out on a deserted road and experimenting with braking too hard (locking up the wheels).
Get used to how that feels or rather, why , when, and how that happens.
That way you can deal with it when it happens in a real hard braking situation.
Don't let the tires skid too long though, as you'll scrub off the tread, and need to replace them miles ahead of their proper replacement time.

p.s. race cars never use ABS (at least that was true a few years ago and I assume still is).
ABS is slower than non-ABS in the right hands.
ABS on gravel can cause you to wreck.
The fastest way to stop means learning how to do it without ABS.
