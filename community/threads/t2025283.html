Cartoon Network, Anime, and the Youth
Hi All,

I started to feel a little sentimental and felt like posting this.

My first REAL exposure can in...about 3rd or 4th grade so figure around 2000-01 and like most of the youngsters my age, exposure came with Cartoonetworks programing block Toonami. At that time they were showing stuff like Dragonball, and Tenchi in Tokyo-and for a short stint Voltron.

This was the stuff that caught my attention and I started to get more into it, then in late 01 CN started airing the original Mobile Suit Gundam. That was the first big anime for me and it still is one of my favorites. It was around this time that I started watching another CN block called Adult Swim. It was a late night block and showed uncut stuff like Tenchi Muyo!, Outlaw Star. Then eventually Inu-Yasha

All good stuff and eventually, started seeing Rurouni Kenshin.

and then in branched out into Escaflowne, FLCL, Basilisk....etc.

My point is that this big medium, started airing all this stuff for kids to see. And this big Anime franchise boomed in the U.S with stuff like Pokémon and Yugio cards and DVDS and Movies.

But the funny thing is a fast as it took this stuff to boom it disappears. Like Mobile Suit Gundam, all the toys and model kits etc.

So, what do you think? I guess what I want to here are your view points on Anime exposure, and the mass commercialization of it.

This might get blamed but. I'm 16 and I still pull out my Rurouni Kenshin DVDs (i even ordered a reverse bladed katana). I think this just illustrates that Anime makes a impression on some people.

cheers,

g

Re: Cartoon Network, Anime, and the Youth
MahiMaki's post in MP made me think about that too- FLCL is probably too dirty for audience Pokemon and Yugioh have, Cowboy Bebop is probably too violent and has some situations that parents don't like kids to be exposed to (Even though Spike, Faye, and Jet were good guys, they commonly dealt with druggies, transvestites, and such). Rurouni Kenshin had a long run on CN (And not on Adult Swim, either) but I get the feeling it had a big neutering. Whereas DB's edits were mainly to cut out some of the more violent scenes (I recall someone telling me about some of these- sometimes a character is simply punched in the stomach instead of being "impaled"), Kenshin also dealt with Opium and such, IIRC, and it was a bit more bloody than DB.

Not that that's a problem- localization isn't necessarily a bad thing. Some people simply have different sensibilities, and if a company edits something to appeal to a certain demographic, then more power to them. I don't think it's appropriate for little kids to see boobies and sex scenes either. I never saw the big problem with blood on American TV, but whatever. And there will have to be edits to remove Japan-specific jokes which american viewers will not get.

Also, Pokemon and Yugioh were made for franchising. The shows were about collecting- anybody wanting to emulate things on the shows is going to want to "collect" the stuff from them. Having twenty million pokemon just makes people want each of them. There are only a handful of characters in Kenshin and Bebop, and they don't appeal as much to the age groups that would buy cards and toys and such.
