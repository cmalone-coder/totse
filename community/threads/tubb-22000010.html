Arsenal
Realistically speaking, moneywise as well. If you had to put together an arsenal what would you include and why?

If I had to do it again mine would consist of Four weapons.

1. Long Range Rifle. This gun would be a bolt gun in a heavy caliber, .308 or over, prefferably .338Laupa. It would be customized for long range shooting only, over 400yds. This would mean a strong action, match grade free floating barrel, a bipod, a fire lapped barrel with a recessed muzzle and fluted. Also atop it would set a good scope with a mil-dot reticle.

2. An assault weapon. An intermediate powered weapon that utilizes high capacity magazines and is relatively short. Bayonet optional, flash hider, and muzzle brake equipped. Semi-auto of course and open sites. Zero would be 200yds. Caliber options-.30 Carbine, 7.62x39mm, .223, 5.5mm Russian, .22Mag (minimum), 5mm Rem, .30-30.

3. A shotgun. This gun would be my work horse. So my specs would be a polychoke, 9shot 2.75" capacity, 3" chamber, 12ga, pump action, ghost ring sites, pistol grip stock, all synthetic, a side saddle, heat sheild, and a 24" barrel max.

4. A pistol. Semiauto, hi cap, adjustable tritium dot sites, full sized. Caliber options:.45ACP, .40S&W, .400Corbon, .357Sig, .38Super, 10mm, 7.62x25mm.

This would be my basic arsenal for a static position. In a mobile situation I'd have to go with the Assault weapon and a pistol. If I could hack it, a single shot cut down 20ga would be the ticket for me.

The only thing I don't have is a great long range rifle. I've been working on a Mauser action and .308 barrel that fits the action. It's been an ongoing project that I've been picking up and putting down. What I would like to do is this: Install a match grade trigger, drill and tap for a Weaver Base, turn the bolt down and use Burris Posi-Lock rings to mount a Leupold Tactical Scope. Install this to a pillar bedded synthetic stock and free float the barrel. All together a $800 project.

Re: Arsenal
An MP5 is much easier for a citizen to get a hold of than an FNP90. A citizen that is not a class 3 dealer cannot legally own a P90. But you are right, there is a lot of Hollywood education here on guns.

Poor choices I've seen: Sawed off shotgun for most situations, a machine pistol for a combat weapon, silenced weapons out the wazoo, a flashhider for pistol, ninja sword, calling grenades artillery pieces, using flechette for main shotgun round, bayonet lug(?).

1. Shotguns are not the best choice for general carry in combat. Though they are very versatile weapons they aren't a good gun if you don't know what is going to happen. Example, combat breaks out and you are fired on from a range of 250yds persay. A shotgun will do you know good, where as an assault rifle or battle rifle will do, even a bolt gun would be better.

2. Pistols for main combat. If you are in combat in pistol range, consider yourself fucked because you have either been very surprised or have failed to destroy the enemy from a safer distance. Tis better to run like hell, take cover, fire and repeat, till the enemy is dead or gets tired of chasing you. Machine pistols have a very high cyclic rate. This means less than 25% of the ammo fired will hit an intended target if you are competently trained. A MAC has a rate of 900-1200rpm, expect to need to change the mag everytime you fire a burst. 32rnds per mag, 1 mag per burst=alot of ammo in no time at all. Can you carry that much ammo and change a mag that fast? I doubt it.

3. Silencers only make a weapon quieter and are useless without proper deployment. Cover noises are required for proper use. Noises like a passing car, a ringing phone, etc. Something louder than the gun itself.
Silencers require heavy maintenance, they must be clean to work properly, and add some length to most guns.

4. A flash hider on a pistol. Again if you are in pistol range in combat a flash hider will do nothing for you. I'd say in shotgun range a flash hider will do nothing for you.

5. Never bring a knife to a gun fight, nough said.

6. A grenade is artillery? Since when?

7. Flechette over all suck ass. I've handloaded thousands of them. They are good for penetrating heavy naturaly cover at close range, 25yds or less. They are too light to do any real damage much further and the wound cavities they leave are horrible. Small wound cavities mean less bleeding, less bleeding means better chance of living. Might as well give the enemy acupuncture treatments while you are at it.

8. Bayonet lug? I'm not sure what you mean. Do you plan to mount a bayonet or do you just want a lug on the stock? In WW2 the most popular job of the bayonet was opening C-Rations and digging, though it did help morale a bit. If things got to bayonet range, most ran, and most that ran lived.

If any of you really plan to build an arsenal, at least make it realistic and practical. I'd rather be armed with my single shot 20ga in most cases than any machine pistol.

Also modern autos are in fact more reliable than revolvers.

I don't mean to flame anyone, but damn stop learning from movies. Pickup Guns Magazine, Guns and Ammo, or something. Join a shooting club that has matches such as ISPCA, etc.

Re: Arsenal
Sure I'm taking in urban combat. It's obvious that you don't know what wound ballistics with flechette or other shotgun ammo is like. Flechette are great for what they were built for, but for everything else they suck. Hell, originally flechette were never designed for use in a shotgun. Flechette are placed in bombs, mines, artillery, and rockets. They were intended to be fired in high volume, at high speeds, at short range. Out of a shotgun you can only achieve the close range. Volume is missing and I've never clocked flechette at over 2000fps, they were intended to go 4000fps+. I also highly doubt that flechette fired from a shotgun will penetrate any type of armor with consistency. I've heard they will penetrate a level 2 vest, I've never seen them fully penetrate a 2x4. There are many other better loads for a shotgun in urban combat. There is #4 buck which will not penetrate too another room, there is also tactical buckshot in OO that also will not penetrate to another room. If you handload, then a whole new world is open to you. You can mix different types of shot and tailor the velocites. But some people get real dumb ideas like loading fishhooks or tacks. Even worse some people buy these joke rounds as avaliable from Firequest.

The shotgun has been used in combat more successfully as a support weapon. A better weapon for urban combat would be a carbine or an assault rifle. I think your selection is good, much better than most I've seen on this board. Just that Flechette are a very specialized round, meaning they cannot handle a generalized task. Weight when considering ammo is very important, as it is one of the factors that determine KE. Another important factor is energy dumping as well, this is the ability of a bullet to transfer all it's energy to the target. To do this the bullet must stop within the body. Flechette at close range, not having to penetrate any cover, will likely not stop. And even if they do stop, they have little energy to transfer. Flechette rounds also suffer from ineffecient powder burning. With no weight to increase pressure in the shell at time of combustion, the powder doesn't burn as it should, weakening velocities. I have overcome this in one way. I buffer flechette with lead shot, the smaller the better. The down fall, it decreases the amount of flechette you can fit into a shell by over half.

As far as the bayonet goes, thanks for clearing that up. And I do agree that though a bayonet is handy and can be useful. The bayonet is only a very last ditch effort.

Please tell me the few situations where a subgun comes out winner. I can think of several where it would be one of the last guns I would want. They eat ammo up fast, max effective range is just a little bit further than a pistol in combat. If you are taking fire from a range of say over 100yds, might as well run if you armed with a subgun, they weren't meant for that. Subguns were designed with close quarters combat in mind. Fast action, high intensity, close range, short lived confrontations. In that arena they excel probably over any other weapon. Take them out of that environment and you have something a little better than throwing rocks. Situations that subguns excel:Room clearing, house to house combat they are formidable (but are better as support). That's it. In general combat they are a poor choice. Coming from a police background I would expect you to know that. Lood at Swat for example. The guys entering the doors are armed with either a handgun, shotgun, or subgun. Subguns usually take the lead, shotguns the rear, pistols in the mid and sometimes up front. This is a setup foor room clearing. Almost always are there several people armed with M-16 variants, Mini 14's, or Mini 30's close by. Remember the LA shoot out in which the bandits were wearing armor? Pistol caliber ammo could not down them, it was a rifle that finally took them out, a much better choice in that scenario. Even in general urban combat the Subgun isn't the best choice. Have you looked at how bullets will penetrate cover commonly found in an urban scenario? Cars, dumpsters, signs, etc. Unless you have AP and are close range pistol calibers are pretty much useless till the bad guy makes a mistake or gets unlucky. Where as an assault rifle will have much less difficulty, a shotgun would even to better, but not better than a .223 or 7.62x39mm.

My experience comes from growing up in a Marine Corp family. I've been shooting since I was 6yrs old and have been an enthusiast since then. I read mags whether they catch my eye or not. I also know may combat veterans and some policemen. I have taken pistol, shotgun, and carbine training classes along side of policemen and was on a division of the USMC shooting team. I've been reloading for 6yrs now and still learning. I don't claim to be an expert, I've never been in what I'd call actual combat. I've shot about every type of full auto there is (by category not my model). MP5's, MAC-10, M16A1, M16A2, M-60, M-249, and an RPK. I've fired modified SKS's, Ruger 10/22's, Ruger MkII, an M2 Carbine (done by a SOP 2 dealer, it was legal). I've fired USMC Nat'l Match target and sniping rifles (M1's, M-14, M40A1, M1).
I too spend a lot of time at the range, the books, and at the bench reloading.

I've been to simulated combat zones and watched soldiers using real life tactics as they practice. I've watched anti-terrorist units practice as well as entry teams. I've read works by Rex Applegate, Jeff Cooper, Masaad Ayoob, et...al. I've also studied FBI studies on cadaver and ballistic gelatin testing.

But I think greater info comes from people who have been in combat, been shot at, returned fire, killed an enemy, etc. Those that don't mind talking about it still remember things as they saw it when it happened. What happened, how they saw it, mentality, where they were shot, where the shot the bad guy, number of hits it took, etc. The best data comes from real time experience, not in a lab. Most of what I have heard comes from Vietnam, Persian Gulf, Panama, and some special forces missions from Army Rangers and Marine Recon.

Someday I hope to be called an expert in the field, though I hope I can earn that title without engaging in combat.
