Nanotechnology
It is expected that before too long nanotechnology will revolutionize everything from manufacturing to medicine. But there is one aspect that I rarely see discussed, and never to my satisfaction.

How will we power the damn things?

OK, they have designed nanotech motors that work on radio waves, and ones that are powered by the breakdown of ATP, which is the basic fuel unit of biology.

That is all well and good for small labrotory applications, and for limited medical procedures.

But how will we power the industrial nanobots? The ones that are suposed to build entire citys from raw materials, or turn carbon to diamonds. That takes a lot of juice, especialy when you are hauling everything around one atom at a time.

I think what you call buckyfibers are the same as nanotubes. Buckyballs have atomic bonds along the lines of a soccer ball. In fact the structure was determined during a soccer match.

Graphite looks like this on an atomic level:
code:

/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \__/
\__/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \
/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \__/
\__/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \

\__/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \
/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \__/
\__/  \__/  \__/  \__/  \


Nanotubes are the same as graphate, just rolled into a tube.
code:

     __________
  __/          \__
 / \_    __    _/ \
|\_/ \__/  \__/ \_/|
|/ \_/  \__/  \_/ \|
|\_/ \__/  \__/ \_/|
|/ \_/  \__/  \_/ \|
|\_/ \__/  \__/ \_/|
|/ \_/  \__/  \_/ \|
|\_/ \__/  \__/ \_/|
|/ \_/  \__/  \_/ \|


Forgive my crappy ASCII rendition of a nonotube.

Now for a little chemistry behind why it is so strong. Each intersection represents a carbon atom. Each carbon atom has 4 valences (electrons free to bond into pairs). However you will notice that there are only 3 bonds shown for each carbon atom. The fourth bond runs paralell to one of the pictured bonds, creating a double bond. This makes the bond much stronger. But this bond is somewhat unstable and bonds parallel to each of the primary bonds in turn, reinforcing them all.
