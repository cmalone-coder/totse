Re: Erlenmeyer from TV tube?
I've 'equilised' quite a few CRT's before, the teknique is to remove the neck circiut board and expose the pins to the electron guns, the pins will be insulated with a plastic socket that has a large nib in the center of it, under the plastic nip is a glass one, you can to one of two things now, either carefully pry the plastick cap off (it is often silliconed on) and snap the glass nip off to release the vacume as you would a light globe, or ignore the cap and, with a pair of pliers, pull and twist one of the pins untill the glass around it cracks and releases the vacume.

A word of warning, Cathode Ray Tube's (CRT's) can contain mercury vapout as well as different kinds of phosphorus, also a lead coating on the inside glass and outside too.

Black & white tubes have a mirror back glass and one type of phosphorus on the front glass, also a mixture of argon and somtimes mercury vapour inside, it would be wise to wash it out with H2SO4 to remove most contaminants, but it is never possible to remove all.

Colour tubes are much more complicated and thus are useless for anything other than imploding, they contain a steel apeture grill used to allign the electrons on the screen. and it is impossible to remove it without destroying the tube. The glass is lined with things like cadnium, red phosphourus, luminous phosphorus (red, green and blue) and may contain mercury vapour.

The chemicals vary form company to company, some use a black substance on the back glass, some use red phos, others use mercury vapour and others use cadnium lining.

Toshiba black stripe tubes appear to contain red phos, also some monitors, but I've never used it because ot the mercury and cadnium.

quote:Originally posted by kirby:

Just a weapon, the M60E3 is a light weight version of the M60, it has a vertical forearm grip instead of the perforated horizontal heat sheild, also a lighter barrel and receiver.

Busting up TV tubes is a favourite past time of mine, but always use a good respirator as the phosphourus will become airborne in a cloud of gasses.

It would pay to swab all of the phosphorus off the B&W tube's front glass before washing with an acid, or you may get phosphoric acid. But the acid will dissolv most of the metal lining that is etched on to the glass.

I have done it myself, but without the acid as I was just finding out how they work. I ended up filling it up with water and hurling it at a brick wall. It was an apple monochrome (green) monitor tube.
