I find (and this applies to anyone, not just that special someone) that a big, expensive gift will always take second place to something small and really thoughtful. Pay attention to what they say, and make mental notes of things they like or want. A generic gift like a costly ring says "I have a lot of money, and you're worth the money", which is very nice, but something meaningful says "I listen to you", which is appreciated more. At least, it is in my experience. Although if the thoughtful thing is expensive too, by all means go for that!

A few examples from my experience would be an ex-girlfriend, who happened once to say "I really like stuff by x brand, but I've never seen it on sale here". Four months later, I gave her a little keyring from the company. Even though I had to order overseas, it hardly cost anything, but she was amazed I'd remembered and I got a lot of "thank you" sex, as well as that nice feeling you get when you give a well-received gift. I got her a pendant with a design she'd showed me once, because I saw it in a shop. Again, this was months later (and it coincided with her birthday, which was perfect), and I just thought "hey, that's that thing she thought was awesome all that time ago". Again, very well-received. And I got my best friend a packet of Jelly Tots and sent them to her for no particular occasion. She's kept them unopened in her room ever since

The same thing applies to guys, by the way. I got a little cross necklace for my birthday from someone who I'd once mentioned to that I really liked the cross on jewellery (I'm not Christian, it just appeals aesthetically), and bemoaned the fact that I couldn't find a nice, solid-feeling but small one. And from that vague description, they got me an absolutely perfect one. I was, needless to say, rather touched.

EDIT: Even if it's something they don't like, if they remember mentioning it, they'll still be really touched. It's what I always try to do when I buy gifts.
