It's a birdeater for sure, but besides that it's impossible to identify because it isn't necessarily a spider native to your area (although I'd assume it is) - it could've been someone's pet that got away.

Assuming it's a native spider, it's an Aphonopelma hentzi or "Oklahoma Brown Tarantula", as far as I know, that's the only birdeating spider living in your area, but if you live close to another state there could be some habitat overlapping so the species might be a bit off but the genus is as close as you'll get.

Also, the genus Aphonopelma contains some 90+ species, and is the most species-rich genus in Theraphosidae (the birdeaters), and the lack of distinctive features or markings makes it impossible to say with 100% certainty exactly what species it is.
Another reason is that they come in several color variations.

Seeing one of these spiders outside at the moment is a bit out of the ordinary, from what I can tell, it's not a mature male looking for a female - it looks too plump to be a mature male, and it's too early for mating season, so I have no idea why it'd run around on your lawn in broad daylight.

Next time you see one of these fellas running around, try getting a good look at the front legs, if they have a little spur pointing forward, located on the middle of the leg on the inside/underside, it's a mature male, and thus most likely a native species.

I'd say you'll probably see more of them in the next two months or so.

EDIT: They aren't considered aggressive, and their venom isn't very potent, if you got bitten it'd probably be no worse than a bee sting or two, but of course there'd be the mechanical damage from the fangs, a birdeater of the size you described would have fangs around 7-8 mm long.
