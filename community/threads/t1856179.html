Speeding right along.

Lets say you have a 20% solution of NaOH, and devil boy is just fine. Somebody tell me, is that 80mls. water and 20g. NaOh, or is that 20g. NaOH and brought up to 100mls total volume. One of you chem students check your book about the making of % solutions using a solid and a liquid, please.

Anyway, I would go with the second method until otherwise convinced. That will be ample volume to base your 6g cook. Actually it will be about 4 times what you need, but you will have to wait until I can go through the notebooks and find an actual documented experiment on the subject, and I won't be going back over there until afternoon or evening or the next day or.....ok, whatever. Here's what you may want to do.

Mix your solution, stirring it well with a plastic stick (or wood). Let it sit until it is no more than very slightly warm to the touch. It should be crystal clear. Stir you rxn fluid and get it spinning pretty good. Then, while it's still spinning, add maybe 10 mls and get it stirring again. It will change colors as soon as you add the base, but then quickly change back. Wait a few minutes then do it again. Then again with 5mls. base. Now, you are getting pretty close (well, not acording to a pH device, but the closer you get the more sensitive to change it becomes). Now you are going to do the same routine, but use only splashes of 1/2 to 1 ml each time. The color change will stay longer and longer each time and then become cloudy. This is where there is a great deal of variance between individuals, cleaning methods, temps, etc.

Think about this. When it has gone all the way, you will see a definite color change that does not go away. Most of the time it will be milky white. Dont worry if it goes nasty brown (it's something about the iodine) that's ok. You will also see turbidity which means that the cloudy white or brown or whatever color solution will look like there is.......maybe you will see it as thickening or crap floating in it, how about cottony lookin shit in it. It all depends on how you see things. For those who know a few chem terms and what they look like, turbidity and flocculance typically means that you are there. Always make sure you have at least SOME of your NP in there before you go too far so that your freebase will have somewhere to go.

The "Lye Lock" method makes perfect sense to SWIL, regardless of how primative it may be. It is very easy to teach, and the Methamphetamine molecule remains unharmed through this approach. Very wise... yet, all too simple!

It is NO wonder that the original Orange GaKK is aimed at exactly this method of extraction!

Nobody ever saw the orange shark until pH 14 was reached, and by then, it took some serious patience and cautious Non-polar extractions to side-step this monster! (Settling of the "salmonella", and passive NP solvents seem to be the method of choice!)

Instead of "Lye Lock", try this instead:

Ala MonkeyBoy, To reach a pH of 12:

Add a 20% basic solution of the base of choice(NaOH seems to be the most plentiful and "pure", however; sodium bicarbonate, sodium triphosphate, etc. will work). When the aqueous layer becomes milky white and clears within several(4-6) minutes, the pH of 10-12 has been reached. One can also tell that a pH change has occured, as many "bubbles" rise from the lower layer towards the organic(upper) layer. With more experience, it is VERY easy to accurately judge the amount of amine movement by watching the aqueous layer.

To reach a pH of 14:

After a pH of 12 has been reached, and 1/2-1 hour has passed, the aqueous(bottom) layer should be quite basic... yet clear on the upper half and milky white in the lower half(possibly dark orange if GaKK infested!)...

...Upon the second addition of basic solution(20%), within several minutes the lower(aqueous) layer will not clear of the milky white coloring. Upon settling, the lower layer will appear clear, yet it appears to contain some type of translucent substance in it(much like that of a NP solvent filled with Freebase amines).

Although the "Lye Lock" method works, there is NO need to go over-board with the basifying process. More is less in this step, as is a selective solvent!!!
