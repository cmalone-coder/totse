Re: My Loving Fear
quote:Originally posted by Harde:

Tell me more.

-LSD, mushrooms.

-Meditation

-Courting danger openly, actively seeking out things/activities that scare the shit out of you. When you shy away from such things, you become more and more sensitive to anxiety and fear and get stuck in a grey little comfort zone, but when you put yourself in these types of situations, you see what you REALLY want and it will lead you to seek out a more intense mode of existence.

-Exhaustive, painfully honest self-analysis & introspection combining one or more of the above methods, WRITTEN DOWN. Writing is the great teacher, it will reveal to you things about yourself you've never consciously been aware of.

-Travel. Sometimes playing the field & gaining worldly experience, both pleasant & unpleasant, is the only way to discover your true desires.

-If you have anxiety/depression issues, see a professional. Such things will definitely cloud one's mind over & they distract you from what's really important to you.

-The book Prometheus Rising by Robert Anton Wilson. I personally found it to be very helpful in opening my mind, overcoming various mental blocks & shattering my own preconceptions/reality tunnels.

-Engaging in some form of creative activity, any kind of art. Writing fiction/poetry, drawing/painting, music, or a number of other endeavors.

-Cutting yourself off from your daily routine.

"When you cut yourself off from the life you've been living and you haven't yet established another life, you're free. If you don't know where you're going, you're even freer."

-Paul Bowles, writer

All in all, forcing yourself out of your normal mindset & lifestyle is the common thread here. Over the course of your life, you develop a series of coping/defense mechanisms to protect yourself from experiencing certain kinds of pain. There's nothing wrong with this inherently, but a lot of people tend to overcompensate in this area, which in my opinion leads to the closing of the mind, identity confusion & some of the issues you're dealing with here.

I'm stoned out of my mind right now, but I hope that was coherent enough for you to get something out of it. I'm certainly no guru or wiseman, some other people here may be able to offer better advice, but the above should help you find what you want.

If all else fails, you might be interested in the writer Fernando Pessoa. He dealt with this problem all his life, and much of his work deals with a theme that can basically be summed up as "the impossibility of wanting anything."
