Backyard Ballistics This forum is dedicated to the safe teaching and responsible handling of high and low explosives. You will not find any information on how to make "bombs" or any such device(s) . Backyard ballistics will teach you the science and the art of pyrotechnics and how to manufacture and use explosives/pyrotechnics safely and responsibly. Backyard ballistics is also a haven for rocketry and pneumatic cannons. All information contained within this forum is for educational purposes ONLY. try the goood ol' smoke-device

if you want to spend more time, and also get better smoke, try the smoke preparation procedures from www.krimzonpyro.com (http://www.krimzonpyro.com), by melting KNO3 and sugar, in a ratio 6:4, by weight. you can also use 6:4 by volume, because sugar and KNO3 have nearly the same density. that's how i did before i got my digital scale.

if you want to just test it fast, powder the KNO3 (if it comes in prill-form, if not, then skip this step), take 3 spoons of KNO3, put them into a disposable container, add 2 spoons of sugar, close the container, shake it well, so the chemicals mix, and then ignite it by fuse, or just ignite a match and put it there (but be careful with this!).

'Prolly can... What you want to do is make charcoal, though, not the ash bits. If you have a local ACE Hardware store, they do have bags of real wood charcoal, something canyon brand I think. I'm pretty sure it's going to be a medium to hard wood charcoal, but I think it might work in pyro, just not as good as Willow charcoal. It's like 5 bux for a 8# bag.

If you want to make your own charcoal, refer to some of the following links for more information.

http://www.velvitoil.com/Charmake.htm

http://www.dutch-net.com/~dennis/Charcoal.html

http://www.drawing.com.au/matirial_charcoal.php

You may do a search on Google or a similar search engine to find more how-tos with pictures and such. It is pretty simple to make once you have the right wood. I am told that using dried pine charcoal - like construction lumber - burns quick and hot, with minimum ash, so pine might be a good alternative if willow is not available.
