Start-up capital: have as much money available as possible, but don't spend it. Suppliers can usually work of credit, and your business can teach them how to wait for their payment. If they push for their money, remember that incorporation can protect you from debts run up by your business. This is where a _really fucking good_ motherfucking jew accountant is good.

Police: Is there a police benevolent thingy in your neighbourhood? Shmooze the local pigs as much as possible, you can never be too fucking safe. Send all your utility people lots of presents at every opportunity, because they can help out.

Delay payment of bills as long as possible, remember to pay your taxes and stuff. Don't expect to make any money in your first year, remember to have nerves of steel, and sell both to cost-conscious consumers and high spending consumers.

Economics has an idea of a "consumer surplus", which is the economic benefit that happens when people pay less for a good than they were prepared to pay. In other words the consumer surplus is what happens when people get a bargain. As a business it is your role to act to minimise the consumer surplus in order to maximise profits, but if you push too far you will lose sales and your profits will suffer.

Some advice - most incorporated businesses are created as a legal convenience, to protect another person or business from legal or financial liability arising out of the operation of that business. As a result the failure rate of new businesses is masssively inflated. Some people create a new business every year.
