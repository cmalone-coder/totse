Re: sound-proofing a room
Have you ever seen a quiet room? The ones that NASA or one of the sound testing companies use? You need to break up the sound wave before it strikes the wall and transfers the energy to the house itself.

It would be a lot of work, but think of it this way; if you took about a thousand egg cartons and used the bottoms, tack/glue/tape them to your walls leaving no flat space where the sound wave can strike. When the wave hits the small cups and small peaks of the egg carton it breaks up, effectively dampening it, much like the way radar bounces off a F-117 stealth fighter.

Playing out in the garage might not be such a bad idea either.
