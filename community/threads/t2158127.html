Re: Undiscovered drugs
While you are here, please tell me your assumption on:
What would happen if i were to decarboxylate D-Phen.

Phenethylamine base. Same as l-phen.

Form the acyl halide, perform a friedel-crafts itnramolecular cyclatization, and reduce the ketone, 'n you'd have the stimulant 2-aminoindane. Perform the intramolecular halogenation of the 2-position and use this to cyclatize on the amine, however, and one would have dihydroindole-2-methanoic acid. No one knows what the fuck that shit does.

Reduce that, and you might have 2-methyldihydroindole... which is probably a stimulant as well, but no one knows really what the hell that shit does, either.

And then there's the a-methyl-aziridine. Everyone knows what the b-carbon aziridine does, and it kinda sucks... but there's reason to believe that the gamma-aziridine would be potentially more interesting.

'n I'm probably spelling aziridine wrong. Oh, the amide remains untested, as does the 2,3-diamine of amphetamine, all of which could be made from phenylalanine.

Don't make diphenylalanine anhydride or its derivatives, though, without further reduction, as it should be carcinogenic as fuck, possibly with other problems.

Re: Undiscovered drugs
Quote:
Originally Posted by Shulgin
Normally, this mushroom species dutifully converts N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) to psilocin, by introducing a 4-hydroxyl group into the molecule by something that is probably called an indole 4-hydroxylase by the biochemists. You put DMT in, and you get 4-hydroxy-DMT out, and this is psilocin. It is as if the mushroom psyche didn't really care what it was working with, it was simply compelled to do its sacred duty to 4-hydroxylate any tryptamine it came across. It was observed that if you put N,N-diethyltryptamine (DET, not a material found in nature) into the growing process, the dutiful and ignorant enzymes would hydroxylate it to 4-hydroxy-N,N-diethyltryptamine (4-HO-DET) a potent drug also not known in nature. This is the title drug of this commentary. What a beautiful burr to thrust into the natural versus synthetic controversy. If a plant (a mushroom mycelium in this case) is given a man-made chemical, and this plant converts it, using its natural capabilities, into a product that had never before been known in nature, is that product natural? What is natural?
Any pseudo-philosophical thread needs good definitions or it will fail from the outset.
