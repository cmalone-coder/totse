Re: Were Satan and God friends?
There is only one mentioning, in Isahia ch 14, of this fall, and it only mentions Lucifer (the lightbearer, or the day star; i.e. Venus, the morning star).

Isa 14:12 begins: "How art thou fallen from heaven, oh Lucifer, son of the morning..."

The passage describes the fall from heaven of one who said "I will make myself like the Most High". Hell is mentioned, but Satan is not even hinted at. Yet, this is the whole - weak - basis for the belief that Satan was an angel who rebelled against God and was cast into hell.

To make it even more strange, the Latin Bible uses the term "lucifer" for the signs of the zodiac (Job somewhere). And for Jesus! In the Second Epistle of Peter 1:19, the english text says:

"We have also a more sure word of prophecy, whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn, and the day star arise in your hearts"

In the Latin version it ends: ...and Lucifer arise in your hearts.

This is undoubtedly a prophecy about the Messiah, whether Jesus or not. And note that it makes much more sense to use day star/morning star/lightbearer/Lucifer, as a metaphor for (the coming of) something positive. We speak of "the light at the end of the tunnel" and "s/he brought light back into my life". We speak of wise men, such as Jesus, as enlightened, while we speak of evil things as dark, and of Satan as the Prince of Darkness.

Satan as Lucifer the fallen angel, is a far-fetched idea by some Church Father(s), and nothing else...

quote:
Originally posted by VeneFrigus:
But you can have darkness without light.

It might seem so, but if you look upon it again, the assumption is made from a literal interpretation of the metaphors good=light/evil=darkness.

At the dawn of cultures, this had (from what we can understand) a literal meaning. Man was afraid of the darkness of the night (also for practical reasons); afraid that the sun wouldn�t come back the next day, or that the moon wouldn�t arrive to shine when it was supposed to. It also seems that man very early realized the importance of the sun; that it actually gave life and not only light. Instinct or intuition, rather than science, but that is beside the point. Worship of the Sun and the Moon, are (among) the earliest religions we know.

Since then mankind has carried this double metaphor through many cultures and religions, using it, and sometimes abusing it.

To assume that evil was the originate state of the Cosmos, because light needs darkness, is abuse of the meataphor.

It also makes a bad metaphor in itself, as light in reality doesn�t need darkness, not even to be seen. Use a bright enough light and you see it in full sunshine. Light needs relative darkness, and that is contained in light itself.

Look at the symbol of Yin and Yang. One half of the circle is black, the other white. Note that they are not divided by a straight line, but by a curve, suggesting that they are interlocked; undivideable. In the black part, there is a white spot inbedded, and in the white part there is a black spot. This suggests that there is no such thing as pure white or pure black, as they each contain a little of the opposite.

Perhaps they existed separately at one point, but even then they contained a little of the other. This would also be true if one existed before the other...

In addition to a "yes" and a "no", the Universe contains a "maybe".
