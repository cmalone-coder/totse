Re: Hollow point ammunition
Because the rifling imparts a spin onto the bullet, you will probably never make as good a hollowpoint as you can buy. Just by being a half a millimeter off of center will give you a heavy side and as soon as it leaves the barrel the centrifugal force will throw it off to who knows where.

Maybe if you had a lathe you could get a little closer to center. Other than that you aren't going to get there with a hand drill. The depth of the hole is very important also. Drill too deep and like Zero said the bullet will either expand and lock in the barrel or you could just blow through the bottom of the bullet and lock that one up too.

Go easy and just cross-top the rounds. That's the way they used to do it before hollow points. Works about the same and doesn't cost you any more than regular rounds. Might give you a feed problem in an automatic though, catching the feed chute with the boogered up tip.

Re: Hollow point ammunition
A word of caution: DO NOT try to make hollowpoints out of FMJ-s. The half-jacket on a hollowpoint is closed in the base/rear end of the bullet. The jacket on a FMJ is closed in the tip/front end of the bullet but open at the base (so the lead can be poured/pressed in during manufacture). If you file off the tip on a FMJ, the jacket becomes opened on both ends, or basically a sort of sleeve, that can come off the bullet core due to the frictio between it and the barrel. If this happens and you don't notice it, the next shot will blow up the barrel.
