With logic like that, it's no wonder people still believe in leprechauns and spontaneous generation.

Using the "we don't understand" argument to attempt to validate a belief. An argument from silence (no strong evidence where we might expect it to be found yet if the hypothesis is true).

This is the only approach useful for dealing with hypotheses that cannot be disproved, such as 'there's an invisible elephant in my living room'. If all non-visual tests fail to reveal the elephant and require ad hoc rationalizations such as 'he's also weightless and doesn't leave footprints' it becomes evidence against its existence.

Ok well I�m not saying that God exists period. I admit that there's a chance he might not, given all the evidence I'm saying that he most likely does. And there is no chance that I can prove it, but I�ll tell you a bit of what I think anyway.

First of all you need to know what I think God is.
Basically I say that he is everything that could possibly ever exist, and he is the infinite sized mind that exists within and 'out' of everything.
From this I could prove that he exists by saying '1: He is everything that exists 2: you know that everything that exists, exists 3: Therefore God exists. But I agree that's pretty weak, especially since it doesn't prove that there is a mind behind it all.

There must however be a mind behind all that exists, because if there wasn't, how would life know how to go about existing. Everything that exists can be broken down. Take a human being. A person is made up of organs, blood muscles etc, and those organs such as a 'heart' are made up of atriums, blood, veins etc. Those can be broken down into proteins, then into atoms, then into neutrons and protons, then into quarks etc. You can go so deep and then you will reach something that cannot be broken down any more, which is pure energy. Everything that exists is made up of this energy, which bundles together and alters its vibrations to make up matter. When you look at a stone it is just energy that is compressed to a certain level and vibrating at a certain level. And this is the same with everything. Everything in the universe is made up of energy. The question is how does the energy know how to move and react? A computer programme needs the mind of a programmer to tell it how to work and behave; otherwise it just doesn't do anything. Well surely energy needs to know how to behave, and God is the mind that tells it how to work. As I said before, if energy had no kind of mental instructions, then life would be like a TV screen with no reception.

Now the fact that we ourselves have a mind also suggests that there is God. No matter how complex a brain is, all it does is just follow instructions, (you could say that it's like a very complex robot). It cannot make decisions because just like a robot, it only follows the set rules that it's been given. Yet we have a mind that exists beyond the brain, which can change its programmed rules, and do what a complex robot can't ever do, and that is to decide our own destiny. What purpose is there for a mind? Surely we would live a more safe and peaceful existence if we only followed set rules of our brains that would ensure our happiness, safety and survival, but instead we have the ability to decide what we do, and what we experience, despite the fact that it can be hazardous to our survival and make us completely miserable. So why is there a need for the mind?
I believe the reason we have a mind is so that we can experience life, how we choose to experience it, and since our ideas of life is made up of how we personally perceive life, then you could say that we are experiencing ourselves.
So where did the mind come from, and where did the idea of wanting to experience ourselves come from?
The mind cannot be in any way physical because it is impossible to be viewed by any of the senses, so if anything it must be spiritual. You can view mind using your sixth sense (if it exists), but that is only mind communicating with mind that both exist on the spiritual plain.
Now one of the main arguments about God is that you can never prove he exists, but you can never prove that a mind exists, because if you were unaware of human behaviour, you would simply view humans as very complex robots, but we put faith in the fact that they have a mind despite the only evidence we have is that we ourselves have one. Now why should we be so arrogant as to believe that we are the only things capable of having a mind?
If we were the only things to have a mind, then surely we would be the most advanced beings in existence, but look at us. We don�t get along in the slightest, nature gets along far better than we do, so it brings me to the conclusion that it also has a mind, but the mind of nature is Godly.
