Walter Brecker and Donald Fagen, a.k.a. Steely Dan!

Heres the chorus to "Deacon Blues":

Learn to work the saxophone
I play just what I feel
Drink Scotch whisky
All night Long
And I die behind the wheel

They got a name for the winners in the world
I want a name when I lose
They call Alabama the Crimson Tide
You can call me Deacon Blues

Great great chorus. I like their lyrics especially because though all of his songs are concept songs, they vary in whether or not they strictly tell a story in narrative form, or whether they are a little more vague. And of course they range anywhere in between as well. As for their style, their metaphors and lyrical ideas use obscure pop culture references rather than more traditional lyrics. Anything from 19th century opera singers, expressionist German artists from the 1920s, to specific restaurants that only people who had been to some particular side on town in some particular city would have heard of.

Heres another favorite, a song called "Kid Charlemagne" which loosely tells the story of famous 1960s LSD chemist and dealer Owsley Stanley, who was eventually caught when he wasn't able to run from the police anymore after running out of gas (references in the last verse). Here are just the verses to the song:

Heres one last song. Its just a simple story song, on verse tells the story of a man who is to be hanger for murder like something in a Western but is spared when their is a problem with the hangman, another verse tells the story of a two timing woman who cheats on their narrator with his best friend, and yet another person tells the story of a gambler in Las Vegas who loses most of his money. And all of these stories have the same general idea, even though you know you shouldn't, you're still going to go out and do the same thing the next day. Be it losing all your money in a card game, going back to a two timing woman, or being quick to the trigger when things don't go your way. But its cool. Who else writes songs that could be the plot of a modern style Western? Not anyone else I can think of. If you like the dark stories found in country music, but prefer rock music...
