Re: Soon to be in college... Long distance?
quote:Originally posted by sinlesssiren:

We've both agreed to date other people, but with a limited fooling around policy until further notice. We want to keep seeing each other when we can because niether of us want to break up unless we should get serious about another person.

So, that's guaranteed to shatter one person, and make the one who gets serious with another person feel guilty and/or burdened.

quote: I know, I know, its a recipe for disaster, but at this point he would marry me if he could...

Why can't he?

quote:I only want to go on dates with people just "to see."

quote: At this point he's the only person I can imagine "being with" and I don't take sex, or our relationship, lightly.

These two statements are contradictory. If you want to explore other possibilities, then you indeed do take your relationship lightly. As well you should. Long distance relationships rarely work, unless they began that way, oddly enough. The transition from seeing each other whenever you want, likely every day, to not seeing each other as often, is jarring.

You'll both have moments when you can't concentrate in class, when you feel you really need the intimacy and closeness of each other but can't get it, when you feel guilty for seeking solace in associating with others, when you feel "held down" by a relationship when you want to associate with others.

It's this strange head game people play with themselves, alternating between having fun experiencing new things which necessarily exclude the other, and feeling guilty for doing so, and hearing about the other's new fun, and feeling left out. I've seen it many, many times. And more often than not, it starts out with both people feeling they're exceptionally "strong" people who, unlike anyone else, can "handle" this sort of thing.

Life is full of growth and transitions, and going away to college and leaving behind old ties is one of the major ones. Don't fool yourself into thinking it's easy.

One more note: if you can drive to the other person on a daily basis without disrupting your life, then it's really not a long-distance relationship. There's a lot of difference between being an hour away, and being three hours away.
