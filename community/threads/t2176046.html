Re: Australian writers
Patrick White is the most famous Australian writer, but a lot of people don't consider his work to be all that Australian.

Banjo Patterson and Henry Lawson are very famous for their Ballads.

It really depends what you're looking for. Do you want to know about the "Australian Story" i.e. shit like Gallipoli and the stolen generation which we're all supposed to care about but don't? Or do you want to get a more contemporary view of Australia. What it's like to live in the cities, the outback, lower socioeconomic areas etc...

Anyway,

I just read A Fortunate Life by A.B. Facey which is considered a classic here. It's really only interesting from a historical point of view, however, so it might not be what you're looking for.

He Died With a Felafel in His Hand by John Birmingham
Dopeland by John Birmingham

That last one is supposed to be about the cannabis culture in Australia. It's kind of inspired by Gonzo Journalism I think. Never got around to reading it and I've lost my copy of it but Birmingham is a funny writer so it's probably worthwhile.

There's lots of books based around racism and other issues relating to Australia's so-called multiculturalism. We read these in school so I can't remember any except for "Follow The Rabbit-Proof Fence" which was made into a movie a few years back. Actually, that's about Aborigines not immigrants. Still deals with racism I guess. Over The Top With Jim is a well-known autobiographical novel about some crazy kid from Russia.

Too much to mention really.

Re: Australian writers
If you're after a contemporary novelist who captures "Australia" and shoves it neatly into a good read, then it's hard to go past Tim Winton.

I can't say I ADORE his books but I have read them all, and I did enjoy each one. Unfortunately I was introduced to Winton via high school a few years back and that ridiculous dissection of Cloudstreet destroyed what would have been an incredibly enjoyable read...but my girlfriend loves his books so I read through them after she gets them and, credit where credit is due, they're great reads.

I really enjoyed all the Patrick White books I've read but I agree with above that they don't capture the Australian spirit like Winton.
