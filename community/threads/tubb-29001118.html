Re: Pain on a Chain (for Zero)
����Blade in the right hand, chain in the left. I keep enough slack in the chain that I can fully extend my stabbing arm without running short.
����I like to open with the chain, since I have more range with it. Either my opponent catches the chain (in which case I stab him), the chain wraps around him (in which case I stab him), or the ring on the chain strikes him upside the head, in which case I stab him if he is thrown off balance. Are we sensing a pattern, here?
����You can mix it up a bit by using some nunchuck style techniques with the chain. You can keep the chain spinning and strike repeatedly from a relatively safe distance until you find a good opening to make with the stabbing.
����Wielding the weapon is basically an excercise in speed and perception. As soon as you have an opportunity you have to take it. Neither the blade nor the chain are exceptionally powerful weapons. You can't just rush right in and start swinging like you could with a bat or a polearm. Used together both parts of the weapon can be deadly.

~Zero
