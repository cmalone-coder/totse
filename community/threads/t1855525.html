The coating on the inside of TV (and other) CRT's is called a "phosphor". It gets its name from the same (greek?) root as the element "phosphorus", which means "light bringer", IIRC.

The phosphor coating inside a CRT consists of compounds that give off visible light when hit by a high-velocity beam of electrons. They are generally made from exotic rare-earth materials like yttrium and lanthanum, as well as more common things like lead silicate, lead sulphide, and barium carbonate, depending on the color of light desired. Color CRT screens actually consist of a matrix of dots or stripes of 3 different phosphors, emitting red, green, and blue light. But they DO NOT contain phosphorus, AFAIK. They are, however, highly toxic substances, and are contained inside a high-vacuum glass envelope that can send sharp glass fragments flying into your face when it implodes. The tubes also can retain a high voltage charge which will give you a healthy shock unless properly discharged. Not a device worth playing around with unless you know what you are doing.

Don't be worried, Raptor. You ask good questions, and you word them in a way that we can understand. I commend you for at least putting forth that effort.

As for the dream:

The nipple in the flask should not be used here. It should be somehow capped for SWIDR's reaction. The top of the flask can be fitted with a 1-hole stopper that will fit the bottom end of the condensor. The condensor should be set straight up for the purpose intended. The 45 degree setup is used only in distillation, and isn't quite like you described.

At the top of the condensor, it should be fitted with a sturdy balloon.

Water needs to be run through the condensor from either a pump or a faucet. The water should go in through the bottom nipple, and out through the top nipple. If it's done opposite, the condensor will never fill, and will never reach full cooling capability.

With a 36 hour reflux, SWIL recommends 185 degrees. But don't quote SWIL, and don't set the timer. Reactions should NEVER be called at the set time, unless all the signs show that success has been made. Read the signs, they tell more about the reaction than the pre-sets.

Dom--

The reason you want the condenser straight up and down is so that a reflux can be established. If the condenser is simply condensing droplets and running them back down into the mix, then it is not properly refluxing. A good reflux is established when the hot vapors are being DRAWN UP into the tube as a result of the cooling jacket of the condenser. When the correct pressure gradient is achieved you will see a thick mass of vapor suddenly stop (at what point depends on the temps of flask and cooling jacket) with very little, if any vapor moving past that point. I have no idea if this even makes any sense, but my goal was to explain the difference between condensing and refluxing. If this sounds like a stupid explaination, someone please bail me out here.
