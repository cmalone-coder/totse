Re: Why NBA isn't as popular as NFL in USA.
Michael Jordan was the savior and the death of the NBA. He was the greatest and everyone watched to see him play. I still remember watching the NBA back in the early 90s and loving his games. But, he was the first to get huge sponsorships, commercials, shoes, etc. All the kids that wanted to grow up and be like Mike are now in the NBA. But they also all have their own shoes, commercials, sponsorships, multi-million dollar contracts and appear on tv shows, etc. It's hardly about the game anymore. It's all about everyone trying to be the next Michael Jordan and being the biggest name. Almost every NBA player has such a huge ego that people get turned off by it. Nobody wants to watch that.

When the Olympics were on, the Spain vs. China games was one of the best basketball games I've seen in 20 years. China was winning for the majority of the game, despite Spain being the clear favorite. They were working hard as a team, it was clear who the best players were (they didn't need to showboat or celebrate, you just need to watch them play). Spain was such a solid team that they kept playing their normal game and eventually ended up winning, but the Chinese team showed so much heart and desperation to win that it made the game very exciting. In the NBA, you never see those kinds of games. If it's not an important post-season game or a game with a rival team, the players just do their thing, don't really care, and give a "fuck it" attitude when they start to lose.

Kids today who practice basketball everyday and might end up in the NBA need to realize it's about the game, not the shoes or the tv shows.

^That's the most common complaint I hear. The argument can be used in reverse though, I've seen many low scoring sports where a fluke play will decide the outcome, and it's hard to believe that the better team won the game.

I can surely understand the "only the end matters" mentality of basketball, but I don't particularly agree.. If a team finishes the 1st quarter down 18-26, they get together and say "okay, we have to be better next quarter." Then maybe they're not, and they head to halftime down 38-53. This shows me that it's a game of fortitude and endurance, that it's important to play hard for every minute.
