Re: background check
This depends on the job you are applying for and the clearance you till be given, as well as the extent of your drug use.

While you may be able to pass a test now, if in the future you want to pursue an avenue with this government job that requires more thorough screening you may find yourself caught in your lie and screwed out of your job as well as your freedom, since lying about this sort of thing is a federal offense.

Background checks are usually done by asking you for the names of people that can be interviewed about your history and reliability, maybe your parents and other family members. If they tell the story the investigators want to hear, the investigators will ask them for four or five names and follow up with them, then ask those people for more names, and so on...depending on the level of scrutiny you will need.

Jobs with corporations that work with government security clearances (defense industry firms like McDonell-Douglas and technology developers like TRW) will be more lax when it comes to overlooking drug use, but a good rule of thumb is to deny ALL use of hallucinogens, since these disqualify you for pretty much any job in any screened field. LSD, psylocibin, and extasy are seen as disqualifiers by the military at the lowest level (enlistment) so you shouldn't admit to them unless you don't want to get hired. I have friends who have done just about every drug imaginable and then went on to become Force Recon Marines and TRW engineers, but they didn't do it by admitting their drug usage.

It all depends on your deniability and that of potential interviewees, and the level of scrutiny you will be placed under.

Good luck.

Re: background check
Did you ever get into legal trouble for drug use? If you did, then you better admit it regardless of your age when it happened. I was a recruiter for the USMC and we had accesss to juvenile records that were supposedly "sealed" after the people turned 18. If you didn't get into legal trouble, then don't worry about it.

I had a top secret clearance and they interviewed every reference that I provided. So just watch who's numbers you put down.

They also do credit checks which screw over alot of people. Military personel are given a little bit of slack on the credit, but civilians are looked at somewhat harder.

LSD isn't necessarily a permanent disqualification for the military, use within the last 2 or 3 years is temporary disqualifying. Habitual and experimental use are also important to remember. They'll most likely ask about the number of times that you used any drug. If you do admit to drug use, admit to a small number like 10-40 times and you should be ok. Don't tell them that you smoked pot every day for 4 years or they'll want to see records from a treatment facility.
