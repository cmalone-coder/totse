Re: Proper Knife Sharpening
Or invest in sharpening service from whoever yo uget your knives from.

Or if you prefer othher knivs (like I do), find a local sharpening service. For example, I use Sabatier, but there is a local company that rotates cooking equipment for rstaurants, including sharpening knives once a week. I get mine done once a month. Its not very expensive, and worth it if you cook a lot.

If you have good enough cutlery, you MIGHT want to consider doing something like this. Especially if you often have people over for dinner and cook a lot.

But for just intermitant sharpening, personally I think its just fine to look for an 'easy' sharpener that requires no skills (just running the sharp end of the blad down a little groove or something), is just as good as using steel or other common methods. EVen an electric sharpener (provided its a decent one) is a

good thing to have. But don't overuse them.

Make sure you're sharpening knives of a high enough grade steel to even make it worth sharpening them. Some knives will lose a razor point VERY quickly, whereas mine, will keep them for days and days.

Of course the only knives I use are two chefs knives, one paring knife, and a bread knife. :P

Re: Proper Knife Sharpening
A steel isn't for sharpening knives. It's for honing the blade (basically a quick temporary realignment of the crystals on the edge). If your knife is dull and you need to 'rebuild' the edge, you need a sharpening kit.
Electric rotary sharpeners have the unfortunate tendency of removing too much of the blade in the process; a good old fashioned foot-powered one is loads better (a case of slower is better; plus you can modulate its revs to your needs).
