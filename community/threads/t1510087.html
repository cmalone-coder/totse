I feel I can't trust my own emotions
I have the emotional stability of a five-year-old, and about as much mental stability. My emotions always seem to be at one extreme or another. I have a very low "social IQ" as well as very low "EQ." I see-saw wildly in temperament, mood, and... well, basically all mental functions. I have a gigantic ego combined with a crippling inferiority complex. I suffer from compulsions, obsessions, paranoia, and jealousy.

Basically, I don't feel like I can trust my emotional judgment. I don't know when it's appropriate to be upset, and when it isn't. I'm afraid of letting my paranoia, jealousy, and other destructive impulses take over... so my strategy was to basically ignore how I felt unless I was extremely, extremely, extremely upset. And even then I toned myself down. I'm worried, though, that now I'm a pushover... I don't know when to be angry, so I never let myself get angry, but what if I'm being weak? When is my anger a tool to fix a problem in our relationship, and when should I just let it go?

I feel proud, on one hand; she's the only one I've ever been able to sublimate my mental illness for. On the other hand, I worry that by doing so, I'm actually weakening our relationship.

Not really much of a point to this, I guess, other than to ask if anyone else feels the same way.

I assume you're after a normal, healthy, slightly male dominant relationship? If not the following won't really apply to you.

So your girlfried does something wrong. Basically, if you feel angry, you have a right to be angry. But here's the key: you have to express that anger in a calm and dignified way. Any boy can snap, but it takes a man to be calm, assertive and forceful.

Snapping is bad for several reasons, not all of which are readily apparent. For one, you're hurting her, and that's not good. Two, you're losing control. Women are generally more emotional than men, and to be a good man I think is to remain calm and cool headed. Third, by snapping at her and beind nasty you hand her an undue amount of leverage, because after the heat of the argument has faded, you end up regretting it, apologising and grovelling for your hurtful remarks.

Basically, trust your emotions, but don't let them overwhelm you. It takes practice, and it starts off slow, but just keep at it.
