Re: a coder's linux
http://fedoraproject.org/wiki/

The very top of the page has "Download Fedora Core 6". Apparently it's nicknamed Zod, and is the last stable version of Fedora Core. FC7 is on Test 4, which shouldn't be too buggy, but I haven't used it so who knows. It's usually not too big of an issue to either upgrade a system or reinstall, though. Just put the OS on one partition and, uh, everything else you want on another (programming code, music etc.).

If you can't save any installed programs, simply make a quick note of which programs you find yourself using often, and write them down on some looseleaf. When you go to reinstall your system, you can just remove others and go back to your tailored selection of applications. Not only do you end up with what you want/need, but it slims down the amount of crap it comes with. There's no need for as much as most Distros come with, but 5 cd's is just ridiculous. Unless you have a sickeningly slow internet connection and can't download programs, it's unnecessary.

Ubuntu is one cd and comes with a decent set of applications (though not especially phenominal in some cases, I dislike Totem personally, I replace it with Amarok), it's very, very easy to simply mark programs you aren't fond of, and install programs you do like.
Synaptic handles dependencies, meaning it downloads the required files for you, versus you trying to hunt them down.


There truly are an astounding number of distros out there, and believe it or not, FC is not the most phenomenal of them all. When it does come down to it, if you've got a decent internet connection and some spare cd's/dvd's, go wild and download 3-4 distros. If you have a large HD, you can even run them all at once (in terms of dual/triple/quad booting, not simultaneous execution) and try them.

If you're a hardcore coder, maybe Slackware would be a fun project to tinker with. Where Ubuntu/FC/Debian are very user-friendly, Slackware is very basic. I wouldn't personally use it as a primary distro, but you can learn large amounts from messing with it.

http://distrowatch.com/

Go there, take a read through the various distros and read opinions. Don't trust just one opinion, even if it -is- a moderator, and by the same stretch, don't trust my comments and more. Do the work yourself and you'll learn more in the long run.

http://distrowatch.com/dwres.php?resource=major

There is also a page which is catering to that question.
