Well, once you're in high school, you *have* to bulk up for running, or else you can't even compete.

Usually almost all weight training done is local muscle endurance training, which helps the muscles utilize oxygen more efficiently. It helps in hill running, and in the kick at the end.

Also, one of the major goals of weight training is to prevent injury; it strengthens tendons and ligaments, and enhances bone density. It prevents stress fractures which are a complete bitch to deal with, since they take you out of the season completely.

Weight training also enhances greatly your postural support muscles in the trunk, which helps you maintain correct form when running, which makes for a more efficient stride.

It shows a huge difference, when you go to races including and up to the 1600. Arm strength allows for an increased stride, and also for better form.

I could go on and on about the merits of weight training, but those are the major points. The difference between a person weight training and person who's not is visible, and is most often large.

About burning out. It's both a psychological and physical.

A lot of people tend to overtrain in the off-season, and when they enter the pre-season, their bodies are already worn down; they're not at peak condition, they've gone past a point where their body can handle the training and recover, and things start to break down, sometimes quite literally.

When you burn out, it's actually obvious, because injuries will go up in a big way. You'll also start feeling tired when training, and you won't get any faster.

On the psychological side, you start feeling tired, and will try to push your body further, beyond it's bounds, thus creating more injury. It's kind of hard to explain, but it's definitely there.

A lot of people burn out in the off-season, but some people burn out while in-season, working too hard on the workouts, etc. Which is why having a coach is so damned important.

It's really hard to explain, unless you've actually seen it and experienced it while training. But I'm sure there are some sites or something that discuss it.

Boris- You're gonna have to start working your ass off getting in shape. In high school, competitive running for the mile means times around 4:30-4:40.

I was running around 5 min. frosh year (I was 13), I still wasn't fast enough to really be competitive (probably because of the psychological aspect of running against seniors 5 years older than I).

To cap off an uncharacteristically long post...Running competitively is damned hard, because of those small details like nutrition, and training programs, and running form, pacing, etc. that affect your performance in a big way.
