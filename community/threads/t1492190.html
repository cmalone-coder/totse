Breaking my own general rule and making a second reply:

At the end of the day, everyone is responsible for how they handle what has befallen them. Polite society doesn't entirely believe that, of course, which is fine; it's good to know others will look out for your well-being, and it engenders a sense of gladness and worth to do the same for them. The downside is that people have a tendency to play by different sets of rules, which lead to situations like mine thousands of times a day all over the world, around a multitude of different catalysts (not every problem is of the boyfriend-girlfriend-third wheel variety, of course).

That, in turn, leaves us with the unfortunate fact that each situation needs to be judged by its own merits. As the human race has this godawful tendency to tell sense it can damn well fuck off, people will often take their blocky, self-made laws and apply them to the letter regardless of what details may warrant a different approach.

So how responsible *are* we for the feelings of others?

I would argue that we are the only ones we have to live with at the end of the day, and that we cannot be held responsible for more than putting forth our best, most honest effort to lessen or erase the negative impact of our actions on others while not screwing ourselves over in the process. There are levels to this, depending on exactly who may be affected by our actions, but it's the general theory I try to put forth in whatever activities I partake.

Despite that, I feel some responsibility for my friend's state of mind; had the girl and I not gotten or stayed together, this wouldn't be happening. But I don't feel bad about doing it, because we tried to find a way to make it better, even though it's likely none exists. In Ben's case, he should feel like an utter fuckhead, because nothing you've said even remotely suggests anything but self-interest in his actions. If someone else comes around with a similar tale, they'll probably fall somewhere in the middle. It varies.
