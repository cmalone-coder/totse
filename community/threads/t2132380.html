Re: Ecstasy addiction
So you just want information (the kind you want to hear) spoon-fed to you? Or for people to say "go ahead dude, lolz."

Doing it frequently is bad because it kills the magic very quickly, and in a cheap/unsatisfying way. If you abuse it, your interest in MDMA will die very quickly, and all the times you do it in the future, you'll find it feels much more "hollow" and meaningless and you idealize how it used to be.

Maybe 2 months or whatever is a bit long to wait when first discovering E, but the longer you wait, the more it pays off. For one, the comedowns are easier when you're not totally depleting your neurotransmitters. The quality of a rolling experience often depends on you not using it too frequently. Ideally, it should be taken only on special occasions, to make sure it's always good/not a waste, and so it's not made into too much of a habit. That way it stays "special", as it is intended to be, for one's enjoyment. Wait a minimum of 2 weeks between rolls. I'd seriously recommend a month. The cheapening of the E experience is lame, and it's really not addictive so there's not much excuse other than addictive personality and/or youthful ignorance/stupidity to be doing it very frequently.

One of the most interesting studies on this I've seen is Hrometz's 2004 work. While the oxidization of serotonin by MAO-B creates a small amount of H2O2, introduction of dopamine to the cell increases H2O2 by 84%...

http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15308297

...and, uhh... oh yeah. Dopamine + MDMA increases H2O2 by 420%. One more reason to keep meth out of your molly, for starters.

not to threadjack, but what would be a good dose/frequency for pure mdma be to avoid this situation? like once/twice a month?

Well, that's a good question.

Most studies have found that a normal dose of pure MDMA is not neurotoxic. 'n they've also found that things like excessive dosages or abusively frequent prolonged use are neurotoxic.

Most all of the mechanisms of cell death have been pretty well discussed, walking through each step of the way on the molecular level. One of the things that hasn't been mentioned, however, is why (besides meth) dopamine might get sucked into a sertonergic axon.

http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/7934616

Apparently, dopamine only gets vacumed up when serotonin runs dry. If you don't deplete it to empty, it seems everything turns out alright, 'n it's a fairly harmless, safe drug with no neurological body load.

However, there's a catch... as mentioned by Stone's work, above, tryptophan hydroxylase activity is reduced for a moderately prolonged period of time after MDMA use. Sadly, no one is willing to mention how long "long-term reduction" is, except that one study...

http://www.pubmedcentral.nih.gov/articlerender.fcgi?artid=1705495

Mentions "months." Not too helpful unless they mention how many months, really... but more to the point, that reduction in serotonin synthesis is going to make it a bit harder to replenish one's stores - which as we've established, is neccesary, as it's running empty that causes your brain to flat-out fry itself. 'n while that TPH is also dopamine-mediated, shutting off 100% dopaminergic neurotransmission in a normal brain isn't particularly viable. Among other things, your frontal cortex would shut down entirely. Best just to not do meth with your E, and accept the natural DA as unavoidable.

Hmm. So, to recap...

- Your raephe nucleus completely fries when your serotonin stores are emtpied.

- MDMA releases a lot of serotonin, lowering stores, and...

- MDMA can make it harder to fill back up.

...that last one is the main reason for spacing it out. It's what keeps us from just eating a lot of tryptophan-rich foods and dosing 2 or 3 times a week - less and less will be converted. 'n by now, it should be clear that diet can affect neurotoxicity a lot - someone who is extremely depressed due to low serotonin is probably having their brain fry without the drugs through the same mechanism and will experience negative impact from MDMA much quicker at lower doses.

Of course, that last reference also showed that rats on a low recreational dose in a chronic, abusive fashion don't have the 5-HT depletion of rats on a "noxious" dose for prolonged periods - another example of depletion overload vs. more-moderate use. How much this applies to the T5H reduction, no one seems willing to say.

Nonetheless, the data regarding T5H versus immediate serotonin depletion (and resulting neurotoxicity) suggests that "how long to wait" is actually two questions : "how long to wait until my serotonin store recover," and "how long to wait until my ability to recover recovers."

Best estimate for that which I've seen is shulgin's 6 month figure, though there's probably some sort of break-even which is closer to the 2-3 months constantly bandied around, where one's recovery ability never recovers, but stays around a medium average rather than moving back to normal or down to further inhibition.

As far as I can tell, what this means is that, while one can probably dose every 4-7 days if one has a good diet for serotonin production, the first two or three times they consume MDMA, the more one deviates from shulgin's "twice a year" figure, the longer one will have to wait. This gives rise to an interesting "pyramiding idea," dosing idea, dosing 1 week later, dosing 2 weeks later, dosing 4 weeks later, etc, but for the most part, I'd try to stay away from that.

Short answer? The more one doses, the longer one will have to wait. If one has a month where one has been rolling once every weekend, it's probably harmless so far, but one should probably give it at least 2-3 months before the next time, at least. If one has been dosing once every six months for the last few years, and no more frequently, but one just rolled four days ago and then was invited to a community session with one's closest friends - it's probably alright, but be aware that your recovery time will get a lot longer with such a repeated dosing, and you'll have to wait a couple weeks 'till next time no matter what the event.

I wish there had been constructed a clear f(x) for the T5H alterations, because they're... sorta what determine the replenishment rate of your serotonin stores. Unfortunately, that data is not availiable. All we know is that 4-7 days would normally do it, if one eats rich foods - but that each use increases the recovery time longer and longer with each time, for some number of months.

Damn this vacum of information. ;) One could probably get away with every couple months, more or less. 'n ascorbic acid and a-lipioic acid have been shown to block peroxide-mediated damage even when deliberately induced, so a really careful person might be able to up it past the saftey point, safely, for special occasions, if they let their T5H come back afterwards.
