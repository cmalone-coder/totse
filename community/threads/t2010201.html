Its really difficult describing the "feeling" of raising kids, because unless you've experienced it is almost impossible to describe. It would be like someone that has never had a drop of alcohol ask...."what does it feel like to be drunk". The only way I can begin to elaborate what its like having kids is by simply saying its the highest of the highs, and the lowest of the lows. You'll get such enjoyment and pride from the silliest things.......the first time they smile at you, first time they say your name, the way they run to you when you first walk in the door, seeing their amazement opening gifts during Christmas and Birthdays. These are all emotions that are nothing short of astounding to feel, and you look back and wonder how did you ever "live" a fulfilled life before you had kids. The whole notion of NOT having children in your life is totally incomprehensible, even if you had a very happy life before they were born.

The flip side is though, you are opening yourself to a whole new set of stresses, frustration and disappointment. Right now my wife and I are dealing with a 4 year old going through an amazingly difficult phase just being defiant and getting into all kinds of mischief. Testing his limits I suppose. Ya wanna know how my morning started? Most mornings if he wakes up before me, he usually turns on the TV or plays....and wakes me when he is hungry or needs something to drink. Today I get up with the declaration of....."daddy, come check it out, I cut the kitties with a scissors". Sure enough, the 2 kittens we just got 2 weeks ago had some hair missing and globs of it were all over the house. I then find his chocolate milk mix all over the kitchen counter, and amazingly enough most of the mix found its way to the living room all around/on top of my surround sound system's sub-woofer. (Needless to say I'm more pissed about him possibly fucking up my surround sound, than giving the cats a little trimming). Oh, and he took it upon himself to make himself breakfast that consisted of Pop Tarts and a huge plate of Cheese Curls! AHHHHHHHHHHHHH!!!! Yes, THIS is how my morning started!!!! : (

One other thing I'd like to add to this great topic is the way our culture seems to be so insistent in the mantra of "raise them, now boot them out". For some reason society now dictates that when people grow up into their 20's they have to move out or be deemed a "loser" still living with Mommy and Daddy. The parents are looked at as failing, and the kids are judged also because they are still living at home. Why is that? It wasn't always that way, and if you look back in history many households had sometimes 3 and 4 generations living comfortably under one roof. Maybe its just selfishness, or over controlling parents, or just a plain old lack of compromise.....but it seems both sides of the equation just can't wait till the younger set move on in today's day and age. I find it very weird how society seems to say we are supposed to love/nurture our kids for 20+ years, but god forbid should they still be under your roof any time after that. Think about how many bad decisions and life altering choices people in their late teens and early 20's make, all in the quest of "escaping" their family. And how many old folks are living alone in nursing homes because of this "isolation" mindset. It appears society has "evolved" to a point where having attachment and devotion to your family is more of a fault/weakness, moreso than an admirable trait.
