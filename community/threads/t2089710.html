'Real Women'
This is about bodies, not humanitarian issues

I opened February's Marie Claire today, and flicked to the editors page where they print some of the letters they've recieved about the previous issue and its articles. ( http://www.marieclaire.co.uk )

The first words to hit me were 'role model' and 'celebrity', amongst the rest of the others you can guess.

"I was so pleased to see America Ferrera on your December cover. I am a size 16, and America, who isn't your typical size-zero celebrity, gives hope to all of us real women with curves.
Hollywood and the media need to realise what they're doing to young people - five girls I went to school with are now battling with eating disorders. America is someone who I look to as a role model, and I hope that when I have children there will be more women like her in the media for them to admire."

- Becky Alchin, Croydon

( http://images.askmen.com/galleries/actress/america-ferrera/pictures/america-ferrera-picture-1.jpg this is America Ferrera, who plays Betty Suarez in 'Ugly Betty')

America Ferrera is just one of the dozens of female celebrities hailed as this 'new role model' for women. I hope they just mean sizewise. She hasn't done any great moral deed, she has merely let herself be photographed and interviewed in regards to her job as an actress. She wants this - she also is capable of making her own decisions regarding her diet and levels of exercise, and how much she wishes to acknowledge the 'skinny' hype.

http://guanabee.com/Ferrera_Glamour_09_04_07.jpg

Photoshopped. The fat has been taken out from under her arms, they've sculpted her a more defined waist, defined her collar bones, flattened her stomach out...and given her the 'lollipop head' so often styled by model Sophie Dahl after her breakneck voyage into size 8 from 12.
Having seen sites like this http://www.judgmentofparis.com , which look perfectly innocent and well meaning, actually unearth a deep discomfort inside. It's just weird. Have a look if you have time.

I think the problem I have with all of this is the absolute bitchiness of these 'empowered women', writing in about larger celebrities and slating anyone under a size 10. I take serious issue with this.

-

Two bodies are never the same - with the exception of identical twins, even then, they have their own minds on their shoulders. What gives these people the right to think they can say who is and who isn't a 'real woman'? What on earth are naturally slim girls, girls with health problems, other than women just like our villains? I do not think being 20lbs heavier than someone gives them any moral highground, whatsoever.

I think a common misconception is that women diet, dress, and groom 'for' men, I disagree with this, it is much more complicated than that. Women dress for women- it gives them a place in the female hierarchy that's been created, and it denotes who is 'queen bee'.
High fashion models and mens pin ups are rarely the same people. Jessica Alba, Alessandra Ambrosio, Angelina Jolie, Adriana Lima...compare those to Euginia Volodina, Julia Stegner, Alek Wek Gemma Ward, all models for their own unique look. It's much more about being able to carry it off with who you are rather than what size you are. Thats a whole different story however.

This is not a 'do you like fat girls' thread, at all. This is to hopefully give you a different slant on the issue.

What gets me is that these women would get so extremely offended if a 'skinny' girl started publicly dissing their weight or size, and it would be hailed as a 'horrific crime against your fellow woman' - but that is just what they are doing to the slimmer girls. They have no right to say this, they are acting like bitchy little girls. I always make a point of this when I hear someone wax lyrical about this in public. People make their own choices about their weight and size, even though this can be difficult for people in some stages of life, but not forever. We are all 'real' people. Size 6 or size 16. The size 16 consumer has her own natural weight inclination, just as the size 6 consumer does. They can change their weight up and down the scale all they like, and it should not be accepted as okay to knock anyone for it.

Personally, I have always been self conscious about my body. I developed early in contrast to my peers, who took another year or two for their hips and boobs to grow. I am now 5'1", UK size 6-8, US size 2-4. 32DD bra size, shoe size 5. People ask me if my breasts are 'real', am I wearing a padded bra, all this bollocks. No I'm not, yes by breasts are real, and I am not apologising for any of it! I am genetically inclined to look like I do, as is everyone. People should not be given a 'tag' in society based on their bodies, it is all sorts of wrong.

What are these people trying to achieve exactly? My inner bitch says that they are trying to make themselves feel better because they've been unhappy about their bodies in the past, or something. Alot of the press gets directed at mothers who have had drastic weight changes (either way) after giving birth. Where it might not be healthy at all to plummet down two dress sizes after pregnancy ( at all!), it is as much a 'valid' decision as eating to a few clothes sizes up. The general concensus is that suddenly being a mother means you have a right to be 10lbs overweight, that you've earnt it. If you think that, fine, so do alot of people. I personally think that being your healthy optimum weight is always more desirable than being under or over too much. This is what I strive for in my own life.

My own mother stayed the same size as she was when she was 18 after she had my sister and myself, and was just like all the other women in our family - small, slim, blonde, blue eyed. She was like that until she was 45 or so. She ate what she wanted, and exercised. She got alot of bitchiness from other mothers for being a toned size 10, and I think that the attitude towards it is sickening. She was not without her problems at all - she suffered from depression, and was ill with PND (Post Natal Depression) following the birth of my sister. In her case, she didn't gain weight as a result of this, but a lot of people do. I think sometimes people should just accept that it is unfortunate that they have yo-yo'ed in weight, but it is mainly dictated by our genetic makeup. We do have a choice, but we are a fully written program. You wouldn't slate someone for having black hair, or blonde hair, so why size, taking this into account?


I am so sick of reading these harsh judgements by 'real women'. We see it everywhere - on TV, in newspapers, on the train, everywhere. We, as people are so quick to judge -

"Oh isn't she skinny? She must be anorexic",

"Oh isn't she fat, she must have an overeating disorder"

- what? These hypothetical girls could be cousins, and it wouldn't make a difference. They might have lost a friend or family member, something else entirely might be going on in their lives, and their weight has taken a back seat. What if they heard them say this? It could be the grain of rice that tips the scale.
When my mother had cancer, I ended up very dangerously ill after not even thinking about eating. It was likely my mother was going to die, I wasn't really thinking about how I looked in 'those jeans' or whatever. My mother didn't die. I got better, and I look back at myself then and see how oblivious I was to how I looked.
Please, before you say anything about someone being too fat, too skinny, whatever - remember that 'real women' are PEOPLE. They're all real people. At the heart of every real person, there is a fragile soul. While we hope for the best circumstances in our lives, some things are predetermined ( genetics) and cannot be helped. We all hope that people will show us kindness in everyday life, but especially when we are in need. If that means one gossip keeping their mouth shut that day and saving some distress and shame, even though no one will ever know it, then it is a very positive thing.

You're more than a clothes size. You're a real woman, you're a real man - but at the heart of this you are a real person. Don't be so quick to judge! Fat or thin, average, struggling, critical...we're all people. We need to be kind to each other.
