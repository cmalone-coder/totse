Re: failing a semester.
You don't get credit for the classes. It affects your gpa negatively. You have to repeat the courses required for your major. If you have a scholarship dependent on maintaining a certain gpa, you lose it. You realise how much you dislike your major, and decide to switch. You become so despondent with your failure you quit school all together. All depends.

It's not something you want to do, but not the end of the world. Figure out why you're failing/failed, figure out what you can do to limit the negative impact, or make sure not to repeat the same mistakes. Where are you in the semester, you might still be able to withdraw with some sort of non gpa affecting designation. Just make sure to figure out what went wrong and not repeat it.

Re: failing a semester.
your university likely has some kind of deficiency points program, you'd have to ask an advisor or look it up on their website or in your student handbook. at least that's how it is here. so many F's will put you on academic probation, and eventually cause academic dismissal.

my university also has something called "freshman forgiveness" where if you get a D or F in something your freshman year, you can retake it and erase that grade from your record. i don't know if you're a freshman, but...there might be something like that available to you.

are you entirely sure you can't pass any of these classes at this point? i doubt your motivated to, but a strong grade on the final and a decent curve could give you C's.

either way, one semester won't get you kicked out.
