Here is something to consider:

When it comes to military hardware, installations, etc. the phrase is neither confirm nor deny. When you begin answering questions, more follow. Once you get to the point of genuine classified information, the questions do no stop and then the allegations of government cover up follow. The easiest course of action is to say nothing.

Let us use the SR-71 as an example. The predecessor of the SR-71 was the YF-12. The YF-12 was supposed to be the high altitude interceptor for the Russian Bear and follow-on bombers. Unlike the US, the Russian nuclear strategy lay primarily in the land and sea launched ICBM's, not manned bombers. Therefore, the need for those interceptors was gone. The airframe had already been developed and it was suitable for high altitude recon. The YF-12 was modified for that purpose. Now comes the press wanting to know specifics. The press already knew most of the airframe capability, but they wanted to know system capabilities (ie camera, SLAR, IR, electronic, et al). This was pure and simple classified information that the Soviet Military also wanted to know. Wasn't gonna happen. So, the US acknowledged the existance of the YF-12 and passed the buck on the SR-71. Was anyone really fooled, I think not. But not every question deserves an answer especially when it comes to national security. Do some people not like that answer, yes they do not. However, national security is paramount. Now, is every issue national security, no. That is where you the citizen must really think through an issue before passing judgement.
