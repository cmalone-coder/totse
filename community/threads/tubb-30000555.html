The following are the notes that formed the basis of a seminar I gave on illegal firearms, earlier this year.

FIREARMS SEMINAR


 The debate surrounding illegal firearms has increased recently. Particularly after three shootings over the same weekend in April, leaving two people dead and six injured.

* The gun laws were dramatically altered in 1996 in the aftermath of the Port Arthur Massacre, to include the registration of all personal firearms. A summary from the Australian Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research shows;

� Murders and robberies involving firearms have declined over the last five years.
� 'Shoot with intent' incidents have risen over the last five years.
� About half of all 'shoot with intent' offences involving handguns and other firearms occur in a public place.
� The increase in firearm offences has been most pronounced in areas of Sydney where drug trafficking is a problem.
� Young males aged 18-19 are much more prone to involvement in shooting incidents than older males.
The bureau compares the situation here to that of the US, and summarises it by saying - The increase in homicides involving youths and handguns (in the US) is thought to have been caused primarily by the development of the 'crack' market, the recruitment of young people to sell drugs on the street, increased arming of drug sellers, the carrying of guns for protection and status, and irresponsible and casual use by young people (Blumstein & Rosenfeld 1998).
There has been a specific study on the Canterbury-Bankstown and Fairfield-Liverpool Subdivisions, which has shown a much larger increase in gun related crimes in the last five years, than the rest of NSW. It is evident that a large proportion of handgun shootings originate from these two Subdivisions. In fact shootings in these areas account for 55 per cent of all handgun shootings which occurred across NSW during the year 2000. While there is no direct evidence that the increase in shootings is drug-related, these are areas known to have significant drug trafficking problems.

The attraction of such laws is that they do not impinge upon those who wish to possess a firearm but have no intention of using it to commit an offence (e.g. sporting shooters). There are two problems, however, with efforts to reduce gun crime focused solely on sanctioning those who use firearms in the commission of an offence. Firstly, many offences involving firearms are not premeditated or are committed by people indifferent to the prospect of apprehension and punishment. Secondly, the larger the supply of legally owned firearms in the community, the easier it is for firearms to fall into the hands of those willing to use them in the commission of crime.

Media release: Firearms and Violent Crime
Release date: 6 June 2001
Homicides and robberies with firearms have declined over the last few years in New South Wales but non-fatal shooting offences have increased. These are two of the key findings to emerge from a new research study on firearm violence released today by the NSW Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research.
The Bureau report shows that in 1995 there were nearly 30 homicides involving firearms in New South Wales but last year only about half this number of firearm homicide offences occurred. Robberies involving firearms increased from 677 in 1995 to more than 1,100 in 1997 but fell back to 655 last year.
Non-fatal shooting offences remained relatively stable between 1995 and 1998 but increased from about 50 offences in 1998 to nearly 100 offences last year. The increase in shooting offences involving handguns has been particularly marked, with less than 10 of these offences in 1995 but more than 40 of them last year.
