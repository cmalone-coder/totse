From the American Journal of Clinical Pathology:

The time frame between exposure to HIV and the ability to detect HIV antibody is known as the window period. The window period consists of 2 phases.

In the first phase, HIV replication can be demonstrated in lymph nodes and the liver, but not in blood.[2] This phase of the window period is known as the eclipse phase, since circulating virus cannot be demonstrated.

In the second or viremic phase of the window period, HIV is present in the circulation, but HIV antibody is not detectable.

Studies in health care workers who acquired HIV via a nosocomial (hospital acquired) exposure estimate that the median length of the entire window period is 40 days.[2] However, the viremic phase of the HIV window period is approximately 22 days.[3]

From what i could find, the actual window period is something like 40-50 days, but the commonly accepted time frame for HIV testing is as cindy said, 3 months.
