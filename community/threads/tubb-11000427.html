    Gnu/Linux on the other hand is based on freedom. Total and absolute.

  • Freedom. The gnu licence system means anyone who wants a copy of gnu/linux is able to download or purchase a copy at very little or no cost. This however, is not a requirement of he licence. The licence, put simply, means that you (the owner of the software) has the ability to sell, change, giveaway, destroy the software. The only requirement of this is that everyone you sell/give this software to must be able to do the same.

  • Security. Quite simply, Linux is (as are most *nix based systems) very secure compared to windows. The fundamental file permission set-up means its harder to break into and even harder again for viruses to penetrate.

  • Stability. Thousands of people work together across the net to improve Linux and Linux programs. If someone finds a bug, they can look at the code and then write a patch, which, if successful, will probably be included in the next version.

  • Knowledge. Since the source code is, ands must, be made available, everyone who wants to know anything about the program can find out. There�s no inexplicable crashes, reboots, no unknown little snippets which send your details off to a central database.

  • The Spirit. The general freedom and set up of gnu/Linux has created a *nix community that is hard to find anywhere else. The people involved rarely work for any guaranteed money and instead code, program and develop out of a love or need for creativity and personal expansion. If you need help with an aspect of using or developing a program, another *nix user will, more often than not, go significantly out of his way to help you. The group effort and development of gnu/linux programs means that they are available to anyone and due to the licenses will stay that way forever.
