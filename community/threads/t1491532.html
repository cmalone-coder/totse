The difficulty of true objectivity
Have you ever known someone who you used to be able to be completely, brutally honest with, who you could confide anything in, who would confide anything to you?

Most have.

Have you ever started to love the person, and notice that one of the first casualties is your collective objectivity, your honesty? Notice how you begin to hold your tongue for fear of hurting the other or making him/her jealous? Have you noticed how, even if the relationship DOES work out, you find part of yourself thinking "...I miss when we were just friends, and didn't have these fights/weren't jealous/weren't having sex/weren't so intense about everything, just simple and happy"?

One would logically think that a true friendship that builds into love over the years is the best, surest kind that could ever form. It's not.

It's the WORST. In said scenario, you know both the simple/innocent/fun friendly side, and the convoluted/deep/loving side, and sometimes, you don't know which one you want more. You can try to have it both ways, but you never can.

An example? Both of you, in the past, when you were friends and dating others, would have problems with your date, and would confide in each other and ask for advice, and do great. However, now that YOU two ARE the couple, and when you have problems, you'll want to talk to nobody but your partner about the issue...only you cannot, because your partner IS the issue. You'll feel trapped, unsure, scared, you'll feel you have nobody to talk to who will understand you like your partner/former best friend, but you can talk and be honest no longer.

Moral of the story?

If you're friends with an opposite-sex member (or same..ugh, but I gotta be PC) and you think you might be starting to love her...

BEAT YOUR FEELINGS DOWN WITH A FUCKING STICK AND NEVER, EVER, EVER TALK ABOUT THEM TO YOUR FRIEND!!

One of two things will happen:

-You'll be ok as friends for awhile, until your building love won't be able to remain inside you any longer, and you'll be very sad until you can express them..and when you do, you'll find he/she feels the same way. You'll explore your love and (hopefully) live happily ever after.

-You'll eventually realize that they were just extreme empathy and understanding for someone you connect to and care about; a feeling of person-to-person connection you share with no one else, and you'll realize that this is what you want with him/her, not love. Hell, you'll feel like love is inferior. Friendship does tend to be a holier bond; one that can be as deep or deeper than a romance, and devoid of the jealousy, control over each other's emotions, inexorable hurt (ther'll be pain in any relationship; whether or not you work through it is the question).

Keep your love close, and your true friends closer. And seperate.

No matter what you feel, it will all work itself out for the better in the end; you just have to persevere. Never try to seek that which isn't there, or shouldn't be sought until you're sure.

Don't, or else you'll end up like ME!! (living in a box, injecting cheap, cut-to-shit drugs with a conch shell and some weird car part, living off tacos and watered-down, half-empty soda cups you find in the trash behind Taco Bell; that is, if the stray cat's don't get it first..etc.)

Seriously, though: Don't try to build romance with someone who you've always known as a friend. It will lead to hurt, and much more hurt than just losing a love alone; trust me, because I can say that, having done both, but I'm not the point here. I'm just trying to relate experience, to release you from T3h Matrix0r. Or something.

Anyway, I'll shut up now.
