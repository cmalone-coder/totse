Re: Concert Photography
It will be difficult with a camera like that. As already said, you'll need to be one of the closet people to the band, and have room to move. I wouldn't recommend setting your camera on too much of a high ISO because the noise will give you terrible picture quality. As far as your F stop goes, you're going to want to let as much light as possible in, so using the lowest number possible would be best. This in turn will mean you will have a smaller depth of field, so you better watch out for your focusing (auto-focus might help, but it depends on how much you trust your camera to auto-focus, it may just pick the wrong object).
If your camera can do it, set it to some form of bulb function, get the movement of the performers and lights and then fire the flash off at the end of the exposure to freeze them in their final 'pose'. I don't know if the flash on your camera will be powerful enough - the couple of times I've photographed a concert I've had a dedicated flash on the top of my DSLR.

Re: Concert Photography
It really depends on the lighting, atmosphere and mood you are trying to capture.

My advice to you is to set your ISO to around 800, if the lighting conditions are bad, don't be afraid to go to 1600. Slightly grainy pictures are better than underexposed or blurred out ones. (Grainy + Black and white always gives a raw and powerful atmosphere to your images)

You will probably be using the widest aperture. Choose a lens (If you have more than one) that can take in the most light, since you will need it.

Change the metering on your camera to spot lighting, to get an accurate reading in those difficult lighting situations.

Now, the shutter speed; you'll want to set the shutter speed as high as you can without underexposing. No 1/40th's or lower. I'm saying; try to get around 1/100th+ of a second (if possible.)

The reason I'm saying this is that you'll want to be moving around taking vantage points of the band, you may have to zoom into their faces which makes having a steady hand much harder. Ask to get a spot in the front row or even to the sides of the stage. You won't have time to set up a tripod and the like; unless you have hands of steel, you will have blurry images. The band will be moving too so keep that into consideration.

If you have an underexposed photo or two, just remember you can touch it up in Photoshop.

Also look at the lighting, when you get to the venue, check around where the performer will be lit, understanding the situation and lighting conditions can give you a great handicap.

Lastly, try not to use flash. Even if you have an external one, which would work much better, try to keep it at an absolute minimum.

Anyway, good luck!
