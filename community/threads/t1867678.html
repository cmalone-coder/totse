You can use a drill press to drill out the barrel and cylinder of many of those cheap tear gas guns. It may not have rifling anyway; why would they have put in rifling? It would be smooth, lousy long-range accuracy but oh well, a snake gun. (which is what people do this for, even now--they WANT a smoothbore pistol, to use with them little .22lr shotshells. And the shotshells actually work pretty well that way. You don't want rifling for those)

------

But if I had done this (for some reason-?) I would be very hesitant to put anything more powerful that standard 22LR ammo in it. You might end up being able to fit 22 mags in the thing, but I wouldn't do that.

Sorry for rambling, I was in a hurry:

Firstly,

I got to hold a starter pistol once when I was in high-school years ago, and it looked just like a (plain steel) very-small-frame .22LR revolver. The main difference was that each of the chambers in the cylinder had some kind of metal "X" thing rammed into them halfway through, leaving just enough room in the back to load a blank, but not a real bullet. The barrel itself was unobstructed, but I don't remember if it had rifling or not. ~~~~~ So, if that was how they are still made, all you'd have to do is drill out the "X" thing in each cylinder, or maybe push it out somehow. I don't recall if it looked pushed in or cast in place. The rest of the parts of the gun appeared to be a casting, with the surface left rough (cheap).

-------

Get a phone book out and call around to sporting goods stores. Sports Authority sells regular guns, they may have starter pistols or maybe can order them (ya probably have to be 18 tho). Johhny Brock's sells high-school type team-sports stuff, they might carry them also....

Re: Can a blank firing gun be modified to shoot ammo?
Here in the UK guns are very strictly controlled etc.. You can however get blanks and air rifles. I have an old .22 cal air rifle and some small .22 blanks. I decided to try and fire a blank with the air rifle, as it has no firing pin etc i didnt think i had a chance but the blast of air actually sets off the blank, very loud lots of smoke etc. so.. I got some .177 ammo and put it in front of the blank and fired it. I was fukin amazed!! The power from those (very small) blanks is quite incredible, the round was easily passing through 3 inch pine wood at 20yds and making sizeable dents in an old aluminium pressure cooker, the lead round is totally blitzed when it hits hard objects. Although not all that reliable I could modify the gun if I wanted to and use full sized blanks to increase the power, you could also add some smokeless reloading powder and youve got pretty lethal gun imo.
