Is the USA a tyranny?
Someone in another thread said that the idea that the US is a dictatorship is "complete BS". While not a dictatorship in the strict sense (i.e. there is no dictator), is the USA a tyranny? Bob Black says in The Abolition of Work that "there is more freedom in any moderately de-Stalinized dictatorship than there is in the ordinary American workplace", and raises an interesting point. If the USA is a tyranny, who are the tyrants? It appears that it is not the government but our employers who hold the greatest control over our lives (at least for most people). And while the government purposely creates artificial scarcity of capital in order to keep unemployment high, they do so at the bidding of capitalists who thus profit from lower wages and higher profit margins at the same time. American politicians are elected, but they do not stand a chance of winning a nomination in the republocrat party without being in the pocket of business who demands it, and those few who manage to be elected anyway (usually be misrepresenting their views) do not come to Congressional power. But political power in the hands of people is a dream -- obviously not everyone can control the government, because the same government has to control millions of people. So how much control does the average American individual have over his own life? Lots, compared to such an individual in the late 1800s or early 1900s -- yet practically none, compared to pre-colonization native Americans.

Your thoughts.

Re: Is the USA a tyranny?
Actually, both abstinence from voting and tactical voting (which includes ignoring third parties) have sound mathematical basis. The fatalism you describe also has a sound mathematical basis and, it appears, a "common sense" basis. It has been said that the state lottery is a "math tax", (i.e. a tax on those citizens lacking mathematical knowledge) -- I find great irony in the fact that those (sound) mathematical arguments which are the basis for ridicule of the lottery apply equally to the ballot.

Indeed, I would consider voter turn-out of late to be far too high. Suffrage is not a panacea, and historically it has been notably impotent to lift oppression. The birth control pill did (vastly) more for women's rights than the ballot. Was the condition of American blacks significantly changed by black suffrage?

The real problem, though, is not impotence, but that the ballot has kept people in a subserviant stupor, entrenched in the delusion that the ballot somehow grants them "equality" and power over their circumstances, despite the fact that their's is one vote among millions. It gives people a false sense of power, and political satisfaction. If people refused the ballot and required of themselves direct action before they were satisfied that they were doing good for society, the condition of the country and world would be greatly improved.

Of course, even direct action cannot guarantee control over society, as that is impossible -- society can have only one condition, and thus it is impossible for each individual to choose what that condition will be. But what it can give -- at least to an unspeakably greater degree than the ballot -- is control over the life of the individual. That is not enough, because an individual cannot function outside of society; but it is the best one can have, and further a society in which people act directly and as individuals will be one in which individuals can best function.

Why does everyone need to go in the same direction? Do you think that otherwise society would sink, so to speak?

> Tactical voting? Is that what the ignorance of third party canidates is? What kind of boneheaded tactic is that?

Pick up an introductory text on game theory, man. "Tactical voting" is a technical term referring to those situations where voting contrary to your preferences actually provides you a greater probable outcome.

see the voting systems FAQ http://www.vision25.demon.co.uk/pol/votefaq.txt for more info on tactical voting and how to avoid it. fairvote.org is another site that addresses the basic game theoretic flaws of the type of voting system implemented in the US.

Here's the voting systems FAQ entry on tactical voting
quote:
Tactical voting occurs when votes vote in any pattern other than their true preference, because doing this allows them to better influence the result of the election in their favour.

Consider a FPTP election with these votes cast:
A 10000
B 9500
C 2000

Most people voting for C happen to greatly prefer B over A. There is no way C can win the election, but if some of C's supporters switch to voting for B instead of C, then B can win instead of A. Obviously, C's supporters would prefer C to win, but if this cannot happen, then B is better than A.

If enough of C's supporters know that this is like likely result, they might decide to vote tactically for B.
