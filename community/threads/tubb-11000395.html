Re: Photography
The "Grey Card" is an indispensable aid for the photographer who is trying to establish proper exposure under difficult lighting conditions. All meters, including those in cameras, are collaborated to read the average scene in shades of grey; the 18% grey card corresponds to the light reflectance of an average scene. For example, if a photographer is trying to photograph a scene with a very dark or very light colored background which would throw his exposure meter reading off, he can instead take his reading off a grey card which would establish proper exposure.

at this point digitals simplyt cannot match film for versatility speed and quality. if your doing 35mm you will be very pleased with a decent digital slr but pros mostly use film for several big reasons.

in small prints digitals can compete but when it comes to magazine covers and other large page layouts there is a noticable difference between the two.

film is far more advanced in terms of speed and efficent use of lighting. if you do a lot of sporting photography then a film slr is essential. some of the new pro digital slrs will be almost as good with light but still lack frame rate of film cameras due to space limitations in the body and the sheer price of memory (ram not cf, sm, etc.). digitals use ram as a buffer to store photos untill they are written to the memory card so when you use high resolutions your pics get above 1 meg a piece which takes time to write to the memory card and must be stored in a small amount of ram.

if you want the low dowm on all the sweet gear then go to www.dpreview.com . in my opinion they are the best digital site. look at the galeries for the pro level slr's like the nikon d1x. read the reviews for any model you are considering.
