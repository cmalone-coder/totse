This is jumping back a bit:

>>nothing forms out of nothing<<

At the moment those cosmologists pose that the universe did just that. OK, you got the big bang and everything is explodided out from a begining point- hence the current shape of the univers and it's continued epansion. Run this proscess in reverse and eventually we should reach the point where the bang ignited so to speak... I'm no mathematician but this is how they say everything did come from nothing: Firstly the theory os relativity stipulates that the affairs (read nature/laws) of matter cannot be seperatated from those of space and time. Imagine running the cosmic expansion movie in reverse, the galaxies get closer and closer together until they merge. Then all this cosmic material gets squeezed together until a state of vast density is reached. How much density can be reached is a matter of some conjecture, it's like constantly dividing by two- in essence there is no limit.
Suppose there was some limit to this compression, this would imply the existence of some outward force to overcome the enormous gravity produced by such density or the compression would continue. Also this outward force would have to be incredibly vast because the inward force of gravity increases in conjunction with the compression. OK we don't know what strange factors nature could throw up under such extreme conditions... (urgh, I've been rooting in some books for evidence but can't find what I'm looking for- trust me the outside force factor is highly improbable.)... Anyway, the sum of this is that the universe is infinatly compressed at the first moment- this infinate state of compression is known as a singularity.
This singularity ties in with my earlier statement about matter been linked to time and space. Over the billions of years of the universe' outward expansion, time and space also expand along with the matter- conversly space and time where origionaly shrunken along with the matter as a consequence of Einstiens laws. If space is infinately shrunk it literally does not exist and the all important linkage of space/time/matter means niether can the other two elements exist- This is the 'space time singularity' where the laws of physics simply collapse. Without space and time the whole concepts of 'before' or 'nothing' become devoid of meaning. The big bang did not occur in a place or at a time because there were no such things. Everything, space, time, matter and all the rules, they came with the big bang- it's not nothing comes from nothing or even something comes from nothing just... bang. You are right about the time of evolution. There is a problem with Darwinism that people often neglect to mention (except for the odd evangelical). For most species to evolve where they are today, they have to have been through many generations. Alterations from generation to generation are distinctly small and it appears we may be short more than a few generations to have reached our present state... give or take a few tens of millions of years. No one seems to have an answer to this, though there are many different theories, the most popular of which suggests that species sometimes jump- radically. Take your pick what could cause this; enviromental ctastrophy, aliens, divine plans or one of W S Burroughs benign viruses- the jury is still out. Taking a step back it seems probable in my opinion that Darwinism is only part of the picture. My personal tastes make aliens or divine plans seem improbable and would rather point to what we are only begining to understand. Darwinism is an entirely matter oriented theory, part of the same determinist school of science that states that the human qua human experience- the mind and perception in itself are simply useless by-products of chemical activity in the brain. This, however valid strikes me as fascile- it's like stamping your foot and shouting "how do I know you exist". Sure I can't prove that I'm not a brain in a tank but it's just not worth worrying about. The point of this is is that our understanding of reality is based on two irreconsilable theories: Relativity and quantum mechanics, which fundamentaly contradict each other- yet both work. Once you get into the quantum nature of matter and potential states- observation affects the outcome of the experiment- /If the Hubble space telescope were acurate enough to see far enough back in time and light to the ignition of the big bang, would our observation of the quantum forces at play define the nature of the universe?/- paradoxes within paradoxes... I can't give a satisfactory answer to the flaws in Darwinism but I suspect things will make more sense when we can finally reconcile relativity with quantum mechanics.

as to creation and gods... The difference between us an apes is as you say about 3%. The difference between us and the nemotode worm is around 12%. Apes are definately brighter than nemotode worms. The upshot of this is that, in terms of genetics intelectual capacity is not covered by a vast aray of genes (relativly speaking) and is more a matter of energy expense. There isn't necessarilly anything particularly important about intellect in terms of evolution, it just seems to be paying off.
