quote:Originally posted by MrMojoRisin:

His unhappiness makes me frustrated. His negativity doesn't bring me down. It's just like a problem to overcome.

And what happens if you solve the puzzle?

In theory, you would want to get back together with him, and all would be well. But look at some of the things you're saying. You're bored. What he says or does has little effect on you. Those aren't the words of someone who is beset by the issue of having someone they care for deeply flip out on them as often as he does.

Only you know exactly how you feel about this guy, but you're walking a potentially dangerous path- for him as well as you. If you stay and he doesn't change, you're wasting your time and no good comes of it. But if you figure out a way to make him happy, are you sure you'll want to stick around?

Think carefully about this. Being the cause of someone's happiness can leave you in a difficult position, because instead of having a mindset where you could leave without it affecting you much, now your conscience is going to take hold and tell you how rotten it would be to have built him back up, only to then pull his support- you- out from under him. That could leave him in even worse shape than he is now.

There's also the unfortunate factor of time. It's not like you can help him get his head together, stick around for a month, then leave him and expect him to be all right. He may be, but it's just as likely he won't. So how long will you have to stick around before he can carry on well enough so that you won't feel bad about leaving him? Can't tell.

Of course, this is solely based on the assumption that your remaining interest in him revolves as much or more around helping him than simply being with him. If you really care that much, and he does too, you can disregard this for the most part. It just doesn't sound that way to me.
