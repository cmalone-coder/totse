Re: tolerance, addition, and various drugs.
There are soooo many experiments that have done what you propose to do.

You are correct about that NO2 though, nobody really builds a tolerance to that. But then again, most people die pretty quickly after being exposed to NO2.

There are a few main mechanisms for drug addictions.
Heroin causes a large amount of endorphins to be released in a very short period of time, esp. if you shoot it. As with most of the bodies chemicals, endorphins are controlled by negative feedback, meaning that if the blood is saturated with endorphins, they will down regulate their own production. Because they are downregulated, they are not produced as frequently without the presence of heroin, causing physical pain/shittyness.

Cocaine addiction works by operant conditioning using a token economy. Yes, the same conditioning that Skinner used to train dogs. Basically, cocaine causes reward channels to become flooded with D1 and D2 agonists. Coincidentally(sort of), this mechanism is also how humans develop behaviorily. An example of this would be doing chores to get allowance. You want the reward, so you do the chores, and when you get the money, your brain gives you a dopamine cookie for increasing your survival rate. Cocaine gives you a dopamine wedding cake. And so, your body becomes conditioned to the idea that cocaine = "great job".

Weed doesn't develop withdrawals though. The only "withdrawal" symptoms are birthed from the day to day habit of smoking weed. Humans don't like to change their psychological schedules when they work well for them. Weed is a good, fun time waster, and when a person who has stopped smoking is bored, the first thing on their mind will usually be, "let's smoke weed b/c weed always takes away boredom". Cognitive dissonance can probably also be factored in here.
