Well many years ago I was at a friend's house. They had a kidney shape pool with a hot tub at one end, raised up a step.

It was night, the house lights and the pool light was on - I couldn�t see too well ahead of me. I was on the level with the hot tub, heading back towards the house.

I didn't see the step, leading from the hot tub level to the rest of the pool area level. So I tripped just like you would any other time you step off a step you don't know is there.

Not being too familiar with the surroundings [I hadn't been at this friend's house too often], and not wanting to look any more foolish by tripping again, I felt, with my foot, ahead to see if there was another step. There was, so I confidently walked off of it...

And right into the pool; the 'step' I felt was the pool's edge.
I didn't know what had happened until my head was under water. let me tell you, it is one hell of an odd feeling when you expect solidness under foot and not getting it.

My friend's mom watched the whole thing; and couldn't believe what she saw.

She said it looked like I thought I could walk on water, the way I just strode off the edge.

No so.

When you're standing on the ground you feel it under your feet because while your feet are pushing down on the ground, the ground is, in effect, pushing up against your feet [one of Newton's laws of gravity, the 'every action...opposite reaction' one].

On a balcony your feet push down on it, which pushes down on whatever...all the way to whatever is touching the ground.

Without the link to the ground, there is nothin pushing back up. So you would feel like you're falling.

It can also be said that the pieces would be falling at the same rate as you so you wouldn't feel them.

As for true feeling of flight, well I would think [hope] that the feeling of flight wasn't like being in free-fall .
