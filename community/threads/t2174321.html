So... by virtue of energy(heat) and energy(light), we know what kind of teas we are and are not going to use. Boiling tea violates energy(heat), and sun tea - charming as it is, and usually good on heat-sensitive compounds - violates energy(light)...

Yes, folks, we're going to have to make a cold-brewed tea... {cue ominous music}

Luckily, most people have this thing called a "refrigerator." It's a device which tends to keep things at ~5c, and the little light doesn't even come on when the door is closed. Cool. 'n it just so happens that this magic fantasy machine is perfect for making cold-brewed tea...

So... looking at chemical(iron), I'll see your lab in greenland with evil minions, and match you with "I have... a glass pot with a lid!!"

Of course, due to chemical(halogen), we're going to have to do one of two things. The superior of these is to find gallons of some really, really bitchin' distilled water at the supermarket. The other option is to dechlorinate tap water.

It just so happens that when you bring water to a boiling point, the solubility of most gases in it tends to drop to about "jack shit." Yes, "about jack shit" is a highly precise technical term, don't worry if it's a little hard to follow. ;)

So, if we run our tap water through some filter to take out any fucking calcium salts in it, and then cheerfully proceed to bring it to a boil, we'll have taken care of chemical(halogen):subcategory(chlorine).

Of course, the water's still fluoridated, probably with sodium fluoride. One way to take care of that is to chuck in a capful of white vinegar. A really, really cool thing about dissolution in water is that it dissassociates and reassociates everything in really impossible ways... and a 1:1:1 ratio of sodium, fluorine, and acetic acid will in fact form the complex salt sodium fluoride acetate, despite this being fucking impossible.

If it should decide to degrade into something more firmly associated, well... some of it will be sodium fluoride, and some of it will be sodium acetate. 'n since decomposition into sodium fluoride leaves free acetic aid and decomposition into sodium acetate leaves free hydrofluoric acid, well, and since acetic acid boils at "slightly above boiling" and hydrofluoric acid boils at "fucktardedly below zero," that means that your hydrofluoric acid will keep boiling off into the air and enriching the acetic acid fraction.

Yay! No moar chemical(halogen).

Of course, since we just boiled off all our O2 and CO2 from the water, we might wanna keep it that way, on account of chemical(oxygen). One really easy way to do this is to just set the still-steaming pot on a board, toss a blanket over it as a thermal insulator, stick it in a plastic trash bag, squeeze all the air out, and partially inflate it with a helium tank from your local party supply store. 'n if you go to a health food store and pick up "ascorbic acid crystals," aka calcium triascorbate, you can even seed a few grams of a charming antioxidant into your water...

So, we've scratched chemical(halogen), chemical(oxygen), and chemical(iron), our chemical(extreme pH) should be pretty bloody neutral right about now, and all we have to do is set this baby in the fridge for a while and we'll have eliminated energy(light) and energy(heat). Perfect!
