Classes really aren't the same Uni to Uni, but they're generally kept similar classes. I'd suggest an illustration class if it's offered, Typography, Drawing II, Design I & II, Layout & Design, History. Printmaking is insanely fun (at least it was for me) but getting used to how pen & ink works will help immensely with printmaking.

Design classes ( at least the way mine were taught) really help to understand the major motivation, parts, and composition of art. We spent a whole semester breaking down and understanding older works of art and why they work like they do. It was much fun and it gave me a better appreciation for modern art.

Design II for my Uni was basically understanding sculpture and 3d mediums, but was still scads of fun (one project was to recycle old stuff and turn it into a functional kinetic sculpture. I made a robotic hobo battlebot out of my RC parts, paperclips, screens, soda bottles, old felt and cloth)

Just read the class description and choose what appeals to you most since it doesn't seem like you're majoring in the art field

Basic Design (to learn the "rules") and more drawing.
Watercolor is generally cheaper, less mess and dries quicker then oil.
Acrylics can be used like WC or Oil depending on how much water
is used. Dries in minutes instead of weeks or months like Oil.
They are versatile and have intensive colors. Cheaper too.

If you like drawing and oil colors, try Pastels- an excellent medium!

Live model Nude drawing is about the best training you can get-
sure hard for a beginner, but you can learn much from watching
more advanced artists and what they do- highly recommended.

Photography is also an excellent medium for learning "how to see"
and improving your visual vocabulary, so to speak...

My 2 cents.

Art is like Music like Music is Art.

There are and aren't rules.
Go at your own speed, learn
from others while developing
your own style. Whoever and
whatever those might be.
