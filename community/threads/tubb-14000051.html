ideas on copyright
Sorry for the essay guys i just have a lot to say.

<essay>

What bugs me about copyright laws and digital media, is such, ANY UNAUTHORIZED copies of data are considered illegal. especially when it comes to the internet, the largest distrubited network on the planet. Im going to explain the process of how a file gets transfered. Lets say you viewing a
page in your favorite browser. What you computer does is send out a request, this request goes to your main uplink it is copied and then sent out to another computer. This process is repeated until it gets to its destination. Once the request for the data is received by the computer, the computer makes
a copy (really it induces the electrial feild of the magnetic domains, which then is turned into a stream of electrons which of course is a copy of the data or file required) of what is on the hard disk, and sends that copy to its uplink. This process is thus repeated until it reaches its destination.
[ Remember this is a gross over-simplification ]

Now what if this data was copyrighted? Would all of the people involved in the transaction of the data be liable for copying and distrubing this data against the copyright? Okay lets assume they aren't. It's just the final user in the end who is responable, becuase they made the initial
request. Now since the end user is probally running a modern OS and a modern browser chances are, that first, the browser has a copy of the page on your disk, and second your OS might have the data in a swap file on your disk as well. Not to mention that also a copy must exist in your RAM and most likely
also in the RAM of your video card. Are you responsible for these as well?

"You know you are responsable for your actions. You turned the computer on, didn't you? And don't give me that you didn't know crap, ignorance is no excuse when it comes to the law! So if you did turn it on, regardless of if you knew it or not, your liable".

- Our law attitude.

Now I have heard the argument that RAM is acceptable becuase it isn't "permanant" but data is data and copying is copying. We talking about copyright, not the right to temporaraly copy data if the ontents are "lost" in a given temporal unit (you know secs, minutes, hours, etc.) Lets also assume for some weird reason that RAM is okay becuase of its volatile nature. Like when the power goes out and you lose 3 hours of your work. Now you can think the RAM ("THANKS RAM".) Lets just say it's when the user intentionally copies the data for the computers RAM, into a permanant storage device, ie. hard disk, holographic cube, record, CD, tape backup, and the billons of other mass storage methods. Now intentionally is a cute word. You can have data copied to a disk with out intentionally doing it. One way is via caching or swapping, and yes you did look at the page, so you did tell the computer what you wanted it to do. You wanted to look at the page, COPYING the page is
required for you to even view it. So in that since you could be considered intentionally copying it or not, depending on how the person looks at it. From one side, yes, you did initiate the transfer. But form the other end you didn't want the data for your own permanent usage. Of course the evidence
points to the fact that yes, you do have the data so you do plan to infridge on someones copyright. How do you prove someone's intentions anyways? I wouldn't trust someone who wants to put me in jail for something my computer does as part of its programming, just because i want to look at a webpage.
Which is most likely what would happen given the physical evidence.

This of course is obsured, and would require a radical change of internet structure, and that of the common day browsers and OS's. This would be a major techonlogical and economic effort. Even more than fixing the Y2K design flaw that existed in most major computer systems(mostly becuase of our cool C library). I feel it is much easier to change the language of the
copyright laws. The intentions of the original laws were to give credit to the
creator, and all the creator to have exclusive rights to all aspects of the
material. This includes selling the work, giving it out for free, and using it
as a trade for goods and services. While this is a great idea in all, we still need to remember that this law was thought out when there was no internet. The original "base" law is, of course, what i am refering to. This law was put into affect when objects were tangable. A painting, a book, an essay on how fungy grows are all tangable. What bugs me is the fact that people think that
data is a tangable object, sure its electrons which are tangable, but how do you own the electons in a line? All electrons are the same. You dont have the picture electron, the text electron, the bank transaction electron, these don't exist. Then how are they really tangable? Atoms don't even consist of just electrons you need the whole sub-atomic soup.

[Time for the author's biased opinion]

Now I realize this is a gray so bear with me. Since data can be copied so readily and easily, copying should be allowed. Now i know what you are thinking so just hang on and let me explain myself. Lets say that any one who knows how to use a computer can copy some data (bad assumption i know but bear
with me.) By this i mean an exact copy, now what an exact copy does for us is the ability to embed a signature into the data to allow the data to be authenticated from you being the source. When it is distrubited an exact copy will most likely circulate. Of course any person proficent enough could remove/change the signatures, the vast majority of copies will show the true owner. Of course monatary conditions must be obtained: there are two major reasons why most people copyright work: credit, and money. I can understand the former, and kinda the later. My understanding of the later is only becuase our society is setup in that way. But we can't ignore our economic engine can we? Charge for access, not content! The setup is like this: So one who wants to setup a place where they charge a monthly fee for unlimited access to a particular area of content or whatever. They get content the old fashion way by hiring or paying people to create content for them. Notice how new
and meaningful content will be important becuase, many of these services could
exist. The service the provides the best ACCESS to outstanding/load of content
will be the best. And since ideas spread fast this will spur creativity not destroy it. Why bother, A) Hunting for data that might be sheilded B) breaking through barriers to find content c) finding unuseful content, when you can just pay a (small) fee to get access? This is of course is the money route,
there should be nothing stopping people who want to give away content they have or find out for free. Just as long as the orginal author has credit (and has been paid, now look at that) and companies have billions and billions of people accessing their nodes(sites). I dont think that anyone will have a problem. See if you provide a quality service, at a cheap price, very little
piracy will go on, becuase they are, like most people, lazy. Only if there is
a "market" for it, will it be worth their time.

This author does believe in freedom of information, but draws the line at personal information. Like credit records, wedding documents, etc. These don't benefit humanity as a whole. Maybe the black-mailer, but not humanity.

The purpose of this paper is just my attempt to put my thoughts to words, and to try to see if people will bite on the line that i have dropped.

</essay>

If your going to flame me on either my grammar or spelling send me an email please, I would apprecate it.

So what do we all think about what i have been saying? I mean am i full of shit or what? :] Awating your reponses...
