I don't usually bother reading names, so I don't know who asked the question about accents, but here's the answer:

Each language has different vowels and consonants, pronunciations, and rhythms. When an area is dominated by one language, and a group moves in speaking another language, the new group will pick up the dominant language's vocabulary, while still maintaining their own pronunciations and rhythms, causing an accent.

For the language we call Modern English, the purest speaking of it (meaning the closest to no accent) is the northern Midwest in the US. Midwesterners still have an accent, but it's closer to the dictionary pronunciations and rhythms than any other English speaking region's accent in the world.
