Ok, I am now going to try and dissect how you are feeling using the knowledge I have so far (of behavioral science, that is). I hope that this helps.

1). You ask if your view about a "great friendship" is egotistical, or optimistic. The answer is: it is neither. Well, in a way it is optimism, but not in the regular sense of the term. It is human instinct to adapt to slow environmental changes, but to resist quick ones. For example, animals can adapt to temperature changes (via evolution) or large spans of time. They cannot, however, adapt to a sudden, drastic change in climate. Therefore, thousands of years of evolution have programmed your brain to resist changes that threaten (what you perceive as) your survival. This way, your brain knows to get out of a situation that could kill you. Although this may help you if you're suddenly in a "Day After Tomorrow" situation, all it does is cause problems in social crises.

For the past two years your world has (probably) centered on your lover. She has become a part of your daily environment. So, since she is suddenly not there, your brain is trying to cope by imagining a world where she still is there. From what I know, this is just a step in a LONG process of healing. The same thing happens when there is a death in the family. Those closest to the departed "cannot believe" that he/she is no longer there. They keep thinking that they will just walk in the door, as if nothing had ever happened.

One experiment conducted on this matter concerned what it's like to lose a spouse (through death, or other events). In the end, the following equation was made: For every year a relationship lasts, it takes 2-5 months to get over the absence of the other member of the party. So, basically, this feeling should diminish with time.
