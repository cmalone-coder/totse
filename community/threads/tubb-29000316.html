Re: The ultimate assault rifle?
I've used both rounds extensively, and I'll admit that the 5.56 has some very nice things going for it. First off, it makes some nasty damage where it hits, regardless of its size (it takes a persons head off almost as bad as a 7.62). An M-16 is also controllable in full auto, and it has a 30 rd magazine that isn't too long to prevent effective firing from the prone position. You can forget about controlling the G3 on full auto (I've even been issued a single auto only conversion once), and even though I've never used the M14, I'd guess the same applies to it.

The controllable 7.62 alternative, namely the 7.62x39, fails in my opinion due to its extremely undesirable ballistic properties. There are some high quality AR-15 type weapons in that calibre (The PCR-6 springs to mind), but even though they are capable of 1.5 MOA gropus at 100 m with good ammo, their useful range stops at about 300 m. Add to that the fact that the rounds weigh almost as much as the 7.62x51, and it drops pretty far down my wish list.

The reason I still stick with the 7.62x51 is its superior range, and you won't always find yourself in the jungle, where contacts chiefly consist of sending a lot of shit flying in the general direction of the enemy and getting the fuck out. Its good penetration properties also come in handy at times, even in CQB. If you don't always have to aim at the enemy, but just the things he is hiding behind, it makes things a lot easier.

Re: The ultimate assault rifle?
Engage at 800meters with an assault rifle accurately? Not hardly. 800m is not a easy shot to do for trained snipers with a weapon made to shoot those distances. You may be able to produce shots hitting the ball park with some luck, providing some kinda cover fire, but scoring kills is highly unlikely. If your darn good you may be able to keep an enemy from moving easily. If you need cover fire when you are 800 meters from your enemy, consider yourself fucked and get the hell out of your position in a hurry.

If you are reffering to sniping you need a different plat form.

I think the 5.56 round is a good round, it does what it's supposed to and does it just fine. Yes it makes a smaller hole and yet a bigger hole is better than a small one, it still does the job. The M-16 is a great assault rifel, there were reasons that when it was first introduced that it suffered from many malfunctions. Those problems have been cleared away for several years now and the M-16 family of rifles are a fine design. I've shot Colts and Bushmasters both only to find I think both are great rifles.

The AK families of rifles would be my choice. Simply because of their renound reliability and avaliabitily of parts and ammo. I agree that the 7.62x39mm is not the best of ammo to have, but it to does the jobs superbly from 400m or less, which is typical assault distance. What it won't do at 400m is penetrate a kevlar helmet, where as steel core .223 will.

No bullets will take a head off, I've shot enough critters with enough guns to know better. Unless the human skull is just so much more fragile than any other animal's, I could be wrong. Unless of course we're talking very heavy calibers, perhaps a .50BMG and up. Make a neat hole sure, take a head off, no. I witnessed a cow being shot in the head 3 days ago. Brad, a guy I work with, shot the cow with a 2.75" 12ga Winchester Foster type slug (aka pumpkins, just normal slugs) from, I'd say 2 feet from gun muzzle to head. The cows head stopped the slug, to my surprise. I figured there'd be blood, bones, and brain all over the barn wall with a hole nicely in the center. Nope, no blood, no bone, no brains, no hole. I'm sure a high powered rifle caliber would've penetrated completely, yet I know it wouldn't have removed the head. Ball ammo which is full metal jacket doesn't expand for crap anyways, it's just make a hole, if it doesn't tumble, pretty close the same size of the bullet diameter.

If you want a firearm that can be used well at close range and still reach out there a ways I'd say first choose a heavy caliber. 7.62Nato or 7.62Russian to be the minimum calibers. Your system should also have a high mounted optical site, giving you the ability to use iron sites as well as you scope. For reliability use a fixed power scope, perhaps 6x, giving you a good combination of zoom as well as field of view. Also use a slim bodied scope so it doesn't snag much, 32mm is a good tube size for that, but they don't perform well in low light conditions. And keep the scope short, no longer than prolly 6-8" for the same reason as diamter constrictions. The rifle itself would be semi-auto (full auto is a waste anyways) and reliable. Carry parts with you that are prone to failure.

Some reasons the US adopted the .223 over the .30-06 or .308 make sense. The ability to make 1000yd shots for one, was not needed. World War 2 changed tactics, people were up close and moving fast. A heavier rifle that used heavier ammo slowed people down. Why deal with the extra weight and size if you didn't need it? The light weight of the .223 ammo made it possible for soldiers to carry more ammo without increasing fatigue. The M1-Carbine was designed for this purpose. It uses an intermediate powered round (though less powerful than the .223), is light in weight and held more ammo than it's M1 brother. In war there are two tactics to look at. If you kill a person you only stop one gun. If you just mortally wound a person you stop 3. The first is the wounded person, the other 2 are the people carrying him away. Wounding people also uses up precioius, few, and expensive med supplies. These people not being able to fight still use up food and water as well, wasting more necessities and making the war more expensive for the enemy. These were also thoughts going through the people's minds who chose the .223 caliber.

The downfalls of the .223 and the M-16 were mostly human. Troops were told the rifles were self cleaning. They weren't even given the supplies to clean their rifles. In repeated heavy use in an especially not so nice environment most any weapon will fail. The reason behind this were out great leaders who did not like Eugene Stoner (the designer) and his plastic gun. They wanted it to perform horribly so they did things to make it look bad, all in the name of their uneducated opinion about ballistics and what a gun should be.

The major design failure was that the M-16 barrel was at first not chrome lined. Another design flaw was in the action. The appearance of the M-16A2 was everything the M-16 was, without the flaws. Also every peron was eventually issued a cleaning kit and was taught how to clean their rifle. Since then, Stoner's rifle has had a pretty much perfect service record. Most of his rifles are little known about, but have influenced much of the world's gun designs. You can see his influence in Daweoo, Israel Arms, Uzi-Galil, and yes even in H&K. Take a peek at the Stoner 63A, especially it's internals. The man was ahead of his times and pushed the gun industry far ahead.

I don't care for hi mounted scopes on rifles either, but in my experience it would be the best combination of quick aiming up close and making somewhat decent shots far off. A quick detach scope is just one more loose piece of equipment to screw with when it's off and I have had problems with losing zero as well. It's also not as quick as just raising your head.

My 98 Mauser in .308 carried sites that were adjustable up to 2000m. At 100m I could pull off a 3" group on a good day with target ammo. Using surplus ammo I gained an inch. So at 2000m I could expect a minimum group size of 80". Enter the conditions of battle and weather (good day for me = no wind) that group could easily double. That would put me at about 64" shooting 800m. Not impressive, but I guess if the shooter were lucky he could hit a car. No other weapon I have owned had such sites, but many WW2 era firearms I have shot did. If you have the ammo supply and proper support it doesn't hurt to take shots that far. However chances are that you don't have the ammo to waste from lead lobbing.

Back to hi mounted scopes, the one thing I do believe they work very well for is shotgunning. Shotguns aren't near as precise of rifles and losing that margin, doesn't make much difference. If you only were to have a shotgun and were in a woodland area with fields in the perimeter, it wouldn't be a bad set up. Again, only if you possess no other weapon than a shotgun.
