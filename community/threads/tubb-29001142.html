����True enough, but what do you want for a buck? The ones labeled "stainless" and "440" are almost always 440A or 440B. These aren't the best steels for blades but you can improve them somewhat. Some of the Furies are actually true 400C.
����The trouble with them is they aren't heat treated at all. You can always seat-of-the-pants harden the lower carbon content ones (below .45 - .50 percent) simply by heating the blade until it becomes nonmagnetic and quenching it in brine. There's really not a whole lot of drawing to do on low carbon steels such as these. About the hardest you can get the edge is about as hard as you need...
����Even still, this is just tinerking for those of us with the knives and the time. I only bother to treat a very small portion of my collection of crappy knives. I figure at that rate I may as well simply buy a better knife or make my own and waste my time heat treating that.
