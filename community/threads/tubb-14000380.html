As a military member you should be proud of that fact. At least the German high command was thinking beyond the Schlieffen Plan.

But to take your comment back to how it is relevant...there should be plans for every contingency. In military terms we talk in terms of colors. Friendlies are blue, enemies are red (makes for easy recognition plus it was convenient for the cold war).

What is important is that today's blue can be tomorrows red. Let's look at some recent examples...Iran...Kuwait (as occupied territory)...Grenada (also as occupied). For the sake of my point, I will use Grenada as my example. This small island was a threat to no one and as far as including it in military planning, it just didn't happen. It was a threat to no one...up until the Cubans started building a very large runway. New situation, the length and thickness and all the associated tarmac and logistic facilities went way beyond what was required for a mere commercial airport on a tiny Caribbean island (although some die-hards who don't understand logistics still argue to the contrary).

Now all of a sudden a plan had to be created. The planning process involves more than just the deployment of the forces. The logistic support (food, supplies, medical, etc) not only has to be provided on location, but where it comes from, who takes it, where it is stored, etc. has to be arranged. This is what the plans are for.

Just because you have made a plan doesn't mean the plan will be used. As a matter of fact, the plan (if used) will be altered considerably because of the situational requirements at the time.

Just because a plan exists, it means little...other than your military planning staff is doing what it's supposed to do...prepare for war.
