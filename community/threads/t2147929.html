Well, lets see...The only experience I have with nootropics is with ginkgo. I've gotten some immediate results from ginkgo, but they're not always consistent. I never hurts through...

Other than that... Lets talk cholinergics. Anyone who's ever taken Benedryl "recreationally" knows that when your acetylcholine levels are down you can't do shit. So, it follows that boosting acetylcholine levels would improve/increase brain function. As for how to do that, the most direct way is with an Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (AChEI, for short), and the most available AChEI is a chemical found naturally in tomato leaves called huperzine A. You can also buy it in pill form at most nutritional outlets, though you'll get the best deals on ebay.

On another note, you have the racetams (http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racetam the most known being piracetam). From what little I have read, they appear promising. I remember reading somewhere that a real study was done (double-blind, placebo, and all that good stuff) and it showed an immediate increase in cognitive ability in various areas that was higher than in placebo groups. Also, they have the benefit of being completely nontoxic (a problem easily encountered when trying to dose yourself on tomato leaves).

And finally, we have ADD meds. The reason everybody suggests these is because they proliferate adrenalin and dopamine, which do a damn good job of increasing brain function (SWIM takes them before all major research projects). The problem is, you're already on 'em, and they're not doing all they should be. Now, I don't know your dosage or current medications, but I imagine after years of any ADD meds, you've developed a tolerance. Now, I've heard places that stimulant tolerance can be reduced/eliminated by using NMDA antagonists in relatively small dosages in combination with the stims. A good example of this would be a robogel or two (DXM as a nootropic, who woulda thunk it?) with the first dose, as well as a booster at noon, or whatever. Even simpler than this would be just taking a little extra magnesium in your diet as it is the body's natural NMDA antagonist.

If you do try any of the above, be sure to post results. I hope it is helpful.
