Background: I've dropped out of college and can't bear the thought of working a dead end job for the rest of my life. I've chosen to try and get involved in politics to make a change for the better. I have the rest of my life to try and make this change, so I might aswell start now.

As a first step, I've decided to write out a manifesto, which will form the basis of my eventual political party. Scrutiny is essential in forming any perfect policy, therefore if any of you have the time, I'd very much appreciate it if you could read over what I've wrote and provide some constructive criticism.

If you can't be bothered to read it, I can respect that as I'm lazy myself, but please don't troll and spam here. Thanks.


Drugs
The government have no right to dictate what people do in private, as long as these actions do not infringe on the rights of others. There's a common misconception that drugs are damaging our society, which is false. It's the irresponsible problem users of drugs who are a drain on themselves, their families and our society.

Through current government policy, over 3,000,000 UK citizens are made criminals, the vast majority of which do not cause any problems. Our policy will focus on not discriminating against people due to which drugs they choose to take, but will focus on providing treatment to those who need it: those who are a drain on our emotional and financial resources.

As it is illogical to make a policy considering all drugs in the same light, we will seek to implement legislation on different drugs in accordance to their associated dangers.



Marijuana
Marijuana is the most popular drug in the UK, and we would seek to legalise it. Whilst we do not advocate its usage, we respect that individuals have the right to do what they like with their own bodies, as long as it does not infringe on the rights of others. Prohibition has FAILED to curb usage of marijuana; it is still easily obtainable, and its legal status only channels the profits towards organised crime syndicates and terrorists.

In line with our stance of not advocating its usage, we will impose a ban on all marijuana related advertising. Due to the nature of how easy it is to cultivate, taxes will not be imposed on its sale to avoid the bureaucratic and administrative costs. Licenses will not be granted to big businesses who wish to sell the drug: production will only be limited to local cultivation.

Magic Mushrooms
Identical to the party's policy on marijuana.


Ecstasy
Ecstasy is currently ranked 18th in a list of the 20 most commonly used drugs, and only 200 people in the UK have died from it's usage since the 1990s: the majority of whom died from over exhaustion, NOT from any toxic effects of the drug. In order to limit ecstasy related deaths, we will seek to legalise the drug in order to remove the social taboo which is currently associated with it. As a result of this, people will be more inclined to exert pressure on club owners to implement adequate safety measures. Legalising ecstasy will also allow for regulation, so consumers will know what, and how much they're actually taking.

Taxes will be imposed on the sale of ecstasy, which will help fund the negative externalities arising from drug use.

In line with our party's policy on not advocating drug use, advertising of ecstasy will not be prohibited, and licenses will only be granted to manufacturers who adhere to strict regulations.


LSD
Similar to the policy on ecstasy, except there'll be even stricter licenses imposed on those who wish to manufacture it. This is designed to limit its usage.

Amphetamines
Not too knowledgable regarding how they should be legislated... Any ideas?


Cocaine
Currently, huge amounts of profits are being channelled towards those involved in the distribution of cocaine. These people do not pay tax, and thus, do not contribute to the economy.

We would seek to legalise cocaine and ensure that users buy it through legitimate retailers. Not only will this generate tax to help deal with problem users, but it will also put many of our organised crime syndicates out of business.

We realise that cocaine has a high problem use potential, but prohibition has failed in curbing its use. The artificially high prices have only resulted in more crime and more profits going towards illegal drug cartels who contribute in corruption and fund terrorism.

Distribution of cocaine will be subject to strict licensing.


Crystal Meth/Heroin/Crack Cocaine
Crack cocaine and heroin are the 2 drugs which are currently causing the most damage to our society through problem use. The negative costs through drug related crime and medical treatment amount to roughly £16.4 billion a year.

As a government, our policy will not focus on heavy handed tactics and zero-tolerance, which has proved to be ineffective, but will focus on limiting the costs which problem drug users impose on our society. We will achieve this through increasing funding for treatment and education.

These treatments will not come at the expense of the general taxpayer, but instead will be funded through taxation on other currently illegal drugs. Through ending the prohibition on drugs; lowering prices, we expect there'll be less burden on the taxpayer as there's a decrease on the burden of our criminal and health services.

Substitute drugs, produced in the UK under strict regulation by the government, will be supplied to problem users in order to assist in treatment. This will also decrease the amount of people who need to buy drugs via the black market: limiting profits of criminals and ensuring users can receive safe produce.

Those who chose to take heroin recreationally and who can manage their use, will be required to obtain a prescription. This will reduce the demand for heroin on the black market.


Through the above policies we expect to see:
A decline in drug related deaths
A decline in the spread of HIV
A decline in the usage of hard drugs
A huge reduction in theft and robberies
A reduction on the burden of our police force
More space in prisons
A reduced burden on the NHS
Less corruption
A decline in children getting involved in the drug industry
An improvement in the state of some of the UK's poor inner city regions: plagued by gangs and drug related crime.
