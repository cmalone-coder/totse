Good heavens no!

Some of what poetry is is form [or lack there of].

It's not always about what the words, together mean, so much as how they sound

The flow of syllables to make a whole textured piece; like some of the works of John Cage.

I know I'm not doing the best at expressing what I want to say - it's 6:30am, and I'm on my third night up, so give me a bit of a break, eh what? [No, not chemically awake for three days. It's natural: pain & bi-polar disease].

It's quite a bit like a lot of what is done in music [poetry is, after all, a song without the music]. So let me explain it for music, then see if I can do any better.

The lyrics to a song do not alway mean something. By this I mean that they don't make any sense were you to say them as a sentence. For example: "I am the Walrus" or "Come Together" by the Beatles; the songs not only don't have a meaning, the lines don't make sense as a understandable sentence. [You may not ever realize it, until comes a day where you need to write down the lyrics or happen to read them somewhere - then it's obvious. Why not when sung?
It's because the part of the brain that handles music is different from the part that handles speech; which is why people who stutter can sing ok].
Why would Lennon & Mccartney - or anyone do that? What's the point?
The point is that they are using the words [and vocals themselves] as just another musical instrument.

Putting words and/or syllables together in a way as to have a certain sound to add to the overall song.

Like listening to a song in a foreign language, kinda.

**

It's like that, too, when dealing with just words alone, though it is much harder to do [and appreciate].

So, am I going to get torn apart by the newbies? Because that's how they react to what they don't/can't/refuse to understand?

Am I the only one on this forum who thinks along these lines?

Am I taking it past were it was meant to go?

Tough I like it; it gives me good ideas and practice.
