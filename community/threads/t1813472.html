In some Jewish Mythology he is not completely a bad guy but rather has an agreement to tempt and test people for God. In some Christian Mythology he is the architect of evil and is at war with God for the souls of the humans. He wants everyone to burn in his kingdom of hell. He is quite powerful in the presence of those who are without God.

In Islam he isn't an ArchAngel like in the prior two religions but rather a Djinn (a being of smokeless fire, energy) which is not at war with God but rather at war with life on Earth and Human kind. Satans name in the Qur'an is Iblis and Iblis might not be the same reference as Shaitan. Shaitan can also be thought of as a metaphor for pride and anger, which is what the word is thought to mean by some (Born out of Anger). If the word Shaitan is replaced with pride (which it is repeated over and over is the quality of Iblis) it might be better understood. There is not Dualism in Islam and Satan is not thought of as much of a power, nor the architect of evil, God being the manifestor of everything.

Satan type beings make their appearance throughout history and in various other religions ranging from Zoroastrianism to Manichaeism and the beliefs of ancient China.

In Zoroastrianism Satan appears as a massive serpent dragon whom is in constant battle with the "Wise Lord" Ahuramazda, God. The Satan Dualism only appears after the introduction of the second book to Zoroastrianism and the death of Zoroaster.
