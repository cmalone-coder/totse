Well obviously I think that I'm slowly starting to loose it, and some recent events sort of reinforced this notion.

Hmm, now how can I make this not look/sound emo?

Recently, I made the decision to "shut down" so to speak. I felt like I was losing control, so instead of doing something foolish or rash, I removed myself from the situation the only way I could. Now this opens up an entire new can of worms. I almost always percieve myself from the 3rd person viewpoint. A result of this is going very hard on myself. I operate balls to the wall with everything relating to myself. To give an example of this, I demand perfection from myself. If it is anything less I write off as a failure. I go much easier with other people. I generally help them learn and help in any way if I am capable. To give an example of me going balls to the wall, I am about halfway through my time frame to have my definition of a perfect body. I don't mean so damn skinny you can see my ribs, but I mean asthetics, a balance between endurance and strength, etc. This has led to me being obsessed with hygeine aswell. If I feel that I am behind with my goals, I just work harder. Inadvertingly this leads to me hurting myself. Stress fractures, lack of sleep, etc.. As for having a perfect body, I know I probably won't reach it, but it won't keep me from trying. I can keep naming examples, but I will wear everydown with pretty much the same thing.

I looked into cognitive therapy found here:

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogniti...vioral_therapy

I honestly think that all it is doing is trying to put a silver lining on something and essentially trying to get you to lower your standards.


On to the next thing, there are certain events in my childhood-end of highschool years that I would rather not talk about, but my friends definately said they noticed changes (mostly negative) after those events took place. I still deal with the aftershock of them, but sometimes it is better to just bury your past so no one knows about it.
