Napalm Bombs and more attrocities in Iraq:

first, What I remember about polystyrene: As I recall, 'Styrofoam' is the Dow Chemical Company trademark name for Polystyrene. Polystyrene is manufactured in different densities depending upon the manufacturing process, typically expanded polystyrene of low density, 1-lb./cubic foot, is white in color, sometimes called 'Beadboard' because it is made by expanding tiny polystyrene plastic beads with hot steam in a mold. Large ingots are made (4 ft. x 4 ft. x 16 ft tall) and these are cut into boards for insulation etc. by using a 'Hotwire' which is basically a piano wire with an electrical current going through it to heat up the wire and it cleanly cuts through polystyrene with very little pressure applied.

Expanded polystyrene is used for things like polystyrene coffee cups, packaging products, and low density insulation boards.

Extruded poystyrene is more dense, stronger, and has a smoothe surface and a higher insulation value. It is usually colored blue (Dow) or Yellow (Celotex) or Pink (I forget who makes that)resistance to heat transfer(insulation) and you will see these used as rigid insulation on the sides of buildings.

The dense polystyrene beads or crystals, before they are expanded, may be the form that is used to make the heavy military Napalm Bombs, but I am not sure.
