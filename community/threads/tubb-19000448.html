Re: Salt Peter??
Potassium nitrate (KNO3)is used as a fertiliser (and in 'hydroponics',meaning growing plants without soil)'cause it contains K and N wich are absolutely necessary for the plants.The name 'saltpeter' come from it's mineral origin.
'Chile saltpeter' is NaNO3,found in great deposits in Chile(surprise!).It is higroscopic.
"Norway saltpeter' is Ca(No3)2.The medieval European armies boiled this with wood ash(contains potassium carbonate)and so was made KNO3 for gunpowder.
KNO3 as a mineral is called "Indian saltpeter' and it has organic origin,same as "Chile saltpeter'.
Another way for obtaining KNO3 is using NH4NO3 and wood ash(containing K2CO3).The ammonium 'carbonate' will not be a problem.
