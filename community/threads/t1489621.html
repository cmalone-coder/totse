Do guys really want to know the exact reason they were "rejected?" My usual tactic of fluttering away into space is starting to not work anymore. Some dudes demand answers! But only the ones they want to hear.

I think most guys like to think of themselves as the "nice guy who was thrown aside for some asshole." I did that exactly once, at age 16, and have suffered bad karma as a result.

These are the only reasons I would take a pass on a guy:

<UL TYPE=SQUARE>

<LI>Not smart enough.

<LI>Demands too much of my time and doesn't give me space when I need it.

<LI>Talks excessively about bodily functions in an adolescent manner.

<LI>Constantly complains or talks too much shit about other people.

<LI>Is rude to waiters and other service people.

<LI>Has a bad attitude toward drugs and those who use them recreationally.

<LI>Has a bad attitude toward animals, children, the disabled and/or the elderly.

<LI>Has an unacknowledged road rage problem.

These are not easy issues to bring up. And the whole lie "It's not you, it's me" doesn't hold up, either.

Guys usually don't seem to have a problem telling me why things won't work. "You do too many drugs" and "I'm afraid I'll eventually end up in prison" are common and understandable. It sucks to be rejected, but to me, it's a relief to know that it's for reasons that I probably can't change too much.

Re: What do guys want to know? Part 2
quote:Originally posted by deadlycindy:

My usual tactic of fluttering away into space is starting to not work anymore.

This tactic is often not a good idea. It is not fair to the guy, and it does not provide a clean break. You need to at least tell someone that you are breaking up with them. In person is preferred, but if you are scared of confrontation, you can do the old answering machine, note, or email.

quote:Originally posted by deadlycindy:

Some dudes demand answers! But only the ones they want to hear.

All situations are somewhat different, but often going into explanations is also not in the spirit of a clean break. Guys say they want to know to improve for the future, curiosity, whatever. But really they are not accepting the breakup and they want to start a convo that could turn things around and they need a list of promises to give you. That is not always the case, but often you need to just be clear that it is over and move on. If you did a good job communicating your needs during the relationship, the dude should have some clue about what is going on. 9/10 times the fact that he does not have a clue is a big part of the reason, and talking more ain't going to help that.

If you are breaking up with someone who is insisting on a reason and is getting psycho, I would recommend making up a reason he can accept or at least something he can do nothing about, as opposed to telling him the truth.
