...the few hours of crossover between alertness and sleep is an interesting, and chaotic, time. Among the things that are common... perfectly non-schizophrenic people become much more prone to auditory hallucinations of a characteristic type, usually out-of-context snippets of ordinary conversation, maddening in their ability to make perfect sense yet with a complete absence of context and thus meaning. People with no tendancy towards seizure or convulsion can get myoclonic jerks; a leg or some such will just twitch all of a sudden, when normally this does not happen. Visual hallucinations are quite common in this strange, nodding-out space between sleep and wakefulness...

...and people with absolutely no trauma are, at this time, prone to being jerked awake by a bolt of sheer terror, unconnected with anything, when this is uncommon for both their waking and sleeping lives.

If she was having constant flashbacks while awake, I'd guess that she was a severely damaged piece of work who would need a lot of help before becoming merely highly neurotic. If she was constantly having nightmares, I'd say it was weighing pretty heavily on her mind...

...but this space between has strange and volatile properties. In the luxuriance of nodding out, visual hallucinations, strange daydreams, and assorted random monkeywrenchings are quite common. It's one of the most chemically-complex times in the human mind, drenching the system in melatonin, serotonin, dopamine, adrenaline, various methyltryptamines, and much, much more in phenomenal quantities.

Decreasing the richness of her diet in tryptophan, tyrosine, and phenylalanine can make that whole span of consciousness go away; your girlfriend almost certainly has very, very good nutrition, if she's nodding-and-tripping nightly, without external drugs, like the human body was meant to.

Same thing happened to me, dude. Back about five years ago. Had to basically throw a good chunk of my life away to pull her out of it.

But it was worth it, because now we're fast friends as well as possible life-long lovers.

But it's a hard, nasty, horrible road to go down that nobody in their right mind would choose. It's one of those things thrust up on you, and, well, here you are.

My best advice, given that I've been through this exact same situation:

-Console her as much as she needs to be.

-Therapy is invaluable, both for her and for you. If she hasn't been yet, get her to a rape crisis center or something like that, and have her start therapy. It'll be hard for her, but much better for her in the long run.

-Therapy for youself as well. It's the best thing in the world to have an impartial, neutral party to talk to in order to help sort your thoughts out.

-Expect about a year or two before getting sexually involved. There's a lot of raw emotion and fear there. And start slowly. In my case, it took about six months of kissing/holding and moderate touching before we really got anywhere. It's therapy as anything.

-DO NOT put your own life on hold/give up. That's what the therapy for you is for.

-Understand that she's unstable and will be for quite some time. It takes years and years to get over something like that. Five years on and my girl is still feeling some effects.

-Get some mutual friends who can help you both out. I'd be even more of a wreck if I didn't have my friends to rely on.

Nobody should ever, ever be in this situation. Keep a positive attitude, a sense of humor, and remember that greatness never was a thing given to people.

Good luck. I'll be around to offer advice if you need it.
