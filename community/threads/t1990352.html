The important thing to keep in mind is that STIs (sexually transmitted infections) and HIV can still be transmitted while having sex in the water. Condoms are not tested for durability against the hot, wet chemicals found in pools and hot tubs. It is quite possible that condoms would deteriorate when placed in chlorine or heated water. In addition, oil-based products such as sunscreen, soaps, and shampoos could also cause condoms to break down. Other problems with wet and wild sex are that spermicide will most likely wash away in the water and water may also be able to seep between the condom and the penis causing the condom to slip off. As with all sexual encounters, it is recommended that you and your partner are tested for sexually transmitted infections before you become sexually intimate.

If you are still planning on having sex in water, its better to use some form of protection rather than none at all. The condom needs to be put on before getting into the water and you may want to look into using a silicone-based lubricant. Natural lubricants tend to get washed away while having sex in the water and this causes an increased amount of friction resulting in condom breakage.

Be safe in all of your splish-splash adventures!
