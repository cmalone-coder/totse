If you're at all worried go get tested. It can take a few weeks for symptoms to appear. (...)

Try three weeks to 6 months.

Consider -- if it's possible this woman has gonorrhea, it's possible she might also have HIV. Any test for HIV antibodies conducted earlier than six months after exposure might show negative despite that person actually having contracted the virus.

As I understand it, the odds of someone producing a false negative after six months are almost astronomically low. Almost as rare as catching HIV at random, I suppose, but if it were me, I'd want to be certain sure.

While the window period for most people is only three months, I wouldn't want to do something dumb like sound the all-clear after three months and a negative test, only to subsequently be proven wrong by the perversity of fate.

Depending on whether you've got free health care in your locale, I'd say you should do the following:

- Get tested now for everything under the sun.
- Optionally return for a test for the usually symptom-less STDs after four months from exposure (HIV, Hep B and C, Herpes, some forms of Chlamydia, et al.)
- Return for this test again at six months.

If you get the all-clear from your doctor after the six-month test (or if you do contract a curable STD and get it successfully cured), consider asking them for the usual letter stating that you're disease-free. Heck, if you've paid for the tests, you should at least get a little something for your trouble. Laminate that sucker. You never know when someone will ask to see it.
