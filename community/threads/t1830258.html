It sounds like you have a lot of time available. Why don't you shave off the match head material and use it in that form? If I was desparate, that's what I'd probably do.(more surface area, too)

Since you haven't stated what type(s) of match heads you're using..or rather attempting to use, here's a little match head compound primer:

quote:The friction match (or the stick match), has one end of the stick dipped in a fireproofing agent so that it will not burn readily and the other end is coated with paraffin. The head of the match contains an oxidizing agent such as potassium chlorate, a substance which oxidizes readily (here's your sulfur or rosin), a filler of clay, a binding material such as glue, dye to give it distinctive color and at the very tip, and a small amount of phosphorus trisulfide, which decomposes and burns at a low temperature, igniting the paraffin.

Safety matches are designed so that the head can be ignited only by striking on a specially prepared surface on the match package. The tip of the safety match contains antimony trisulfide and an oxidizing agent (such as potassium chlorate), again held in place with casein or glue. The difference is the striking surface on the package contains powdered glass for friction, red phosphorus and glue. When the match is struck the heat of friction converts the red phosphorus, which ignites and, in turn, ignites the head of the match.

source (http://www.energyquest.ca.gov/ask_quester/answers_general.html)

You may want to read EP's paper match tests page (http://krimzonpyro.com/ep/projectsdevicesdir/matchtest.html) for ideas and inspiration.

What you are trying to do can work.

Re: Cratermaker question
Me and my friend have made TONS of COB's. We almost always use a sparkler as a fuse, and used to use the 'BP' from rocket engines, until we got our hands on the real deal. If you are going to use the sparkler as a fuse the hole at the top of the co2 catridge can be made bigger by hacksawing the neck until the sparkler will fit in. In order to use the 'black powder' from the rocket engine you have to peel apart all the tubing (we used pliers) and then once you have that stick of black stuff, break the clay caps off of both the ends, and then crush the black hard tube up with a hammer or something hard. From there we always use a paper funnel to transfer the powder from the zip-lock to the cartridge. We roll a standard piece of computer paper up and then scotch tape it. On of us will hold the paper onto the COB and the other will pour in the powder ~3/4 full. (That's all you need) From there we stick the sparkler in after we have cut the metal part off, because if you try to bend it the sparkler will break apart, and your fuse is ruined. Once the thing is in we usually tape all around the sparkler where it sticks out of the COB. This is to ensure maximum safety, just in case of the freak chance that when you light the thing, a spark flies in the tiny gap where the sparkler goes into the COB and ignites the black powder. And another thing about rocket engine 'BP.' I do not think it is entirely black powder or what it is, because when a hole big enough for the sparkler is made the COB will make a loud sound and you could swear the casing has been torn apart, but almost 1/2 the time with black powder from rocket engines the casing is still intact. The COB will propel itself when a hole big enough for a sparkler to fit in is made! One time when we lit one it dug it's way straight underground! The soil was soft as it was right next to a marsh, but when I stuck my finger slowly in the hole I couldn't even feel the remnants! It probably lodged itself 6 inches to 2 feet under the earth! Be extremely careful when using metal casings of any sort, and always take the utmost precautions. If you are debating whether or not to save a little extra fuse by making the fuse shorter DONT! Lots of people across the states have accidents every single year due to these co2 bombs. I actually recomend that you use cardboard casings when making salutes, but I am not your father, and you can do whatever the fuck you want. Bottom line - be careful dude, we don't want you to get some shrapnel in any part of your body!

Re: Cratermaker question
finally, a question i can answer from experience...

for the matches, all you need is a 24 oz. bottle, some boiling water, and a cookie sheet-pan thing. first, cut the heads off of a box of matches, about 50 books worth is the max i found for this method. take the match heads and put them in the bottle, then fill about 3/4 of the way with boiling water. cap and shake vigorously for about 10 minutes, or until all but the cardboard has dissolved. filter this really fast, before anything settles out, and pour into the cookie sheet. let dry for a few days, and you have a cheap, relatively powerful powder for cobs.

as for the sparkler thing, i have found 2 things that work. if you're talking about morning-glory sparklers -- the ones with the powder in a cardboard tube -- the powder works well in cobs. just mix it all evenly, theres usually a few different powders in them. if they're the wire type sparklers, scrape the powder off about a gross of them. me and my cousin used to take about a gross worth of this powder and put it in a toilet paper tube with only one end capped. put a fuse in it, and you've got a simple fountain type device. its kind of expensive and time consuming, but when we created it we were 10 and really bored.

hope this helped...
