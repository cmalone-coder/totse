Re: is non-polar meth possible?
Please disregard all that was stated above. Especially that paragraph by angelstruth I have no idea where they learned that but it wasn't from an institute of higher learning.

Polarity has to do with the types of bonds that form in a molecule. The types of bonds are based on how electronegative the component atoms are that take part in the bond. Electronegativity is how strongly an atom holds onto electrons.

When two atoms have similar electronegative properties they form bonds that are based on equal(close to) sharing of electrons these are co-valent bonds. These bonds tend to be non-polar, or un-charged as the electron density is evenly distributed. Carbon-Nitrogen(as in meth) Carbon-Hydrogen, and Carbon-Halogen are all quite co-valent and are relatively nonpolar.

On the other hand you can have two atoms that are very different in electronegativity. Such as sodium and flourine, the flourine attracts the electron so strongly that it attains a negative charge and the sodium gets stuck with a deficiency and ends up positive. This is an ionic bond and is quite strong.

There are grey areas where things arent quite ionic but still have a dipole moment(unequal charge). The most common example is water. There is a dipole moment in water which gives it polar properties(one such property is that it freezes into a crystalline structure)

In the end when we talk about solvents, like dissolves like. A nonpolar solvent like hexane (just Carbon-Hydrogen bonds) will disolve nonpolar things like paradichlorobenzene. And water disolves ionic salts and some other solids that are in the grey area. Note that water dissolves some things poorly, this is because they have more nonpolar character but still have some polarity.

Now the money question: Meth freebase is an amine, amines are very slightly basic but because meth has a huge nonpolar area it will dissolve in non-polar solvents and not water. The hydrophobic(water hating) part of the molecule keeps it from disolving in water. Being a base it can react with acids to form salts. The salt is ionic due to the strong electronegativity of the acid. (The acid gives a proton to the amine base and thus makes itself negative and the amine nitrogen positive)Thsi ionic amine salt dissolves in water(or other polar solvent) freely.

Please note that there is very little that is purely 100% non-polar or 100% polar, most everything is on a sliding scale. And this is what allows us to to chromatography.

Any more questions or clarifications, feel free to ask.
