Joe, you pedo, I've been prescribed opiate based meds in the past what makes those different from heroin to allow for oral consumption?
Well, in the opiate derivatives (as opposed to wholly-synthetic opioid agonists, sub/fent/methadone/etc), my humble understanding is that it usually involves lipid solubility...

Morphine has one of the lower ends of oral bioavaliability, I think at around 20%. Paradoxically enough, I think codiene does a bit better, orally, which is opposite the lipophilia hypotheosis.

I think the oxidizatilon to a ketone (-morphone, -codone) increases absorbtion, as does (I think) the 14(?)-oxidization (oxy___). One would have to look up the bioavaliabilities again to be sure, but that's my vague impression/recollection which may be wrong...

So, the more hydrophilic oxygenization = good. On the other end, heroin is (a) actually kinda fragile as fuck, honestly. Not only does it deacetylate in the presence of moisture, acids, or bases, but the acetic acid can help decyclatize to produce trace amounts of apomorphine, not an opiate and not good...

'n (b), it's a completely-masked, heavily lipophilic molecule that won't absorb 'till it gets to damn near the end of the intestine, if at all. Since it can't survive long enough to make it to the end of your intestine... you're fucked. Though deacetylation to morphine, rather than heroin, can add a little consolation prize, with high enough quantity.

tl;dr : half of everything you look for in injecting, you try to avoid in swallowing.
