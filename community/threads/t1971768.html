Re: Time we abolish the penny
quote:Originally posted by Spade:

It costs money to make anything, whether or not the value of the coin/bill is more or less than how much it costs to make it. They don't just make new money and then spend it, that would make out money worthless. They destroy money as they make more.

OK, heres the scoop on the cost of penny-production...

To whoever quoted me saying I thought we lost money on the production/worth deal of pennies, I looked it up on several sources and heres what I found...

"The penny has by far the lowest seigniorage (profit) rate of any U.S. coin. Each costs four-fifths of a cent to make, netting Uncle Sam just one-fifth of a cent, or 20 percent. The profit on a quarter, by comparison, is more than 20 cents--80 percent. Were the penny still made mostly of copper, as it was until 1982, the government would have to manufacture them at a loss. (The coins are now copper-plated zinc.) The time has come to ask--hell, it came long ago--why are we doing this, anyway?" "

Anyway, although this shows that the US DOES indeed make money on the penny-producion (about 1/5 a penny each), I think if everything in the US were rounded to the nearest nickle, and taxes were rounded up (yea, yea, bitch and moan), most people would not care too much about the 2 or 3 cents more they pay annually on the "repaying of pennies" to the government, and it would replace the void of the "penny producion" financially (if not replace it, partially anyway).

Overall, though I was wrong about the cost of the making the modern penny, I still believe that since roughly 2/3 of all coinage produced are pennies, it would simplify daily finances to some degree (digging for pennies at the grocery store) and for several reasons metioned by Jim Benfield:

1) wrapping charges (the store pays about 60 cents for each roll of 50 pennies)

2) lost store productivity from penny users slowing the checkout line; and

3) lost wages paid to clerks counting pennies in the register on each shift.

Now, although these are small amounts we are talking about, such as the lost productivity from stores due to the time counting pennies, if you multiply that times the number of stores in the US paying taxes, times the number of days in a year, that adds up to alot for just a "penny" problem, doesnt it?



Heres a couple sources I used, or the best ones anyway: http://money.cnn.com/2002/04/11/pf/q_pennies/ http://www.straightdope.com/classics/a981009a.html
