Re: Best drug for music / Best music for drugs
I'm going to think carefully about this, so it will be (sonewhat) long.

Ok.

Prescription opiates (silence preferred for benzos):long, drawn-out, spacey instrumentals. Not shitty "jam-band" type though. Can, Fela Kuti.

For a few weeks, I was obsessed with Gorillaz, and listened to their self titled and remix CDs every time I smoked. Gorillaz was the default weed music. (that title, however, has now been transferred to the various theme music for Tetris and Super Mario Brothers.)

Uppers: Iggy Pop, Bob Marley (that makes no sense, but still I particularly enjoy it.) Electronic music--not trance/techno but bleeps-and-scratches type. Venetian Snares.

Ecstasy: Manitoba, Parliament/funkadelic/george clinton.

Acid: I really like to turn on this one freeform radio station in my area. One of those stations where it sounds like the person DJing/the people who created the music the DJ is playing must be on drugs as well, or have some kind of unchecked psychiatric disorder. Seriously, listening turns the whole trip into a more unpredictable adventure than it already is. They have a live audio stream at their website, wfmu.org, so you could try that experiment out if you wanted to.

Mushrooms, ketamine, pcp, crack/coke, h, opium, and DXM have felt/feel better for me with no music.

The funny thing is, sometimes the best "drug music" is not actually that interesting while on the drug expected. (With the exception of "Horses" by Patti Smith.) You know, I only listen to the Velvet Underground when I'm wishing/looking for/coming down from drugs, but never on them.

It really also depends on the situation and location. This list is just for "drugs at home" or "in an indoor location with one or zero other people." For "out," it is completely different.

Jeez, thinking about this just made me realize how much i HATE the Grateful Dead. Just thought I'd add that bit of cool, mostly irrelevant information to the end.
