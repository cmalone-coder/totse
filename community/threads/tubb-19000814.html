Re: Flash Powder?
Magnesium can be used and is indeedpreferable because it is more powerful. However, magnesium mixtures are usually more sensitive and thus more dangerous. Also, magnesium is usually more expensive than aluminum.

If you grind your magnesium into a fine enough powder you can use it in firecrackers. It will be hard to do so with hand tools, but with the right fuel and oxidizer mixture it is possible.

Re: Flash Powder?
You can't grind the stuff as best you could without tools. Use a ball mill with ceramic media and let it go for a few days, sieve out the proper mesh and re-mill the remaining Mg. It takes a while but works.

The most powerful flash powders I've seen were made of magnesium and chlorate or perchlorates as oxidizers. Exploding targets I believe are filled with one of the above mixtures as when burnt the tell tale chlorine smell exists. Mg-Chlorate flash powders are impact sensitive, yet make workable blasting caps in compound detonators or alone if the explosive is an easy to detonate variety.

If you don't have one, you can make a ball mill with a plastic bucket and a low rpm, high torque motor. Use a potentiometer or variable resistor to control speed settings. If you have a high enough torque motor you can set it up to turn the bucket at a 1:1 ratio. If not use a worm gear or reduce with a pulley. You will however grind the bucket away eventually, but they do the job. Speed to use on this set up is generally experimental, don't go so fast as too build up heat or you will grind way too much of your bucket away as well.

Your mixture, if you can get it fine enough, will work well in crackers.

explodes.

all of the chemicals separately. Sift
the oxidizer through a piece of screen
wire to break up the material into
small pieces. Then sift the other

through a piece of screen wire or by
