Re: speeding modem
When you say 56k, do you know what it actualy stands for? The K is not kilobytes, its kilobits. There are 8 bits in one byte. Your computer measures your speed in kilobytes, not kilobits. So take your download speed and times it by 8. This tells us that you can never download anything faster than 7kilobytes per second because 7kbytes = 56kbits. When I was on dialup I had occationaly gotten way above 7kbytes/s but I blame unaccurate software and inconsistant software.

A few years ago when dialup modems and the internet were becomming popular to the public (after Al Gore passed a law that allowed the public to have access to the internet), the phone company became concerned that the phone lines would get horribly clogged and cause errors resulting in no phone service. They stopped this before it happened by saying that at no time should anyone's phone line be using more than 64kbits of data per second, to ensure constant availability of phone service. When people hosted conference calls the data rate shot up massivly, this is why the phone company also put a restriction on this by saying that if there were more than 4 people connected that the phone company itself would host it (and charge you outragous fees as well). I am not entirely sure, but as I recall, this is also how ISDN came about, multiple phone lines all used together to make one connection. It multiplies the bandwidth by 6 (or however many lines you had) and didn't take up more than 64kbytes of data per line.

Modern day modems are not supposed to go over 56kbytes/sec even though the limit that the phone company put on it is only 64kbytes/sec. This ensures an overlap.

So to answer your question: yes you can rewire crap inside your modem to make it faster, but the phone company will catch you and crucify you.
