1 partition is all you need.
I prefer to allow these to exist:

/ (root director)
/home
/usr
/var
/tmp

/home is typically under 4.7 GB in order to back it all up to a DVD
/usr is about 6GB+ to install programs in
/var is about 4GB+ to download programs
/tmp is about 3GB+ for whatever misc. crap i might be playing with and needs that much

If anything, have the root and the home (two partitions).
/
/home

It's really nice to be able to backup /home onto a DVD.

You can install it onto one partition, but FreeBSD is much more enterprise-oriented, hence the recommendation. You can use it as a desktop, sure, but not as easily, and it doesn't get really cool or have any different features from Gnu/Linux until you start having enough hard drives to play with zfs/gmirror/gpool, or have tons of jails, or have "NEAR LINEAR SMP CAPABILITIES" or all of that.

BSD partitioning is slightly different than everything else. You choose a slice, and then make partitions within that slice. The slice is recognized as a single partition by most other things.
