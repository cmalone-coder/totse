Backyard Ballistics This forum is dedicated to the safe teaching and responsible handling of high and low explosives. You will not find any information on how to make "bombs" or any such device(s) . Backyard ballistics will teach you the science and the art of pyrotechnics and how to manufacture and use explosives/pyrotechnics safely and responsibly. Backyard ballistics is also a haven for rocketry and pneumatic cannons. All information contained within this forum is for educational purposes ONLY. Hey Bazooka. Long time no talk.

This guy's alright, folks, not a kewl, jsut needs a little direction.

Thermite's nature is very simple.

In it's most common form;

Al + Fe2O3 --&gt; Al2O3 + Fe + Heat

It just swaps the oxide from the iron to the aluminium.

To ignite it, use KMnO4 and Glycerine or Ethylene Glycol or a chlorate ignition mix.

Also, if you're willing to pay through the nose, UnitedNuclear.com sells a fairly decent thermite ignition mix.

And don't worry, we know all about what it's used for, no need to explain that. As it is, these days the military usually uses thermate instead.

I can't believe no one covered mesh / particle shape.

Thermite's performance varys greatly with the chemicals mesh. Very fine chemicals like 325mesh make a very easy to ignite thermite with a slag that doesn't last. Courser meshs are harded to ignite and produce longer lasting slag.

Plus you have to take into consideration the material that its on, standoff/no standoff, etc... you would have to do tests on your particular mix to really know.
