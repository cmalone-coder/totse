ALONGSIDE NIGHT, a novel by J. Neil Schulman. SoftServ Exclusive,
Cadenza Communications. © 1979 by J. Neil Schulman. In 1973,
shortly after interviewing Robert Heinlein, J. Neil Schulman got an
idea for a near-future story in which New York City was in such
economic collapse that even getting across town to a stash of gold was
a major problem. The resulting novel was published hardcover in 1979,
and reprinted twice in paperback. It received endorsements from Nobel-
laureate Milton Friedman and Anthony Burgess. In 1989, the Libertarian
Futurist Society awarded it a Prometheus "Hall of Fame" gold medallion
for classic works of fiction promoting liberty. In the years since
\Alongside Night\ was written, much of its short-term projections have
come to pass. But what of the novel's longer-term projections? Can a
book which ends with the collapse of the United States really have a
happy ending? J. Neil Schulman thought so in 1974, and he still does.
This book projects the collapse of the United States ... and a way for
America to save itself as a free land. If you're interested in seeing
liberty prevail, this book is a blueprint to the future. If you're
not ... it's the face of the nemesis you can't escape. "One of the most
widely hailed libertarian novels since the classic works of Ayn Rand."
--\Reason Magazine\

from being the son of a concert violinist and the husband of a popular
composer/performer -- to write a story about professional conflicts in
the world of \music\ -- not just a soap opera where the characters are
gratuitously musicians.
