To the Nth Degree - The A-Team returns. Lt. Barclay returns, but this time
his social problem is excessively hyped-up intelligence. Worf will appear in
tights.

Q-Pid - There isn't to be an episode called "Q and Order", its been killed
off. Instead, we have this one, in which Q taunts and teases Captain Picard
by bringing back the woman in "Captain's Holiday." Part of this plot involves
transporting the Enterprise crew to a planet, where they gets transformed
into the Robin Hood time. Only, whilst I don't know what Picard's reactions
are, apparently Q falls in love with Vash. Vash's thoughts on this may well
go undiscovered.

The Drumhead - Stars Jean Simmons (Elizabeth Stoddard in the new Dark
Shadows) as a Starfleet Admiral. This episode is being directed by Jonathon
Frakes, and is a satire on the McCarthy trials. Unfortunately, it sounds like
a boring episode, along the lines of LA Law meets TNG.

Half A Life (was: Civil Wars) - Guest Stars Majel Barrett as Lwaxana Troi and
David Orgen Stiers (from M*A*S*H)

Redemption - In the season finale, which Patrick Stewart directs, we see the
conclusion of the "Sins of the Father" trilogy. This will focus primarily on
Worf discommendation, and will be a cliffhanger. Sergei, Helena, and
Alexander are supposed to be returning. It will also feature the return of
Denise Crosby to Star Trek: The Next Generation. There are currently two
versions of Denise Crosby's return:

1. Supposedly, Tasha survived the battle (Yesterday's Enterprise), but was
kept in cryogenic stasis in order not to mess up the time lines. She will
appear in the final 30 seconds of this episode, and will be a member of
the fifth season. (This bit about Tasha's return from Fidonet, so be
warned.)

2. Denise Crosby returns, but not as Yar. It will be as a recurring guest
role, like Whoppie Goldberg. It may be as Tasha's other sister?!?!?!?

Operation SNAFU
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
"The Menagerie" - The ONLY doorknob seen in a Federation setting (ship or
starbase etc.) was on the door to Christopher Pike's quarters, which was kind
of ironic, since he's about the only person who COULDN'T use a doorknob! :-)

"Space Seed" - As Kirk is bashing in Khan's glass coffin, his phaser falls
off his belt. McCoy keeps looking down at it, like he's wondering when
they're going to yell 'cut' so they can re-shoot the scene. They never did
re-shoot because they didn't want to invest in more glass.

"Operation: Annihilate!" - In a well-known ST blooper, the amoeba-creature
accidentally hits Spock's read end instead of his back.

- Kirk mentions "One to the fourth power" (which the writers, or Shatner,
seemed to think sounded more impressive than "one") :-)

- James Doohan is missing the middle finger of his left hand. It can be seen
in brief shots (especially in the early episodes). Whenever they needed to
show Scotty's hands (like when he operated the transporter) they had a
stand-in and showed a close-up. ("Cut! All right, bring in the stunt hands.")

Ranks and Insignia
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Admiral - Triangular shoulder board with gold braid enclosing six gold
circles of varying size.

Rear Admiral - The same as an admiral, except with five gold circles of
varying size.

Branch Admiral - A color-coded triangle with the apex facing inward set on a
white shoulder board surrounded by silver oak leaves. The triangle colors
are:

Inspector Generals office - Silver
Medical Corps - Green
Security - Gold
Engineering - Red

Fleet Captain - Five solid gold circles
Commodore - Five solid silver circles
Captain - Four solid silver circles
Commander - three solid silver circles
Lieutenant Commander - two solid silver circles and one empty silver circle
Lieutenant - two solid silver circles
Lieutenant (Junior Grade) - One solid silver circle and one empty
Ensign - One solid silver circle
Ensign (Junior Grade) - One empty silver circle
Midshipman - One silver starburst worn on the right uniform lapel.

There are no enlisted ranks in Starfleet (at least, that's what Gene says!).
However, in "Family", Sergei Sarjenka (Worf's Father) mentions something
about meeting another Chief Petty Officer. Later on, in the same episode,
Sargei mentions that he, an enlisted man, managed to raise an officer. This
will indeed contradict what Gene has said.

In "Future Imperfect", some of the ranks in effect 15 years from season 4
are:

Admiral - Four Silver Bars
Captain - Four Gold Bars

The rank is part of the insignia/communicator.

In-Jokes
~~~~~~~~
Doctor Who: A cult British Science Fiction series which ran on BBC1 from 1963
to 1989. The title character is known only as "The Doctor", a time lord who
travels through time and space in a TARDIS (Time And Relative Dimensions In
Space). The TARDIS is bigger on the inside than on the outside, and can
change outward appearance, however, the Doctor's TARDIS, being a broken down
Type-40, is stuck in the shape of blue British Police Call Box. The Doctor
himself can, at point of death, regenerate into a totally different body and
personality. The series can be seen on several PBS stations and the (to be
launched in September or October, 1991) Sci-Fi Channel.

"The Neutral Zone" - On the family tree which is recalled by Troi, there is
references to Dr. Who, including: W. Hartnell m. P. Troughton, J. Pertwee m.
T. Baker, and P. Davison m. C. Baker.

"Transfigurations" - In the sick bay, against the wall, there is a outline of
the top portion of the TARDIS, upside down. Also, three roundals can be seen
against the wall. They show up again in several episodes after this one.

Lovely Angels/Dirty Pair: A Japanese anime series where the two main
characters, "Kei" and "Yuri", are members of World Welfare Foundation. Their
job is to take care of problems that crop up. However, they usually leave the
place in worse shape then they arrived, and it's usually not their fault. The
series itself has had TOS injokes in it.

"Loud As A Whisper" - The conference table, "made to resemble indigenous
rock", had various marking, among them, "Kei" and "Yuri".

"Icarus Factor" - The ship's gymnasium has lots of markings, including "Kei"
and "Yuri".

"Contagion" - The Iconian artifact has various marking on it, including "Kei
and Yuri" and "Dirty Pair"

"Measure Of A Man" - Part of Data's construction is made out of something
called "Yurium".

"Menage a Troi" - The Ferengi Security code begins with "Kei Yuri"

Other:
"Samaritan Snare" - Picard mentions "Nausicaans" in a conversation with
Wesley on the shuttlecraft.

"Icarus Factor" - The ship's gymnasium has markings that also include "Akira"
and "Tonari No Totoro". The scrolls hanging on the wall also say "Ataru",
"Lum", and "Uresai Yatsura".

"Contagion" - The Iconian artifact includes, besides Dirty Pair references,
"Gundam" and "Totoro."

"Measure Of A Man" - When Riker is showing off Data's arm to the JAG
representative, there is a pad on her desk which lists Data's part, including
"Nausican Valve" and "Totoro Interface."

"Up The Long Ladder" - When Picard is looking at the list of ships trying to
find the Mariposa, another ship listed is the Buckaroo Banzaii captained by
John Whorfin and built by the company that the red Lectroids had made. The
same company name was seen on the USS Hathaway (Yoyodyne Propulsion Systems
(of Grover's Mill, NJ :-) )

Operation SNAFU
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
"Datalore" - Upon beaming down to the planet's surface, Commander Riker gives
the stardate as 4124.5. A little later, Captain Picard gives it as 41242.45.

"Conspiracy" - Picard says "Mr. LaForge, ahead warp six." Geordi responds
with "Aye, sir, full impulse."

"The Royale" - The surface temperature of the planet is less than absolute
zero. Also, after they beam the piece of the ship out of orbit, O'Brien and
Riker pick it up with their bare hands (coming from space, it should have
been close to zero Kelvin itself).

"Sins of the Father" - The sound effects people must have fallen asleep every
time someone got slapped.

"Brothers" - The code which Data gives to lock the Enterprise computer system
differs between the spoken version and the version displayed on the screen.

"Reunion" - After K'Eyhlar dies, Alexander the child runs away, and Worf lets
out his primal scream, we see her body being lowered to the floor. As she is
finally settled, her eyes flutter open and closed several times (as,
presumably, the actress, Suzie Plakson, "gets her bearings"). Also notice
that her head is facing towards her left. However, when her body is shown
full-on from the perspective of her feet (as Worf tells Alexander to "look at
death, and never forget"), her head is facing slightly towards her *RIGHT*,
and remains that way for the rest of the scene (a prime example of a scene
being shot in several takes).
