"Animaniacs" is a cartoon from Steven Spielberg/Amblin Entertainment,
Warner Brothers and Fox Television, the same group that brought you
"Tiny Toon Adventures". Many of the people that worked on TTA
(Sherri Stoner, Tom Ruegger, Tress MacNeille, etc.) are working on
Animaniacs.

Animaniacs first aired on Monday, Sept 13th 1993 on Fox in what used to be
the Tiny Toon Adventures time-slot. 60 of the 65 episodes (none
have been reported lost to the BS&P) have aired on Fox television and
its afiliated stations. The remaining five episodes will air sometime
this May. Keep an eye on the AFEL for updates.

Frequently Asked Questions:

1. "What does Animaniacs abbreviate to?"

There is no official standard abbreviation for Animaniacs. Many have been
proposed but few have caught on. You are welcome to use one of your own
creation provided you don't post about abbreviations.

2. "Wraparound scmraparound. It's all filler!"

Many segments of the show are being considered "filler" and the argument of
the validity of this claim has consumed many megabytes of network
bandwidth. If you would like to continue the discussion, voice your
support for alt.fan.filler!

Frequently Disputed Lyrics:
---------------------------

"Animaniacs" -- "We have pay or play contracts" vs. "pay for play"

Reasons:
- It is printed as "pay or play" in the CD booklet.
- Sally Smith spoke with a director who said it was "or"
- A "pay or play" contract is a real thing. Such a contract means that
the holder will get paid wether or not their part is shown (in Hollywood)
or that the player will get paid even if they are not played (in
Football). Logically, it should be a "pay even without play" contract,
but it isn't.

"Chicken Boo" -- "You wear a disguise to look like human guys"
vs. "...look like human guise"

Reasons:
- "guys" makes sense: Chicken Boo is disguising himself as a person.
- "guise" is not correct syntactically, or even logically.

And for those who are not fluent in Japanese, here's Yakko's short

Yakko: Tokyo wa tottemo omoshiroi tokoro desu ne?
<Tokyo is an extremely interesting place, isn't it.>

<By all means, please visit.>

"Froinleven" is not a real word. Although it may sound German, do not let
it deceive you! It was specially designed by Jerry Lewis to have a
sedating effect, presumably part of one of Brain's plans to Take Over
The World.

Official Terminology:
---------------------

Cold Opener: A short animated sequence _before_ the opening song. For example,
the `Flipper' parody.

End Tag: The closing or parting shot of the water tower and lines. This
is the same as the "That's all folks!" from the Looney Tunes
shows.

Into Tower Closing: The parting segment featuring the water tower door.

Wacky Sack: Wakko's incredible omnipresent all inclusive prop bag. Also
called a "Gag Bag" by Yakko.

Wraparounds: Short segments placed between shorts and advertising.

Local Terminology:
------------------

The Variable Verse: The line in the theme song which changes.
(e.g. "How Urbaney", "Here's the show's namey")

Filler: Used to describe The Newsreel of the Stars and any other
piece of animation not listed in the sacred AFEL and assumed
to be put in the show to kill time.

Proof: Something which is never presented, yet is supposed to prove
the unbelievable.

The Warners:
------------

The Warners are not to be confused with the Two Tones. Here's a few
reasons if you still have any doubts:

The Warners:
* live in the Warner Brothers water tower
* are not a recognizable species
* have distinct ears and non-bushy tails
* wear coloured clothing
* are not black and white (Warners have red noses!)
* are zany
* are out of control

The Two Tones (Foxy & Roxy):
* Live in Two Tone Town, a suburb of Acme Acres
* are foxes
* have fox-like ears
* were "drawn in shades of gray"
* are generally predictable
* are usually quiet

The Warners do not fall into any general species category. They are not dogs,
cats, llamas, puppy-children, or even skunks. A complicated explanation is
that they are styled after the 1930's characters which were not meant to
be any species at all, only a humanized `inkblot' of sorts. If you want
the simple explanation, they're Warners (or just plain cute). One thing's
for sure: the Warners are very good at irritating people in the show.

Animaniacs, unlike the Tiny Toon Adventures episode of a similar name, does
NOT end with an exclamation point (!). Animaniacs is in no obvious way
related to "Animaniacs!".

Discussions about animation using a variety of techniques. Good for
general questions, but remember that many newsgroups exist for
specialized discussion (i.e. rec.arts.disney).

Discussion of another Amblin production, "Tiny Toon Adventures".
Several characters from TTA periodically show up in Animaniacs.
