Getting better quality with VHS copying

Video Quality in Editing
February 15, 1994
Moe Rubenzahl, Videonics, 76702,466

A certain amount of quality loss is inevitable whenever videotapes are copied, as they are in editinhis note discusses some ways to maximize the quality of your copies.

- Most important: Reduce the number of "generations" (successive copies). Whenever possible, plan yoditing so that the final results are second-generation (copies of the original) rather than thirdgnrtion (copies of a copy). - Important: Always use the fastest tape speed (SP). It will deliver much better results than slowereds.

- Use the best formats available. Super-VHS and Hi8 are better than VHS and 8-mm (and pro formats artter still).

- Use high-quality equipment.

- Many VCRs and camcorders have extra processing circuits designed to enhance the playback of tapes.s enhancement boosts the edges and makes the picture look sharper to some viewers. But it actuall erdes the overall quality and when a tape is copied, it can cause a major degradation of the finl eslt especially when used in successive generations. Look for a switch marked EDIT. Turn it ON to disable the processing circuitry that is designed for vng but tends to screw up copies. (Yes, it's confusing -- EDIT ON means the processing is disabledfreiting use.)

Look for a switch or knob marked SHARPNESS. Turn it off, or to its middle position.

- If all your equipment has S-video (Y/C) connectors, use them rather than composite (RCA-style) cotions. These will deliver moderately better color separation.

- Use decent cables. Use good-quality cables for connecting your equipment. It's not necessary to use fancy, very expensive cables with gold-plated connectors and big advertising claims, but cheap,fis cables can cause problems. Something in between is probably a good idea.

- Simplify connections. Anything you add to your setup is a potential source of problems. Avoid swits, enhancers, etc. that you don't need. Try making a copy with and without any intermediate equipett see if it is improving or damaging your quality.

- Use a time base corrector (TBC). A TBC will remove the jitter and wobbley action inherent in all Vand camcorders. This wobble is not too evident in the first generation but successive generationsgety increase the problem and may even cause color shifts and waving at the top of the picture (fagin).

The TBC will not usually affect the color or noise in the picture but its correction of the time basds quite a lot to the quality. I have seen fifth generation copies that were TBC'd at each step. hywre amazingly good (though it's still a good idea to avoid excess generations). Use the TBC at ac cpyng stage as it cannot clean up all the time base error introduced at earlier copying steps.
